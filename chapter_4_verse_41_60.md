## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:41

> So how will it be when We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours?

**Commentary:**

### Witnesses before Allah

The verse directs the imagination to the Day of Judgment, when Allah will bring a witness from every community and the Prophet ﷺ will be a witness concerning his community. The scene shows that human history is not forgotten or left without testimony. Every people received guidance, and their response will be brought into the open.

### The Prophet’s Witness

The Prophet ﷺ will testify that he conveyed the message to his community. His testimony is not based on guesswork or incomplete information; it is part of the truth Allah reveals on the Last Day. Those who followed the message will be honoured, while those who rejected or distorted it will face accountability.

The verse should make Muslims concerned about whether they actually follow the Messenger rather than merely claiming his name.

### Accountability of Communities

A faith-community is not saved by labels, ancestry, or history alone. Individuals and communities are judged by their response to truth, their treatment of revelation, and their conduct toward people. Religious knowledge increases responsibility when it is accompanied by the opportunity to act.

### A Serious Question

“How will it be?” is a question intended to awaken fear and preparation. The Day of Judgment will not be an abstract theory; it will be a day when every claim is tested and every hidden injustice is known. A believer prepares through faith, repentance, truthful conduct, and fulfilling trusts.

### Witnessing Truth in This Life

The verse also encourages believers to be truthful witnesses now. Muslims must not hide injustice, falsify testimony, or allow loyalty to a group to override truth. The Prophet ﷺ was a witness through conveying revelation and embodying its ethics; his followers should represent the message with honesty and mercy.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:41–42 on witnesses, the Prophet’s testimony, and the exposure of deeds.
- Qur’an 2:143 and 22:78 on the Muslim community’s responsibility to bear witness to humanity.
- Qur’an 16:89 on a messenger as a witness over his people.
- Qur’an 5:109 and 28:75 on witnesses being brought from every nation.
- Qur’an 24:24 and 36:65 on the body and limbs testifying on the Day of Resurrection.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on truthful testimony and justice.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the Prophet ﷺ conveying the message and bearing witness concerning his community.
- For *shahīd*, *shahādah*, *ummāh*, *rasūl*, and the eschatological witness, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:41.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:42

> On that Day, those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust. And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah.

**Commentary:**

### Regret on the Day of Judgement

Those who denied Allah and disobeyed His Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust. This expression conveys extreme regret and the desire to escape the exposure of judgement. They will see the truth clearly but will no longer have the opportunity to return and change their deeds.

The verse is a warning to the living, while the door of repentance remains open.

### Revelation Cannot Be Hidden

People may conceal disbelief, betrayal, or injustice from other human beings, but they cannot hide anything from Allah. His knowledge includes public actions, private motives, unspoken calculations, and harm that victims were unable to prove.

The final judgement therefore corrects the limitations of worldly courts and public reputation.

### The Messenger’s Authority

The verse joins denial of Allah with disobedience to the Messenger ﷺ because the Messenger conveys and explains Allah’s command. Obeying him is not worship of a human being; it is obedience to the revelation entrusted to him.

Rejecting his guidance after it becomes clear is a spiritual and moral loss.

### No Escape through Excuses

On that Day, false narratives and social status will not protect anyone. A person cannot blame a community, claim that he was only following others, or hide behind wealth and office. Allah knows the degree of knowledge and choice each person possessed.

This should make believers humble rather than eager to condemn particular people.

### Preparing before Exposure

A wise person reviews his conduct before the Day when every hidden thing is exposed. He seeks forgiveness, returns rights, corrects false testimony, and repairs relationships while opportunities remain.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:41–42 on witnesses, regret, and the impossibility of hiding from Allah.
- Qur’an 78:40 and 69:18 on the disbeliever’s regret and the exposure of deeds.
- Qur’an 18:49 and 36:65 on the complete record and testimony of the body.
- Qur’an 2:170 and 33:36 on obeying Allah and the Messenger rather than following custom or preference.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on repentance before the final opportunity ends.
- Qur’an 6:164 on individual responsibility and no soul bearing another’s burden.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the gathering, accountability, and the Prophet’s intercession by Allah’s permission.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:43

> O believers! Do not approach prayer while intoxicated[199] until you are aware of what you say, nor in a state of ˹full˺ impurity[200]—unless you merely pass through ˹the mosque˺—until you have bathed. But if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved yourselves, or been intimate with your wives and cannot find water, then purify yourselves with clean earth, wiping your faces and hands.[201] And Allah is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving.

**Commentary:**

### Conscious Prayer

Prayer requires awareness of what is being said and done. The verse first prohibited approaching prayer while intoxicated until the worshipper could understand his words. This was part of the gradual legislation that culminated in the complete prohibition of intoxicants in Qur’an 5:90–91.

The current Islamic ruling is that intoxicants are forbidden, not that a person may drink outside prayer. The verse’s immediate lesson is that prayer must not be treated as empty movement without consciousness.

### Full Ritual Impurity

The verse also addresses *janābah*, the state of full ritual impurity after sexual intercourse or ejaculation. The required purification is *ghusl*, a complete ritual bath. The ruling teaches that intimacy within marriage is lawful while also establishing a clear preparation for worship.

The exception for merely passing through a mosque concerns necessity and transit, not using the mosque as a place to remain while in a state requiring ghusl.

### Purification Is Not a Burden

When a person is ill, travelling, has relieved himself, or has been intimate and cannot find water, Allah provides *tayammum*: purifying with clean earth by wiping the face and hands. This concession shows that ritual purity is important, but Allah does not demand what a person cannot safely perform.

Scholars explain the details of water availability, harm from its use, the type of earth, and the duration of the concession through the Sunnah and jurisprudence.

### Body and Heart

Physical purification prepares the body for standing before Allah, while repentance and sincerity purify the heart. Neither should replace the other. A person may wash perfectly but remain unjust, and another may have a sincere heart while still needing to fulfil the outward requirements of prayer.

### Divine Pardon and Mercy

The verse closes with Allah’s pardon and forgiveness. The concessions are not loopholes for laziness; they are mercy for genuine difficulty. Believers should learn the rulings carefully and use the ease Allah granted without manipulating it.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:43 and 5:90–91 on sobriety, intoxicants, and conscious worship.
- Qur’an 5:6 on ablution, full bathing, and *tayammum*.
- Qur’an 2:222 on purification and Allah’s love for those who purify themselves.
- Qur’an 9:108 on the mosque of those who love purification.
- Qur’an 2:185 and 22:78 on ease and the absence of hardship in religion.
- Qur’an 24:58 on household privacy and times of rest and vulnerability.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on *wuḍūʾ*, *ghusl*, *tayammum*, and the gradual prohibition of intoxicants.
- For *sukr*, *janābah*, *ghusl*, *tayammum*, *ṣaʿīd ṭayyib*, and ritual and moral purification, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:43.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:44

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet trade it for misguidance and wish to see you deviate from the ˹Right˺ Path?

**Commentary:**

### Possessing Part of Scripture

The verse addresses people who had received a portion of Scripture and therefore possessed religious knowledge, yet exchanged guidance for misguidance. Knowledge by itself does not guarantee obedience. A person can quote revelation while selectively ignoring its demands.

The wording “a portion” also reminds believers that incomplete knowledge can create false confidence when people speak without studying the whole message.

### Trading Guidance for Misguidance

The language of trade describes a deliberate exchange. They gave up truth for influence, factional loyalty, worldly status, or the preservation of old power. Misguidance may be attractive when it protects wealth or keeps an audience satisfied, but its apparent gain is temporary.

The same warning applies to anyone who sells religious integrity for political access, money, popularity, or sectarian advantage.

### Wanting Others to Deviate

Their problem was not only personal rejection. They wished to see the Prophet ﷺ and the believers leave the Straight Path. A person may feel threatened when others embrace guidance because their example exposes the emptiness of his own compromise.

Believers should resist this pressure without responding with injustice or hatred toward every member of another community.

### Knowledge and Integrity

True scholarship requires honesty about evidence, humility about limits, and willingness to follow truth even when it challenges one’s group. Teachers must not conceal a ruling, manipulate a quotation, or present personal preference as Allah’s command.

### The Right Path

The Straight Path, *al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*, is defined by worshipping Allah, obeying His Messenger, justice, mercy, and moral consistency. Deviation may occur through an obvious rejection or through gradual compromise that makes the self the final authority.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:44–45 on trading Scripture for misguidance and Allah’s protection from enemies.
- Qur’an 2:41–42 and 3:78 on concealing, distorting, or mixing truth with falsehood.
- Qur’an 5:44–48 on judging by revelation and the Qur’an as a criterion.
- Qur’an 3:187 on the covenant to make Scripture known and not hide it.
- Qur’an 16:125 and 41:33 on calling to Allah with wisdom and excellence.
- Qur’an 1:6–7 on asking for the Straight Path and avoiding paths of deviation.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on conveying knowledge honestly, avoiding religious deception, and following the Prophet’s guidance.
- For *u tū al-kitāb*, *ishtarā*, *ḍalāl*, *hidāyah*, and trading religious truth for worldly advantage, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:44.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:45

> Allah knows best who your enemies are! And Allah is sufficient as a Guardian, and He is sufficient as a Helper.

**Commentary:**

### Allah Knows Hidden Hostility

Allah knows best who the enemies of the believers are. Human beings see outward speech and public alliances, but Allah knows concealed motives, plans, betrayals, and the difference between honest disagreement and deliberate hostility.

This knowledge calls believers to caution without giving them permission to accuse people on speculation.

### Guardian and Helper

Allah is sufficient as a Guardian, *walī*, and Helper, *naṣīr*. His guardianship includes guidance, protection, moral clarity, and care. His help may appear as victory, patience, exposure of deception, or preservation of faith when enemies seek to distort it.

Created people can assist, but no human ally can replace Allah or guarantee the final outcome.

### Caution with Justice

The surrounding verses speak about people who trade Scripture for misguidance and wish believers to deviate. The warning concerns real spiritual and political influence. It does not require Muslims to treat every non-Muslim, critic, or neighbour as an enemy.

The Qur’an commands justice toward peaceful people and requires evidence before serious accusations.

### Protection from Manipulation

When hostility is hidden, believers need knowledge, consultation, truthful reporting, and patience. They should not be driven by rumours or emotional propaganda. Reliance upon Allah strengthens discernment rather than replacing it.

### A Source of Reassurance

The verse reassures the Prophet ﷺ and the believers that revelation cannot be defeated by those who distort it. Even when opponents possess wealth, institutions, or influence, Allah remains the final Protector of His servants and His guidance.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:44–46 on hidden enemies, distortion, and Allah’s protection.
- Qur’an 8:40 and 3:150 on Allah as Guardian and Helper.
- Qur’an 60:8–9 on kindness and justice toward peaceful and hostile groups.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports before taking action.
- Qur’an 5:8 on maintaining justice even toward those one dislikes.
- Qur’an 3:173 and 9:51 on reliance upon Allah in the face of threats.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on reliance, caution, treaties, and the Prophet’s just dealings with opponents.
- For *walī*, *naṣīr*, *ʿadāwah*, hidden hostility, and divine protection, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:45.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:46

> Some Jews take words out of context and say, “We listen and we disobey,” “Hear! May you never hear,” and “Râ’ina!” [Herd us!]—playing with words and discrediting the faith. Had they said ˹courteously˺, “We hear and obey,” “Listen to us,” and “Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!] it would have been better for them and more proper. Allah has condemned them for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few.

**Commentary:**

### Distorting Speech

The verse describes some Jews who twisted words and used ambiguous expressions to mock or insult the Prophet ﷺ. Their speech appeared outwardly acceptable to listeners but carried a hostile meaning in their intention. This is *layy al-lisān*, bending the tongue away from honest communication.

The Qur’an criticizes the behaviour of particular people, not every Jew. Elsewhere it recognizes upright members of the People of the Book.

### The Danger of Ambiguous Language

The word *rāʿinā* could be understood in Arabic as “pay attention to us,” but it also resembled an insulting expression used by those who wished to mock the Prophet. Believers were taught to use the clearer and more respectful *unẓurnā*, “look toward us” or “give us time.”

The lesson extends beyond one word: when a phrase can be used to insult, deceive, or provoke, a believer chooses clearer language.

### “We Listen and Disobey”

Their statement reverses the proper response to revelation. Believers say, “We hear and obey,” not because they abandon reason, but because they trust Allah’s wisdom after the truth is clear. Pretending to listen while secretly planning disobedience is hypocrisy.

### Respectful Speech to the Messenger

The Prophet ﷺ deserved respectful speech because he conveyed Allah’s message. The Qur’an also teaches believers to speak courteously to one another. Mockery, coded insults, and deliberate misrepresentation damage trust and can turn disagreement into hostility.

### A Few Believe

The verse says they believe only a few, while other passages clarify that the People of the Book are not all alike. This preserves the balance between condemning rejection and avoiding collective guilt. Allah judges individuals by their response to truth and their deeds.

### Communication Ethics Today

Modern forms include clipped quotations, sarcastic ambiguity, edited recordings, misleading headlines, and coded slurs. A believer should quote fairly, clarify meaning, and avoid language designed to create offence while preserving deniability.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:46 and 2:104 on respectful speech and avoiding ambiguous expressions used for insult.
- Qur’an 3:78 on twisting Scripture with the tongue and presenting falsehood as revelation.
- Qur’an 49:11–12 on mockery, offensive names, suspicion, and backbiting.
- Qur’an 58:8 and 9:74 on secret speech, insults, and concealed hostility.
- Qur’an 3:113–115 and 5:66 on upright people among the People of the Book.
- Qur’an 33:70 on speaking words that are straight and true.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on using respectful forms of address with the Prophet ﷺ and avoiding speech that can be used as insult.
- For *rāʿinā*, *unẓurnā*, *layy al-lisān*, *samʿ*, *ṭāʿah*, and the ethics of clear speech, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:46.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:47

> O you who were given the Book! Believe in what We have revealed— confirming your own Scriptures—before We wipe out ˹your˺ faces, turning them backwards, or We condemn the defiant as We did to the Sabbath-breakers.[203] And Allah’s command is always executed!

**Commentary:**

### A Call to Recognize Revelation

Allah calls the People of the Book to believe in the Qur’an, which confirms the original revelation they received. The appeal is not to abandon the true message of Moses or Jesus; it is to recognize the final revelation that confirms their prophetic foundation and corrects later distortion.

The call is urgent because religious knowledge carries responsibility. Those who know the language of revelation and its promises should not reject truth merely because it comes through a messenger outside their preferred group.

### A Severe Warning

The warning about faces being wiped out and turned backwards conveys humiliation, loss of identity, and divine punishment. Classical commentators discuss its literal and figurative dimensions. In either case, the verse warns against stubborn defiance after clear truth has arrived.

It is not a command for Muslims to harm or mutilate people. It describes Allah’s judgement and power, not a human licence for violence.

### The Sabbath-Breakers

The reference recalls those who violated Allah’s command concerning the Sabbath, described in Qur’an 7:163–166. Their story demonstrates that sacred law cannot be manipulated through technical tricks while the heart intends disobedience.

External religious forms do not protect a person who deliberately undermines their purpose.

### “Allah’s Command Is Executed”

Allah’s command cannot be frustrated by mockery, political power, or religious argument. His warning may be delayed by mercy, but it is never defeated. The believer should respond to warnings through repentance rather than testing the limits of divine forbearance.

### Respect and Distinction

The verse maintains theological clarity while still addressing the People of the Book directly. Muslims are to believe in the Qur’an and its final authority, while treating people justly and recognizing that individuals among earlier communities vary in sincerity.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:46–48 on distortion, belief in the Qur’an, shirk, and divine punishment.
- Qur’an 2:40–42 and 3:81 on the covenant to recognize later revelation and not conceal truth.
- Qur’an 5:44–48 on the Torah, Gospel, and Qur’an as revelation and criterion.
- Qur’an 7:163–166 on the Sabbath-breakers and the consequence of defiance.
- Qur’an 59:2 and 66:6 on Allah’s power, warning, and punishment.
- Qur’an 60:8 on justice toward those who are peaceful despite theological disagreement.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on believing in all revealed Books and messengers and on the seriousness of violating sacred commands.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:48

> Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating others with Him ˹in worship˺, [204] but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. And whoever associates others with Allah has indeed committed a grave sin.

**Commentary:**

### The Gravity of Shirk

The verse identifies *shirk*—giving any share of Allah’s exclusive worship, divinity, or lordship to another—as the gravest sin. Tawḥīd belongs to Allah alone. No prophet, angel, saint, idol, power, wealth, desire, or ideology may receive worship that is due only to Him.

Shirk is grave because it overturns the foundation of the relationship between Creator and creation.

### The Condition of Dying without Repentance

The statement that Allah does not forgive shirk concerns a person who dies persisting in it without repentance. If someone turns sincerely to Allah before death, the door of repentance is open. Islam therefore calls idolaters and all people toward tawḥīd rather than declaring repentance impossible during life.

### Other Sins Are under Allah’s Will

Allah may forgive sins other than shirk for whoever He wills. This gives hope but does not make other sins harmless. Murder, oppression, theft, sexual abuse, financial betrayal, and false testimony remain serious, especially when the rights of people are involved.

Forgiveness may require repentance, and human rights must be restored where possible.

### Allah’s Right and People’s Rights

Shirk primarily violates Allah’s exclusive right to worship. Sins against people add another dimension: the wrongdoer must seek the victim’s forgiveness or restore the right. A person cannot claim that private worship cancels public injustice.

### A Grave Sin, Not a Licence for Takfīr

The verse condemns the belief and act of shirk, but Muslims must not recklessly label individuals disbelievers. Judging a particular person requires knowledge of what they understood, intended, and persisted in. Final judgement belongs to Allah.

### Mercy and Warning Together

The verse balances the greatest warning with the greatest invitation. Tawḥīd is not a distant theory; it is a call to return to the One who created and sustains every soul. Fear of shirk should produce sincerity and humility, not despair or hatred of people.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:48, 4:116, and 39:53–54 on shirk, forgiveness, repentance, and Allah’s mercy.
- Qur’an 2:21–22 and 17:22–39 on worshipping Allah alone and avoiding rival deities.
- Qur’an 31:13 on shirk as a tremendous wrong.
- Qur’an 6:151 and 5:72 on the prohibition of associating partners with Allah.
- Qur’an 25:68–71 on repentance after even grave sins.
- Qur’an 6:108 and 49:11–12 on avoiding reckless abuse and judgement of people.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the rights of tawḥīd, the gravity of shirk, and the acceptance of sincere repentance before death.
- For *shirk*, *tawḥīd*, *maghfirah*, *kabīrah*, and the condition of dying without repentance, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:48.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:49

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who ˹falsely˺ elevate themselves?

> It is Allah who elevates whoever He wills. And none will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone.

**Commentary:**

### False Self-Purification

The verse challenges people who falsely elevate themselves, claiming superior purity, righteousness, or spiritual rank. The Arabic idea of *tazkiyah* can mean purification, but human self-certification is not the same as Allah’s declaration of a person’s virtue.

A person may speak about humility while despising others, or claim religious superiority while violating people’s rights.

### Allah Is the One Who Elevates

Allah elevates whom He wills through faith, sincerity, knowledge, patience, service, and good character. Human praise can be mistaken, purchased, or manipulated. Allah’s elevation is based on reality, and it may not be visible in worldly status.

The believer seeks Allah’s acceptance rather than constructing a public image of holiness.

### No One Is Wronged

The thread of a date stone, *fatīl*, is an image of the smallest conceivable amount. Allah will not deprive anyone of the slightest reward or impose the slightest unjust punishment. This assures the humble believer whose goodness is overlooked and warns the person who relies upon claims that cannot withstand divine examination.

### Humility and Healthy Self-Assessment

Humility does not require denying a genuine skill, accepting false accusations, or refusing to acknowledge a blessing. A person may state a qualification when necessary without turning it into arrogance. The danger is claiming moral superiority and using it to silence correction.

The better response to a blessing is gratitude and responsibility.

### Religious and Social Applications

Communities should be cautious of titles, reputations, family prestige, and public displays that replace actual character. Scholars, leaders, and charitable figures must welcome accountability. Ordinary believers should avoid judging their own salvation or another person’s final rank.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:49–50 on false self-purification, divine elevation, and fabricating lies.

- Qur’an 49:13 on honour through taqwā rather than ancestry or status.
- Qur’an 58:11 on Allah raising believers and those given knowledge.
- Qur’an 16:74 and 2:264 on avoiding arrogance and the destruction of deeds through self-display.
- Qur’an 99:7–8 and 18:49 on the precise record of every deed.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on humility, avoiding praise of oneself, and Allah’s judgement of intentions and deeds.
- For *yuzakkū*, *tazkiyah*, *fatīl*, *rifʿah*, and the difference between divine elevation and self-righteousness, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:49.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:50

> See how they fabricate lies against Allah—this alone is a blatant sin.

**Commentary:**

### Inventing Claims about Allah

The verse exposes the seriousness of fabricating lies against Allah. This includes claiming that Allah has declared someone pure without revelation, attributing personal customs to divine law, inventing doctrines, and presenting false interpretations as Allah’s command.

Lying about Allah is especially dangerous because it can mislead many people and make human preference appear sacred.

### Self-Righteousness as Falsehood

In context, the lie concerns people who falsely elevated themselves. They may have claimed automatic salvation because of ancestry, religious identity, or membership in a particular group. The Qur’an rejects the idea that a person can certify himself before Allah while ignoring faith and conduct.

### Speaking about Religion Carefully

The Qur’an warns believers not to say “this is lawful and this is forbidden” without knowledge. Religious speech requires evidence, humility, and awareness of context. A teacher should distinguish revelation from interpretation, and interpretation from personal opinion.

### A Blatant Sin

The sin is *ithm mubīn*, a clear and manifest wrongdoing. It harms both the speaker and those who trust him. False religious claims can justify discrimination, financial exploitation, violence, or despair, which increases the seriousness of the offence.

### Repentance and Correction

Someone who has attributed falsehood to Allah must repent by withdrawing the claim, correcting those misled, and repairing any harm caused. Silent private regret is not enough when the false teaching was public.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 7:33 on speaking about Allah without knowledge and declaring unlawful things without authority.
- Qur’an 10:59 and 16:116 on inventing lawful and unlawful rulings.
- Qur’an 2:79 on writing religious claims and attributing them to Allah for gain.
- Qur’an 24:15–16 on spreading unverified statements and treating them lightly.
- Qur’an 53:32 on Allah knowing who is truly pure.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:51

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet believe in idols and false gods and reassure the disbelievers[205] that they are better guided than the believers?

**Commentary:**

### Religious Knowledge without Faithfulness

The verse describes people who possess part of Scripture yet believe in idols and false authorities. Knowledge becomes a proof against a person when it is used to justify falsehood rather than to follow truth. A religious label cannot protect someone who abandons the foundations of worship.

The criticism concerns particular people and behaviour; it is not a judgement upon every member of the People of the Book.

### Idols and False Authorities

“Idols and false gods” translates concepts such as *jibt* and *ṭāghūt*: false objects of worship, superstition, sorcery, and authorities obeyed in opposition to Allah. Modern forms can include political power, wealth, ideology, celebrity, or an institution treated as the final source of right and wrong.

Respecting lawful authority is different from giving it Allah’s exclusive rights.

### Corrupting the Moral Compass

The people praised disbelievers as better guided than the believers. This was not an innocent difference of opinion; it was a distortion that favoured power and hostility over revelation. A person may betray truth in order to gain political protection, social influence, or material advantage.

The warning applies whenever religious language is used to flatter oppression or portray faithfulness as backward and falsehood as superior.

### Honest Interfaith Relations

The verse does not forbid fair cooperation or respectful dialogue with non-Muslims. Muslims can recognize useful knowledge, honour peaceful neighbours, and cooperate in goodness while maintaining clarity about tawḥīd. The problem is praising disbelief or injustice as guidance when one knows it contradicts truth.

### Testing Religious Authority

Believers should examine claims by revelation, evidence, justice, and character rather than assuming that a person is correct because he has a title or inherited tradition. Knowledge should produce humility and obedience, not manipulation of the ignorant.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:48–52 on shirk, self-righteousness, false authorities, and Allah’s judgement.
- Qur’an 2:165 and 31:13 on giving love and worship due to Allah to false rivals.
- Qur’an 16:36 and 39:17 on avoiding *ṭāghūt* and worshipping Allah alone.
- Qur’an 5:44–50 on judging by revelation rather than desire or corrupt authority.
- Qur’an 60:8–9 and 29:46 on justice and respectful dialogue with peaceful People of the Book.
- Qur’an 22:30 on avoiding idols and false speech.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:52

> It is they who have been condemned by Allah. And whoever is condemned by Allah will have no helper.

**Commentary:**

### Divine Condemnation

The verse states that those who corrupted religious truth and supported falsehood have been condemned by Allah. Divine condemnation, *laʿnah*, means being excluded from Allah’s mercy because of persistent rejection and wrongdoing. It is not a casual insult or a licence for believers to curse people indiscriminately.

### No Created Power Can Rescue

Whoever Allah condemns will have no helper capable of overriding His judgement. Political alliances, wealth, religious status, family connections, and flattering supporters cannot rescue a person from accountability. A false authority may provide temporary protection in this life, but it cannot control the Hereafter.

### The Danger of Moral Reversal

The preceding verse described praising disbelievers as better guided than believers while holding Scripture. The punishment reflects a moral reversal: people who should have recognized truth instead used knowledge to defend falsehood and elevate oppressive power.

### Repentance before the End

The warning concerns those who persist until death. During life, repentance remains open. A person who abandons false worship, rejects injustice, and returns sincerely to Allah may receive forgiveness. The believer should fear condemnation for himself while hoping for guidance for others.

### Human Judgement Has Limits

Muslims may condemn an idea or an action as false and sinful, but should be cautious about declaring a particular person permanently condemned. Allah knows what evidence reached them, what they understood, whether they were coerced, and whether they repented.

- Qur’an 4:51–52 on false authorities, corrupted judgement, divine condemnation, and the absence of a rescuer.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:53

> Do they have control over shares of the kingdom? If so, they would not have given anyone so much as the speck on a date stone.

**Commentary:**

### Exposing Envy

Allah challenges those who envied the Prophet ﷺ and the believers: do they control any share of Allah’s kingdom? If they possessed such control, their miserliness would prevent them from giving anyone even the smallest speck attached to a date stone.

The rhetorical question exposes the contradiction of resenting Allah’s favour while lacking the power to distribute even a particle of provision.

### Allah Owns All Provision

The kingdom, *mulk*, belongs to Allah. Knowledge, revelation, authority, wealth, and opportunity are distributed by His wisdom. Human beings may receive, manage, and share resources, but they are not absolute owners independent of Him.

This should make the prosperous grateful and the deprived hopeful, while requiring the powerful to use their temporary authority responsibly.

### Envy Is Not Justice

The problem is not merely that they disliked someone else receiving a blessing. Their envy led them to resist truth and to wish that others lose guidance. *Ḥasad* corrupts the heart by treating another person’s blessing as a personal injury.

The cure is to ask Allah for lawful bounty, bless the other person, and focus on one’s own duties.

### Stinginess and Power

The verse links envy with miserliness. People who resent Allah’s gifts often become unable to share even what they have. A leader who hoards opportunities may block education, employment, recognition, or public resources in order to preserve status.

Justice requires opening access and returning rights, not treating every benefit as personal property.

### Revelation Is a Mercy

The favour given to the descendants of Abraham in the next verse includes Scripture, wisdom, and authority. Revelation is a blessing meant to guide humanity, not a possession that a religious elite may restrict to themselves.

- Qur’an 4:52–54 on condemnation, kingdom, envy, and Allah’s favours.

- Qur’an 16:71 and 43:32 on differences in provision and the distribution of worldly means.
- Qur’an 113:5 on seeking refuge from the evil of envy.
- Qur’an 59:7 on preventing wealth from circulating only among the rich.
- Qur’an 57:7 and 64:15–17 on stewardship and spending from Allah’s provision.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports warning against envy and praising generosity and sharing Allah’s blessings.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:54

> Or do they envy the people for Allah’s bounties? Indeed, We have given the descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom, along with great authority.

**Commentary:**

### Envy of Divine Favour

The verse asks whether the opposition arose from envy of the favour Allah gave to Muhammad ﷺ and the believers. Envy can make a person resent guidance when it comes through someone outside his expected lineage, tribe, or religious leadership. Instead of celebrating truth, the envious person wants the blessing removed.

### The Legacy of Abraham

Allah recalls that the descendants of Abraham received the Book, wisdom, and great authority. This includes the prophetic and scriptural legacy given through the families of Isaac, Jacob, Moses, David, and others, as well as the blessing associated with Ismāʿīl and the final Messenger ﷺ.

The reference shows that Allah has always chosen and blessed messengers by His wisdom. No group can claim that divine guidance is restricted to its own preferences.

### Book, Wisdom, and Authority

The Book is revealed guidance; wisdom, *ḥikmah*, is sound understanding and faithful application; authority, *mulk*, may include prophethood, leadership, evidence, and the ability to establish justice. These gifts are responsibilities, not badges of automatic superiority.

A person or community given knowledge must use it to guide and serve, not to hoard status or oppress others.

### Blessing Is Not a Zero-Sum Loss

Allah’s favour upon one people does not reduce His ability to bless another. The envious imagine that another person’s guidance diminishes them, but Allah’s bounty is unlimited. The correct response to someone’s blessing is gratitude, cooperation in good, and asking Allah for one’s own lawful share.

### Lineage Does Not Replace Faith

Being descended from Abraham does not guarantee righteousness. The Qur’an praises people from those communities who believe and condemns those who reject truth. Honour comes through faith and obedience, not genealogy alone.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:53–55 on envy, Allah’s kingdom, the descendants of Abraham, and rejection.
- Qur’an 2:47 and 2:122 on blessings to the Children of Israel and their responsibility.

- Qur’an 6:84–89 on the prophets given the Book, wisdom, and authority.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:55

> Yet some believed in him while others turned away from him.[206] Hell is sufficient as a torment!

**Commentary:**

### Different Responses to Revelation

Among those connected to the scriptural legacy of Abraham, some believed in the messenger and revelation while others turned away. Classical commentators commonly understand the immediate reference as people who believed in Muhammad ﷺ and others who rejected him, though the wording also captures the wider pattern of accepting or rejecting divine guidance.

Shared scripture and religious history do not guarantee a shared response.

### Belief Requires Submission

Belief is more than recognizing a messenger’s name or admiring a tradition. It requires accepting truth, submitting to Allah, and allowing revelation to correct inherited prejudice. A person may possess knowledge of prophecy yet reject its fulfilment because of pride or rivalry.

### Turning Away

Turning away can involve explicit denial, deliberate concealment, mockery, or refusing to follow guidance after recognizing it. Allah knows the evidence each person received and the motives behind rejection. Muslims should therefore condemn falsehood without claiming knowledge of every individual’s final state.

### Hell Is Sufficient as a Warning

Hell is described as sufficient punishment, meaning that no additional threat is needed to show the seriousness of persistent rejection and wrongdoing. The short verse creates a sober pause after mentioning envy, authority, and the division between believers and rejecters.

The warning is intended to move the living toward repentance before death.

### Lineage and Choice

A person cannot rely on descent from Abraham, association with a prophet, or membership in a religious community while turning away from truth. Honourable lineage is a blessing only when joined to faith and righteous conduct.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:56

> Surely those who reject Our signs, We will cast them into the Fire.

> Whenever their skin is burnt completely, We will replace it so they will ˹constantly˺ taste the punishment. Indeed, Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### Rejecting Allah’s Signs

Allah warns those who reject His signs, *āyāt*. These signs include revelation, the messengers, the evidence within creation, and the moral reminders placed before humanity. The warning concerns persistent rejection after truth has been made clear, not an honest question or an involuntary lack of access to guidance.

### The Reality of the Hereafter

The description of skin being replaced portrays the continuity and reality of punishment in the Hereafter. Human beings are not merely souls floating without accountability; resurrection involves a real embodied existence known fully to Allah.

The exact nature of the Hereafter is part of the unseen, so believers affirm the revealed description without pretending to measure it by worldly experience.

### Allah’s Power and Wisdom

The verse ends with Allah’s might and wisdom. His power makes resurrection and judgement possible, while His wisdom means punishment is not arbitrary. He knows the truth that was rejected, the intention of the rejecter, and the harm caused by persistent disbelief and injustice.

### A Warning, Not a License for Cruelty

This is a divine warning about the Hereafter. It does not permit human beings to torture, mutilate, or punish others outside lawful justice. The Qur’an’s descriptions of divine judgement should produce humility and repentance, not cruelty or a desire to imitate divine authority.

### Hope before Death

The warning is paired with Allah’s invitation to repent. A person should not wait until the consequences become visible. While life remains, turning to Allah, affirming truth, and repairing wrongdoing can transform the final outcome.

### The Moral Meaning

The passage exposes the failure of those who trusted status, religious claims, or worldly power while rejecting Allah’s signs. The body that was used in disobedience will testify to the reality of the judgement. Nothing is lost from Allah’s account.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:57

> As for those who believe and do good, We will admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. There they will have pure spouses,[207] and We will place them under a vast shade.

**Commentary:**

### Faith and Good Deeds

The verse joins belief to good action. Faith, *īmān*, is not merely an internal claim; it produces worship, honesty, mercy, justice, and service. Good deeds without faith are not treated as a substitute for knowing and worshipping Allah, while faith that produces no obedience is incomplete and endangered.

### Gardens of Permanence

The Gardens beneath which rivers flow represent life, abundance, beauty, and peace. Their permanence contrasts with the Fire described in the preceding verse and with the temporary pleasures of worldly life. Allah grants believers an existence free from death, loss, fear, illness, and injustice.

### Pure Spouses

The “pure spouses,” *azwāj muṭahharah*, are purified from the harms and defects that damage relationships in this life. Paradise contains companionship without betrayal, resentment, abuse, envy, or emotional pain. The promise applies to believing men and women according to Allah’s perfect generosity.

The description should not be reduced to a male fantasy. Paradise is a place in which every believing person receives complete happiness and dignity, beyond the limitations of worldly gendered assumptions.

### Vast Shade

The vast shade conveys comfort, protection, and rest. It is not merely relief from heat; it symbolizes security after a life of trial. The believer who sought Allah’s shelter in the world receives a lasting place of safety in the Hereafter.

### Reward from Allah’s Grace

Admission to Paradise is ultimately by Allah’s mercy and grace, even though faith and deeds are the means He commands. This prevents pride in worship and prevents despair over imperfect deeds. The servant strives sincerely, asks forgiveness, and hopes in Allah’s generosity.

### A Moral Contrast

The passage contrasts people who reject signs and face punishment with believers who respond through faith and good works. It shows that divine justice distinguishes between paths while divine mercy offers people the opportunity to choose before death.

- Qur’an 4:56–57 on punishment, faith, good deeds, Paradise, pure companionship, and shade.
- Qur’an 2:25 and 3:15 on Gardens, purified spouses, and Allah’s pleasure.
- Qur’an 36:55–58 and 43:70–73 on the joy, peace, and welcome of the people of Paradise.
- Qur’an 16:97 and 40:40 on good life and equal reward for believing men and women.
- Qur’an 9:72 on Allah’s pleasure being greater than the delights of Paradise.
- Qur’an 76:11–22 on protection, shade, purity, and reward.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on Paradise, its purified blessings, and entry through Allah’s mercy.
- For *āmana*, *ʿamila al-ṣāliḥāt*, *azwāj muṭahharah*, *ẓill*, and the permanence of Paradise, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:57.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:58

> Indeed, Allah commands you to return trusts to their rightful owners;

> [208] and when you judge between people, judge with fairness. What a noble commandment from Allah to you! Surely Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing.

**Commentary:**

### Returning Trusts

Allah commands the return of trusts, *amānāt*, to their rightful owners. A trust can be property, money, confidential information, a responsibility, a public office, a child’s welfare, or knowledge entrusted for teaching. It must be preserved and returned without betrayal, delay, or hidden deduction.

The command applies to individuals, families, businesses, courts, charities, and governments.

### Justice in Judgement

Those who judge between people must judge with fairness, *ʿadl*. Judges, mediators, parents, employers, teachers, and community leaders all make decisions that affect others. They must not favour relatives, the wealthy, the powerful, or those who flatter them.

Fair judgement requires hearing both sides, verifying evidence, allowing response, and separating personal interest from public responsibility.

### Trust and Authority

Public authority is a trust, not a privilege for personal enrichment. A leader who takes office but serves family, party, or private wealth has betrayed the community. A judge who accepts a bribe has corrupted both the trust and the judgement.

The higher the authority, the wider the responsibility and the more serious the accountability.

### Justice beyond Affiliation

“Between people” is broad. Justice is owed to Muslims and non-Muslims, relatives and strangers, supporters and opponents. Faith does not permit a believer to manipulate law against someone he dislikes, and family loyalty does not justify protecting a guilty relative.

### Allah Hears and Sees

Allah is All-Hearing and All-Seeing. He hears the testimony, the private promise, the plea of the victim, and the excuse of the betrayer. He sees the hidden transaction, the forged record, and the fair decision made when no one is watching.

### A Noble Commandment

The verse calls this a noble commandment because stable societies depend upon trust and justice. Without them, wealth becomes theft, leadership becomes domination, and law becomes a tool of the powerful. Fulfilling trusts and judging fairly are acts of worship.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:2, 4:58, and 8:27 on trusts, orphans’ property, and responsibility.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 6:152 on justice even toward disliked people and relatives.
- Qur’an 2:282 on documentation, witnesses, and financial accountability.
- Qur’an 33:72 on the weight of the trust accepted by humanity.
- Qur’an 23:8 and 70:32 on believers preserving trusts and covenants.
- Qur’an 38:26 on judging by truth and not following personal desire.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on leadership as a trust, the signs of betrayal, and the obligation to judge fairly.
- For *amānah*, *ahl*, *ḥukm*, *ʿadl*, *samīʿ*, and *baṣīr*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:58.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:59

> O believers! Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. Should you disagree on anything, then refer it to Allah and His Messenger, if you ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. This is the best and fairest resolution.

**Commentary:**

### The Order of Obedience

The verse commands obedience to Allah, obedience to the Messenger ﷺ, and obedience to those in authority. The repetition of “obey” with Allah and the Messenger highlights their independent authority from revelation, while obedience to human authorities is subordinate and conditional.

The phrase *ulū al-amr* can include legitimate rulers, judges, administrators, and people responsible for knowledge and community affairs. Their authority is a trust, not an invitation to tyranny.

### No Obedience in Sin

Human obedience does not override Allah’s command. If an authority orders sin, injustice, betrayal, or the violation of a clear obligation, that order is not obeyed. The Prophet ﷺ taught that obedience is only in what is right and that there is no obedience to a created being in disobedience to the Creator.

This principle protects both faith and society from religiously justified oppression.

### Returning Disputes to Revelation

When believers disagree, they return the matter to Allah and His Messenger: to the Qur’an and the authentic Sunnah, interpreted through sound scholarship and context. Personal desire, tribal loyalty, political slogans, and majority emotion cannot be the final standard when revelation provides guidance.

For matters that revelation leaves open, consultation, expertise, public welfare, and lawful governance remain important.

### The Last Day as Accountability

The condition “if you truly believe” connects dispute resolution to belief in Allah and the Last Day. A person who knows he will answer for decisions is less likely to manipulate law or use authority for revenge. The Hereafter gives moral seriousness to both obedience and dissent.

### The Best and Fairest Resolution

Returning to revelation is described as the best outcome because it protects unity without demanding blind conformity. It offers a shared standard above competing personalities. A genuine reference to revelation must include justice, evidence, mercy, and the Prophetic method—not merely quoting a verse to win an argument.

### Order and Constructive Criticism

The verse does not forbid advising leaders or correcting errors. Obedience includes lawful cooperation, while sincere counsel and justice protect authority from becoming corruption. Communities need both order and accountability.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:58–59 on trusts, justice, obedience, authority, and resolving disputes.
- Qur’an 24:51 and 33:36 on submitting to Allah and the Messenger when a matter is decided.
- Qur’an 59:7 on accepting the Messenger’s guidance and avoiding what he forbids.
- Qur’an 5:44–50 on judging by what Allah revealed rather than desire.
- Qur’an 16:43–44 on the Messenger explaining revelation.
- Qur’an 5:2 and 49:9–10 on cooperation, resolving disputes, and justice.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports that obedience is only in righteousness, that there is no obedience in sin, and that rulers are responsible for those under their care.
- For *ulū al-amr*, *ṭāʿah*, *tanāzuʿ*, *radd*, Qur’an and Sunnah as the reference, and conditional obedience, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:59.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:60

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who claim they believe in what has been revealed to you and what was revealed before you? They seek the judgment of false judges, which they were commanded to reject. And Satan ˹only˺ desires to lead them farther away.

**Commentary:**

### Claim and Conduct

The verse exposes people who claim belief in revelation while refusing its authority when it conflicts with their desires. Their claim is tested by conduct. They seek the judgement of false authorities, *ṭāghūt*, even though revelation commanded them to reject such authority.

This is not a criticism of using a neutral court or lawful administrative system for a legitimate dispute. The issue is deliberately choosing a corrupt standard because it will deliver an unjust result.

### False Judges and False Authority

*Ṭāghūt* refers to whatever is followed or obeyed in opposition to Allah’s guidance: idols, tyrannical rulers, corrupt judges, superstition, or a personal desire treated as the final law. A human judge can be respected for applying justice, but no created authority has the right to make oppression righteous.

### Cherry-Picking Revelation

Hypocrisy appears when a person accepts revelation in speech but abandons it whenever it limits wealth, power, revenge, or tribal loyalty. Believers must guard against using religious language selectively while submitting to a preferred authority in private.

### Satan’s Direction

Satan leads people farther away in stages. A person may begin with a minor compromise, justify it as practical, then reject the moral standard altogether. Seeking a false judgement does not solve the conflict; it deepens spiritual distance and normalises injustice.

### Law, Courts, and Justice

Islamic obedience does not mean refusing every human legal system. Muslims may seek courts, arbitration, contracts, and civic law to protect rights, especially where no religious court exists. The ethical requirement is to pursue justice, tell the truth, respect lawful agreements, and avoid deliberately selecting a forum for oppression.

### A Test of Sincerity

The verse asks believers to examine whether their submission survives an unfavourable ruling. True faith accepts justice even when it benefits an opponent and seeks correction through honest evidence rather than manipulation.
