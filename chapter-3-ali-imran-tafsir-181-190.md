# Surah Ali 'Imran (The Family of 'Imran, 3) — Verse-by-Verse Tafsir, Verses 181–190

The last batch closed the Uḥud recovery arc: martyrs' joy completed, wounded answering again with *ḥasbunā Allāh*, Satan's fear unmasked, respite unmasked, sorting promised, hands ordered open. These ten verses turn the camera back toward the People of the Book — and toward death, testing, and the people of understanding.

What follows is among the sharpest exchanges in the surah. God has heard those who said "God is poor; we are rich"; He records the slur and their killing of prophets without right, and will say taste the burning (181). That is for what their hands sent ahead; God is not unjust to His servants (182). Those who claim God covenanted them not to believe any messenger until he brings a sacrifice the fire consumes are answered: messengers came before with clear proofs and even with what you demand — why then did you kill them if you are truthful (183)? If they deny you, messengers before you were denied who came with clear proofs, scriptures, and the illuminating Book (184). Every soul will taste death; full wages on the Day of Rising; whoever is kept from the Fire and entered into the Garden has triumphed; the life of this world is only enjoyment of delusion (185). You will be tested in wealth and selves, and will hear much hurt from those given the Book before you and from the associators; if you have *ṣabr* and *taqwā*, that is of the matters of firm resolve (186). God took a covenant from those given the Scripture to make it clear to people and not hide it; they cast it behind their backs and sold it for a small price (187). Do not think those who rejoice in what they have brought and love to be praised for what they have not done are free of punishment (188). To God belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth; He is powerful over everything (189). In the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of reason — *ulū al-albāb* (190).

Five cruxes govern the reading. The first is 181's reported calumny — "God is poor, we are rich" — classically linked to mockery of the "beautiful loan" vocabulary when spending was urged, and paired with the standing charge of killing prophets without right. The second is 183's fire-sacrifice condition as an evasive covenant-claim, broken by the fact that earlier messengers who brought proofs (and even such signs) were still killed. The third is 185 — death as universal taste, triumph as escape from Fire into Garden, world as *matāʿ al-ghurūr*. The fourth is 186 — testing in wealth and selves plus hearing abuse; *ṣabr* + *taqwā* as *ʿazm al-umūr*. The fifth is 187–190: the covenant to publish the Book not hide it; rejoicing in misdeeds and loving unearned praise; mulk and power; and the return of *ulū al-albāb* — the surah's perception vocabulary coming home in creation's signs.

The batch is confrontation without abandoning the surah's party-precision, and consolation without abandoning the demand to endure. It prepares the closing movement of Āl 'Imrān, where the people of understanding will remember God standing, sitting, and on their sides.

## Verse 181

**"Indeed, Allah has heard those ˹among the Jews˺ who said, “Allah is poor; we are rich!” We have certainly recorded their slurs and their killing of prophets unjustly. Then We will say, “Taste the torment of burning!"**

In simple terms: God has heard those who said, "God is poor and we are rich." He has recorded that insult, and their unjust killing of the prophets. Then He will say: taste the punishment of the Fire.

**The overheard blasphemy — and the ledger that does not forget.**

**"Indeed, Allah has heard those among the Jews who said, 'Allah is poor; we are rich!'"** *La-qad samiʿa Allāhu qawla alladhīna qālū inna Allāha faqīrun wa-naḥnu aghniyā'.*

*Samiʿa Allāh* — God has heard. The hearing is not passive. It is the hearing that records and will answer. *Inna Allāha faqīrun wa-naḥnu aghniyā'* — God is poor and we are rich. The half-bracketed "among the Jews" is the classical identification of the speakers' community-context, not a racial sentence on every Jew; the surah's long party-precision still holds. The occasion climate in the tafsir: when the Qur'an urged spending as a "beautiful loan" to God (2:245, and the spending ethics threaded through this surah), some mockers inverted the metaphor — if God asks for a loan, He must be poor, and we who are asked must be the rich. Finḥāṣ ibn ʿĀzūrā' and fellows are named in some routes; the verse's claim does not depend on locking one name. The slur is the point: a theology of divine need invented to refuse human spending.

**"We have certainly recorded their slurs and their killing of prophets unjustly."** *Sa-naktubu mā qālū wa-qatlahumu al-anbiyā'a bi-ghayri ḥaqq.*

*Sa-naktubu* — We will write / We record. Speech is written. *Wa-qatlahumu al-anbiyā'a bi-ghayri ḥaqq* — and their killing of the prophets without right. The charge is not new in the Qur'an (2:61, 2:91, 3:21, 3:112). Here it is paired with the present slur so that mockery of God's "need" and murder of God's messengers sit on one ledger. A community that can kill prophets can also invent a theology that makes God the beggar.

**"Then We will say, 'Taste the torment of burning!'"** *Wa-naqūlu dhūqū ʿadhāba al-ḥarīq.*

*Dhūqū* — taste. The verb makes the punishment sensory and inescapable. *Al-ḥarīq* — the burning. The answer to "we are rich" is not a debate about net worth. It is a day when the mouth that boasted tastes fire.

**A necessary caution on address.** The verse names a saying and a crime-pattern. It does not authorise collective hatred of a people as lineage. *Alladhīna qālū* — those who said. Conduct and party, as the surah has insisted since its early precision.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Watch how metaphors of giving to God get twisted into excuses not to give. "If God wanted the poor fed, He would feed them." "Religion is always asking." Verse 181 hears the sneer inside those lines when they become a claim that God is the needy party and the hoarder is the rich one. God hears. He records. Spending ethics are not divine poverty. They are human trusteeship under a Rich Lord who needs nothing and still commands open hands.

**Ghinā of God against the poor-God slur.** The Qur'an's theology of divine self-sufficiency (*Allāh al-ghanī*) is the silent backdrop. God does not ask for a loan because He lacks. The "beautiful loan" is a mercy-metaphor that multiplies the giver's return. Twisting it into divine poverty is not clever exegesis. It is refusal wearing a joke.

**Recording as threat and as justice.** *Sa-naktubu* means the universe has a memory that is not Twitter's. Speech that felt safe in a circle is already on a page that will be read back. Pair that with the murder of prophets and the ledger becomes civilisational, not only verbal.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — the joke that records.** Be careful with clever lines about God and money. Humour can be a vehicle for the 181-slur without the speaker noticing. If the punchline is that God is the needy one and the tight-fisted are the free ones, the ledger may already be open.

**Connection to 3:180 and the beautiful loan.** The previous batch ended on *bukhl* and God's inheritance of the heavens and the earth. Verse 181 shows the theological sneer that refuses the open hand: call God poor, call yourself rich, keep the purse closed. The two verses are one moral world — withholding and the blasphemy that justifies withholding.

## Verse 182

**"This is ˹the reward˺ for what your hands have done. And Allah is never unjust to ˹His˺ creation.”"**

In simple terms: this is the repayment for what your own hands sent ahead. God is never unjust to His creation.

**The justice-seal under the burning.**

**"This is the reward for what your hands have done. And Allah is never unjust to His creation."** *Dhālika bi-mā qaddamat aydīkum wa-anna Allāha laysa bi-ẓallāmin li-l-ʿabīd.*

*Bi-mā qaddamat aydīkum* — for what your hands sent forward / forwarded. Deeds arrive at the meeting before the person finishes arriving. *Laysa bi-ẓallāmin li-l-ʿabīd* — He is not a wrongdoer to the servants. The form *ẓallām* (intensive) is negated: not in the least unjust, not a perpetrator of *ẓulm* toward those who serve Him. Continuous with the Qur'an's standing theodicy: punishment tracks what was sent ahead; God does not invent guilt.

**Why this verse is only two short claims.** After the horror of tasting fire, the text refuses any reading in which God becomes a tyrant. The fire is *bi-mā qaddamat aydīkum*. The Lord remains not unjust. Both sentences are required for adult faith: take the end seriously; take God's justice as more precise than your self-defence.

**Connection to 3:182 and 3:108, 3:117.** Faces dark and bright; harvests wronged by their owners; now hands that forwarded a slur and a murder-pattern. The surah keeps returning agency to the human side under a just Lord.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When consequence arrives — public or private, worldly or final — the adult question is not only "why is this happening to me?" It is "what did my hands send ahead?" Verse 182 does not say every pain is punishment. It says this punishment is. Learn the difference. And never use the name of God to authorise *ẓulm* while claiming religion. He is not unjust to the servants; do not be unjust in His name.

**Aydīkum — your hands.** The Qur'an often marks agency through hands: what hands put forward, what hands earn. The slur was mouth-work; the murders were hand-work; both are "what hands sent ahead" in the verse's moral grammar. Responsibility is embodied.

**Laysa bi-ẓallāmin as creed under punishment-verses.** Every time the Qur'an names Fire for named crimes, it tends to seal with justice. Adult readers need both seals. Soft readers want justice without Fire. Harsh readers want Fire without the insistence that God does no *ẓulm*. Verse 182 refuses both amputations.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you teach children about consequences, keep both halves: what hands send ahead, and that God is not unjust. A religion of only fear produces neurosis. A religion of only "God is nice" produces shamelessness. Verse 182 is both halves in two clauses.

**Qaddamat aydīkum and the time-lag of deeds.** What hands send ahead may sit in the world for years as a joke that "landed," a murder that became legend, a policy that looked like wisdom. The verse says the package still arrives. Delay of consequence is not cancellation of consequence. Continuous with 178's respite that is not *khayr*.

**ʿAbīd — the servants.** God is not unjust to those who serve Him — and the word also simply means His human creatures under His *rubūbiyyah*. The justice claim is cosmic, not only for the pious club. Even the one who slurred and killed is punished under justice, not under divine mood.

**Connection to 3:117 and self-wrong.** The freezing wind struck a harvest they wronged themselves to earn. Here the burning is for what hands sent ahead. Across the surah, catastrophe-language keeps finding human forwarding of deeds under a Lord who does not wrong the servants.

## Verse 183

**"Those ˹are the same people˺ who say, “Allah has commanded us not to believe in any messenger unless he brings us an offering to be consumed by fire ˹from the sky˺.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Other prophets did in fact come to you before me with clear proofs and ˹even˺ what you demanded—why then did you kill them, if what you say is true?”"**

In simple terms: these are the same people who say, "God ordered us not to believe any messenger until he brings a sacrifice that fire from heaven consumes." Say: messengers came to you before me with clear proofs and even with what you ask for — so why did you kill them, if you are telling the truth?

**The conditional faith that never intends to believe.**

**"Those are the same people who say, 'Allah has commanded us not to believe in any messenger unless he brings us an offering to be consumed by fire from the sky.'"** *Alladhīna qālū inna Allāha ʿahida ilaynā allā nu'mina li-rasūlin ḥattā ya'tiyanā bi-qurbānin ta'kuluhu al-nār.*

*ʿAhida ilaynā* — covenanted / enjoined upon us. They claim a divine condition on belief: no messenger is to be credited until he brings a *qurbān* — an offering, a sacrifice — that the fire consumes. Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan are among those who explain the claimed sign as fire from heaven consuming accepted charity or offering. The half-brackets in the translation unpack the sky-fire and the identity link to the previous speakers. Whether every detail of the claimed rite matches one biblical tableau is less important than the moral structure: **belief postponed behind a spectacular condition.**

**"Say, O Prophet, 'Other prophets did in fact come to you before me with clear proofs and even what you demanded — why then did you kill them, if what you say is true?'"** *Qul qad jā'akum rusulun min qablī bi-l-bayyināti wa-bi-lladhī qultum fa-lima qataltumūhum in kuntum ṣādiqīn.*

The reply is devastating and simple. Messengers came before with *bayyināt* — clear proofs. And with *alladhī qultum* — that which you speak of: even the kind of sign you now demand. *Fa-lima qataltumūhum* — so why did you kill them? *In kuntum ṣādiqīn* — if you are truthful (in claiming that such a sign would have produced belief). The killing of prophets is the empirical refutation of the conditional. If the condition were sincere, those who brought it would have been followed, not murdered. The condition is therefore a shield for refusal, not a criterion of truth.

**Connection to 3:183 and 2:91, 3:21.** The Qur'an repeatedly faces the pattern: demand a sign, then reject the messenger who comes with signs, then kill. Verse 183 is that pattern in one question.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Conditional faith still thrives: "I will practise when I see a miracle," "I will commit when the proof matches my private checklist," "I will obey when the leader is perfect." Verse 183 asks whether your conditions are criteria or exits. If people who already met your conditions were still refused, your conditions are not honest. Drop the theatre. Face the message that has already come with *bayyināt*.

**Qurbān ta'kuluhu al-nār — spectacle as gatekeeping.** Spectacular conditions on belief privilege the theatre of proof over the moral demand of the message. The Qur'an is not against signs — it multiplies *bayyināt*. It is against using a preferred sign as a permanent exit when signs have already come.

**In kuntum ṣādiqīn as the sitters' echo.** The same "if you are truthful" that faced the Uḥud sitters at 168 now faces the conditional believers. Truthfulness would have meant following those who already brought the demanded sign. Murder is the evidence of untruth.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — inventory your conditions.** Write the private "I will believe/obey when…" list. Then ask which items on the list have already been met in some form you refused. The murderers of prophets are the extreme of a habit that begins as a checklist of exits.

**Connection to 5:112–115 and the table-spread.** The Qur'an knows requests for heavenly food-signs from earlier communities. The moral is consistent: demanding a feast from heaven is not the same as readiness to believe. Verse 183's kill-question is the hardest version of that consistency.

## Verse 184

**"If you are rejected by them, so too were messengers before you who came with clear proofs, divine Books, and enlightening Scriptures."**

In simple terms: if they reject you, messengers before you were also rejected — messengers who came with clear proofs, scriptures, and the illuminating Book.

**Consolation to the Prophet by placing him in a line of denied messengers.**

**"If you are rejected by them, so too were messengers before you who came with clear proofs, divine Books, and enlightening Scriptures."** *Fa-in kadhdhabūka fa-qad kudhdhiba rusulun min qablika jā'ū bi-l-bayyināti wa-al-zuburi wa-al-kitābi al-munīr.*

*Fa-in kadhdhabūka* — if they cry lies against you / deny you. *Fa-qad kudhdhiba rusulun min qablika* — then messengers before you were already denied. The passive keeps the long history visible: denial is not a new invention aimed only at Muḥammad ﷺ. *Bi-l-bayyināt* — with clear proofs. *Wa-al-zubur* — and the scriptures / psalms / written portions (often linked to the Zabūr and to scriptural revelations in parts). *Wa-al-kitābi al-munīr* — and the illuminating Book. The stack says: they did not lack evidence. They lacked willingness.

**Why this follows 183.** After the specific fire-sacrifice evasion, the general law: denial happens to messengers who came fully equipped. Your rejection, O Prophet, is not proof that you lack *bayyināt*. It is proof that you stand where earlier messengers stood.

**Connection to 3:184 and 6:34, 35:4.** The Qur'an's consolation of the Prophet by the fate of earlier messengers is a standing mercy. It protects him from reading denial as personal failure of evidence.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you carry a clear truth and meet denial, do not immediately rewrite the truth to win the room. Check whether you have *bayyināt*. If you do, learn the older pattern: messengers with proofs were denied. Persistence under denial is part of the inheritance. So is refusing bitterness that would make you harsh after 159's gentleness.

**Zubur and kitāb munīr — layers of written light.** The verse stacks forms of revelation so that no one can say the denied messengers came empty. Proofs, written portions, illuminating Book. Denial under that stack is culpable, not tragic lack of information.

**Consolation without passivity.** Being in the line of denied messengers is comfort. It is not permission to stop clarifying. The Prophet ﷺ continues to recite, purify, teach (164). Consolation fuels perseverance, not resignation.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** If your *daʿwah* or your parenting or your public truth meets denial, place yourself in the longer line before you place yourself in despair. Proofs have been denied before. Continue with clarity and with the gentleness 159 ordered where gentleness holds the circle.

**Kadhdhabūka / kudhdhiba — the same verb across time.** Your denial and their denial are one species of act. The Prophet ﷺ is not an unprecedented victim of an unprecedented hardness. He is the latest in a line that includes those who brought *zubur* and the illuminating Book. That historical solidarity is part of how the Qur'an loves its Messenger: it will not let him think he failed alone.

**Three genres of evidence.** *Bayyināt* (clear proofs), *zubur* (written revelations/portions), *al-kitāb al-munīr* (the illuminating Book). The stack blocks the excuse "we never received enough light." Light came. Denial still happened. Therefore denial is not primarily an information problem.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — denial is not a referendum on truth.** When a clear teaching meets a hard no, check your proofs, then check the pattern. Messengers with illuminating Books were denied. Your job is fidelity, not a guaranteed reception. Keep the gentleness of 159 where it holds people; keep the clarity of *bayyināt* where clarity is owed.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 185

**"Every soul will taste death. And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment. Whoever is spared from the Fire and is admitted into Paradise will ˹indeed˺ triumph, whereas the life of this world is no more than the delusion of enjoyment."**

In simple terms: every soul will taste death. You will be paid your wages in full only on the Day of Judgment. Whoever is kept away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has truly succeeded. The life of this world is nothing but enjoyment that deceives.

**One of the Qur'an's densest life-verses — death, full pay, real success, world as delusion.**

**"Every soul will taste death."** *Kullu nafsin dhā'iqatu al-mawt.*

*Kullu nafs* — every soul. *Dhā'iqah* — tasting. Death is not a rumour for other people. It is a taste every soul will know. Angels who carry the Throne, jinn, humans — all under the statement in the classical expansion; God alone is the Ever-Living who does not die. After Uḥud's graves and the living martyrs, the verse universalises: not only the seventy, every soul.

**"And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment."** *Wa-innamā tuwaffawna ujūrakum yawma al-qiyāmah.*

*Tuwaffawna ujūrakum* — you are paid your wages in full — the *w-f-y* root again. Full payment is eschatological. Worldly outcomes are not the full wage. Continuous with 171's guarantee that wages are not wasted, and with 161's full payment of every soul.

**"Whoever is spared from the Fire and is admitted into Paradise will indeed triumph."** *Fa-man zuḥziḥa ʿani al-nāri wa-udkhila al-jannata fa-qad fāz.*

*Zuḥziḥa* — is moved away / drawn away / kept at a distance from the Fire. *Wa-udkhila al-jannah* — and is entered into the Garden. *Fa-qad fāz* — then he has triumphed / succeeded / won. Real *fawz* is defined here with surgical clarity: not wealth, not the hill's scoreboard, not the mockers' richness. Escape from Fire plus entry to Garden. The half-bracketed "indeed" marks the force of *fa-qad*.

**"Whereas the life of this world is no more than the delusion of enjoyment."** *Wa-mā al-ḥayātu al-dunyā illā matāʿu al-ghurūr.*

*Matāʿ* — enjoyment, provision, goods. *Al-ghurūr* — delusion, that which deceives. The world is not nothing. It is enjoyment that deceives when taken as the real success. Continuous with 3:14's beautified loves and 3:185's placement after death and full pay.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Let this verse audit your definition of winning. Promotion, victory narrative, viral praise, even religious reputation — if Fire is not escaped and Garden not entered, the Qur'an withholds the word *fawz*. Taste of death is coming. Full wages are later. Live as someone who wants to be *zuḥziḥa* from the Fire. Treat the world's *matāʿ* as real enough to use and false enough not to worship.

**Dhā'iqah — tasting as intimate knowledge.** Death is not only an event that happens to the body from outside. It is a taste the soul knows. The verb makes denial of death's relevance harder. You will not only hear of it. You will taste it.

**Zuḥziḥa — the passive of being moved away.** Triumph includes being drawn off a path into Fire. The passive keeps God's mercy in the grammar of success. You strive; you are also moved by a mercy that distances you from the Fire. Both belong in a full reading of *fawz*.

**Matāʿ al-ghurūr after Uḥud's spoils.** The world-want that emptied a post is *matāʿ* behaving as *ghurūr*. Verse 185 names the whole life of this world under that risk — not to ban joy, but to ban taking joy as the definition of success.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — redefine the win.** Before a decision, ask: does this path help me be moved away from the Fire and into the Garden, or only help me enlarge *matāʿ*? The question does not ban career or marriage or medicine. It bans letting those goods become the definition of *fawz*.

**Fāz and the Uḥud scoreboard.** Men who fled, men who held, men who wanted spoils, men who wanted the hereafter — the world's scoreboard after the hill is not the *fawz* of 185. Some who looked like losers on the afternoon are alive with their Lord (169–170). Some who looked clever in speech are nearer to disbelief (167). Real triumph is Fire and Garden, not the evening's narrative.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 186

**"You ˹believers˺ will surely be tested in your wealth and yourselves, and you will certainly hear many hurtful words from those who were given the Scripture before you and ˹from˺ the polytheists. But if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺—surely this is a resolve to aspire to."**

In simple terms: you believers will certainly be tested in your wealth and in your own selves, and you will hear many hurtful things from those who were given the Scripture before you and from those who associate partners with God. But if you are patient and mindful of God, that is among the most resolute of courses.

**The testing-verse that names the bill of Madinan life.**

**"You believers will surely be tested in your wealth and yourselves."** *La-tublawunna fī amwālikum wa-anfusikum.*

*La-tublawunna* — you will most surely be tried / tested. The emphatic *nūn* makes it certain, not optional. *Fī amwālikum* — in your wealth: loss, demand to spend, *bukhl*'s temptation, spoils' temptation, the beautiful loan mocked in 181. *Wa-anfusikum* — and in your selves: bodies, lives, fear, injury, Uḥud's *qarḥ*, the wish for death that met death (143). Wealth-test and self-test are the twin classrooms of the path.

**"And you will certainly hear many hurtful words from those who were given the Scripture before you and from the polytheists."** *Wa-la-tasmaʿunna min alladhīna ūtū al-kitāba min qablikum wa-min alladhīna ashrakū adhan kathīran.*

*La-tasmaʿunna* — you will most surely hear. *Adhan kathīran* — much hurt, much harm in speech: insults, slurs, "God is poor," mockery, abuse. From two sources named: those given the Book before you, and the associators. The Madinan believer lives between scriptural communities and pagan pressure — and the verse does not promise silence. It promises hearing.

**"But if you are patient and mindful of Allah — surely this is a resolve to aspire to."** *Wa-in taṣbirū wa-tattaqū fa-inna dhālika min ʿazmi al-umūr.*

*Taṣbirū wa-tattaqū* — if you have *ṣabr* and *taqwā*: the pair that has threaded the surah from the angel-condition (125) through the shield against *kayd* (120) to the *muttaqīn* portrait. *Min ʿazmi al-umūr* — of the firm resolves / the matters of strong determination. The supplied "a resolve to aspire to" is fair. Patience plus mindfulness under wealth-test, self-test, and verbal abuse is not soft virtue. It is *ʿazm* — the same root as the Prophet's firm decision after consultation (159).

**Connection to 3:186 and 2:155.** Al-Baqarah promised testing with fear, hunger, loss of wealth and lives and fruits. Āl 'Imrān specifies wealth, selves, and hearing hurt from Book-communities and associators. Uḥud is inside this promise; so is the long Madinan argument.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Do not be surprised by the invoice. Wealth will be tested. The self will be tested. Tongues will hurt — including religious tongues. The response that rises to *ʿazm al-umūr* is not clever retaliation first. It is *ṣabr* and *taqwā*. Hold the post of character. Spend when spending is the test. Guard the self when fear is the test. Do not let abuse write your theology.

**Wealth and selves — the two treasures people clutch.** Test them and you find out what was worship. Uḥud tested selves with injury and death. Spending verses and *bukhl* verses test wealth. Abuse tests the self's need for honour. The verse says all of this is certain (*la-tublawunna*, *la-tasmaʿunna*).

**Adhā from People of the Book and associators.** The double source matters for Madinan reality and for later minority life: hurtful speech may come from near scriptural cousins and from open *shirk*. The response prescribed is the same pair: *ṣabr* and *taqwā*. Not naive friendship, not feral hatred — resolve.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Expect tests in money and in body and in reputation. When the hurtful sentence arrives from a religious mouth or a pagan one, do not be shocked into either rage-theology or exit. Reach for *ṣabr* and *taqwā* as the resolve the verse calls *ʿazm*.

**ʿAzm al-umūr and 159's ʿazamta.** The Prophet decides with *ʿazm* then trusts. The community endures with *ṣabr* and *taqwā* as *ʿazm al-umūr*. Firmness is not only a leader's decisive moment. It is a people's long holding under tests of money, body, and tongue.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 187

**"˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it, yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain. What a miserable profit!"**

In simple terms: remember when God took a covenant from those given the Scripture that they would make it clear to people and not hide it — but they threw it behind their backs and sold it for a small price. What a wretched bargain.

**The covenant to publish — and the sale of concealment.**

**"Remember, O Prophet, when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it."** *Wa-idh akhadha Allāhu mīthāqa alladhīna ūtū al-kitāba la-tubayyinunnahu li-l-nāsi wa-lā taktumūnahu.*

*Mīthāq* — covenant, the binding taken. *Alladhīna ūtū al-kitāb* — those given the Scripture. *La-tubayyinunnahu li-l-nās* — that you will most surely make it clear / plain to people. *Wa-lā taktumūnahu* — and not conceal it. The covenant's content is publicity of revelation, not private ownership of revelation. The half-bracketed "Remember, O Prophet" marks the addressee of the recall. Continuous with the surah's interest in making clear and with 2:159, 2:174 on concealing what God sent down.

**"Yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain. What a miserable profit!"** *Fa-nabadhūhu warā'a ẓuhūrihim wa-ishtaraw bihi thamanan qalīlan; fa-bi'sa mā yashtarūn.*

*Nabadhūhu warā'a ẓuhūrihim* — they threw it behind their backs: the body-language of discard. *Ishtaraw bihi thamanan qalīlan* — and bought with it a small price: the standing Qur'anic image of selling revelation for a little worldly return — status, bribes, factional advantage, the comfort of not confronting power with the text. *Fa-bi'sa mā yashtarūn* — evil is what they purchase / what a miserable bargain.

**Connection to 3:77, 3:187 and the small price.** The surah has already condemned selling God's covenant for a small price. Here the sold thing is the duty to *tabyīn* — clarification. Hiding becomes a market.

**Why this lands after 186's hearing hurt.** Believers will hear abuse from People of the Book. Verse 187 explains part of the moral climate without flattening all People of the Book into one guilt (the surah already gave *laysū sawā'* at 113–115). A party took a covenant to clarify and chose concealment and sale. That history is named so that Madinan believers understand the argument they are in.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Every community given a Book faces the same *mīthāq*-shape: clarify to people, do not hide. The modern forms of *kitmān* are not only locked manuscripts. They are selective quotation, silencing inconvenient verses, monetising access, using scripture as faction-brand while burying its claims on the self. Verse 187 asks whether you are making the Book plain or throwing it behind your back for a small price — a like, a post, a salary, a seat.

**Tabyīn li-l-nās — clarification as duty to the public.** Revelation is not a private club's password. The covenant says *for people*. Hiding it is a breach against the public as well as against God. Scholars, translators, teachers, and ordinary carriers of the Book all sit under a shadow of this *mīthāq*.

**Warā'a ẓuhūrihim — behind the backs.** The image is almost comic if it were not tragic: the Book tossed where you do not look. Selective memory is a way of throwing behind the back. So is shelving the parts that cost status.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — publish what you know of the Book.** Make plain the part that costs you. Do not only teach the parts that gather praise. The covenant's spirit for any carrier of revelation is *tabyīn*, not brand-management.

**Connection to 3:78 and twisting the tongue with the Book.** Hiding and twisting are cousins. One removes the text from sight; the other keeps it visible but bent. Both violate the *mīthāq* to make clear. The small price may be paid in coin or in the softer currency of not losing a following.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 188

**"Do not let those who rejoice in their misdeeds and love to take credit for what they have not done think they will escape torment. They will suffer a painful punishment."**

In simple terms: do not think that people who celebrate their own wrongdoing and love to be praised for things they did not do will escape punishment. Theirs is a painful torment.

**The verse against performative religion and stolen credit.**

**"Do not let those who rejoice in their misdeeds and love to take credit for what they have not done think they will escape torment. They will suffer a painful punishment."** *Lā taḥsabanna alladhīna yafraḥūna bi-mā ataw wa-yuḥibbūna an yuḥmadū bi-mā lam yafʿalū fa-lā taḥsabannahum bi-mafāzatin min al-ʿadhāb; wa-lahum ʿadhābun alīm.*

Two diseases named:

1. ***Yafraḥūna bi-mā ataw*** — they rejoice in what they have brought / done: delight in their own deeds even when the deeds are misdeeds, or delight in their "contribution" as self-celebration. Classical readings include rejoicing in their rejection and schemes, and rejoicing in what they produced of distortion.
2. ***Yuḥibbūna an yuḥmadū bi-mā lam yafʿalū*** — they love to be praised for what they have not done: hunger for credit without the work, reputation without the substance, the appearance of virtue without the deed.

*Fa-lā taḥsabannahum bi-mafāzatin min al-ʿadhāb* — so do not reckon them in a place of safety from the punishment. *Wa-lahum ʿadhābun alīm* — and theirs is a painful punishment.

**Connection to 3:188 and the praise-economy.** After a surah that has sorted *riḍwān* from *sakhaṭ*, held posts from abandoned posts, mouths from hearts, this verse attacks the social media of every age: joy in one's own performance and love of unearned *ḥamd*. The Prophet ﷺ and the believers are told not to misread such people's public glow as safety.

**Occasion colour.** Some routes link the verse to people who wanted praise for religious standing they had not earned, or who rejoiced in opposing the Prophet while seeking a reputation for guidance. The verse's moral structure is clear without forcing one exclusive asbāb.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Audit two hungers: Do I rejoice in what I actually did as worship, or as self-monument? Do I want praise for work I did not do — credit for a team's labour, a scholarly pose without study, a public piety without private obedience? Verse 188 says that economy does not end in *mafāzah* (safety/success) from punishment. It ends in *ʿadhāb alīm*. Prefer being unseen with a real deed to being praised for an empty one.

**Two hungers of the ego.** Joy in one's production and love of praise for non-production. The first can infect even real work with vanity. The second is pure theft of reputation. Together they describe a religious (or secular) personality that cannot stand to be small. The verse denies that personality safety from *ʿadhāb*.

**Lā taḥsabanna — do not think.** Addressed so that believers do not misread glamour. The glowing activist, the praised scholar, the community hero who did not do what the praise claims — do not assign them *mafāzah* from punishment based on optics.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Decline praise for what you did not do. Redirect credit. And when you do real work, starve the joy that is only self-celebration — keep enough joy to thank God, not enough to build a statue of yourself.

**Connection to 3:188 and the hypocrites' optics.** Uḥud already showed mouths not matching hearts (167). Verse 188 adds joy in the product of the wrong path and hunger for praise without the deed. The social performance of religion is now fully in the dock.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 189

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is Most Capable of everything."**

In simple terms: to God alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. God has power over everything.

**The sovereignty-seal before the signs-verse.**

**"To Allah alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is Most Capable of everything."** *Wa-li-llāhi mulku al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ; wa-Allāhu ʿalā kulli shay'in qadīr.*

*Mulk* — kingship, dominion, ownership of command. Heavens and earth: the widest frame. Continuous with 3:129, 3:180's inheritance, and the surah's refusal to let anyone own the final *amr*. *Qadīr ʿalā kulli shay'* — powerful over everything: the same seal as 165 after "from yourselves."

**Why here.** After slurs about God's poverty, after conditions on messengers, after death and testing and hidden Books and false praise, the text restates the obvious that pride keeps forgetting: the kingdom is His; power is His. The mockers who called themselves rich do not own the heavens. The praise-seekers do not own the earth. God does.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When argument thickens, return to *mulk* and *qudrah*. Not as a conversation-stopper that ends thought, but as the frame that ends idolatry of self, faction, and wealth. You are not the king of the story. He is. Work inside that.

**Mulk before āyāt.** Sovereignty is stated before the invitation to read creation. That order matters. Nature-signs without *mulk* can become pagan nature-worship. *Mulk* without attention to creation can become abstract dogma. Verse 189 then 190 holds both.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** In a money argument, a family power struggle, a political panic, say inwardly: *li-llāhi mulk al-samāwāt wa-al-arḍ*. Then act as a trustee under a King, not as a rival king.

**Mulk after the small-price sale and the false praise.** Those who sold the Book for a little and those who love unearned *ḥamd* are both acting as if a corner of the kingdom were theirs to monetise or to brand. Verse 189 confiscates the corner. The heavens and the earth are not a market stall for revelation-brokers.

**Qadīr as the bridge into 190.** Power over everything includes the power that wrote night and day and the power that will sort *khabīth* from *ṭayyib*. The signs of 190 sit under a capable King, not under a weak watchmaker.

**Connection to 3:26–27 and the mulk vocabulary.** Earlier the surah taught that God gives mulk to whom He wills and pulls it from whom He wills, and enters the night into the day. Verse 189–190 return that theology as seal and as invitation to read the alternation as *āyah*.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — confiscate the false throne.** In a quarrel over money, status, or religious authority, return the *mulk* to God before you return fire. You may still need to act, spend, refuse, or speak. You do not need to act as if the heavens were your property.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 190

**"Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the day and night there are signs for people of reason."**

In simple terms: in the creation of the heavens and the earth, and in the alternation of night and day, there are signs for people who have understanding.

**The return of ulū al-albāb — the surah's people of inner core.**

**"Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the day and night there are signs for people of reason."** *Inna fī khalqi al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍi wa-ikhtilāfi al-layli wa-al-nahāri la-āyātin li-ulī al-albāb.*

*Khalq al-samāwāt wa-al-arḍ* — the creation of the heavens and the earth. *Ikhtilāf al-layl wa-al-nahār* — the alternation / difference of night and day: turn and return, dark and light, the rhythm that structures mortal time. *La-āyātin* — surely signs. *Li-ulī al-albāb* — for those of understanding / people of kernels, cores, intellects that reach the inside of things. The supplied "people of reason" is fair; the Arabic *albāb* (plural of *lubb*) is the inner core, not bare cleverness.

**The surah's perception vocabulary comes home.** *Ulū al-albāb* appeared at 3:7 among those who say "we believe in it; all is from our Lord." *Ulī al-abṣār* at 3:13; *ulū al-ʿilm* at 3:18. Now creation itself is the open book for *ulū al-albāb*. After pages of argument, war, hypocrisy, and Book-polemic, the text points at the sky and the night and the day and says: signs are here for those who have cores.

**Connection forward to 191–195.** The next batch will show what *ulū al-albāb* do: remember God standing, sitting, and on their sides; reflect on creation; pray not to be disgraced on the Day; and receive God's answer about the acceptance of their prayer and the reward of those who emigrated and were harmed. Verse 190 is the doorway.

**Why this is not "nature spirituality" detached from the Book.** The signs are *āyāt* — the same word as revelation's verses. Creation-reading and Book-reading are meant to train the same *lubb*. A person who can parse factional tafsir but cannot stand under a night sky with *dhikr* has not yet finished what *ulū al-albāb* means in this surah.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Practise the verse without waiting for a retreat. Look at the sky. Notice a sunset as *ikhtilāf* — turn of the day. Ask what sign it is under a Lord whose *mulk* was just named. Let the *lubb* work: not only aesthetic pleasure, but evidence that leads to the remembrance the next verses will describe. The surah has taken you through blood and argument. It will not end without returning you to creation and the people who understand.

**And the spiritual centre of the ten verses.** God heard the slur that made Him the poor one and the mockers the rich; He records speech and the murder of prophets; the fire is tasted for what hands sent ahead under a Lord who is not unjust. Conditional faith that demands fire-sacrifice is exposed by the killing of messengers who already came with proofs and with what was demanded. Denial of the Prophet sits in a long line of denied messengers with *bayyināt*, *zubur*, and the illuminating Book. Every soul tastes death; full wages later; real triumph is distance from Fire and entry to Garden; the world is enjoyment that deceives. You will be tested in wealth and selves and will hear much hurt — *ṣabr* and *taqwā* are of the firmest resolves. Those given the Book were covenanted to clarify it, not hide it; they threw it behind their backs for a small price. Do not think joy in misdeeds and love of unearned praise are safety from punishment. The kingdom is God's; He is powerful over everything. In creation and in the turn of night and day are signs for *ulū al-albāb*.

The surah is turning toward its close. The people of understanding are about to speak. The next batch will let them remember, reflect, and be answered.

**Ikhtilāf al-layl wa-al-nahār — alternation as sign.** Not only static cosmos but time's turn: nights that follow days that follow nights. Mortality is written into the alternation. *Ulū al-albāb* read the clock of night and day as *āyah*, not only as schedule.

**Doorway function.** This verse is intentionally a threshold. The next batch's *ulū al-albāb* will remember God in postures and reflect on creation with a prayer. Reading 190 without anticipating that remembrance is reading a door as a wall. The signs want a response the next verses supply.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Tonight, before sleep, look at the dark as *āyah*. Morning, look at the light as *āyah*. One sentence of *dhikr* under each. That is how *ulū al-albāb* begin to be trained outside the classroom of polemic.

**Āyāt in creation and āyāt in the Book.** The same word binds both. A person of *lubb* does not choose nature against revelation or revelation against the sky. The surah that began with *Alif Lām Mīm* and the living Book (3:1–3) is now pointing at the created horizon as another page for the same readers — *ulū al-albāb* who said at 3:7 that all is from their Lord.


===VERSE-END===

## Synthesis of the Ten Verses

These ten verses reopen the People of the Book file after Uḥud's long autopsy, then lift the reader into death, testing, covenant, and the signs that train *ulū al-albāb*.

**181–184: slur, justice, conditional faith, denied messengers.** God heard "God is poor; we are rich"; He records the saying and the killing of prophets without right; taste the burning (181). That is for what hands sent ahead; God is not unjust to the servants (182). The claim of a covenant not to believe until a fire-consumed offering is answered by prior messengers who came with proofs and with what was demanded — yet were killed (183). If you are denied, so were messengers before you with *bayyināt*, *zubur*, and the illuminating Book (184).

**185–186: death, triumph, testing.** Every soul tastes death; full wages on the Day of Rising; triumph is being kept from the Fire and entered into the Garden; the world is *matāʿ al-ghurūr* (185). You will be tested in wealth and selves and will hear much *adhā* from People of the Book and associators; *ṣabr* and *taqwā* are of *ʿazm al-umūr* (186).

**187–188: covenant broken, praise corrupted.** Covenant to make the Book clear and not hide it; they cast it behind their backs and sold it for a small price (187). Those who rejoice in what they brought and love praise for what they did not do are not safe from punishment (188).

**189–190: mulk and albāb.** Kingdom of the heavens and the earth is God's; He is powerful over everything (189). In creation and the alternation of night and day are signs for *ulū al-albāb* (190) — the surah's perception vocabulary returning as the door to the closing prayers.

**Threads carried and extended.**

- ***Party-precision*** — *alladhīna qālū*, not a people as lineage; continuous with *laysū sawā'* (113–115).
- ***Killing prophets without right*** — 3:21, 3:112 → 181–183.
- ***Small price / selling revelation*** — 3:77 → 187.
- ***W-f-y / full wages*** — 161, 171 → 185's *tuwaffawna ujūrakum*.
- ***Ṣabr + taqwā*** — 120, 125, 186 as *ʿazm al-umūr* under wealth/self/abuse tests.
- ***Ulū al-albāb*** — 3:7 → 3:190 (and into 191+).
- ***Mulk / qudrah*** — 129, 165, 180, 189.
- ***World as ghurūr*** — 3:14's beautified loves → 185's *matāʿ al-ghurūr*.
- **Hearing hurt** — after Uḥud's internal autopsy, external *adhā* from Book-communities and associators named as expected.

**Where the supplied translation has chosen, it has been flagged.** Half-brackets ("among the Jews," "the reward," "His," "are the same people," "from the sky," "O Prophet," "even," "indeed," "believers," "from," "of Allah," "Remember, O Prophet," "alone") are interpretive aids. "Slurs" for recorded speech is strong and fair. "Divine Books" / "enlightening Scriptures" for *zubur* and *kitāb munīr* is dynamic. "Delusion of enjoyment" for *matāʿ al-ghurūr* is excellent. "Resolve to aspire to" for *ʿazm al-umūr* is fair. "People of reason" for *ulū al-albāb* is fair; *lubb* as core is restored in commentary. "Misdeeds" for *mā ataw* is one classical lean. None is distortion; each is a choice.

**The verses to leave with are 185 and 190.** Every soul tastes death; real triumph is Fire escaped and Garden entered; the world deceives as enjoyment. And the sky and the night and the day are signs for people who still have a core. After argument and blood, Āl 'Imrān is preparing you to remember God in all postures. The next batch begins that remembrance.

**A note on honesty in citation.** No page numbers, volumes or publication details are given, because this is a synthesized commentary rather than a citation apparatus, and inventing precise references would be worse than omitting them.

Five places in this batch required particular care.

**The first is 181.** "God is poor; we are rich" as mockery of spending-as-loan vocabulary in the dominant climate; killing prophets paired on the ledger; half-bracket "among the Jews" as classical context, not racial collective guilt; party-precision maintained.

**The second is 183.** Fire-consumed offering as claimed condition; prior messengers with proofs and with the demanded kind of sign still killed — the condition exposed as insincere. Details of the rite held within classical explanation without over-confident biblical matching.

**The third is 185.** Universal taste of death; full wages only at Rising; *fawz* defined as distance from Fire plus entry to Garden; world as deluding enjoyment — not as pure nothing.

**The fourth is 186–188.** Certain testing in wealth and selves; hearing hurt from Book-communities and associators; *ṣabr*+*taqwā* as firm resolve. Covenant to clarify not hide; small-price sale. Joy in what they brought and love of praise for what they did not do — without forcing one exclusive asbāb.

**The fifth is 189–190.** *Mulk* and *qudrah* as seal; *ulū al-albāb* as return of the surah's perception thread and door to 191+.

**On the translation.** Choices noted above. Openings preserved byte-for-byte. The translation is not wrong; it has chosen, and the reader is entitled to know what was chosen.

Two boundaries observed throughout. No reading of 181–183 has been allowed to become racial hatred — the Qur'an's own *alladhīna qālū* and earlier *laysū sawā'* remain normative. And no reading of 185 has been allowed to produce contempt for lawful worldly good — *matāʿ al-ghurūr* condemns delusion, not the use of provision under *taqwā*.
