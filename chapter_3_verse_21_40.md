## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:21

> Indeed, those who deny Allah’s signs, kill the prophets unjustly, and kill people who stand up for justice—give them good news of a painful punishment.

**Commentary:**

### Three Forms of Rebellion

This verse identifies a destructive pattern: rejecting Allah’s signs, killing prophets, and killing people who call for justice. These are not unrelated crimes. Rejection of revelation removes the moral restraint that protects life, while opposition to justice protects power and privilege. When a society silences those who speak truth, it has moved from private disbelief toward public corruption.

The verse describes people who deny *āyāt Allāh*, the signs of Allah. These signs include revealed verses, the miracles of prophets, and the evidence of truth in creation and history. Denial here is a settled refusal, not an honest question or a temporary struggle to understand. Allah knows the difference between a seeker who asks and an arrogant person who rejects.

### Killing Prophets and the Sanctity of Life

The killing of prophets is mentioned because it represents the most extreme hostility toward divine guidance. Prophets come with truth and a call to justice, yet tyrants and religious elites may resist them when revelation threatens their power. The Qur’an repeatedly mentions that some earlier communities rejected or killed messengers (2:87; 5:70), while also praising those among them who believed and acted righteously.

A prophet’s sanctity does not mean that Muslims may harm people who disagree with them. The verse condemns the murder of those who stand for justice; it cannot be used to license vigilante violence. Legal punishment, warfare, and public order belong to legitimate authority and strict ethical limits, not to individuals acting from anger.

### Defending Those Who Uphold Justice

The phrase “people who stand up for justice” refers to those who command *al-qisṭ*, fairness and equity. They may be scholars, reformers, witnesses, judges, journalists, community leaders, or ordinary people who refuse to participate in oppression. Their value is not their profession but their commitment to truth and justice.

The Qur’an commands believers to stand firmly for justice even when it is against themselves, parents, or relatives (4:135). Justice is not reserved for one’s allies. A person who speaks about justice only when his own group benefits has not fully accepted the Qur’anic standard.

### “Give Them Good News”

The wording “give them good news” is deliberately ironic. Good news normally announces mercy and reward, but these people have earned a painful punishment through persistent rejection and oppression. The expression exposes the absurdity of those who imagine that their worldly status guarantees safety while they destroy the rights of others.

The warning is not an invitation to take pleasure in another person’s suffering. It is a call to repentance before the final account. The Qur’an describes punishment so that people abandon the path leading to it. Even severe warnings remain part of divine mercy because they give the wrongdoer an opportunity to turn back.

### A Warning to Every Community

Although the immediate historical context includes earlier communities, the principle is universal. Muslims can fall into the same sins if they reject clear revelation, silence truthful criticism, or harm people who expose corruption. A religious label cannot protect a community that abandons justice. The Qur’an often recounts the failures of earlier nations to make the present community examine itself.

The Prophet ﷺ praised speaking a word of truth before an unjust ruler, and the Sunnah repeatedly commands believers to prevent oppression according to their ability and wisdom. Speaking for justice must also remain truthful and must not become a cover for slander, chaos, or revenge.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:61, 2:87, and 5:70 on the rejection and killing of prophets by earlier communities.
- Qur’an 4:135, 5:8, and 16:90 on standing firmly for justice.
- Qur’an 22:40 on defending places where Allah’s name is remembered and restraining religious persecution.
- Qur’an 17:33 on the sanctity of life and the prohibition of unlawful killing.
- Sunan Abī Dāwūd and Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, reports praising a word of justice before an oppressive ruler.
- For *āyāt Allāh*, *qisṭ*, the sanctity of prophetic life, and the ironic wording of “good news,” see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:21.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:22

> They are the ones whose deeds are wasted in this world and the Hereafter. And they will have no helpers.

**Commentary:**

### When Deeds Lose Their Foundation

This verse describes the consequence of the rejection and oppression named previously. Deeds are “wasted” when they lose their intended spiritual result. A person may perform impressive acts, build institutions, help people, or gain public honour, but if he deliberately rejects Allah and uses his abilities against truth and justice, those acts cannot purchase salvation or erase his rebellion.

The verse does not mean that Allah fails to recognize any worldly benefit produced by an action. A good deed may help people in this world even if the one who performed it did not seek Allah. Nor does it permit people to deny the moral value of benefiting others. It teaches that the lasting reward with Allah requires faith, sincerity, and obedience, and that deliberate rejection destroys the deed’s claim to eternal reward.

### The Difference between Worldly and Ultimate Reward

The Qur’an explains that some people may receive the result of their efforts in this world while receiving nothing in the Hereafter (17:18–20). This can include wealth, reputation, influence, or the gratitude of those they helped. Worldly recognition is not proof that Allah is pleased with a person, just as worldly hardship is not proof that He has rejected him.

The Hereafter reveals the true value of every deed. An act performed for Allah, even if unseen, remains secure. An act performed for applause may disappear when the audience disappears. The believer therefore seeks both benefit for people and sincerity before Allah.

### Rejection and Moral Accountability

The preceding verse concerns those who deny signs and attack prophets and advocates of justice. Their problem is not simply that they lack a religious label; it is that they knowingly place themselves against truth and righteousness. Persistent rejection corrupts the purpose of human life and turns gifts into evidence against the recipient.

The verse should make the believer examine his own intentions. Showing faith outwardly while pursuing injustice, hypocrisy, or oppression can also destroy the value of deeds. The Qur’an warns that charity accompanied by reminders and harm is nullified like a smooth stone covered with dust and then washed away (2:264).

### No Helpers against Allah

The statement that they will have no helpers means that no ally can rescue a person from Allah’s final judgement. In this life, a person may rely on a political party, family name, army, wealth, or public relations. In the Hereafter, all created power is exposed as dependent. No one can overturn Allah’s judgement or negotiate salvation through worldly status.

This does not deny the intercession Allah grants by His permission. Intercession belongs to Allah, and it benefits only those for whom He permits it (2:255; 20:109). It cannot be bought or claimed independently by a prophet, angel, or saint.

### Hope through Repentance

The warning remains open to repentance during life. The Qur’an condemns persistent rejection, not a person who sincerely turns to Allah after error. When someone abandons disbelief, repairs harm, and follows faith with righteous action, Allah can replace evil with good (25:70). The believer therefore warns against the path while never declaring repentance impossible.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 17:18–20, 18:103–106, and 25:23 on deeds that do not produce lasting reward.
- Qur’an 2:264–267 on sincerity in charity and the danger of nullifying good deeds.
- Qur’an 14:18 and 24:39 on the loss of deeds and the mirage of false confidence.
- Qur’an 2:255 and 20:109 on intercession only by Allah’s permission.
- Qur’an 25:70 and 39:53 on repentance and the possibility of transformation.
- For the meaning of wasted deeds, worldly and eternal reward, and the absence of helpers, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:22.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:23

> Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures?[131] Yet when they are invited to the Book of Allah to settle their disputes, some of them turn away heedlessly.

**Commentary:**

### A Portion of Scripture

The verse describes people who possess a portion of scripture and therefore have access to guidance, but who do not submit when the guidance challenges their preferences. “A portion” can indicate that they know some of revelation while failing to carry its whole message. Possessing selected texts or religious information does not guarantee obedience.

The Qur’an often distinguishes between receiving a book and upholding it. A person may recite scripture, cite its authority, and defend its honour while refusing its commands concerning justice, truthfulness, or recognition of later revelation. The real test is not possession but submission.

### The Book of Allah as Judge

They are invited to the Book of Allah to settle a dispute. The invitation recognizes revelation as a source of judgement above personal preference and group advantage. The Qur’an is not merely a source of spiritual inspiration; it also establishes principles of justice, rights, responsibility, and reconciliation.

This does not mean that every complex legal matter can be settled by quoting an isolated verse without scholarship. The Qur’an is interpreted through its context, the authentic Sunnah, Arabic language, principles of law, and qualified learning. To invite people to Allah’s Book is to invite them to fair, informed submission, not to slogan-based argument.

### Turning Away Heedlessly

The phrase “turn away heedlessly” describes deliberate avoidance. They do not necessarily reject scripture in speech; they avoid its judgement when it threatens their position. This is a form of practical denial. A person may profess belief in Allah’s command but search for a way around it whenever the command costs wealth, reputation, or power.

The verse therefore warns against selective religious commitment. The believer cannot accept the verses that confirm his preferences and ignore the verses that correct him. He must be willing to let revelation judge him before he tries to use revelation to judge others.

### Justice beyond Group Loyalty

Disputes often reveal whether people love truth or victory. Group identity can make a person resist a fair judgement simply because it came from outside his community. The Qur’an praises those who listen to speech and follow the best of it (39:18), and commands believers to return disputes to Allah and the Messenger (4:59).

This principle protects minorities, opponents, and the socially weak. A judgement is not Islamic merely because it benefits Muslims, and it is not un-Islamic merely because it corrects a Muslim. The standard is truth and justice.

### A Mirror for Muslims

Although the verse addresses a historical group, Muslims are not exempt. A Muslim may turn away from Qur’anic guidance when it concerns inheritance, business, marriage, leadership, treatment of enemies, or care for the poor. He may seek a convenient opinion while avoiding the responsibility attached to it.

The cure is *taqwā*: approaching the Qur’an as a servant who wants correction. The believer asks not only, “What does this verse say?” but also, “What must I change because it says this?”

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:44, 5:43–50, and 4:60 on refusing revelation’s judgement while claiming religious authority.
- Qur’an 4:59 on referring disputes to Allah and the Messenger.
- Qur’an 39:18 and 17:36 on listening carefully and following knowledge rather than assumption.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on justice beyond group loyalty.
- Qur’an 2:121 and 62:5 on the difference between carrying scripture and upholding it.
- For “a portion of the Scripture,” turning away, and revelation as a judge, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:23.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:24

> This is because they say, “The Fire will not touch us except for a few days.” They have been deceived in their faith by their wishful lying.

**Commentary:**

### False Religious Security

The verse exposes a claim of automatic safety: the Fire will touch them only for a few days. The claim turns religious affiliation into a guarantee while detaching it from faith, repentance, and righteous action. It is the illusion that ancestry, membership, or a historic covenant protects a person even while he rejects truth and harms others.

The Qur’an does not deny that Allah may forgive whom He wills or that punishment can have different degrees and durations according to His judgement. It rejects the fabricated certainty that a person can promise himself safety without evidence. No one may assign himself Paradise or decide that divine judgement cannot reach him.

### Wishful Thinking Disguised as Faith

The expression “wishful lying” is *gharrāhum fī dīnihim mā kānū yaftarūn*: they were deceived by inventions in their religion. False hope becomes especially dangerous when it is presented as sacred doctrine. A person may repeat a comforting claim until it feels like faith, but repetition cannot turn an invention into revelation.

True hope is based on Allah’s promises and accompanied by obedience. False hope ignores warnings, excuses injustice, and treats repentance as unnecessary. The believer hopes in Allah’s mercy while fearing that his own actions may distance him from it.

### No Privilege without Responsibility

The Qur’an had already addressed the claim that the Fire would touch the Children of Israel only briefly (2:80–82). It asked them to produce evidence for their claim and reminded them that whoever earns evil and is surrounded by sin faces its consequence. The same principle is universal: a person’s relationship with Allah is not secured by a label while the heart and conduct contradict it.

The Qur’an also rejects the claims of any community that assumes exclusive ownership of Paradise (2:111–112). Salvation belongs to those who submit to Allah and do good, not to those who rely on inherited honour.

### The Danger of Selective Scripture

The preceding verse described turning away from scripture when it is asked to settle disputes. This verse explains the inner reason: a false sense of exemption. If a person assumes that punishment cannot reach him, he will have little motivation to accept correction. He may read warnings as applying only to others.

The remedy is to read revelation as a message to oneself. The believer asks Allah for forgiveness, fulfils rights, and avoids both despair and careless confidence. He knows that no good deed is too small to matter and no deliberate injustice is too small to be ignored.

### Hope and Fear in Balance

Islam teaches hope in Allah’s mercy, but hope must be distinguished from *tamannī*, idle wishful thinking. A person who hopes for a harvest plants and tends the soil; a person who merely wishes waits without effort. Likewise, the hope of forgiveness produces repentance, prayer, restitution, and good deeds.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the intelligent person holds himself accountable and works for what follows death, while the incapable person follows his desires and merely hopes for Allah’s pardon, in a report recorded by Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:80–82 and 2:111–112 on false claims of limited punishment and exclusive salvation.
- Qur’an 4:123–124 on reward not being determined by wishful claims or group affiliation.
- Qur’an 7:169 and 57:14 on deception through false religious hopes.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on real hope through repentance and return.
- Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, the hadith describing the intelligent person who holds himself accountable and prepares for the Hereafter.
- For *tamannī*, religious deception, false security, and the balance of hope and fear, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:24.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:25

> But how ˹horrible˺ will it be when We gather them together on the Day about which there is no doubt—when every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged!

**Commentary:**

### The Day That Exposes Illusions

The verse asks the reader to imagine the Day when the claims of religious privilege and worldly security are tested. It is a rhetorical question of warning: what will they do when the promised Day arrives and every person is gathered before Allah? The false confidence of “only a few days” disappears when the reality of judgement is visible.

The Day is described as one about which there is no doubt. Certainty about the Hereafter is not a minor detail of faith. It determines whether a person sees life as accountable stewardship or as a period without final meaning. The resurrection gives significance to hidden deeds and hope to those whose rights were ignored in the world.

### Every Soul Paid in Full

“Every soul will be paid in full” means that each person receives the full consequence of what he earned. Allah’s judgement is not based on group slogans, family status, or public reputation. The record includes belief, intention, speech, action, repentance, and the rights of other people.

The wording “what it has done” joins inner accountability to outward conduct. A person cannot claim righteousness while his actions demonstrate injustice. Nor should a believer despair because people do not see his efforts. Allah knows and repays everything accurately.

### No One Is Wronged

Allah wrongs no one. A good deed is not lost, a sin is not transferred without justice, and a person is not blamed for what he could not control. The Qur’an describes the scales as precise and says that no soul will be treated unjustly even by the weight of a grain (21:47).

Allah’s justice includes knowing circumstances that human courts cannot fully know: access to knowledge, intention, capacity, coercion, ignorance, and repentance. This should make believers cautious about declaring the final fate of particular individuals. The final account belongs to the One who sees all realities.

### The Responsibility of Privilege

The immediate audience relied upon a claim of special religious status. The verse reminds them that privilege increases responsibility. Those given scripture, knowledge, wealth, or authority will answer for how they used it. A gift can become evidence for a person if he turns it into a reason for arrogance.

Muslims also need this warning. The Qur’an does not allow them to inherit another community’s errors while feeling secure in their own label. Every prayer and act of worship should deepen accountability, not produce contempt for others.

### Preparation for the Gathering

Preparation for the Day is practical: sincere faith, obligatory worship, repentance, fulfilling rights, charity, truthfulness, and leaving injustice. A person should settle debts, seek forgiveness, and repair relationships where possible. He cannot control the date of the gathering, but he can control how he meets it.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:9, 18:47, and 64:9 on the gathering of humanity.
- Qur’an 21:47 and 99:7–8 on the exactness of the scales and the record of every deed.
- Qur’an 4:40, 10:44, and 18:49 on Allah’s absolute justice.
- Qur’an 4:123–124 on reward not being determined by wishful claims.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the record of deeds, intercession, and the final account.
- For the rhetorical warning, full recompense, and the principle that Allah wrongs no one, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:25.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:26

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O Allah! Lord over all authorities! You give authority to whoever You please and remove it from who You please; You honour whoever You please and disgrace who You please—all good is in Your Hands. Surely You ˹alone˺ are Most Capable of everything.

**Commentary:**

### The Owner of All Authority

This verse teaches the Prophet ﷺ to call upon Allah as *Mālik al-Mulk*, the Owner and Sovereign of all authority. Every kingdom, government, institution, family influence, and personal capacity exists under Allah’s ownership. Human beings may exercise delegated power, but none owns authority independently or permanently.

The verse came in a period when the believers appeared politically weak and faced powerful opponents. It corrected their perception of history: power is not outside Allah’s control. The rise and fall of rulers occur within His knowledge and decree, even when human beings are responsible for their choices and crimes.

### Giving and Removing Authority

Allah gives authority to whom He wills and removes it from whom He wills. This does not mean that every ruler is morally approved or that every transfer of power is a reward. Authority may be a blessing, a test, a temporary opportunity, or a means by which a tyrant’s injustice becomes fully exposed.

Those who receive power must understand it as a trust. They are required to uphold justice, protect life, fulfil contracts, and consult responsibly. Those who lose power should examine their deeds but avoid claiming certainty about Allah’s hidden wisdom. Communities should use lawful means to establish justice rather than waiting passively for divine intervention.

### Honour and Disgrace

Allah grants honour and removes it. True honour is not identical to public fame, wealth, or office. The Qur’an says that the most honoured people in Allah’s sight are those most mindful of Him (49:13). A person may be publicly disgraced yet honoured before Allah through patience and integrity; another may be celebrated while carrying disgrace through oppression and hypocrisy.

The verse therefore frees believers from judging worth by worldly rank. It also warns the powerful that their position can disappear. Honour should produce gratitude and service, not arrogance.

### All Good Is in Allah’s Hand

The declaration “all good is in Your Hands” affirms Allah as the source of every true blessing. The believer does not attribute good to Allah while blaming Him for evil in a simplistic manner. Allah creates and permits events with wisdom, while human beings may be guilty of choosing and causing injustice. The good that comes from Allah’s decree is never absent, even when a servant cannot immediately see it.

The phrase also teaches positive hope. Allah can bring good from a political reversal, a personal loss, a delayed answer, or a change in circumstances. A believer may dislike an event and still trust that Allah’s wisdom contains good.

### Power and Supplication

The verse is a prayer as well as a doctrine. It teaches people to ask Allah for authority that can serve justice and to seek protection from authority that corrupts. It also reminds individuals that their influence over a family, workplace, or community is a small trust within Allah’s greater sovereignty.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that whoever Allah guides cannot be led astray and whoever He misguides cannot be guided by anyone else, in a hadith of his opening sermon. This does not cancel human responsibility; it places all ability beneath Allah’s power.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:26–27 on sovereignty, alternation of night and day, life and death, and provision.
- Qur’an 17:111 and 67:1 on Allah’s ownership and power.
- Qur’an 28:5–6 on the rise of the oppressed and the downfall of Pharaoh.
- Qur’an 49:13 on true honour through taqwā.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the supplication concerning Allah’s control of all affairs and the hadith on Allah’s guidance and misguidance.
- For *Mālik al-Mulk*, delegated authority, honour, disgrace, and *bi-yadika al-khayr*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:26.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:27

> You cause the night to pass into the day and the day into the night. You bring forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living. And You provide for whoever You will without limit.”

**Commentary:**

### The Alternation of Night and Day

This verse continues the description of Allah’s power with signs visible to every generation. Night enters day and day enters night in a continual alternation. The transition is gradual and ordered, not a chaotic collision. It gives time for work, rest, worship, reflection, and the rhythms of life.

The alternation also teaches that conditions change. Darkness is not permanent, and daylight does not last forever. A believer facing hardship may hope for relief, while a person enjoying power should remember that circumstances can turn. The One who changes the length and appearance of the day can change the conditions of a people and an individual.

### Life from Death and Death from Life

The phrase “You bring forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living” points to Allah’s power to create life, cause death, and bring what appears lifeless into living form. The Qur’an uses similar language for vegetation emerging from dead earth (30:19; 36:33). It also directs attention to the resurrection: the One who brings life from apparent death is fully capable of raising people after they die.

The exact physical examples may include a living creature emerging from non-living material, a plant from barren soil, or a living person from parents. The verse is broader than one scientific illustration. It establishes Allah’s creative power and invites reflection on the ordered transformations that sustain life.

### Provision without Limit

Allah provides for whom He wills without limit—*bighayri ḥisāb*. This means that His giving is not constrained by the stores of creation or by human calculations. He provides food, health, knowledge, opportunities, family, forgiveness, and spiritual openings. His treasures do not diminish when He gives.

This does not promise that every person receives identical wealth or that believers will never experience poverty. Provision includes different forms and comes according to wisdom. A person may have little money but abundant faith, health, or supportive relationships; another may have wealth that becomes a trial.

### The Ethics of Provision

Because Allah is the Provider, wealth should produce gratitude rather than arrogance. The wealthy are trustees, not absolute owners, and the poor are not worthless because their provision is limited. The Qur’an commands fair dealing, charity, and protection of those whose needs are greater (51:19; 70:24–25).

Trust in provision must not become laziness. The Prophet ﷺ taught that people should tie their camel and then rely upon Allah, according to a well-known report in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī. Work, planning, agriculture, trade, and education are means; Allah is the ultimate Provider.

### A Sign for the Heart

The verse joins political authority from the previous verse to cosmic power. Rulers rise and fall because the One who changes night and day controls all circumstances. Provision comes from the same Lord who gives and removes authority. The servant therefore turns to Allah in both public and private need.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:164, 24:44, and 35:13 on the alternation of night and day.
- Qur’an 30:19 and 36:33 on bringing life from apparent death and reviving the earth.
- Qur’an 51:58 and 11:6 on Allah as the Provider and His knowledge of every creature’s provision.
- Qur’an 16:71 and 17:30 on the wisdom and variation of provision.
- Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, the report teaching the balance between taking means and relying upon Allah.
- For *tūlij*, life and death, *rizq*, and *bighayri ḥisāb*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:27.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:28

> Believers should not take disbelievers as guardians instead of the believers—and whoever does so will have nothing to hope for from Allah— unless it is a precaution against their tyranny. And Allah warns you about Himself. And to Allah is the final return.

**Commentary:**

### The Meaning of Awliyāʾ

The key word in this verse is *awliyāʾ*, the plural of *walī*. It can mean intimate allies, protectors, patrons, or those whose authority and loyalty are relied upon. The verse warns believers not to replace the solidarity of the believing community with an allegiance that puts faith, safety, and justice under the control of people committed to opposing Allah’s guidance.

This is not a command to hate every non-Muslim or to treat people outside the Muslim community unjustly. The Qur’an explicitly permits kindness and fairness toward those who do not fight Muslims because of religion or expel them from their homes (60:8). The Prophet ﷺ entered contracts, traded, accepted gifts, visited the sick, and dealt justly with non-Muslims.

### Loyalty and Justice

Islam distinguishes between *walāʾ*, the deep loyalty of religious and moral allegiance, and ordinary human cooperation. A Muslim can have a non-Muslim neighbour, colleague, physician, teacher, business partner, or friend, while remaining committed to justice and to the Muslim community’s moral identity.

The prohibition concerns surrendering the community’s security, secrets, authority, or religious direction to hostile forces in a way that harms believers and strengthens oppression. It also warns against choosing an ally merely because he offers worldly advantage while treating faith as disposable.

### Context of Hostility

The surrounding verses discuss people who dispute revelation, seek to turn believers back from faith, and rejoice at their suffering (3:21–30). The verse must be read in that context. It is not a universal ban on civic cooperation or peaceful relationships. It is a protection against political or intimate allegiance that enables persecution and betrayal.

The same principle appears in 3:118, where believers are warned against taking as close confidants those who spare no effort to harm them. At the same time, justice and good conduct remain required. A community can protect its security without abandoning fairness.

### Precaution under Tyranny

The exception “unless it is a precaution against their tyranny” recognizes coercion and danger. A person who conceals faith or avoids confrontation to protect life and safety is not necessarily guilty of forbidden allegiance. The Qur’an gives an example of a believer from Pharaoh’s family who concealed his faith (40:28), and it permits an oppressed person to protect himself when forced, provided the heart remains firm in faith.

This exception is not a licence for treachery, deception for personal gain, or harming innocent people. It is a narrow allowance for protection from serious tyranny. The details require knowledge, honesty, and attention to the circumstances.

### Allah’s Warning

“Allah warns you about Himself” is a powerful reminder that the believer’s ultimate fear must be directed to Allah. Social pressure, political power, and economic threats can be real, but they are temporary. The final return is to Allah, who judges whether a person protected faith and justice or sold them for worldly security.

### Building Internal Solidarity

The verse calls believers to strengthen their own community through mercy, trust, consultation, education, and mutual aid. If believers abandon one another, hostile powers can exploit their division. Internal solidarity does not mean excusing wrongdoing within the community; it means correcting it with justice and protecting people from oppression.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:118–120 and 5:51–57 on hostile alliances and protecting communal trust, read with context and justice.
- Qur’an 60:8–9 on kindness and fairness toward peaceful non-Muslims and the limits of hostile alliance.
- Qur’an 4:144 and 40:28 on allegiance, coercion, and the believing person in Pharaoh’s household.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on maintaining justice even toward opponents.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the Prophet’s contracts and just dealings with non-Muslims.
- For *awliyāʾ*, *walāʾ*, coercion, precaution, and the balance between communal loyalty and justice, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:28.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:29

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Whether you conceal what is in your hearts or reveal it, it is known to Allah. For He knows whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is Most Capable of everything.”

**Commentary:**

### The Heart Is Not Hidden

This verse follows the warning about misplaced allegiance and reminds believers that Allah knows what is concealed and what is revealed. People may control their speech, public associations, and outward appearance, but they cannot hide their motives from Allah. He knows whether loyalty is sincere, whether an excuse is genuine, and whether a public religious action conceals a private betrayal.

The “heart” in Qur’anic language includes intention, conviction, desire, and moral orientation. It is not merely the physical organ. Allah knows the inner choices that eventually shape speech and action, even before other people can see their effects.

### Knowledge of the Heavens and Earth

The verse widens from the human heart to the whole universe. The One who knows every hidden intention also knows everything in the heavens and earth. Nothing is too distant, too small, or too complex for Him. His knowledge is not divided by number or distracted by simultaneous events.

This perfect knowledge supports perfect judgement. Allah does not judge on the basis of incomplete evidence or misleading appearances. He knows circumstances, capacity, coercion, intention, and consequences.

### Thoughts and Accountability

The verse does not mean that every involuntary thought is a sin. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah has pardoned his community for passing thoughts so long as they do not speak of them or act upon them, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. A sudden fear, temptation, or intrusive thought is not the same as choosing and cultivating it.

Accountability begins when a person welcomes a sinful intention, repeatedly nourishes it, speaks it, or acts upon it. The believer therefore seeks Allah’s help when harmful thoughts arise, redirects the mind, and does not despair merely because temptation exists.

### Sincerity and Hypocrisy

The verse is a warning against hypocrisy and a call to *ikhlāṣ*, sincerity. Worship is not made valuable by public visibility. A small deed performed for Allah may outweigh a large deed performed for reputation. Conversely, a person who uses religious language to gain power cannot deceive Allah.

Sincerity does not require hiding every good deed. Public worship may be necessary or beneficial, but the heart must seek Allah’s pleasure rather than the audience’s praise. The believer asks Allah to purify both intention and action.

### Comfort for the Truthful

Divine knowledge is also a comfort. If a person is falsely accused, his sincerity is not lost. If a good deed is hidden, Allah sees it. If a believer resists temptation privately, that struggle is known. The verse therefore produces both fear for the dishonest and hope for the truthful.

### Knowledge and Power

Allah closes by saying that He is capable of everything. Knowledge without power would not complete judgement, while power without knowledge could be arbitrary. Allah combines perfect knowledge and perfect power. He can expose hidden realities, resurrect every person, and bring every intention into account.

- Qur’an 40:19 and 6:59 on the knowledge of secret actions and the unseen.
- Qur’an 99:7–8 on the presentation of every deed.
- Qur’an 2:225 on Allah’s distinction between deliberate oaths and unintentional speech.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:30

> ˹Watch for˺ the Day when every soul will be presented with whatever good it has done. And it will wish that its misdeeds were far off. And Allah warns you about Himself. And Allah is Ever Gracious to ˹His˺ servants.

**Commentary:**

### Deeds Made Present

The verse directs attention to the Day when every soul will find its deeds presented before it. Good deeds that were forgotten, hidden, or undervalued will be present. So too will misdeeds that a person concealed or treated as insignificant. The final account is not based on memory, reputation, or the record preserved by people; it is based on Allah’s complete knowledge.

The presentation of deeds makes accountability personal. No person can say that a nation, family, leader, or crowd made every choice for him. Circumstances influence responsibility, but each soul meets its own record and its own intentions.

### Wishing Evil Were Distant

The sinner will wish that his misdeeds were far away. In this life, a person may try to distance himself from guilt through denial, excuses, blame, or distraction. On the Day of Judgement, the deed is no longer hidden behind language. The person sees the harm and consequence clearly and wishes there were a vast distance between himself and what he did.

This is a warning to repent before the record is closed. Sincere repentance can erase sins, and good deeds can remove minor sins when accompanied by faith and avoidance of major wrongdoing. Delaying repentance is dangerous because death can arrive before a person repairs what he damaged.

### Allah Warns about Himself

The repeated warning “Allah warns you about Himself” means that Allah warns people against meeting Him while persisting in rebellion. It is an expression of mercy, not a threat from a tyrant. A loving parent warns a child away from danger; divine warning is infinitely more informed and serious.

The warning also directs fear to its proper place. People may fear loss of wealth, public opinion, or political power, but none of these compares with standing before Allah. Fear of Allah should lead to obedience, not paralysis or despair.

### Allah Is Gracious to His Servants

The verse ends with grace. Allah warns because He wants people to avoid the Fire. He gives time to repent, multiplies good deeds, conceals many faults, and accepts return. His grace appears in the fact that a person is informed of danger while there is still an opportunity to change.

The believer holds the two endings together: Allah’s warning is real, and His grace is vast. False security ignores the warning; despair ignores the grace. Correct faith turns both into responsible action.

### Daily Self-Accounting

A practical response is *muḥāsabah*, examining one’s deeds before the Day examines them. At the end of a day, a person can ask: Whom did I wrong? Which obligation did I neglect? Which blessing did I misuse? What can I repair tomorrow? This is not self-hatred. It is preparation for a meeting with Allah.

- Qur’an 18:49, 69:18–19, and 99:7–8 on the record and presentation of deeds.
- Qur’an 2:286, 4:110, and 25:70 on responsibility, repentance, and forgiveness.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on Allah’s grace and the command not to despair.
- Qur’an 3:132 and 66:6 on obeying Allah and protecting oneself from the Fire.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on repentance, the record of deeds, and the final judgement.
- For *taḥḍur*, *sūʾ*, divine warning, grace, and *muḥāsabah*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:30.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:31

> Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “If you ˹sincerely˺ love Allah, then follow me; Allah will love you and forgive your sins. For Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.”

**Commentary:**

### The Test of Love

Many people claim to love Allah, but this verse gives the claim a test: follow the Messenger ﷺ. Love is not merely an emotion or a statement. It appears in obedience, imitation, trust, and willingness to let revelation correct the self. The Prophet’s Sunnah shows how the Qur’an becomes lived worship and character.

The command does not make the Prophet an object of worship. Muslims love and follow Muhammad ﷺ because Allah sent him as a messenger and model. Worship remains for Allah alone. Following the Messenger is therefore an expression of tawḥīd, not a rival to it.

### Ittibāʿ: Deliberate Following

The Arabic word *ittibāʿ* means following closely. It includes accepting the Prophet’s teachings, practising his acts of worship, adopting his ethical character, and respecting his judgements. It is more than admiring his biography. A person who praises the Prophet but rejects his clear guidance has not fulfilled the verse’s test.

Following also requires learning which reports genuinely belong to the Sunnah and understanding them correctly. It does not mean treating every cultural custom attributed to the Prophet as obligatory, nor does it mean refusing legitimate differences among qualified scholars.

### Allah’s Love

The reward is extraordinary: Allah will love them. Human love may be unstable and dependent on benefit; Allah’s love is an honour that brings guidance, protection, acceptance, and nearness. The Qur’an identifies qualities beloved to Allah, including patience, excellence, justice, repentance, and reliance upon Him.

Divine love does not mean that the servant becomes divine or that all hardship disappears. The prophets were beloved yet tested severely. It means that the servant is under Allah’s care and receives the grace to continue on the path.

### Forgiveness Follows Following

Allah promises forgiveness of sins to those who sincerely follow the Messenger. Forgiveness requires faith, repentance, and obedience, and it is ultimately granted by Allah’s mercy. The promise prevents people from thinking their past makes return impossible. It also prevents claims of love that produce no moral change.

A person should ask: Does my prayer resemble the Prophet’s instruction? Does my trade reflect his honesty? Does my family life reflect his mercy? Does my response to disagreement reflect his fairness? These questions turn love into practice.

### Love of Allah in Daily Life

Following the Prophet includes the great acts of worship and the small manners that shape character: truthfulness, kindness to neighbours, keeping promises, controlling anger, visiting the sick, caring for children, and avoiding harm. The Sunnah is not limited to outward symbols; it includes mercy, justice, modesty, and courage.

The Prophet ﷺ said that none truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself, in a hadith recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. This shows that love of Allah must reshape relationships with people.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:31–32 on love expressed through following and obeying Allah and His Messenger.
- Qur’an 4:80 and 59:7 on obeying and following the Messenger ﷺ.
- Qur’an 9:24 on loving Allah and His Messenger above worldly attachments.
- Qur’an 2:222, 3:134, and 3:159 on qualities beloved to Allah.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:32

> Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “Obey Allah and His Messenger.” If they still turn away, then truly Allah does not like the disbelievers.

**Commentary:**

### Love Becomes Obedience

The previous verse presented following the Messenger ﷺ as the proof of love for Allah. This verse states the command directly: obey Allah and His Messenger. Obedience is not a substitute for love; it is love made visible in choices. A person who claims devotion while treating Allah’s commands as optional has made a claim without proof.

The order “Allah and His Messenger” reflects the Messenger’s role as the one who conveys and explains Allah’s revelation. Obeying the Messenger is not obeying a competing authority. It is obeying Allah through the messenger whom He appointed (4:80).

### Obedience Is Not Blindness

Islamic obedience is based on revelation and sound understanding. It does not require obedience to fabricated reports, abusive leaders, or personal claims made in the name of religion. The Qur’an commands believers to verify information (49:6) and to refer disputes to Allah and the Messenger (4:59).

When a human being commands disobedience to Allah, that command is not obeyed. The Prophet ﷺ said that there is no obedience to a created being in disobedience to the Creator. This principle protects believers from religious authoritarianism and political tyranny.

### Turning Away

“Turning away” describes refusing the call after it has been made clear, not an honest person’s need for time, study, or clarification. Human beings can be weak and commit sins while still believing; the verse’s conclusion addresses settled rejection of Allah’s truth, not every believer who falls short.

The Qur’an distinguishes between sin and disbelief, while warning that persistent disobedience can harden the heart. The right response to a lapse is repentance and renewed obedience, not despair.

### Allah Does Not Love Disbelief

The verse states that Allah does not love the disbelievers. This is a moral judgement on rejection of truth, not a command for Muslims to behave unjustly toward non-Muslims. Allah commands justice and kindness toward those who are peaceful (60:8), and the Prophet ﷺ dealt fairly with people of different faiths.

A believer may dislike disbelief as a false belief while still treating a person with dignity, fulfilling contracts, protecting his rights, and inviting him with wisdom. The Qur’an’s moral categories should not be turned into permission for hatred or abuse.

### The Messenger’s Example

Obedience to the Messenger includes his manner of applying commands. He prayed, fasted, gave charity, forgave, consulted, kept treaties, protected neighbours, and opposed oppression. A claim of obedience that imitates only the outward forms while abandoning his honesty and mercy is incomplete.

The Prophet ﷺ said, “Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah,” and he also warned against disobeying his guidance. His authority is therefore bound to his revealed mission, not to personal glorification.

- Qur’an 3:31–32 on following and obeying Allah and His Messenger.
- Qur’an 4:59, 4:80, and 24:54 on referring disputes to Allah and obeying the Messenger.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 60:8–9 on justice and kindness toward non-Muslims who are not hostile.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports before acting upon them.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, “Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah,” and the principle that obedience is not due in disobedience to the Creator.
- For *ṭāʿah*, turning away, the limits of human authority, and Allah’s disapproval of disbelief, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:32.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:33

> Indeed, Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of ’Imrân above all people ˹of their time˺.

**Commentary:**

### Divine Choice and Prophetic Mission

Allah declares that He chose Adam, Nūḥ, the family of Ibrāhīm, and the family of ʿImrān. This choice is a choice for guidance, service, responsibility, and honour—not permission for racial arrogance. Allah selected people to carry revelation and exemplify submission so that humanity could be guided through them.

Adam was the first human and prophet, Nūḥ was a foundational messenger whose people faced long resistance, and the families of Abraham and ʿImrān produced major figures in the history of revelation. The verse introduces a family of stories that will include Mary, Jesus, Zakariyyā, Yaḥyā, and their communities.

### Adam and Noah

Adam’s selection included the honour of human origin, knowledge, repentance, and stewardship on earth. His story teaches that a chosen servant may make a mistake but can return to Allah with humility. Nūḥ’s selection displayed perseverance. He called his people for centuries, facing mockery and rejection while remaining faithful to the message.

Their inclusion reminds believers that prophetic honour is tied to obedience. Adam’s descendants do not inherit salvation simply through ancestry, and Nūḥ’s relatives who rejected faith were not protected by family connection.

### The Family of Abraham

The family of Ibrāhīm includes the prophetic line associated with Isḥāq and Yaʿqūb and the line associated with Ismāʿīl, culminating in Muhammad ﷺ. Abraham’s family was chosen because of its service to tawḥīd, sacrifice, hospitality, justice, and the transmission of revelation.

This choice does not mean that every descendant was righteous or that others were excluded from Allah’s mercy. The Qur’an repeatedly makes faith and conduct the basis of honour. The family’s privilege created greater responsibility to preserve the covenant and convey truth.

### The Family of ʿImrān

The mention of the family of ʿImrān prepares for the account of Mary and Jesus. Classical exegetes discuss the precise identity of ʿImrān and note that the Qur’an mentions an earlier family of ʿImrān in connection with Mūsā and Hārūn and another in the account of Mary. The shared name does not require confusing the individuals; the verse honours the family connected with the sacred history being narrated.

The selection of this family includes purity, worship, service to the sanctuary, and the birth of Mary and Jesus by Allah’s command. Their story will answer disputes about Jesus and reaffirm his human prophetic mission.

### “Above the People of Their Time”

The translation’s phrase “of their time” reflects the classical understanding that being chosen above the worlds can refer to the people of a particular era. Allah’s selection is not a permanent licence for every descendant or a claim that one ethnic group is inherently superior. The Qur’an explicitly says that the most honoured before Allah are the most mindful of Him (49:13).

The prophets’ families were honoured because they carried a trust. When later members betrayed that trust, lineage did not protect them. This is a warning to every community that receives revelation.

### Family as a School of Faith

A righteous family can be a school in which faith is taught through worship, sacrifice, knowledge, and character. Parents are responsible for creating an environment of obedience, but children remain morally responsible for their own choices. The coming verses show both the prayer of ʿImrān’s wife and the special upbringing of Mary.

- Qur’an 11:25–49 and 71:1–28 on Noah’s mission and perseverance.
- Qur’an 2:124–141 and 14:35–41 on Abraham’s family, covenant, and prayer.
- Qur’an 49:13 on honour through taqwā rather than lineage.
- Qur’an 66:10–12 on family connection not replacing individual faith.
- For divine selection, the families named, “the worlds” of their era, and the distinction between honour and inherited entitlement, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:33.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:34

> They are descendants of one another. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Commentary:**

### A Connected Prophetic Line

The verse explains that the chosen individuals and families are descendants of one another. The prophetic history is connected by family, teaching, covenant, and shared submission. Revelation is not a series of unrelated religions invented by different peoples; it is a continuing call from one Lord.

The family connection includes biological descent, but the greater connection is spiritual. The prophets inherited faith, responsibility, and the duty to convey truth. Their communities were expected to preserve the covenant, not merely celebrate their ancestry.

### Lineage and Responsibility

The verse should not be read as saying that every descendant of a prophet is automatically righteous. The Qur’an gives examples of relatives who rejected faith, including the son of Nūḥ (11:42–46) and the father of Ibrāhīm (19:41–48). Conversely, Allah’s guidance is open to people who do not belong to a prophetic family.

Lineage can be an honour when it inspires gratitude and emulation, but it becomes a trial when it produces entitlement. A person who claims the legacy of a prophet must uphold truth, prayer, mercy, and justice. Family status cannot replace obedience.

### Allah Hears the Prayers of Families

Allah is *al-Samīʿ*, the All-Hearing. He hears the prayers of the prophets and their families, including the prayers that will be recorded in the following verses. He hears spoken words, private supplications, and the unspoken need behind them. No sincere prayer disappears because it is quiet or uttered by a socially insignificant person.

His hearing is not limited by distance or number. He can hear every servant simultaneously without confusion. This gives believers confidence when they call upon Him.

### Allah Knows the Hidden Reality

Allah is also *al-ʿAlīm*, the All-Knowing. He knows who truly carries the prophetic character and who merely claims a family connection. He knows the intention behind a parent’s dedication, the sincerity of a child’s worship, and the future consequences of a family’s choices.

The pairing of hearing and knowledge is significant. Allah hears the words of a covenant and knows whether the heart supports them. People may be impressed by religious lineage, but Allah knows the difference between genuine inheritance and empty claim.

### One Message across Generations

The connected descendants show that worship of Allah alone is the central thread of prophetic history. Adam received guidance, Nūḥ called his people to Allah, Abraham rejected idolatry, and later prophets continued the same call. Differences in law and historical circumstance do not erase the unity of revelation.

The Muslim community joins this chain by believing in all prophets and committing itself to the final revelation. It should honour the chain by living the message, not merely reciting the names.

- Qur’an 2:132–133 and 2:136 on the shared faith of Abraham’s descendants and belief in all prophets.
- Qur’an 11:42–46 and 19:41–48 on lineage not guaranteeing faith.
- Qur’an 6:84–87 on the prophetic family and guidance.
- Qur’an 49:13 on honour through taqwā rather than ancestry.
- Qur’an 40:60 and 2:186 on Allah hearing and answering supplication.
- For *dhurriyyah*, prophetic inheritance, *al-Samīʿ*, and *al-ʿAlīm*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:34.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:35

> ˹Remember˺ when the wife of ’Imrân said, “My Lord! I dedicate what is in my womb entirely to Your service,[133] so accept it from me. You ˹alone˺ are truly the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”

**Commentary:**

### A Mother’s Consecration

The wife of ʿImrān dedicates the child in her womb to Allah’s service. Classical tafsīr works preserve reports that she was called Ḥannah, but the Qur’an leaves her unnamed and focuses on her devotion and prayer. Her vow expresses gratitude before the child is born and a desire that the child serve revelation and the sanctuary.

At that time, dedicating a child could mean assigning him or her to religious service, study, and care for the sacred place. It was not a sacrifice of the child. The mother was entrusting the child’s life to Allah’s worship and asking Allah to accept the intention.

### Intention before Achievement

The prayer occurs before the mother knows the outcome. This teaches the value of intention. A servant can turn an expected blessing into a commitment to Allah before receiving it. Parents should want children who know, worship, and serve Allah, rather than treating them only as extensions of family status or economic plans.

The prayer also teaches that a good intention does not guarantee the exact form of its fulfilment. Allah may answer in a way beyond the petitioner’s expectation. The mother expected a male child suitable for the sanctuary’s service, but Allah’s wisdom chose Mary, a girl whose service and purity would be extraordinary.

### A Vow and Its Limits

The Arabic word *naḏr* refers to a vow made for Allah. In Islamic law, voluntary vows are not encouraged as a way to force an outcome from Allah, and a vow involving disobedience must not be fulfilled. But a lawful vow creates responsibility when made sincerely. The broader lesson is to avoid careless promises in worship and to fulfil lawful commitments.

A parent cannot use a vow to erase the child’s rights or impose injustice. Children are trusts with their own dignity and responsibilities. Dedication to Allah should produce care, education, and mercy, not coercive ownership.

### “Accept It from Me”

The mother asks Allah to accept her offering. This is the attitude of the righteous: they act, but they do not assume acceptance. The Qur’an praises those who give while their hearts remain fearful because they will return to their Lord (23:60). Sincerity, lawful action, and humility accompany one another.

The request for acceptance also teaches parents that raising a child is an act of worship only when done with a sound intention and lawful means. They should ask Allah to accept their efforts while recognizing their limitations.

### Allah Hears and Knows

She closes by invoking Allah as All-Hearing and All-Knowing. Allah hears her spoken prayer and knows the private intention behind it. He knows the child in the womb, the future of the family, and the needs of the community better than the mother herself.

This gives comfort to every parent whose prayer seems small or whose child’s future is uncertain. Allah knows the concern before it becomes words. He also knows whether a religious dedication is sincere or driven by reputation.

- Qur’an 2:128 and 37:100 on dedicating one’s life and family to Allah.
- Qur’an 22:29 and 76:7 on fulfilling lawful vows.
- Qur’an 23:57–61 on righteous people acting while fearing whether their deeds are accepted.
- Qur’an 66:6 and 31:13–19 on parents’ responsibility for nurturing faith and character.
- Qur’an 6:98 and 22:5 on Allah’s knowledge of human formation in the womb.
- For *naḏr*, consecration to the sanctuary, intention, and acceptance of deeds, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:35.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:36

> When she delivered, she said, “My Lord! I have given birth to a girl,”—and Allah fully knew what she had delivered—“and the male is not like the female.[134] I have named her Mary, and I seek Your protection for her and her offspring from Satan, the accursed.”[135]

**Commentary:**

### Allah Knows What She Delivered

The mother had dedicated the child to service, likely expecting a male who could undertake the customary duties of the sanctuary. When she gave birth to a girl, she spoke from the expectations of her society. The Qur’an immediately says that Allah knew best what she had delivered. This divine comment corrects any assumption that a girl was a lesser answer to the prayer.

Mary would fulfil a unique form of service and become the mother of Jesus. Her life would be a sign of Allah’s power, purity, and care. The verse teaches that human expectations about usefulness, honour, or leadership are limited. Allah may place the greatest spiritual mission in the person people initially undervalue.

### “The Male Is Not Like the Female”

The statement reflects the mother’s practical expectations about the sanctuary’s service, not a universal declaration that men are superior to women in worth before Allah. The Qur’an repeatedly affirms that believing men and women receive equal spiritual reward (33:35; 16:97) and that honour is based upon taqwā (49:13).

Biological differences can create different responsibilities, but difference is not inferiority. Mary’s story itself demonstrates that a woman can be chosen, purified, honoured, and made a sign for all humanity. A community must not use social assumptions to limit what Allah can accomplish through a person.

### Naming Mary

She names the child Maryam and places her under Allah’s care. Naming can express a parent’s prayer and hope. The mother’s disappointment does not turn into rejection; she continues to entrust the child to Allah. This is an important lesson for parents when a child’s abilities, personality, or life path differs from what they expected.

The Qur’an later describes Mary as truthful, chosen, and purified (3:42; 5:75). Her honour is linked to faith and Allah’s selection, not to worldly power.

### Seeking Protection from Satan

The mother seeks protection for Mary and her offspring from Satan, the accursed. This is a model of parental supplication. Parents cannot control every influence around their children, but they can seek Allah’s protection, teach faith, provide a sound environment, and model righteousness.

Protection from Satan does not mean that a person will face no temptation or hardship. It means seeking safety from his whispering, deception, and domination. The Qur’an teaches people to seek refuge with Allah when Satan incites them (41:36; 23:97–98).

### Mary and Jesus in Prophetic Tradition

The Prophet ﷺ said that every child is touched by Satan at birth except Mary and her son, according to reports in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Classical scholars connect this report with the mother’s supplication and Allah’s special acceptance of it. The hadith honours Mary and Jesus and does not make them divine; it highlights Allah’s protection and their created status.

### The Importance of Maternal Prayer

The prayer of the mother is recorded in revelation even though she is not named. This honours the spiritual labour of mothers and shows that a parent’s private supplication may shape history in ways no one can foresee. A mother or father should never underestimate the value of praying for a child’s faith, character, and protection.

- Qur’an 41:36 and 23:97–98 on seeking refuge from Satan’s whispering.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith concerning Satan’s touch at birth and the exception of Mary and her son.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:37

> So her Lord accepted her graciously and blessed her with a pleasant upbringing—entrusting her to the care of Zachariah. Whenever Zachariah visited her in the sanctuary, he found her supplied with provisions. He exclaimed, “O Mary! Where did this come from?” She replied, “It is from Allah. Surely Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit.”

**Commentary:**

### Acceptance and Good Upbringing

Allah accepted Mary graciously and caused her to grow in a pleasant and excellent manner. The expression suggests both physical development and spiritual nurture. Allah did not merely accept the mother’s vow; He cared for the child’s formation and prepared her for a unique mission.

A child’s upbringing is a trust shared by parents, guardians, teachers, and the wider community. Physical care, education, protection, affection, and moral formation all matter. Mary’s story shows that a sacred environment should cultivate character, not merely transmit information.

### Zakariyyā as Guardian

Mary was entrusted to Zakariyyā, a prophet and servant of the sanctuary. His guardianship was a responsibility of care, education, protection, and spiritual companionship. The Qur’an presents him not as someone who claims ownership over Mary but as a trustworthy caretaker of a divine trust.

This is a model for guardianship generally. Those responsible for children, orphans, students, and vulnerable people must remember that authority is service. A guardian should protect dignity, provide necessities, and help the person grow toward Allah rather than exploit dependence.

### The Sanctuary and Private Worship

Zakariyyā found Mary in the sanctuary, in a place devoted to worship and remembrance. The setting indicates that Mary’s life was shaped by prayer, modesty, learning, and sacred service. Worship is not an escape from responsibility; it forms the character needed to bear responsibility well.

The Qur’an presents Mary as a model of devotion without reducing her to a passive figure. Her worship, truthfulness, courage, and response to Allah’s command will become central to the account of Jesus.

### Provision from Allah

Zakariyyā found Mary supplied with provisions that were unusual for the season or beyond what he expected. He asked where they came from, and she answered that they were from Allah. Classical tafsīr works discuss whether this was a miracle or an extraordinary provision through means unknown to Zakariyyā. The important point is that Allah is the true Provider and can provide through familiar or extraordinary means.

The event also teaches that miraculous provision does not cancel gratitude or lawful effort. Mary received without arrogance, and Zakariyyā’s question was not an accusation. It was an opportunity for her to testify to Allah’s generosity.

### Provision without Limit

Mary’s answer uses the same principle found in 2:212 and 3:27: Allah provides without limit or measure. His giving is not restricted by human calculations. He can open a door where people see none, provide knowledge where circumstances seem barren, and bring spiritual growth from hardship.

This does not mean that every desire is granted immediately or that poverty is proof of divine rejection. Allah provides according to wisdom and may make one person’s provision a means of testing another through charity and care.

### The Effect on Zakariyyā

Mary’s answer awakens Zakariyyā’s own supplication for a child, recorded in the next verses. A person’s blessing can remind another of Allah’s power without becoming an object of envy. The believer sees another person’s gift and asks Allah, not the gift’s possessor, for what is good.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:38

> Then and there Zachariah prayed to his Lord, saying, “My Lord! Grant me—by your grace—righteous offspring. You are certainly the Hearer of ˹all˺ prayers.”

**Commentary:**

### Prayer Inspired by Another’s Blessing

When Zakariyyā witnessed Allah’s special provision for Mary, he turned immediately to prayer. He did not respond with jealousy or conclude that Allah’s generosity was limited. Another person’s blessing became a reminder of Allah’s power and an encouragement to ask.

This is a noble response to seeing good in others. The believer may admire a blessing, ask Allah for something similar if it is good, and pray that the other person retains it. Envy wishes that the blessing disappear; gratitude recognizes the Giver.

### Righteous Offspring

Zakariyyā asks not merely for a child but for “righteous offspring.” Parenthood is not an end in itself. A child is a trust whose life should be directed toward faith, character, service, and beneficial knowledge. The prayer is therefore a request for a continuing legacy of worship, not simply for biological continuity.

The Qur’an records several prayers for righteous families (25:74; 46:15). These prayers teach parents to combine effort with supplication: choose lawful means, nurture children wisely, and ask Allah to protect the result from corruption.

### Asking by Allah’s Grace

He asks “by Your grace,” acknowledging that the desired gift cannot be demanded as a right. Allah gives out of generosity, and the servant approaches Him with humility. The phrase also expresses hope: even when ordinary circumstances seem difficult, divine grace is not limited by them.

A servant may ask Allah for a spouse, child, health, livelihood, or knowledge, but he should remain content with Allah’s wisdom concerning the timing and form of the answer. Supplication is worship even before the requested outcome appears.

### The Hearer of Prayer

Zakariyyā calls Allah *samīʿ al-duʿāʾ*, the Hearer of prayer. Allah hears the voice, understands the need, and knows the deeper good behind the request. He hears public and private prayer, words spoken fluently and words broken by grief.

The next verse shows that the answer came through the angels, but the true source was Allah. Means do not replace the One who hears and answers. A believer may ask people for lawful help while keeping the heart directed to Allah.

### Prayer and Apparent Impossibility

Zakariyyā’s circumstances, described in the following verses and in Sūrah Maryam, made the request appear unlikely. He was advanced in age and his wife was barren. His prayer therefore teaches that apparent impossibility is not a reason to stop asking Allah. At the same time, asking does not mean rejecting reality or refusing medical and practical means.

Faith recognizes ordinary causes while knowing that Allah created and controls them. When He wills an extraordinary answer, it is not difficult for Him.

### A Prayer for the Muslim Family

Parents can use this verse as a model: ask for children who are righteous, truthful, beneficial, and devoted to Allah. Children can also pray for their parents and seek to become a continuing source of reward through good character and service.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:39

> So the angels called out to him while he stood praying in the sanctuary, “Allah gives you good news of ˹the birth of˺ John who will confirm the Word of Allah and will be a great leader, chaste, and a prophet among the righteous.”

**Commentary:**

### Good News during Worship

The angels called to Zakariyyā while he stood praying in the sanctuary. The good news came in a place and moment of worship, showing that supplication is not wasted even when its answer arrives later than expected. Zakariyyā had asked for righteous offspring, and Allah announced a son with a name and qualities chosen by divine wisdom.

The Qur’an calls the child Yaḥyā, translated here as John. His name itself is associated with life. His birth was a sign that Allah gives life and fulfils promises beyond ordinary expectation.

### Confirming the Word of Allah

Yaḥyā would “confirm the Word of Allah.” Classical tafsīr explains this in several connected ways. He would confirm the truth of Allah’s promise and revelation, and he would confirm ʿĪsā, who is called a word from Allah in the verses that follow (3:45). Yaḥyā and Jesus were closely connected in mission, calling the Children of Israel back to obedience.

The phrase also teaches that prophets do not compete for personal authority. One prophet confirms another, and revelation is a chain of truth. A believer should not accept one prophet while rejecting another whom Allah sent.

### A Great Leader

Yaḥyā is described as *sayyid*, a leader of virtue and honour. This does not mean political domination or social privilege. It means leadership in knowledge, worship, character, courage, and service. A true religious leader guides people toward Allah and does not make them dependent upon his personality.

Leadership is therefore a trust. Parents, teachers, scholars, managers, and officials should ask whether their influence produces righteousness or merely obedience to themselves.

### Chastity and Self-Control

Yaḥyā is described as *ḥaṣūr*, chaste and restrained. His purity indicates control over desire and freedom from sexual corruption. Chastity is not contempt for marriage or the body. It is directing desire into lawful channels, guarding the gaze and private parts, and refusing to exploit others.

The quality also represents broader self-control. A person who cannot govern anger, appetite, wealth, or speech cannot easily carry religious leadership. Purity is active discipline, not simply absence of opportunity.

### A Prophet among the Righteous

Yaḥyā is both a prophet and one of the righteous. Prophethood is a divine selection and not acquired through ambition. Righteousness is the character expected of every prophet: worship, truthfulness, mercy, courage, and obedience.

The verse honours Yaḥyā without making him divine. Like every prophet, he is a servant chosen to convey Allah’s message. The following account of Jesus will establish the same distinction concerning the Messiah.

### The Place of Prayer

The sanctuary is mentioned again. Zakariyyā’s private worship and public service are linked. The sacred place is not valuable merely for architecture; its value appears when people pray, learn, purify themselves, and serve Allah’s creation there.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:40

> Zachariah exclaimed, “My Lord! How can I have a son when I am very old and my wife is barren?” He replied, “So will it be. Allah does what He wills.”

**Commentary:**

### Wonder, Not Rejection

Zakariyyā’s question expresses wonder at an extraordinary promise, not rejection of Allah’s power. He recognizes the ordinary circumstances: he is very old and his wife is barren. The angels answer that Allah will bring it about because Allah does what He wills.

This distinction matters. A believer may ask how an event will occur while remaining certain that Allah can make it occur. Curiosity and seeking clarification are not the same as denial. Zakariyyā’s question leads to a sign and deeper gratitude rather than to rebellion.

### Allah Is Not Bound by Ordinary Causes

Human life normally depends upon parents of reproductive age and fertility. Allah created these means, but He is not limited by them. He created Adam without parents, ʿĪsā without a father, and Yaḥyā from an elderly father and barren mother. These extraordinary births all point to divine power rather than to the divinity of the created child.

The verse does not encourage people to deny ordinary causes or reject medical knowledge. It teaches that causes are not independent. A person may seek treatment, consult specialists, and pursue lawful means while knowing that Allah controls every outcome.

### A Promise Beyond Calculation

Zakariyyā had asked for righteous offspring, not for a miracle to satisfy curiosity. Allah answered according to wisdom and gave him a son with a remarkable character. The promise was a gift to the prophet, his wife, the sanctuary, and the wider community that would receive Yaḥyā’s guidance.

The believer should therefore ask Allah for what is good even when calculations appear unfavourable. He should avoid turning prayer into a demand that Allah fulfil a particular timetable or method. Divine wisdom may answer with the requested gift, a different gift, protection from harm, or reward in the Hereafter.

### Allah Does What He Wills

The statement “Allah does what He wills” affirms divine will without implying arbitrariness. Allah’s will is joined to His knowledge and wisdom. He does not act from ignorance, need, or injustice. What seems impossible to creation is easy for the Creator, and what He withholds may contain wisdom beyond the servant’s perception.

This truth should produce both hope and humility. Hope, because no difficulty is beyond Allah; humility, because no person can compel Allah or fully comprehend every decree.

### The Moral of the Miracle

The miracle is not merely a display of power. It prepares the reader for the later miracle of Mary’s child and for the Qur’an’s argument about Jesus. If Allah can create a son for elderly parents, then the unusual birth of Jesus does not make him divine. Allah creates however He wills, while the created being remains His servant.

The miracle also shows that a late answer is not an ignored prayer. Zakariyyā’s supplication may have been made after years of waiting, but Allah heard it and answered at the appointed time.
