# Surah al-Baqarah (The Cow, 2) — Verse-by-Verse Tafsir, Verses 281–286

This is the last batch, and these six verses are the closing of the longest surah in the Qur'an.

2:281 closes the *ribā* passage with a Day of return. **2:282 is the Verse of Debt — the longest verse in the Book**, and it is placed exactly where the prohibition of interest means credit has to be restructured. 2:283 handles the case where no scribe can be found. 2:284 states the hardest thing in the surah — that what is inside you will be accounted for. **And 2:285–286 are the two verses the Companions wept over, that were given from a treasure beneath the Throne, and that a Muslim recites at night.**

**One caution that governs 2:282 in particular.** It contains the most contested clause in the Qur'an regarding women — the requirement of two women witnesses in place of one man. **I will state the classical position plainly, because it is the majority position and hiding it would be dishonest. I will then state the range of readings that exist, the limits the classical jurists themselves placed on the ruling, and the internal Qur'anic evidence that bears on it.** This is a live and painful disagreement, and it should be presented as one rather than either flattened into an apology or weaponised.

**And one thing to say about the shape of the ending.** The longest surah in the Qur'an — 286 verses of law, narrative, polemic and argument — **does not end with a ruling. It ends with a prayer.** That is the last thing the reader is left with.

---

## Verse 281

**"Be mindful of the Day when you will ˹all˺ be returned to Allah, then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged."**

In simple terms: and fear a Day in which you will be returned to God; then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned, and they will not be wronged.

*Wa-ittaqū yawman turja'ūna fīhi ilā Allāhi thumma tuwaffā kullu nafsin mā kasabat wa hum lā yuẓlamūn.*

*Wa-ittaqū **yawman*** — "and fear **a Day**."

**Note the object of the *taqwā*: a Day, not a Person.** Not "fear God" here, though that is the normal construction and it appears in the same passage (2:278). **Here the command is to be mindful of a Day.** Which is the surah's method throughout: **the fear is anchored to a specific event with a specific date, not to a vague dread.**

*Turja'ūna fīhi **ilā Allāh*** — "in which you will be **returned to God**."

***Turja'ūna*** — passive: **you will be returned.** Not "you will go." **You will be brought back**, which carries the agent with it. **You do not arrive there by your own movement; you are returned.**

**And note *ilā Allāh* — to God.** Not to a place, not to a court. **To Him.** The same phrase closes 2:285 ahead: *wa ilayka al-maṣīr* — and to You is the journey's end.

*Thumma tuwaffā kullu nafsin **mā kasabat*** — "then every soul will be **paid in full** for what it earned."

***Tuwaffā*** — the same verb as 2:272's *yuwaffa ilaykum*: **paid in full, the measure completed, nothing short.** Which is why the two verses read as a pair: **what you give is repaid in full (2:272), and what you earned is paid in full (2:281).** The same accounting runs in both directions.

***Mā kasabat*** — what it **earned**. **Note the verb: *kasaba*, to earn, to acquire by one's own effort.** The reckoning is for what you acquired, not for what you were.

*Wa hum **lā yuẓlamūn*** — "and they will not be wronged."

**The third "you will not be wronged" in this passage — 2:272, 2:279, and here.** Which is the framing of the whole *ribā* legislation: **the prohibition is not a confiscation. It is a refusal to let either party be wronged.**

**And the placement.** This verse stands between the declaration of war (2:279) and the law of debt (2:282). **Which is to say: the enforcement mechanism for the entire financial code is not a state. It is a Day.** A community can evade a court. **Nobody evades this.**

**A note on chronology.** The reports differ over which verse was the last of the Qur'an to be revealed, and 2:281 is among the candidates — along with 2:278, 2:282, 4:176, 5:3 and 9:128–129. **A transmitted report from Ibn 'Abbās places 2:281 at the very end and states that the Prophet ﷺ lived only a short time afterwards.** The disagreement is genuine and I present it as one; **no single report should be asserted over the others.**

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 282

**"O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing. Let the scribe maintain justice between the parties. The scribe should not refuse to write as Allah has taught them to write. They will write what the debtor dictates, bearing Allah in mind and not defrauding the debt. If the debtor is incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate, let their guardian dictate for them with justice. Call upon two of your men to witness. If two men cannot be found, then one man and two women of your choice will witness—so if one of the women forgets the other may remind her. The witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned. You must not be against writing ˹contracts˺ for a fixed period—whether the sum is small or great. This is more just ˹for you˺ in the sight of Allah, and more convenient to establish evidence and remove doubts. However, if you conduct an immediate transaction among yourselves, then there is no need for you to record it, but call upon witnesses when a deal is finalized. Let no harm come to the scribe or witnesses. If you do, then you have gravely exceeded ˹your limits˺. Be mindful of Allah, for Allah ˹is the One Who˺ teaches you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things."**

In simple terms: O you who believe, when you take on a debt for a stated term, write it down. Let a scribe write it between you with justice; and let no scribe refuse to write, as God has taught him — so let him write, and let the one who owes dictate, fearing God his Lord and not diminishing anything of it. And if the one who owes is of unsound mind, or weak, or unable to dictate himself, then let his guardian dictate with justice. And call two witnesses from among your men; and if there are not two men, then one man and two women from those you accept as witnesses — so that if one of the two errs, the other may remind her. And let not the witnesses refuse when they are called. And do not grow weary of writing it, small or great, with its term. That is more just with God, more upright for the testimony, and more likely that you will not doubt. Unless it is a present transaction you conduct among yourselves — then there is no blame on you if you do not write it; but call witnesses when you trade. And let no harm be done to scribe or witness; and if you do, it is transgression on your part. And fear God — and God teaches you. And God is Knowing of everything.

*Yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā tadāyantum bi-daynin ilā ajalin musamman fa-ktubūhu, wa-l-yaktub baynakum kātibun bi-al-'adli, wa lā ya'ba kātibun an yaktuba kamā 'allamahu Allāhu, falyaktub walyumlil alladhī 'alayhi al-ḥaqqu walyattaqi Allāha rabbahu wa lā yabkhas minhu shay'an, fa-in kāna alladhī 'alayhi al-ḥaqqu safīhan aw ḍa'īfan aw lā yastaṭī'u an yumilla huwa falyumlil waliyyuhu bi-al-'adli, wa-stashhidū shahīdayni min rijālikum, fa-in lam yakūnā rajulayni fa-rajulun wa-mra'atāni mimman tarḍawna mina al-shuhadā'i an taḍilla iḥdāhumā fatudhakkira iḥdāhumā al-ukhrā, wa lā ya'ba al-shuhadā'u idhā mā du'ū, wa lā tas'amū an taktubūhu ṣaghīran aw kabīran ilā ajalih, dhālikum aqsaṭu 'inda Allāhi wa aqwamu li-al-shahādati wa adnā allā tartābū, illā an takūna tijāratan ḥāḍiratan tudīrūnahā baynakum fa-laysa 'alaykum junāḥun allā taktubūhā, wa ash-hidū idhā tabāya'tum, wa lā yuḍārra kātibun wa lā shahīdun, wa in taf'alū fa-innahu fusūqun bikum, wa-ittaqū Allāha wa yu'allimukumu Allāhu wa Allāhu bi-kulli shay'in 'alīm.*

**This is the longest verse in the Qur'an. And the first thing to ask is why it is here.**

**The answer the commentators give is structural: the prohibition of *ribā* creates the need for a law of debt.** Once interest is forbidden, credit cannot be priced by time. **So it has to be secured another way — by documentation.** The two things are one reform: **you may not charge for the delay; you must write down the obligation.** 2:279 cancelled the return on credit. 2:282 supplies the machinery that replaces it.

**Now the verse, in its parts.**

*Idhā tadāyantum bi-daynin **ilā ajalin musamman*** — "when you take on a debt **for a stated term**."

**Note the qualifier: *ajal musammā* — a named, specified term.** The whole verse is about **deferred** obligations. **The delay is the thing that creates the risk, and the risk is the thing the verse is managing.**

*Fa-**ktubūhu*** — "then **write it**."

The command. **And from it the jurists derived the principle: for deferred transactions, writing is the default** — the majority holding it to be strongly recommended or obligatory, with the minority treating it as strictly obligatory, and all agreeing that it is the normative form.

*Wa-l-yaktub baynakum kātibun **bi-al-'adl*** — "and let a scribe write between you **with justice**."

**Note: *bi-al-'adl*.** The scribe is not a stenographer. **He is bound to justice** — which means he must not favour either party in the wording, and he must not write a term that is unfair.

*Wa lā ya'ba kātibun an yaktuba **kamā 'allamahu Allāh*** — "and let no scribe refuse to write, **as God has taught him**."

**This is a principle of immense reach, and it is stated in nine words.**

**The skill you have is a thing God taught you. Therefore you owe its use.** The scribe who declines because it is inconvenient, or because the parties are unimportant, or because there is no fee in it, **is refusing a debt he owes on his own knowledge.**

**The jurists derived from this that the scribe's service is a communal obligation (*farḍ kifāyah*)** — someone in every community must hold the skill, and the one who holds it must deploy it when needed. **The same logic extends, by the jurists' own reasoning, to every skill the community cannot do without.** The doctor, the engineer, the teacher, the translator: **God taught you, so you cannot refuse.**

*Falyaktub wa-**lyumlil** alladhī 'alayhi al-ḥaqqu* — "so let him write, and let the one who owes **dictate**."

**Note who dictates: the debtor.** Not the creditor. **The person who owes is the one who states what he owes**, which is itself a protection — he cannot later say he did not understand the figure.

*Wa-l-yattaqi Allāha rabbahu **wa lā yabkhas minhu shay'an*** — "and let him fear God his Lord, and not **diminish anything** of it."

***Yabkhas*** — to give short measure, to underpay. **The debtor is commanded not to understate his own debt.** Which is the verse's fairness again: **the protection runs both ways.**

*Fa-in kāna alladhī 'alayhi al-ḥaqqu **safīhan aw ḍa'īfan aw lā yastaṭī'u an yumilla huwa** fa-lyumlil waliyyuhu bi-al-'adl* — "and if the one who owes is **of unsound mind, or weak, or unable to dictate himself** — then let his guardian dictate **with justice**."

**Three categories of incapacity, and they cover the range:**
- ***Safīh*** — of deficient understanding, unable to manage his own affairs.
- ***Ḍa'īf*** — weak: by age, by illness, by youth.
- ***Lā yastaṭī'u an yumilla huwa*** — unable to dictate himself: by speech impediment, by not knowing the language, by absence.

**And note the constraint on the guardian: *bi-al-'adl* — with justice.** **He is not authorised to dictate in his ward's interest. He is authorised to dictate in justice.** Which means: **if the ward owes something the guardian would prefer not to record, he must record it.** The guardian's brief is the truth, not the ward's advantage.

*Wa-**stashhidū** shahīdayni min rijālikum* — "and **call to witness** two witnesses from among your men."

*Fa-in lam yakūnā rajulayni fa-rajulun wa-**mra'atāni** mimman tarḍawna mina al-shuhadā'i an **taḍilla** iḥdāhumā fa-**tudhakkira** iḥdāhumā al-ukhrā* — "and if there are not two men, then one man and **two women** from those you accept as witnesses — **so that if one of the two errs, the other may remind her**."

**And here is the clause, and it must be handled with complete honesty in both directions.**

**What the text says, without addition:**
1. **The context is a financial contract with a deferred term.** This is not a general ruling about testimony, and it is not a ruling about women's intellect, religion, character, or legal capacity in general.
2. **The stated reason is given in the verse itself:** *an taḍilla iḥdāhumā fatudhakkira iḥdāhumā al-ukhrā* — "so that if one of the two errs, the other may remind her." **The purpose named is accuracy in the record.**
3. **The structure is symmetrical.** The verse does **not** say "the reliable one reminds the forgetful one." It says **if either of the two errs, the other reminds her.** Both are witnesses; either may be the one who needs reminding; either may be the one who does the reminding.
4. **A variant reading is recorded** (noted by al-Jalālayn among others): *fa-tudhakkira* / *fa-tudhkira*, and the reading of *an* as the conditional *in* with *tudhakkiru*, making "she will remind" the beginning of a new sentence.

**What the classical commentators said.** The majority position, stated plainly by Ibn Kathīr, is that **God requires two women in place of one man "because of the woman's shortcomings, as the Prophet ﷺ described"** — and he cites the ḥadīth in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* from Abū Hurayrah, in which the Prophet ﷺ said: **"As for the deficiency in her intellect, the testimony of two women equals the testimony of one man — and this is the deficiency in the intellect. As for the deficiency in her religion, she remains nights at a time when she does not pray, and she breaks the fast in Ramaḍān."**

Al-Jalālayn glosses the clause similarly: **"if one of the two women errs — forgets the testimony given — the other, the one who remembers, will remind her."**

**And what must be said about that ḥadīth, because it is quoted constantly and often without its context.** The phrase "deficiency of intellect" (*nuqṣān 'aql*) is **defined by the Prophet ﷺ himself in the same sentence** — he does not leave it as a general verdict on women's minds. He defines it by reference to the testimony rule, **and the second half of the statement (the "deficiency in religion") is not a moral defect at all: it names the ritual exemption of menstruation and post-natal bleeding, which is a mercy, not a failing.** The commentators — al-Nawawī among them — understand the "deficiency" as **a matter of precision (*ḍabṭ*) in the specific setting of financial litigation, not a claim about intelligence, moral worth, or legal capacity in general.**

**What the jurists themselves held about the scope.** The classical jurists did **not** apply the ratio universally:
- **A single woman's testimony is accepted alone in matters that only women can know** — childbirth, breastfeeding, menstruation, physical defects, virginity. The Sunnah is explicit: **the Prophet ﷺ acted on the testimony of one woman that she had suckled both spouses, and separated them** (in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, from 'Uqbah ibn al-Ḥārith).
- **Women's testimony is not accepted at all by the majority in *ḥudūd* and *qiṣāṣ*** — a separate rule with a separate rationale.
- **24:6–9 is an internal counter-text.** In the *li'ān* procedure, **a husband testifies four times and a wife testifies four times** — and her oaths count equally against his, and are in fact what averts the punishment from her. **In that setting, one woman's sworn word matches one man's, one for one.** A verse that treats a woman's testimony as equal in one legal setting cannot be read as a general statement about her reliability.
- **A position argued by a number of scholars, classical and modern, holds that the effective cause (*'illah*) is customary unfamiliarity with commercial litigation rather than any intrinsic deficiency — and that where a woman has that familiarity, the ratio is satisfied by her alone.** This reading is associated in particular with the work of **Ibn al-Qayyim**, who argued generally that rulings follow their effective causes and that the application shifts when the cause does. **I am stating the position as it is argued; I am not able to supply a verified page reference for it and will not invent one.**
- **Others read *an taḍilla* as "forgets" or "is distracted" rather than "errs in judgement,"** and emphasise the mutuality already noted.

**And what should not be done with this verse — in either direction.** It should not be read as a statement that a woman's intellect is half a man's: **the text does not say that, and the tradition's own reading of the ḥadīth is narrower than that.** And it should not be quietly reinterpreted until it no longer says what it says: **the text does require two women where it requires one man, in this setting, and that requirement is the majority ruling of the Schools.**

**The honest position is this: the verse legislates a rule of evidence for a specific seventh-century commercial context, gives its own reason for it, and is read by the classical jurists as a rule about evidence rather than about persons. How that rule applies where the reason no longer holds is a genuine juristic question, and it is being argued now. I present it as open.**

*Wa lā ya'ba al-shuhadā'u **idhā mā du'ū*** — "and let not the witnesses refuse **when they are called**."

**The same obligation as the scribe's: the witness owes his testimony.** And the ḥadīth in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* from Zayd ibn Khālid: **"Shall I not tell you of the best of witnesses? The one who gives his testimony before he is asked for it."**

*Wa lā tas'amū an taktubūhu **ṣaghīran aw kabīran** ilā ajalih* — "and do not grow weary of writing it, **small or great**, with its term."

**Note what is closed off here: the small-amounts exemption.** The natural human move is to say that the small ones don't matter. **The verse forecloses it.** *Ṣaghīran aw kabīran* — small or great. **Both.**

*Dhālikum **aqsaṭu 'inda Allāhi** wa **aqwamu li-al-shahādati** wa **adnā allā tartābū*** — "that is **more just with God**, and **more upright for the testimony**, and **more likely that you will not doubt**."

**Three reasons, given explicitly. And this matters more than it looks.**

**The verse states its own purposes:** justice, integrity of evidence, and the removal of doubt. **Which means the ruling is given with its reasons attached — and a ruling given with its reasons is a ruling that can be reasoned about.** The jurists have always read stated purposes as the ground for extension and for the reading of hard cases. **A verse that tells you why it says what it says is inviting you to think about what it is for.**

*Illā an takūna **tijāratan ḥāḍiratan** tudīrūnahā baynakum fa-laysa 'alaykum junāḥun allā taktubūhā* — "unless it is a **present transaction** you conduct among yourselves — then there is no blame on you if you do not write it."

**The exemption: spot trade, hand to hand, no deferral.** **And note why: the risk the verse is managing is created by the delay.** Where there is no delay, the writing requirement lapses. **The exemption is not a concession to convenience. It is the logic of the ruling applied.**

*Wa **ash-hidū** idhā tabāya'tum* — "and **call witnesses** when you trade."

**But note: the witnesses are still called, even in the exempted case.** The writing lapses; **the witnessing does not.** Which tells you which of the two is doing more work.

*Wa lā **yuḍārra** kātibun wa lā shahīdun* — "and let **no harm** be done to scribe or witness."

***Yuḍārra*** — the same root as 2:231 and 2:233: *ḍirār*, harm. **And note the passive: the scribe and the witness are not to be harmed.** In practice this means: **the party who loses must not punish the person whose record or testimony lost it for him.** The verse anticipates the revenge against the witness and forbids it.

*Wa in taf'alū fa-innahu **fusūqun bikum*** — "and if you do, it is **transgression on your part**."

***Fusūq*** — going out, departing from the bounds. **And note: *bikum* — "on your part," "in you."** The corruption is located in the person, not merely in the act.

*Wa-ittaqū Allāha wa **yu'allimukumu Allāh*** — "and fear God — and **God teaches you**."

**And this is the verse's last word before its closing Name, and it is one of the great sentences in the Qur'an.**

**Note the order: *ittaqū* — fear God. And then: *yu'allimukumu* — He teaches you.**

**The sequence is causal. Knowledge follows *taqwā*, not the other way round.** Not "learn, then be godly." **Be godly, and you will be taught.** The verse places the acquisition of knowledge in the hands of God and makes it a consequence of the fear of Him.

**Which is the resolution of a problem every serious student hits: you cannot learn everything first and start obeying afterwards.** The verse says: **begin where you are, with what you have, in awe of God — and what you lack will be given to you.** And it is stated here, in the middle of the most technical, legal, procedural verse in the whole Book. **The law ends by telling you that the law is not the point.**

*Wa Allāhu bi-kulli shay'in **'alīm*** — "and God is **Knowing of everything**."

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 283

**"If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found, then a security can be taken. If you trust one another, then ˹there is no need for a security, but˺ the debtor should honour this trust ˹by repaying the debt˺—and let them fear Allah, their Lord. And do not conceal the testimony, for whoever conceals it, their hearts are indeed sinful. And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you do."**

In simple terms: and if you are on a journey and find no scribe, then a pledge taken into possession. And if one of you trusts another, then let the one who was trusted discharge his trust, and let him fear God his Lord. And do not conceal the testimony — and whoever conceals it, his heart is sinful. And God knows what you do.

*Wa in kuntum 'alā safarin wa lam tajidū kātiban fa-rihānun maqbūḍah, fa-in amina ba'ḍukum ba'ḍan falyu'addi alladhī u'tumina amānatahu wa-l-yattaqi Allāha rabbah, wa lā taktumū al-shahādata wa man yaktumhā fa-innahu āthimun qalbuh, wa Allāhu bi-mā ta'malūna 'alīm.*

*Wa in kuntum **'alā safarin** wa lam tajidū kātiban* — "and if you are **on a journey** and find no scribe."

**The concession begins with the condition: travel, and the absence of a scribe.** The verse legislates the exception rather than leaving it to be assumed — **which is itself the method of this whole passage: the duties are stated, and then the conditions under which they lift are stated too.** Nothing is left to anxiety.

*Fa-**rihānun maqbūḍah*** — "then **a pledge taken into possession**."

***Rihān*** — pledges, securities. ***Maqbūḍah*** — **taken, grasped, in hand.**

**And the operative word is the second one.** The classical jurists derived from *maqbūḍah* that **a pledge which the creditor has not actually taken into his possession is not a valid pledge.** The security consists in the holding, not in the naming. **A phrase in a contract that says "such-and-such is pledged" while the thing remains in the debtor's hands creates nothing.**

**Which is a principle with a long reach: a security interest that is not actually in hand is not a security interest.** The whole law of pledge turns on this one adjective.

*Fa-in amina ba'ḍukum ba'ḍan fa-**lyu'addi alladhī u'tumina amānatah*** — "and if one of you trusts another, then **let the one who was trusted discharge his trust**."

**And this clause is the heart of the verse, and it is one of the most important sentences in the whole passage.**

**Mutual trust suspends the pledge. It does not suspend the debt.**

**Read that carefully, because it is the answer to a very common confusion.** The documentation, the witnesses, the pledge — **these are instruments of protection. They are not the obligation.** If you skip the paperwork because you trust each other, **you still owe the money.** The verse's instruction in that case is not "then it doesn't matter." **It is: *falyu'addi alladhī u'tumina amānatahu* — the one who was trusted must discharge his trust.**

**Which means: the trust raises the obligation, it does not lower it.** A person who borrows from a friend without paperwork and then treats the absence of paperwork as licence is not merely a bad debtor. **He has broken an *amānah*.** And the word used here — ***amānah*** — is the word for the trust that the heavens and the earth refused to carry (33:72). **That is the weight the verse puts on an unwitnessed loan between friends.**

*Wa-l-yattaqi Allāha rabbah* — "and let him fear God his Lord."

**The reminder that closes it: the witness you skipped is replaced by a greater one.**

*Wa lā taktumū **al-shahādata*** — "and do not **conceal the testimony**."

*Wa man yaktumhā fa-innahu **āthimun qalbuh*** — "and whoever conceals it — **his heart is sinful**."

**Note the grammar, which is unusual and deliberate: *āthimun qalbuh* — "sinful is his heart."** Not "he is a sinner." **The sin is located in the heart specifically.**

**And that is exactly right, because concealing testimony is the sin that leaves no trace.** The hand does not move, the tongue does not speak, nothing outward happens. **And the verse says the corruption is nonetheless real and nonetheless located: in the heart.** Which is a perfect preparation for 2:284, one verse later.

**And the ḥadīth already cited belongs here too: "the best of witnesses is the one who gives his testimony before he is asked for it" (*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, from Zayd ibn Khālid).**

*Wa Allāhu bi-mā ta'malūna 'alīm* — "and God knows what you do."

**Which is the answer to the concealed testimony.** The thing nobody saw is the thing He knows.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 284

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Whether you reveal what is in your hearts or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it. He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills. And Allah is Most Capable of everything."**

In simple terms: to God belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And whether you disclose what is in your souls or conceal it, God will call you to account for it. Then He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills. And God is powerful over everything.

*Lillāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍi, wa in tubdū mā fī anfusikum aw tukhfūhu yuḥāsibkum bihi Allāhu, fa-yaghfiru li-man yashā'u wa yu'adhdhibu man yashā'u, wa Allāhu 'alā kulli shay'in qadīr.*

**This is the verse that terrified the Companions, and the story of what they did with it is one of the most moving in the whole *sīrah*.**

*Wa in tubdū mā fī **anfusikum** aw tukhfūhu **yuḥāsibkum bihi Allāh*** — "and whether you disclose what is in **your souls** or conceal it, **God will call you to account for it**."

**The claim is total: the interior is real, and it will be accounted for.** Not only what you did. Not only what you said. **What was in you.**

**And the ḥadīth — in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* and *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, from Abū Hurayrah — records what the Companions did with it.**

Reported from Ibn 'Abbās: when this verse was revealed, **something entered their hearts that had never entered them before.** They came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: *"O Messenger of God, we have been charged with deeds we cannot bear — the prayer, the fasting, the *jihād*, the charity — and now this verse has been revealed to you, and we cannot bear it."*

The Messenger of God ﷺ said: **"Do you want to say what the people of the two Books said before you — 'we hear and we disobey'? Rather say: *sami'nā wa-aṭa'nā, ghufrānaka rabbanā, wa ilayka al-maṣīr* — we hear and we obey; Your forgiveness, our Lord, and to You is the return."**

**And they said it, and their tongues became soft with it.** And God revealed immediately afterwards **2:285 and 2:286.**

**And the report continues, and it is the part to hold: when the Prophet ﷺ recited each petition of 2:286, God said — *"I have done so."*** "Our Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or err." — **"I have done so."** "Our Lord, do not lay on us a burden as You laid on those before us." — **"I have done so."** "Our Lord, do not charge us with what we have no capacity to bear." — **"I have done so."** "Pardon us, forgive us, have mercy on us." — **"I have done so."**

**Now the question of what this verse means, and I will present the disagreement.**

**Some of the early authorities held that 2:284 was abrogated by 2:286** — and this is the wording that appears in Ibn Kathīr's account.

**Others — and this is the weightier and more careful position — hold that it is firm and not abrogated, but qualified.** On this reading, **the accounting is real, and what was lifted was the burden of the involuntary.** And the qualifier is supplied by the Sunnah, not by cancellation:

- In the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s from Abū Hurayrah: **"God has pardoned my community for what their souls whisper to them, as long as they do not act on it or speak of it."**
- And the widely transmitted ḥadīth: **"God has overlooked for my community: error, forgetfulness, and what they are forced into"** — recorded by Ibn Mājah and al-Ṭabarānī, **its grading disputed, strengthened by some on account of its multiple chains.**

**Both positions are held and I will not resolve them. But the practical consequence is the same on either reading, and it is the thing that matters:**

**A settled intention is a real thing and is accounted for. An involuntary thought is not.** The worry you did not invite, the image that arrived unbidden, the anger that flashed and passed, the *waswasa* that horrifies you precisely because you would never want it — **these are not yours.** The line the ḥadīth draws is exact: **as long as you do not act on it or speak of it.**

**And that is the verse's mercy, and it is a mercy aimed directly at scrupulous people.** The people who are destroyed by 2:284 are not the careless. **They are the ones who take the interior seriously enough to be frightened of it.** The Companions who came to the Prophet ﷺ that day were the best of the community, **and they were the ones it terrified.**

**A note for the scrupulous, then, because this verse is used against them.** If you are tormented by thoughts you did not choose and do not want, **the tradition's answer is not that you should try harder to stop having them. It is that they have already been pardoned, and that the trying harder is often the thing that feeds them.** What you are accountable for is what you do with them. **Let them pass. They are not yours.**

*Fa-yaghfiru li-man yashā'u wa yu'adhdhibu man yashā'* — "and He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills."

**Note that this clause is *in* the verse.** The accounting is stated, **and the forgiveness is stated in the same breath.** The verse that frightened them contains its own relief — **it is just that they were too frightened to see it, which is why 2:286 had to spell it out.**

*Wa Allāhu 'alā kulli shay'in **qadīr*** — "and God is **powerful over everything**."

***Qadīr*** — the same Name that closes 2:259, where the man who had been dead a hundred years said: *a'lamu anna Allāha 'alā kulli shay'in qadīr.* **The surah's argument about resurrection and its argument about the interior close on the same Name.** He is powerful over the heart as over the grave.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 285

**"The Messenger ˹firmly˺ believes in what has been revealed to him from his Lord, and so do the believers. They ˹all˺ believe in Allah, His angels, His Books, and His messengers. ˹They proclaim,˺ “We make no distinction between any of His messengers.” And they say, “We hear and obey. ˹We seek˺ Your forgiveness, our Lord! And to You ˹alone˺ is the final return.”"**

In simple terms: the Messenger believes in what was sent down to him from his Lord, and the believers [do too]. Each one believes in God, His angels, His books and His messengers: "we make no distinction between any of His messengers." And they said: "we hear and we obey; Your forgiveness, our Lord — and to You is the return."

*Āmana al-rasūlu bimā unzila ilayhi min rabbihi wa-al-mu'minūna, kullun āmana bi-Allāhi wa malā'ikatihi wa kutubihi wa rusulihi, lā nufarriqu bayna aḥadin min rusulih, wa qālū sami'nā wa-aṭa'nā ghufrānaka rabbanā wa ilayka al-maṣīr.*

**Note who is named first: *āmana al-rasūlu* — the Messenger believes.**

**The Prophet ﷺ is the first believer in what was sent to him.** Not the conveyor of it only. **He is the first person to hold it.** Which is a point the Qur'an makes about him repeatedly and which matters against any reading that treats him as a mere transmitter of something he did not himself receive.

*Wa-al-mu'minūna* — "and the believers."

**And then the community, in the same belief.** The verse joins him to them in a single act of faith.

*Kullun āmana bi-Allāhi wa **malā'ikatihi** wa **kutubihi** wa **rusulih*** — "each one believes in God, and **His angels**, and **His books**, and **His messengers**."

**The four articles, in this order.** God, then the angels, then the books, then the messengers. **This is the standard enumeration of faith, and it is the one the hadith of Jibrīl gives** (in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, from 'Umar: *īmān* is "that you believe in God, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and the decree, its good and its ill").

*Lā nufarriqu bayna aḥadin min **rusulih*** — "**we make no distinction between any of His messengers**."

**And this clause closes the question 2:253 opened.**

At 2:253 the surah stated that God has preferred some messengers over others. **Here, thirteen verses later, it states the believers' response to that: we do not discriminate.** The two verses are not in tension. **God ranks; we do not.** Which is exactly the position the Sunnah enforces, with the ḥadīth already cited at 2:253: **"No one should say that I am better than Yūnus ibn Mattā."**

**The surah that discussed ranking ends by stating the believers' refusal to rank for themselves.** That is the discipline of it: **the preference is His knowledge, and our duty is the whole company.**

*Wa qālū **sami'nā wa-aṭa'nā*** — "and they said: **we hear and we obey**."

**And here is the bookend, and it is one of the most deliberate in the whole surah.**

At **2:93** the surah described the Israelites: *wa qālū **sami'nā wa-'aṣaynā*** — "and they said: **we hear and we disobey**."

**Two hundred verses apart, the same three words with one letter changed.** *Aṭa'nā* against *'aṣaynā*. **Obeyed against disobeyed.**

**And the surah has been building toward this from the beginning.** 2:285 is the believing community answering the Children of Israel's answer. **The surah that spent its length describing what the earlier community did with their covenant now states what this one says with theirs.**

*Ghufrānaka rabbanā* — "**Your forgiveness**, our Lord."

**Note the order: obedience first, then the request for forgiveness.** Not "forgive us, and then we'll obey." **"We hear and we obey — and we still need Your forgiveness."** Which is the honest sequence: **the obedience is offered and it is still insufficient, and they know it.**

*Wa ilayka **al-maṣīr*** — "and to You is the **return**."

***Maṣīr*** — the journey's end, the place one ends up. **The same claim as 2:281's *turja'ūna ilā Allāh*, in the believer's own mouth rather than in a command.**

**The ḥadīth of these verses, in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* (806), from Ibn 'Abbās:**

*While the Messenger of God ﷺ was sitting with Jibrīl, he heard a creaking sound above him. Jibrīl raised his gaze toward the sky and said: "This is a gate in the heaven being opened, which has never been opened before today." An angel descended from it and came to the Prophet ﷺ and said:*

**"Receive glad tidings of two lights which you have been given, which no prophet before you was given: the Opening of the Book (al-Fātiḥah) and the last verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah. You will not recite a letter of them but you will be given it."**

**And in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, from Ibn Mas'ūd:** the Messenger of God ﷺ said — **"I was given the last verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah from a treasure beneath the Throne."** In some transmissions: **which no prophet before me was given.**

**And in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* (5009), from Abū Mas'ūd al-Anṣārī: "Whoever recites the last two verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah at night, they suffice him."**

**A further narration, from al-Nu'mān ibn Bashīr** (recorded by al-Tirmidhī and others): **"God inscribed a book two thousand years before He created the heavens and the earth, from which the last two verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah were revealed. If they are recited for three nights in a home, no devil will come near it."** **Its grading is disputed and I flag it as such.**

**Now note the pairing in the ḥadīth of the two lights: *al-Fātiḥah* and the closing of al-Baqarah.** The surah that this commentary has been working through for 286 verses is bracketed with the seven verses that open the Qur'an. **The Book opens with praise and its longest chapter closes with supplication — and both are called light.**

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 286

**"Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford. All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss. ˹The believers pray,˺ “Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or make a mistake. Our Lord! Do not place a burden on us like the one you placed on those before us. Our Lord! Do not burden us with what we cannot bear. Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us. You are our ˹only˺ Guardian. So grant us victory over the disbelieving people.”"**

In simple terms: God does not charge a soul except with its capacity. For it is what it earned, and against it is what it earned. "Our Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or err. Our Lord, and do not lay on us a burden as You laid it on those before us. Our Lord, and do not charge us with what we have no capacity to bear. And pardon us, and forgive us, and have mercy on us. You are our Guardian, so give us victory over the disbelieving people."

*Lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wus'ahā, lahā mā kasabat wa 'alayhā mā iktasabat, rabbanā lā tu'ākhidhnā in nasīnā aw akhṭa'nā, rabbanā wa lā taḥmil 'alaynā iṣran kamā ḥamaltahu 'alā alladhīna min qablinā, rabbanā wa lā tuḥammilnā mā lā ṭāqata lanā bihi, wa-'fu 'annā wa-ghfir lanā wa-rḥamnā, anta mawlānā fa-nsurnā 'alā al-qawmi al-kāfirīn.*

***Lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wus'ahā*** — "God does not charge a soul except **with its capacity**."

**And this is the bookend that closes the surah.**

**The same clause appeared at 2:233, in the middle of the law of nursing mothers: *lā tukallafu nafsun illā wus'ahā* — no soul is charged beyond its capacity.** The surah stated it there as a ruling about a mother and a father and a child. **And it states it here, 53 verses later, as the final principle of the whole Book.**

**That is the shape of al-Baqarah: the principle is given first in the smallest human setting — a woman feeding a baby, a man who cannot pay — and then it is handed to the entire community as the last thing they hear.**

**And it is the direct answer to 2:284.** The Companions said: "we cannot bear this." **And God's response opens: *you are not charged beyond what you can bear.*** The verse that frightened them is answered by the verse that relieves them, **and the relief comes as the very first clause.**

**Note the word: *wus'ah* — capacity, room, the width of what a thing can hold.** Not *ṭāqah*, which appears later in the verse. ***Wus'ah* is the room something has.** The claim is: **the obligation is sized to you.** It is not that you are heroic. **It is that what is asked of you fits.**

**And this is one of the most load-bearing sentences in the entire religion, and it should be stated as firmly as possible against the way it is often used.** *Lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wus'ahā* is **not** a counsel of low standards. It is: **whatever you are actually unable to do is not actually required of you.** The tradition has built enormous practical law on this sentence — the concession for the traveller, the sick, the coerced, the forgetful, the person in hardship, the debtor given time (2:280). **It is the principle behind every *rukhṣah* in the *fiqh*.**

*Lahā mā kasabat wa 'alayhā mā **iktasabat*** — "for it is what it earned, and against it is what it **earned**."

**Note the two verbs: *kasabat* and *iktasabat*.** Both from the same root. **The commentators note the variation and give several readings: that the first is the good and the second the evil; or that *iktasabat* carries the sense of effort and artifice — the thing a person went out of his way to acquire.**

**Either way the claim is exact: **the account is yours.** Nothing is transferred to you and nothing is taken from you.

**And then the prayer — five petitions, and they are the surah's last words.**

**1. *Rabbanā lā tu'ākhidhnā in nasīnā aw akhṭa'nā*** — "Our Lord, **do not take us to task if we forget or err**."

**Note that this petition is the one the ḥadīth answers first: "I have done so."** And it is the direct remedy for 2:284's terror. **Forgetfulness and error — the two things that make the interior unmanageable — are lifted.**

**2. *Rabbanā wa lā taḥmil 'alaynā **iṣran** kamā ḥamaltahu 'alā alladhīna min qablinā*** — "Our Lord, and do not lay on us a **burden** as You laid it on those before us."

***Iṣr*** — a burden, a load, and specifically the kind that binds and constricts; the same word used at 7:157 of the "burdens and shackles" that were upon the earlier communities. **The prayer is not to be given a lighter religion than anyone else. It is: do not give us the religion of impossibility, where a sin required a punishment that could only be satisfied by something unbearable.**

**3. *Rabbanā wa lā tuḥammilnā mā lā **ṭāqata** lanā bih*** — "Our Lord, and do not charge us with what we have **no capacity** to bear."

**And note the word: *ṭāqah*.** The same word the believing remnant used at 2:249 when they looked at Goliath's army and said: ***lā ṭāqata lanā al-yawma bi-Jālūta wa junūdih*** — "we have no capacity today against Goliath and his soldiers."

**The surah's last petition uses the word of the outnumbered.** Which ties the closing of al-Baqarah to the small company that stood in front of the large one and asked for patience, firm feet, and victory. **The community ends the longest surah in the Qur'an asking, in the vocabulary of the people who were afraid and went anyway.**

**4. *Wa-'fu 'annā wa-ghfir lanā wa-rḥamnā*** — "and **pardon us, forgive us, have mercy on us**."

**Three verbs, and they ascend:**
- ***'Afu*** — **pardon, efface entirely**, as though it never was. (The same root as 2:237's *'afw* and 2:109's *fa-'fū wa-ṣfaḥū*.)
- ***Ghfir*** — **forgive, cover**, the sin hidden.
- ***Irḥam*** — **have mercy**, which is not the cancellation of a debt but the gift of something.

**Pardon, then covering, then mercy.** The movement is from the removal of what was wrong to the giving of what was never owed. **And mercy is the last of the three, and there is nothing after it.**

**5. *Anta **mawlānā** fa-nsurnā 'alā al-qawmi al-kāfirīn*** — "You are our **Guardian**, so give us **victory** over the disbelieving people."

***Mawlā*** — the same word as 2:257's *Allāhu walīyu alladhīna āmanū*: **Guardian, Protector, the one in charge of your affair and close to you.** The surah stated at 2:257 that God is the Guardian of the believers; **the believers now address Him by that word.**

*Fa-nsurnā 'alā al-qawmi al-kāfirīn* — "so give us victory over the disbelieving people."

**And the last line of the longest surah in the Qur'an is a request for victory — and here the standing boundary must be stated one final time.**

**This is a prayer the believing community makes to God. It is not a licence for any individual to declare any other individual a disbeliever, and it is not a verdict on any person.** The surah has spent 286 verses making the distinction between belief and its counterfeit one of the most difficult things a human being can assess — **and a Book that careful about who is a believer is not handing the reader the right to name who is not.**

**And note how it ends. The surah does not close on "we have won."** It closes on **"give us victory"** — a request, not a claim. **The believing community's last posture in the longest chapter of the Qur'an is the posture of asking.**

**Which is the same posture 2:250 left the remnant in, and it is the note the whole Book ends on.**

===VERSE-END===

## Synthesis: Verses 281–286 — and the closing of al-Baqarah

**The arc.** 2:281 closes the *ribā* passage with the Day of return. 2:282 — the longest verse in the Qur'an — supplies the machinery of documented, witnessed credit that replaces priced credit. 2:283 handles the case where no scribe can be found, and locates the sin of concealed testimony in the heart. 2:284 states that the interior is accounted for. **And 2:285–286 give the creed, the capacity, and the prayer that close the surah.**

**Three things to carry away.**

**First: the surah ends with a prayer, not a ruling.** Two hundred and eighty-six verses of law, narrative, argument and polemic — 
and the last thing the reader hears is *rabbanā*. **The Book that opened in al-Fātiḥah with praise closes its longest chapter with supplication, and the ḥadīth joins the two and calls them both light.**

**Second: the bookends.** *Lā tukallafu nafsun illā wus'ahā* (2:233) reappears as *lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wus'ahā* (2:286) — **given first to a mother and a father and a child, then handed to the whole community as the closing principle.** *Sami'nā wa-'aṣaynā* (2:93) is answered by *sami'nā wa-aṭa'nā* (2:285) — **one letter, two hundred verses.** And *lā ṭāqata lanā* (2:249) supplies the word for the final petition. **The surah has been building its closing for its entire length.**

**Third: what the Verse of Debt is for.** 2:279 cancelled the return on credit. 2:282 replaced it with documentation. **And the verse closes by saying: *ittaqū Allāha wa yu'allimukumu Allāh* — fear God, and God teaches you.** The most procedural, technical, legal verse in the Book ends by telling you that **knowledge follows awe, not the other way round.** Which is the answer to everyone who has ever delayed obeying until they had finished learning.

**Where the supplied translation resolves an ambiguity, it has been marked.** At 2:282, "incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate" renders the three categories *safīhan aw ḍa'īfan aw lā yastaṭī'u an yumilla huwa*, and the guardian's constraint *bi-al-'adl* has been drawn out. At 2:282, "so if one of the women forgets the other may remind her" follows the reading of *taḍilla* as forgetting; the variant readings recorded by the commentators have been noted. At 2:283, "a security can be taken" is expanded in the commentary to **a pledge taken into possession**, which is what *maqbūḍah* requires and what the jurists built the law of pledge on. And at 2:286, "more than what it can afford" renders *wus'ah* — **capacity, the room a thing has** — which has been distinguished from the *ṭāqah* of the verse's third petition.

**A note on honesty in citation.** No page numbers, volumes or publication details are given. This is a synthesised commentary rather than a citation apparatus, and supplying precise references from memory would mean inventing them. **Where the scholars differ, the disagreement has been presented rather than resolved:** whether 2:284 was abrogated by 2:286 or is firm and merely qualified; which verse was the last of the Qur'an to be revealed; whether the debt-writing requirement is obligatory or strongly recommended; how the two-women clause of 2:282 is to be understood and how far it extends; and how the "deficiency of intellect" ḥadīth is to be read.

**Transmitted reports have been flagged as reports throughout** — the narration of al-Nu'mān ibn Bashīr on the "two thousand years," **whose grading is disputed**; the "overlooked for my community" narration, **likewise disputed**; and the various reports on the final verse revealed.

**Three boundaries have been observed.** On 2:282: **the classical majority position has been stated plainly rather than explained away**, and the scope-limitations the jurists themselves placed on it, the internal evidence of 24:6–9, and the alternative readings have been given alongside — **with the question of its application where the stated reason no longer holds left open, as a live juristic question rather than a settled one.** On 2:284: **the verse is not a weapon against scrupulous people** — the tradition's answer to uninvited thoughts is that they are already pardoned, and the remedy is not to try harder to stop having them. And on 2:286: **the closing petition for victory is a prayer the community makes to God, not a licence for any individual to declare any other person a disbeliever.**

And with this batch the commentary on Sūrat al-Baqarah is complete: **286 verses, in twenty-eight files beside the opening commentary on al-Fātiḥah.**

The last line of the longest surah in the Qur'an is *fa-nsurnā 'alā al-qawmi al-kāfirīn* — **so give us victory.** A request, not a claim. **The surah that opened by describing those who believe in the unseen closes with the believers asking the One who sees them, in the vocabulary of a small company that was afraid and stood anyway.**
