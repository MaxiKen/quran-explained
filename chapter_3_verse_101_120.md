## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:101

> How can you disbelieve when Allah’s revelations are recited to you and His Messenger is in your midst? Whoever holds firmly to Allah is surely guided to the Straight Path.

**Commentary:**

### The Nearness of Revelation

The verse expresses astonishment at disbelief in a community surrounded by revelation and guided by the Messenger ﷺ. The Qur’an is recited, the Prophet’s example is present, and the path of truth is clear. Rejecting such guidance is not an innocent lack of access; it is a serious choice to turn away from a powerful opportunity.

For later generations, the Prophet’s physical presence is no longer among us, but his message remains through the Qur’an and authentic Sunnah. The responsibility is to approach them sincerely, learn correctly, and obey what Allah has made clear.

### Holding Firmly to Allah

“Whoever holds firmly to Allah” translates *yaʿtaṣim billāh*. The image is one of grasping a strong support when surrounded by danger. Holding firmly includes faith, prayer, remembrance, reliance, obedience, and remaining attached to the community of truth.

The servant does not hold Allah physically. He holds firmly to Allah’s covenant and guidance. A person who claims reliance upon Allah while rejecting His revelation has not grasped the support he claims.

### Guidance to the Straight Path

The result of holding firmly is guidance to the Straight Path. Guidance is not only information. It includes understanding, accepting, practising, and remaining firm. Allah’s path joins tawḥīd with justice, worship with character, and personal devotion with responsibility toward people.

The Straight Path is protected from the confusion of competing loyalties. It does not require isolation from the world, but it prevents worldly influence from becoming the final judge over faith.

### The Messenger as Teacher and Example

The Prophet ﷺ was in the midst of the first community as reciter, teacher, judge, leader, and model. His Sunnah explained how to live the Qur’an. Muslims today hold firmly to Allah by studying the Prophet’s authentic guidance and applying it with knowledge and wisdom.

Following the Sunnah does not mean turning every historical custom into an obligation or rejecting legitimate scholarly differences. It means preserving the revealed principles and prophetic character.

### Protection from Disbelief

The verse is also a warning to believers. Faith can weaken when people neglect the Qur’an, isolate religious knowledge from daily life, or allow anger and factionalism to replace submission. Regular recitation, prayer, good companionship, and honest self-examination protect the heart.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:103 and 4:175 on holding firmly to Allah and receiving guidance.
- Qur’an 2:151 and 62:2 on the Messenger reciting, teaching, and purifying the community.
- Qur’an 16:43 and 59:7 on following the Messenger and asking people of knowledge.
- Qur’an 6:153 and 42:52–53 on the Straight Path.
- Qur’an 49:7 on the presence of the Messenger among the believers and the danger of following personal opinion over his guidance.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on holding to the Qur’an and Sunnah and on following the Messenger ﷺ.
- For *iʿtiṣām*, revelation, prophetic guidance, and the Straight Path, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:101.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:102

> O believers! Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves,[151] and do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission ˹to Him˺.[152]

**Commentary:**

### Taqwā as a Lifelong Duty

Allah commands believers to practise *taqwā* in the way He deserves. Taqwā means placing a protective barrier between oneself and Allah’s displeasure through faith, obedience, repentance, and careful avoidance of sin. It is not a momentary emotion; it is a lifelong orientation.

The phrase is a call to take Allah seriously. The believer does not worship only during Ramadan, crisis, or public gatherings. He seeks consistency in prayer, honesty, family duty, work, and private conduct.

### According to One’s Capacity

The command to fear Allah as He deserves does not mean that a human being must achieve a level beyond his capacity. The Qur’an later states, “Be mindful of Allah as much as you are able” (64:16). The two verses are understood together: strive for the highest sincere taqwā while acknowledging human limitation and seeking forgiveness for shortcomings.

Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity (2:286). The command therefore inspires effort rather than producing despair.

### Do Not Die Except in Submission

“Do not die except in submission” means remain committed to Allah until death. No person controls the exact moment of death, but he can control the state in which he lives and the direction in which he continues. The believer asks Allah for a good end and avoids knowingly placing himself in rebellion.

A good end is built through daily habits. The person who repeatedly returns to prayer, repentance, truth, and obedience is preparing to meet Allah in submission.

### Submission and Perseverance

Submission, *Islām*, does not mean a life free from difficulty. Prophets and righteous people were tested, but they did not abandon Allah. The command asks believers to submit in prosperity and adversity, when answers are clear and when wisdom is hidden.

The next verse will show that holding firmly to Allah also involves community unity. Individual faith is strengthened through a righteous community, and a community is strengthened by individuals who practise taqwā.

### The Supplication of the Heart

Because the heart changes, believers should ask Allah for steadfastness. The Prophet ﷺ frequently supplicated, “O Turner of the hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion,” as recorded in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī. Certainty about one’s present state should not become complacency about one’s future.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 64:16 on practising taqwā according to ability.
- Qur’an 2:286 on Allah not burdening a soul beyond its capacity.
- Qur’an 2:132–133 on dying in submission and advising descendants to remain upon Islam.
- Qur’an 16:127 and 3:200 on patience, perseverance, and reliance upon Allah.
- Qur’an 41:30 on angels strengthening those who remain steadfast.
- Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, the supplication asking Allah to make the heart firm upon religion; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on steadfastness and the good end.
- For *taqwā*, *Islām*, steadfastness, capacity, and the command to die in submission, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:102.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:103

> And hold firmly to the rope of Allah[153] and do not be divided. Remember Allah’s favour upon you when you were enemies, then He united your hearts, so you—by His grace—became brothers. And you were at the brink of a fiery pit and He saved you from it. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided.

**Commentary:**

### The Rope of Allah

The “rope of Allah” has been explained by the scholars as the Qur’an, Islam, Allah’s covenant, and the guidance that connects servants to their Lord. These meanings complement one another. The Qur’an gives the guidance, Islam is submission to it, and the covenant binds the community to Allah.

Holding firmly means accepting revelation collectively and individually. A person should not grasp only the verses that support his preferences while abandoning the rest. The community must return disputes to Allah and His Messenger rather than allowing factional loyalty to become the final authority.

### Do Not Be Divided

The command not to divide does not prohibit every difference of opinion. Legitimate differences among qualified scholars can occur in secondary matters, and diversity of culture and lawful practice is not automatically division. The warning concerns turning differences into hostility, sectarian arrogance, rejection of clear truth, or rival communities that place group identity above revelation.

Unity does not mean excusing injustice or silencing sincere correction. It means remaining united upon tawḥīd, justice, mercy, and the shared foundations of Islam.

### From Enmity to Brotherhood

Allah reminds the early Muslims that He united their hearts after they had been enemies. The Aws and Khazraj of Madinah had endured cycles of tribal conflict, especially before Islam. Faith transformed their relationship into brotherhood and mutual responsibility.

This transformation shows that social reconciliation is a divine favour supported by human effort. The believers must forgive, settle disputes, control tribal pride, and build institutions that preserve peace.

### The Edge of the Fire

They had been on the brink of a fiery pit. This refers to the spiritual danger of disbelief and the practical danger of endless conflict. Division can destroy a community from within, while revelation rescues it by giving a shared purpose and moral framework.

The image also warns present communities. A society may appear stable while envy, racism, political faction, and religious insult prepare it for violence. Holding Allah’s rope means addressing these causes before they become an inferno.

### Brotherhood and Rights

Qur’anic brotherhood is not merely emotion. It requires reconciliation, protection from harm, care for the poor, truthful advice, and refusal to spread rumours. The Prophet ﷺ compared believers to a building whose parts support one another and to one body that responds to pain.

A community cannot claim unity while abandoning oppressed members or protecting powerful wrongdoers. Justice is part of brotherhood.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 8:63 and 49:10 on Allah uniting hearts and reconciling believers.
- Qur’an 6:153 and 42:13 on Allah’s path and the prohibition of division in religion.
- Qur’an 4:59 on returning disputes to Allah and the Messenger.
- Qur’an 49:9–13 on reconciling believers, avoiding mockery, and overcoming tribal pride.
- Qur’an 59:9 and 9:71 on solidarity, mutual care, and preferring others.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith comparing believers to one body and to a building whose parts support one another.
- For *ḥabl Allāh*, unity, reconciliation, brotherhood, and the fire of division, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:103.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:104

> Let there be a group among you who call ˹others˺ to goodness, encourage what is good, and forbid what is evil—it is they who will be successful.

**Commentary:**

### A Community with a Mission

Allah commands the Muslim community to maintain a group devoted to calling people toward goodness, encouraging what is right, and preventing what is wrong. This is not a licence for self-appointed control; it is a collective responsibility requiring knowledge, wisdom, courage, and compassion.

A community cannot remain healthy if everyone ignores wrongdoing. At the same time, correction must be conducted in a way that does not create greater harm or become an excuse for humiliation and abuse.

### Calling to Goodness

“Goodness” is *al-khayr*, which includes faith, worship, justice, honesty, mercy, beneficial knowledge, and service. The call should begin with the greatest good—tawḥīd and obedience to Allah—while also addressing the rights of people and the needs of society.

Calling people to good is not limited to sermons. Parents teach, scholars clarify, neighbours support, professionals serve ethically, and institutions protect the vulnerable. Each can participate according to ability.

### Encouraging What Is Recognized as Right

*Al-maʿrūf* means what revelation recognizes as good and what sound human nature and upright custom can recognize. It includes prayer, truthfulness, keeping promises, generosity, and justice. A matter of personal taste or a disputed secondary opinion should not be treated as though it were an unquestionable obligation.

Those who encourage good must know what they are encouraging. Religious enthusiasm without knowledge can produce harm and turn a good command into an unjust accusation.

### Forbidding What Is Wrong

*Al-munkar* includes actions clearly rejected by revelation and sound moral understanding: oppression, fraud, abuse, murder, public corruption, and deliberate neglect of obligations. Forbidding evil may involve education, private advice, legal accountability, institutional reform, or physical prevention by legitimate authority.

The method must fit the situation. Public humiliation may harden a person, while private counsel may open the heart. A person without authority must not create a greater evil while claiming to prevent a smaller one.

### The Prophetic Method

The Prophet ﷺ taught that whoever sees wrongdoing should change it with his hand if able, otherwise with his tongue, and otherwise hate it in the heart; this hadith is recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Ability, knowledge, wisdom, and likely consequences are essential. The hadith is not permission for random violence.

The Prophet also combined firmness with mercy. He corrected mistakes, protected people’s dignity, and established justice through lawful authority.

### Success through Service

The verse promises success to those who carry out this mission. Success is not measured only by immediate agreement. A truthful call may be rejected at first but remain rewarded by Allah. The caller must seek Allah’s pleasure, not popularity or control.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:110 and 9:71 on encouraging good and preventing evil as qualities of the believing community.
- Qur’an 16:125 and 20:44 on calling with wisdom and gentle speech.
- Qur’an 5:2 on cooperation in righteousness and not sin.
- Qur’an 49:6 and 17:36 on knowledge and verification before acting.
- Qur’an 4:59 and 24:55 on lawful authority and responsibility.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith on changing wrongdoing according to ability; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, reports on gentleness and wisdom in correction.
- For *ummah*, *daʿwah*, *maʿrūf*, *munkar*, and the limits of enjoining good and forbidding evil, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:104.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:105

> And do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed after clear proofs had come to them. It is they who will suffer a tremendous punishment.

**Commentary:**

### Division after Clarity

The verse warns believers not to imitate communities that split into hostile sects after clear proofs had come. The problem is not every difference of interpretation. It is division rooted in pride, rivalry, rejection of clear truth, and willingness to sacrifice brotherhood for group victory.

Clear evidence increases responsibility. When a matter is genuinely established, people should submit rather than manufacture factions around it. When a matter is interpretive, they should disagree with humility and avoid treating a legitimate difference as a reason for hatred.

### Sectarian Identity

A sect becomes destructive when its name, leader, or political interest becomes more important than the Qur’an and Sunnah. The group may claim exclusive salvation, manipulate texts, or condemn every outsider. Such division weakens truth and makes the community vulnerable to injustice.

The cure is to return to revelation, qualified scholarship, and the ethics of brotherhood. Unity is not created by suppressing all discussion; it is created by placing discussion under shared principles.

### Difference and Mercy

The scholars recognized differences in secondary legal matters while sharing the foundations of faith. This is distinct from dividing religion into rival camps that reject clear evidence or accuse one another without knowledge. A believer can say, “This opinion is stronger,” without saying that every person who differs is outside Islam.

The Qur’an commands consultation and reflection, so intellectual effort is not itself condemned. What is condemned is arrogant fragmentation after truth has become clear.

### The Seriousness of Punishment

The punishment is described as tremendous because sectarian division damages both individual souls and the community. It can lead to false testimony, violence, broken family ties, and the concealment of truth. People who began with a theological difference may end by making loyalty to the faction a substitute for loyalty to Allah.

The warning should produce reconciliation, not panic. Believers should repair disputes, apologize, and judge themselves before accusing others.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 6:159 and 30:31–32 on avoiding sectarian division.
- Qur’an 8:46 on not disputing in a way that weakens the community.
- Qur’an 49:9–10 on reconciliation and brotherhood.
- Qur’an 39:17–18 on listening carefully and following the best evidence.
- Qur’an 16:43 and 4:59 on qualified knowledge and returning disputes to Allah and the Messenger.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on brotherhood, avoiding accusation, and reconciling disputants.
- For *furqah*, *ikhtilāf*, clear proofs, legitimate scholarly difference, and destructive sectarianism, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:105.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:106

> On that Day some faces will be bright while others gloomy. To the gloomy-faced it will be said, “Did you disbelieve after having believed? So taste the punishment for your disbelief.”

**Commentary:**

### The Day of Visible Consequences

The verse describes the Day when inner realities become visible on people’s faces. Some faces will shine with light, joy, and hope; others will be darkened by grief, fear, and humiliation. The image shows that the Hereafter exposes what worldly appearance can conceal.

A person may appear successful or respectable in this life while hiding disbelief, hypocrisy, or injustice. On the Day of Judgement, no public image can conceal the state of the heart.

### Disbelief after Belief

The gloomy-faced are asked whether they disbelieved after believing. In context, this concerns people who turned away after guidance and clear proof, including those who divided and rebelled after revelation. It is not a command to accuse every sinner or confused person of apostasy.

The Qur’an distinguishes between a believer who falls into sin and a person who deliberately rejects faith after recognizing its truth. Detailed judgements about individuals require knowledge, justice, and legitimate authority; the final account belongs to Allah.

### The Light of the Believers

The bright faces of the following verse are associated with Allah’s mercy. Other Qur’anic passages describe believers’ light proceeding before them on the Day of Resurrection (57:12; 66:8). Their light is the result of faith, sincerity, obedience, and Allah’s grace.

This imagery encourages believers to cultivate inner truth now. The face may eventually reveal what the heart repeatedly chose.

### Warning as Mercy

The question “Did you disbelieve after having believed?” is a warning before the punishment is described. It makes the moral cause clear. Allah does not punish without knowledge of the person’s choice, evidence, and persistence.

The purpose of hearing the warning now is to repent while life remains. Fear of the Day should lead to prayer, reconciliation, and reform.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:107–108 and 57:12 on bright faces, Allah’s mercy, and believers’ light.
- Qur’an 66:8 on the prayer for perfected light and forgiveness.
- Qur’an 2:217 and 4:137 on disbelief after belief and increasing rejection.
- Qur’an 3:86–90 on rejection after clear proof and the opening of repentance.
- Qur’an 75:22–25 and 80:38–42 on faces bright and faces gloomy on the Day.
- For *tabyaḍḍ*, *taswadd*, apostasy after faith, and visible consequences in the Hereafter, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:106.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:107

> As for the bright-faced, they will be in Allah’s mercy, where they will remain forever.

**Commentary:**

### Faces Bright with Mercy

The bright faces belong to those whose faith and deeds were accepted by Allah. Their brightness expresses safety, joy, honour, and the removal of fear. In this world, a person’s appearance cannot reliably reveal his spiritual state; in the Hereafter, Allah makes the inner reality clear.

The verse says that they are “in Allah’s mercy.” Paradise is not obtained by deeds independently of Allah. Deeds are the means through which the servant seeks mercy, while acceptance and entry are gifts from Allah.

### Everlasting Residence

They remain in Allah’s mercy forever. This contrasts with the temporary nature of worldly comfort and with the severe, lasting punishment described for those who die upon rejection. Paradise is security from death, sickness, fear, grief, resentment, and loss.

The permanence of the reward gives meaning to patient obedience. A person may sacrifice a short pleasure, forgive an injury, or give wealth for Allah’s sake, knowing that Allah’s mercy is more enduring than what he leaves.

### Mercy and Righteous Action

Allah’s mercy does not mean that deeds are irrelevant. The previous verses described believers who uphold the covenant, encourage good, and do righteous acts. Mercy meets sincere effort. It also covers weaknesses and mistakes when the servant repents.

The believer should therefore work without pride and hope without laziness. He does not say, “My deeds guarantee me Paradise,” nor does he say, “Because Allah is merciful, I need not obey.”

### A Promise beyond Circumstance

Some believers suffer injustice, poverty, illness, or grief in this life. Their bright faces in the Hereafter show that worldly hardship is not a measure of Allah’s final approval. The oppressed may receive a reward that makes the difficulty seem small, while a prosperous wrongdoer may face a terrible account.

This does not make believers indifferent to suffering. They must seek justice and relieve hardship, while trusting Allah with the final result.

- Qur’an 3:106–108 on faces, mercy, punishment, truth, and Allah’s justice.
- Qur’an 57:12 and 66:8 on the light of believers on the Day of Resurrection.
- Qur’an 9:72 on Allah’s pleasure and the everlasting gardens.
- Qur’an 76:8–22 on the qualities and reward of the righteous.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith that no one enters Paradise solely through deeds but through Allah’s mercy.
- For *tabyaḍḍ*, *raḥmah*, everlasting residence, and the relationship between deeds and divine mercy, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:107.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:108

> These are Allah’s revelations We recite to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth. And Allah desires no injustice to ˹His˺ creation.

**Commentary:**

### Revelation Recited in Truth

Allah identifies the preceding warnings and promises as His revelations recited to the Prophet ﷺ in truth. They are not human speculation about salvation, punishment, or the history of religious communities. They are guidance from the One who knows the visible and unseen realities.

The Qur’an’s narratives and judgements should therefore be received with trust and studied with context. A person should not select only the passages that flatter him or weaponize warnings while ignoring mercy and repentance.

### Allah Does Not Wrong Creation

Allah desires no injustice to creation. He does not punish without reason, burden beyond capacity, lose a good deed, or assign one person another’s guilt. His judgement takes account of knowledge, intention, ability, coercion, and action.

This is a foundation of Islamic belief. Allah’s commands are not arbitrary oppression; they protect human life, dignity, faith, family, property, and reason. When a person does not understand a particular decree, he trusts Allah’s wisdom without accusing Him of injustice.

### Justice in Human Application

Because Allah does not wrong creation, believers must not use revelation to justify their own injustice. Interpreting a verse in a way that permits oppression contradicts the Qur’an’s larger purpose. Judges, teachers, parents, leaders, and activists must apply religious principles with evidence and fairness.

The warning about earlier communities is also a warning to Muslims. They should not repeat the same pattern of selective scripture, factionalism, or claims of superiority.

### The Balance of the Passage

The surrounding verses contain warnings against rejection and promises for believers. Both are aspects of justice. Reward for righteousness and consequence for deliberate wrongdoing are not contradictions of mercy. Mercy includes warning people before harm and opening the door to repentance.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:109

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺.

**Commentary:**

### Universal Ownership

Everything in the heavens and earth belongs to Allah. Ownership here is absolute and original; human ownership is temporary, delegated, and bound by ethical conditions. A person may possess property lawfully, but he does not own himself or the world independently of Allah.

This principle influences how wealth, land, knowledge, authority, and natural resources are used. They are trusts, not permissions for unlimited exploitation. The owner will ask how each trust was acquired and used.

### All Matters Return to Allah

“All matters will be returned” means that every dispute, intention, deed, injustice, and unresolved affair ultimately returns to Allah for judgement. A problem may appear to belong only to politics, family, economics, or history, but no matter is outside divine accountability.

This does not excuse people from seeking justice in this world. It assures them that when worldly justice is incomplete, the case is not lost.

### The End of Religious Dispute

The context includes disputes about scripture, prophecy, Jesus, Abraham, and the identity of the true community. Allah declares that all such matters return to Him. Human argument cannot change the truth or control the final verdict.

The proper response is sincere investigation, submission to evidence, and humility about what remains uncertain.

### Ownership and Gratitude

Recognizing Allah’s ownership produces gratitude. A person uses his body in obedience, his wealth in charity, his authority in justice, and his knowledge in service. He does not ask only, “Can I do this?” but also, “Will the Owner be pleased with how I use what He entrusted to me?”

- Qur’an 6:164 and 53:38–39 on individual responsibility.
- Qur’an 42:10 and 45:17 on Allah as the final judge of disputes.
- Qur’an 99:7–8 on every deed returning to its maker’s record.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:110

> You are the best community ever raised for humanity—you encourage good, forbid evil, and believe in Allah. Had the People of the Book believed, it would have been better for them. Some of them are faithful, but most are rebellious.

**Commentary:**

### The Best Community for Humanity

The Muslim community is described as the best community raised for humanity, but the verse immediately gives the conditions: encouraging good, forbidding evil, and believing in Allah. This is a moral commission, not a racial privilege or an automatic guarantee.

The community is “for humanity,” meaning its goodness should benefit people through justice, mercy, knowledge, protection, and truthful guidance. It is not best because it dominates others; it is best when it serves Allah and humanity responsibly.

### Faith and Social Responsibility

The verse joins belief to public responsibility. Faith is not confined to private feeling. It should produce institutions and people that protect the vulnerable, oppose corruption, support truth, and make goodness easier.

Enjoining good and forbidding evil require knowledge, wisdom, legitimate authority, and attention to consequences. Ignorant accusation, vigilante violence, and public humiliation are not fulfilments of the verse.

### A Conditional Honour

The word “best” is conditional upon continued obedience. Earlier communities were also favoured when they upheld revelation and lost honour when they betrayed it. Muslims should therefore read this verse as a covenant and responsibility, not as a reason for self-congratulation.

A community that claims superiority while tolerating corruption has abandoned the qualities by which the verse praises it.

### The People of the Book

The verse says that if the People of the Book believed, it would be better for them, then immediately adds that some are faithful and most are rebellious. This preserves fairness. The criticism is not directed at every Jew or Christian. The Qur’an recognizes sincere believers and righteous people among them.

The same wording should produce self-examination among Muslims. Majority status does not equal righteousness, and minority status does not equal falsehood.

### Serving Humanity

A community fulfils this verse through education, fair courts, honest trade, care for the poor, reconciliation, protection of life, and inviting people to Allah with wisdom. It must oppose evil without becoming evil itself.

The Prophet ﷺ built a community in which worship and social responsibility were inseparable. Prayer produced generosity, brotherhood, and public justice.

- Qur’an 2:143 on the community as a balanced witness over humanity.
- Qur’an 49:13 on honour through taqwā rather than ethnicity.
- Qur’an 22:41 on communities establishing prayer, charity, good, and opposition to evil.
- Qur’an 3:113–115 on faithful and righteous People of the Book.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on justice as a condition of religious integrity.
- For *khayr ummah*, conditional honour, *maʿrūf*, *munkar*, and service to humanity, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:110.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:111

> They can never inflict harm on you, except a little annoyance.[154] But if they meet you in battle, they will flee and they will have no helpers.

**Commentary:**

### The Limits of Hostile Plots

The verse reassures the believers that hostile opponents cannot ultimately harm them beyond limited annoyance if Allah protects their faith and community. This is not a promise that believers will feel no pain or suffer no losses. It is a promise that hostile schemes cannot defeat Allah’s truth or determine the final outcome.

The immediate context concerns a group among the People of the Book who sought to mislead and obstruct believers. The verse does not apply indiscriminately to all Jews and Christians, nor does it authorise aggression against peaceful people.

### The Context of Battle

The statement about fleeing is specifically conditional: “if they meet you in battle.” It concerns armed confrontation, not ordinary religious disagreement, trade, neighbourhood relations, or civic life. Islamic law regulates war through legitimate authority, just cause, protection of noncombatants, and fulfilment of treaties.

The Qur’an forbids transgression even in conflict (2:190) and commands believers to honour treaties and respond to peace when the other side genuinely seeks it (8:61; 9:4).

### No Helpers against Truth

The opponents will have no helpers because worldly alliances cannot rescue a force that persists in aggression and rejection. This echoes the Qur’an’s repeated reminder that power belongs to Allah. A military defeat is not the only meaning of having no helpers; it also describes the collapse of false support before divine judgement.

The believers should not become arrogant when an enemy is weakened. Victory is a test requiring mercy, discipline, and justice.

### Patience under Annoyance

The phrase “a little annoyance” does not trivialize the suffering of individuals. It places temporary suffering within the larger horizon of Allah’s justice. Believers are encouraged to remain patient without allowing insult or provocation to make them abandon ethics.

Patience includes seeking lawful protection, exposing injustice, and supporting victims. It does not require silence about abuse.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:100–112 on misleading influence, community responsibility, and the consequences of hostility.
- Qur’an 2:190–193 on fighting aggression without transgression.
- Qur’an 8:61 and 9:4 on peace, treaties, and honouring agreements.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 60:8 on justice and kindness toward peaceful people.
- Qur’an 8:46 and 3:160 on patience, unity, and Allah’s help.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:112

> They will be stricken with disgrace wherever they go, unless they are protected by a covenant with Allah or a treaty with the people. They have invited the displeasure of Allah and have been branded with misery for rejecting Allah’s revelations and murdering ˹His˺ prophets unjustly. This is ˹a fair reward˺ for their disobedience and violations.

**Commentary:**

### Disgrace as a Consequence of Betrayal

The verse describes disgrace and misery as consequences of persistent rejection, covenant-breaking, and persecution of prophets. It addresses a historical pattern of wrongdoing, not an ethnic destiny applied to every Jew. The Qur’an itself says that the People of the Book are not all alike and praises those who are upright (3:113–115).

Disgrace can refer to loss of honour before Allah, internal misery, political vulnerability, or the consequences of a community’s repeated betrayal. It should not be used to justify prejudice or to interpret every hardship suffered by a people as divine punishment.

### Protection through Covenant

The exception mentions protection through a covenant with Allah or a treaty with people. This establishes the value of covenants and lawful protection. A community that enters a treaty receives security so long as the treaty is honoured. Muslims are commanded to fulfil agreements even with opponents.

The phrase also indicates that social security is not a private entitlement. It is structured through promises, justice, and mutual responsibility. Breaking a covenant destroys the trust that makes community possible.

### Rejecting Revelation and Killing Prophets

The verse names the crimes that led to the condemnation: rejecting Allah’s revelations and killing prophets unjustly. The Qur’an repeatedly mentions that some among earlier communities did this. It does not claim that every individual shared the crime, nor does it permit collective blame.

The killing of prophets represents the attempt of power to silence truth. A community that attacks those sent to guide it has turned religious identity against revelation itself.

### Disobedience and Transgression

The final phrase emphasizes disobedience and transgression. Sin becomes spiritually destructive when it is repeated, defended, and institutionalized. The problem is not a momentary error followed by repentance but a pattern of deliberate violation.

The believer should fear creating such a pattern in his own life. Small compromises can become habitual, and habitual wrongdoing can become a public culture.

### Justice and Restraint

The verse’s historical warning must be read with the Qur’an’s commands to justice. Muslims cannot use it to mistreat Jews or any other people. Allah judges individuals according to their own faith and conduct, and peaceful people retain their rights.

- Qur’an 5:1 and 9:4 on honouring covenants and treaties.
- Qur’an 4:135 and 5:8 on justice even against oneself or an enemy.
- Qur’an 2:61 and 17:4–8 on transgression and its consequences in the history of the Children of Israel.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:113

> Yet they are not all alike: there are some among the People of the Book who are upright, who recite Allah’s revelations throughout the night, prostrating ˹in prayer˺.

**Commentary:**

### Qur’anic Fairness

After describing the wrongdoing of some among the People of the Book, Allah explicitly says that they are not all alike. This is one of the Qur’an’s clearest safeguards against collective prejudice. People are judged by faith, character, and conduct rather than by a label alone.

The verse requires Muslims to be fair even when discussing serious historical and contemporary wrongdoing. Criticizing a crime is not a licence to condemn every member of a community.

### Upright People

The upright are *ummah qāʾimah*, a community or group standing firm upon truth. Their uprightness is shown through worship and moral action, not merely through ancestry or claims of religious knowledge. They recite Allah’s revelations at night and prostrate before Him.

Night worship indicates sincerity because it occurs away from public applause. Their public character is supported by private devotion.

### Recitation and Prostration

Reciting revelation throughout the night means engaging with Allah’s words in a manner that produces humility. The prostration that accompanies recitation expresses submission. Scripture is not honoured merely by being stored or quoted; it is honoured when it changes the worshipper.

The verse also shows that revelation and prayer were present among righteous people before Islam. Their sincere worship is recognized by Allah, and Muslims are commanded to acknowledge it.

### Worship across Communities

A person can belong to a community with a history of wrongdoing and still be personally upright. Conversely, a person can belong to a community praised in principle while behaving unjustly. Allah’s judgement is precise and individual.

This principle should shape interfaith relations. Muslims can disagree with a doctrine while recognizing sincerity and righteousness in particular people.

### A Mirror for Muslims

The verse also asks whether Muslims are upright in the same way. Do they recite the Qur’an at night and prostrate with humility? Does their worship make them truthful, just, and merciful? The Qur’an’s praise of others should not become an excuse for Muslims to admire themselves; it should inspire emulation.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:110–115 on the best community, criticism, and upright People of the Book.
- Qur’an 5:82–85 on sincere Christians who recognize truth and respond with humility.
- Qur’an 19:58 and 17:107–109 on prostration upon hearing revelation.
- Qur’an 32:15–16 and 51:17–18 on night worship and prostration.
- Qur’an 6:164 and 49:13 on individual responsibility and the rejection of group-based superiority.
- For *ummah qāʾimah*, night recitation, *sujūd*, and the Qur’an’s fairness toward the People of the Book, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:113.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:114

> They believe in Allah and the Last Day, encourage good and forbid evil, and race with one another in doing good. They are ˹truly˺ among the righteous.

**Commentary:**

### The Qualities of the Righteous

This verse continues the description of upright People of the Book. Their faith is expressed through belief in Allah, belief in the Last Day, moral responsibility, and eagerness to do good. The Qur’an measures righteousness by qualities and conduct rather than by inherited identity.

The same qualities are required of Muslims. Faith is not a claim reserved for one community; it is a trust that produces worship and service.

### Belief in Allah and the Last Day

Belief in Allah establishes the object of worship and the source of guidance. Belief in the Last Day establishes accountability. Together they prevent morality from becoming dependent only on public opinion or worldly reward.

A person who knows he will return to Allah is more likely to protect trusts, resist oppression, and continue doing good when no one is watching.

### Encouraging Good and Resisting Evil

The righteous encourage *maʿrūf* and resist *munkar*. They do not treat religion as a private identity detached from society. They support prayer, honesty, justice, care for the poor, reconciliation, and protection from harm.

Their method must be guided by knowledge and wisdom. The goal is reform and protection, not humiliation or the satisfaction of anger.

### Racing in Goodness

“Race with one another in doing good” describes eager competition in charity and virtue. Unlike worldly competition, which often seeks to defeat others, this competition increases benefit for everyone. One person’s generosity inspires another, and the community becomes stronger.

The Qur’an uses the image of racing to overcome procrastination. Opportunities to help, forgive, learn, and repent may not remain available. The believer does not wait until the perfect moment to do good.

### Righteousness beyond Labels

The verse’s praise is important in the middle of a passage criticizing some People of the Book. It teaches Muslims to acknowledge truth wherever it appears. A person should not reject a good deed because of the actor’s religious identity, nor assume that his own group owns righteousness.

At the same time, recognizing goodness does not require pretending that theological differences are irrelevant. Fairness includes both truth and accurate acknowledgement.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:110–115 on the qualities of the best community and upright People of the Book.
- Qur’an 5:65–66 and 5:82–85 on faith, humility, and the response of sincere People of the Book.
- Qur’an 2:177 on righteousness as belief joined to generosity, covenant, and patience.
- Qur’an 9:71 and 22:41 on encouraging good and preventing evil.
- Qur’an 2:148 and 5:48 on racing toward good deeds.
- For *īmān*, *maʿrūf*, *munkar*, *yusāriʿūn*, and fair recognition of righteous individuals, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:114.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:115

> They will never be denied the reward for any good they have done. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of those mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Commentary:**

### No Good Deed Is Lost

Allah promises that the righteous will not be denied the reward for any good they have done. This includes public and private deeds, large and small acts, and good actions that people forget. Allah’s knowledge preserves every effort.

The promise is especially meaningful for people whose goodness is unnoticed or whose sacrifices are misunderstood. Human beings may fail to thank them, but Allah does not lose the record.

### The Condition of Acceptance

The surrounding verses describe faith in Allah, the Last Day, and active righteousness. The promise is not a claim that every outwardly useful act automatically earns the same eternal reward regardless of belief and intention. Allah judges the full reality of each deed, including its purpose and the person’s relationship with Him.

This should not lead Muslims to deny the worldly benefit of good actions by people of different faiths. A good act can help people and deserves gratitude, while ultimate reward and judgement belong to Allah.

### Allah Knows the Mindful

Allah knows those who have *taqwā*. He knows whether a person’s charity was sincere, whether his moral action was courageous, and whether he avoided sin for Allah’s sake. Taqwā is not a public badge; it is the heart’s awareness and the conduct that follows.

The statement is also a comfort to those who are judged unfairly by people. Allah knows the inner truth better than any audience.

### Encouragement to Goodness

The verse encourages immediate action. A person should not say that his contribution is too small or that nobody will notice. Removing harm from a path, speaking truth, feeding a hungry person, teaching beneficial knowledge, and forgiving can all be valuable before Allah.

Likewise, a person should not become discouraged when good seems to produce no visible result. The reward may be delayed, but it is not denied.

- Qur’an 9:120–121 and 18:30 on Allah not wasting the reward of good-doers.
- Qur’an 4:40 and 99:7–8 on the preservation and multiplication of deeds.
- Qur’an 2:197 and 49:13 on taqwā and Allah’s knowledge of the mindful.
- Qur’an 16:97 and 40:40 on the reward of righteous men and women.
- Qur’an 2:274 on public and private charity.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on intention, charity, and Allah’s knowledge of deeds.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:116

> Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah. It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.

**Commentary:**

### Wealth and Children Cannot Shield

This verse repeats a warning given earlier in the sūrah: wealth and children cannot protect a person from Allah’s judgement when he dies in rejection. Wealth may purchase worldly comfort, and children may provide emotional support or social status, but neither can substitute for faith, repentance, and righteousness.

The repetition shows the danger of relying on worldly security. A person may hear the warning once and still continue to live as though possessions guarantee safety.

### Blessings and Tests

Wealth and children are not evil. The Qur’an calls them adornments and tests. Wealth can support family, charity, education, and justice. Children can become a source of continuing reward when raised in righteousness. The problem is treating them as proof of divine approval or as a shield against accountability.

The believer receives blessings with gratitude and uses them as trusts. He does not allow love of them to become disobedience to Allah.

### The Residents of the Fire

The verse describes persistent disbelievers as residents of the Fire. The language of residence indicates a settled consequence, not a brief visit. It applies to those who die upon rejection after the truth and warning, not to every person who experiences doubt or falls into sin.

Allah alone knows the circumstances and final state of individuals. Believers affirm the warning while avoiding arrogant declarations about people whose full account is hidden from them.

### The Failure of Worldly Calculation

Worldly society often measures success through wealth, family size, influence, and legacy. The verse reverses that calculation. Success is not what a person gathers but how he meets Allah. A poor believer may be successful, while a powerful rejecter may be ruined.

The Qur’an’s moral scale makes private deeds and sincere faith more important than public prestige.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:117

> The good they do in this worldly life is like the harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind, destroying it ˹completely˺. Allah never wronged them, but they wronged themselves.

**Commentary:**

### The Image of a Destroyed Harvest

The verse compares the good that disbelievers do in worldly life to a harvest struck by a bitter, destructive wind. A farmer may cultivate carefully and expect a rich harvest, only to see it destroyed in a moment. The image represents the loss of ultimate benefit when a person rejects Allah and uses his life without the foundation of faith.

The comparison does not deny that their actions may produce real worldly benefits. A person who feeds the poor helps the poor; a just policy can protect people; a scientific discovery can save lives. The verse addresses the loss of the eternal reward and spiritual harvest, not the immediate benefit experienced by creation.

### Why the Harvest Is Lost

The harvest is destroyed because the people “wronged themselves.” Their loss is not caused by Allah’s injustice. They rejected the source and purpose of life, and their good actions were not joined to sincere faith and submission. The Qur’an elsewhere compares deeds without faith to ashes blown away by wind (14:18).

This should produce humility, not a licence to dismiss good done by people outside one’s community. Muslims must acknowledge worldly benefit, thank people for kindness, and deal justly while understanding that Allah’s final reward belongs to His judgement.

### The Foundation of Deeds

Islam joins three dimensions: correct belief, sincere intention, and righteous action. A deed done for Allah and in a lawful way becomes a lasting investment. A deed done for reputation may be praised by people but lose its spiritual value. Faith gives the deed its ultimate direction.

This also warns Muslims. A person may perform charitable acts while seeking fame, political influence, or control. If the intention is corrupt, the apparent harvest may not survive before Allah.

### Bitter Wind

The bitter wind represents a force that destroys what appeared productive. It can symbolize punishment, sudden loss, or the futility of worldly confidence. The harvest’s destruction is not random; it reveals that the people’s efforts lacked the foundation needed for lasting success.

The believer does not assume that his good deed is safe merely because it looks impressive. He asks Allah for sincerity and acceptance.

### Allah Does Not Wrong Them

The final statement establishes divine justice. Allah does not arbitrarily erase good or punish without reason. He knows the person’s faith, intention, access to truth, and actions. The people wronged themselves by choosing rejection and persistently turning away.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:118

> O believers! Do not associate closely with others who would not miss a chance to harm you. Their only desire is to see you suffer. Their prejudice has become evident from what they say—and what their hearts hide is far worse. We have made Our revelations clear to you, if only you understood.

**Commentary:**

### Guarding Intimate Trust

Allah warns believers not to take as close confidants people who actively seek their harm. The Arabic word *biṭānah* refers to intimate advisers and trusted insiders—people who know private affairs and influence decisions. The verse concerns hostile individuals, not every non-Muslim or every person outside the community.

A believer can deal kindly and fairly with peaceful neighbours, colleagues, and citizens while exercising caution about who receives confidential information and strategic authority. Trust should be based on character and conduct.

### Evidence of Hostility

The verse says their prejudice has become evident through their words. This teaches believers not to build relationships of intimate trust while ignoring clear evidence of betrayal, incitement, or harm. Caution should follow verified behaviour, not rumours or stereotypes.

The second part—what their hearts hide—is known perfectly only to Allah. People must not claim access to another person’s unseen motives merely because they dislike him. They judge by apparent evidence and leave hidden realities to Allah.

### Trust and Community Security

Every community needs internal trust. Leaders, advisers, administrators, and family members may possess information that can be used to harm others. Giving such access to someone who openly seeks suffering is negligence. Protection of the community is a legitimate responsibility.

This does not mean that Muslims should isolate themselves or refuse cooperation. It means that cooperation should be structured with clear rights, accountability, and safeguards against betrayal.

### Justice toward Peaceful People

The warning must be read with Qur’anic commands to kindness and justice toward peaceful non-Muslims (60:8). It also must be read with the verse’s precision: the issue is hostile closeness, not religious difference alone. The Prophet ﷺ maintained treaties and dealt fairly with people of other faiths.

A Muslim who uses this verse to justify racism or mistreatment has ignored the Qur’an’s context and ethical balance.

### Understanding the Revelation

Allah concludes by saying that the revelations are made clear if believers understand. Understanding includes reading context, distinguishing hostile individuals from whole communities, and applying caution without injustice. It also includes examining whether one’s own close associates encourage faith and justice or lead toward corruption.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:119

> Here you are! You love them but they do not love you, and you believe in all Scriptures. When they meet you they say, “We believe.” But when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage. Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “˹May you˺ die of your rage!” Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart.

**Commentary:**

### Affection without Naivety

The verse describes believers who showed goodwill while hostile people did not reciprocate it. Believers believe in all revealed scriptures and therefore have a broader, more generous relationship to revelation. The hostile group, however, concealed resentment and used outward words of belief deceptively.

This does not command Muslims to abandon kindness. It warns against confusing kindness with surrendering trust to people whose conduct repeatedly demonstrates hostility. A believer can be compassionate without being naïve.

### Belief in All Scripture

The believers’ openness is connected to their comprehensive faith. They believe in the original revelations sent by Allah and do not reject prophets because of ethnicity or historical rivalry. This gives them a principled respect for the truth wherever it appears.

It does not require accepting every later claim or manuscript statement without scrutiny. The Qur’an confirms the original revelation and serves as guardian and criterion over what people attribute to it (5:48).

### Outward Words and Inner Hostility

The hostile people said “We believe” when meeting the believers, but concealed resentment when alone. This is a picture of hypocrisy and double dealing. Allah knows both speech and the heart, so an outward declaration cannot hide a settled intention to harm.

The verse also teaches believers to evaluate patterns of conduct. One polite sentence does not erase repeated betrayal, but neither should one suspicion be used to condemn a person without evidence.

### Biting the Fingertips

Biting the fingertips is an Arabic expression of intense rage and frustration. It portrays the emotional cost of envy: the hostile person is consumed by anger at the believers’ faith and unity. The believer should not imitate this rage when facing opposition. He seeks Allah’s protection and remains disciplined.

The Prophet’s reply is a declaration that their rage will not change truth. It is not permission for Muslims to curse people casually or delight in personal suffering.

### Allah Knows the Heart

The closing statement returns final knowledge to Allah. He knows who sincerely believes, who pretends, who is confused, and who deliberately plots. This prevents believers from assuming the role of divine judges while still requiring them to protect themselves from evident harm.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:120

> When you ˹believers˺ are touched with good, they grieve; but when you are afflicted with evil, they rejoice. ˹Yet,˺ if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺, their schemes will not harm you in the least. Surely Allah is Fully Aware of what they do.

**Commentary:**

### Envy Revealed by Reactions

The verse identifies hostility through reactions to the believers’ circumstances. When good reaches the believers, the hostile people grieve; when hardship touches them, they rejoice. Such reactions reveal envy and malice more clearly than polite public words.

The warning remains specific to those who behave this way. It must not be generalized to every member of a religious community. People who rejoice at another’s suffering may be found in any group, including among Muslims.

### Patience and Taqwā

Allah gives the believers two protections: patience and taqwā. Patience, *ṣabr*, keeps them from responding to provocation with injustice or panic. Taqwā keeps them within Allah’s commands while they defend themselves and pursue justice.

Patience does not mean accepting abuse without action. It means refusing to let anger push the believer into forbidden conduct. Taqwā guides practical responses: verification, consultation, lawful protection, and proportionate action.

### Schemes Cannot Defeat Allah’s Plan

The verse says that the schemes will not harm believers “in the least” when they maintain patience and taqwā. This does not mean believers will feel no pain or experience no worldly loss. Prophets and companions were harmed, and the Qur’an discusses battles in which believers suffered casualties.

It means that hostile plots cannot destroy their faith, erase their reward, or produce a final victory over Allah’s guidance. Even a temporary setback can become a means of purification and learning.

### Allah Is Fully Aware

Allah knows everything the hostile people do: their words, hidden plans, emotional reactions, and effects on the community. The statement comforts believers who are targeted secretly and warns plotters who assume that concealment protects them.

The delay of consequences does not mean Allah is unaware. He may give time for repentance, expose the scheme at the right moment, or reserve complete justice for the Hereafter.

### Emotional Discipline

The verse teaches believers not to measure their worth by the reactions of enemies. If an opponent rejoices at hardship, the believer seeks Allah’s help rather than becoming consumed by revenge. If good comes, he remains grateful and humble rather than provoking envy.
