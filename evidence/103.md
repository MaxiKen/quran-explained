## Introduction

### A MAKKAN SŪRAH OF THREE VERSES

- `103:0.1` · history · Makkan according to the majority (Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr, in Alūsī); Qatādah, Mujāhid and Muqātil call it Madinan. Three verses, with no disagreement. Baghawī adds a report from Ibn ʿAbbās (Ibn Mardawayh) that it came down at Makkah. [qurtubi, alusi, baghawi, saadi]

### WHAT THE EARLY GENERATIONS SAID IT IS WORTH

- `103:0.2` · athar · Al-Shāfiʿī: "If people reflected on this sūrah, it would be enough for them." Alūsī words it: had nothing else been revealed it would have sufficed, since it gathers all the sciences of the Qurʾān. Ibn ʿUthaymīn: enough as admonition, not as legislation, for it holds no ruling on purity, prayer, zakāh, pilgrimage or fasting. [kathir, maarif, alusi, uthaymeen]
- `103:0.3` · athar · A practice of the Companions: when two met, they did not part until one had recited Sūrat al-ʿAṣr to the other and they had exchanged greetings of peace. Al-Ṭabarānī, from ʿAbdullāh (Maʿārif: ʿUbaydullāh) ibn Ḥiṣn Abī Madīnah; Alūsī: al-Ṭabarānī (al-Awsaṭ) and al-Bayhaqī (al-Shuʿab), from Abū Ḥudhayfah. [kathir, maarif, alusi]

### MUSAYLIMAH'S IMITATION AND ʿAMR'S ANSWER

- `103:0.4` · history · Before accepting Islam, ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ visited Musaylimah the liar, who asked what had been revealed to "your friend". ʿAmr recited this sūrah. Musaylimah answered with his own "revelation" about the wabr (hyrax): "you are only two ears and a chest, and the rest of you is digging and burrowing." ʿAmr: you know that I know you are lying. [kathir]
- `103:0.5` · history · Ibn Kathīr adds that al-Khara'iti gave a chain for part of this story in Masāwiʾ al-Akhlāq; the wabr is a small cat-like animal; Musaylimah meant to produce something against the Qurʾān, yet it could not convince even an idol-worshipper of that day. [kathir]

### WHERE THE SŪRAH STANDS AND WHAT IT GATHERS

- `103:0.6` · lesson · In three verses it lays out a whole way of life: an oath by time, mankind in loss, and an exception for four qualities — faith, righteous deeds, counsel to truth, counsel to patience. The first two perfect oneself, the last two perfect others. [maarif, saadi]
- `103:0.7` · lesson · Alūsī: it follows its neighbour, Sūrat al-Takāthur, because it points to the state of one whom rivalry in accumulation did not distract. [alusi]

## Verse 103:1

> By the ˹passage of˺ time

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### THE MEANINGS OF AL-ʿAṢR

- `103:1.1` · language · Time itself (dahr): Ibn ʿAbbās (Qurṭubī, Baghawī, Alūsī). Al-Ṭabarī judges this right: ʿaṣr names time — evening, night and day — and nothing in the name is excluded from the oath. Ibn ʿUthaymīn: the soundest view; al-Saʿdī: night and day, where deeds happen; Ibn Kathīr: the time of people's movements, good or evil. [tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, alusi, saadi, uthaymeen, kathir]
- `103:1.2` · athar · A part of the day: the late afternoon — al-Ḥasan and Qatādah. Al-Ḥasan: from the sun's decline to its setting; Qatādah: the last hour of the day; Ibn ʿAbbās (in Ṭabarī): an hour of the day; Zayd ibn Aslam (Mālik, in Ibn Kathīr): the evening; Jalālayn: from the sun's decline to sunset. [tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, jalalayn]
- `103:1.3` · language · "The two ʿaṣrs" (al-ʿaṣrān) means day and night (Ibn Kaysān; al-Zajjāj in Alūsī) or morning and evening (Qurṭubī; Alūsī: "the two coolings"). Qurṭubī and Alūsī both quote Ḥumayd ibn Thawr's line on "the two ʿaṣrs, a day and a night". [qurtubi, baghawi, alusi]

### THE ʿAṢR PRAYER AS THE THING SWORN BY

- `103:1.4` · athar · Muqātil: the oath is by the ʿAṣr prayer, for its merit, as the middle prayer (Qurṭubī, Baghawī, Alūsī). Ibn ʿAbbās gives it as one gloss ("Allah swears by the ʿAṣr prayer"), with "the hardships of time" as another; Jalālayn offers it as an alternative. [qurtubi, baghawi, alusi, abbas, jalalayn]
- `103:1.5` · quran · 2:238: the "middle prayer" is the ʿAṣr prayer — Qurṭubī refers back to its treatment under Sūrat al-Baqarah, and the study draft links this verse to 2:238 while noting that verse is read in many ways. [qurtubi, initial]
- `103:1.6` · hadith · "The middle prayer is the ʿAṣr prayer" — Qurṭubī calls it an authentic report, and Alūsī gives "They kept us from the middle prayer, the ʿAṣr prayer", plus Ḥafṣah's codex reading "and the middle prayer, the ʿAṣr prayer". Neither passage names a collection. [qurtubi, alusi]
- `103:1.7` · hadith · "Whoever misses the ʿAṣr prayer, it is as though his family and his wealth were taken from him" — given by Alūsī as "in the hadith", with no collection named. [alusi]
- `103:1.8` · lesson · Alūsī: that prayer is singled out because the duty is hardest then, as people rush about in trade and livelihood at the end of the day. [alusi]
- `103:1.9` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: the end of the day is its best part, and the ʿAṣr prayer is called the middle prayer in the sense of the best, as the Prophet named it. [uthaymeen]

### THE ERA OF THE PROPHET AND ITS MEASURE

- `103:1.10` · athar · The oath may be by the era of the Prophet ﷺ, for its merit through the renewal of prophethood in it (Qurṭubī). Alūsī: his lifetime and all that follows to the Day of Resurrection, honoured as the time of the Prophet and his community; set against past time it is like the ʿAṣr portion of a day. [qurtubi, alusi, initial]
- `103:1.11` · hadith · Alūsī's support, from al-Bukhārī (Sālim ibn ʿAbdillāh from his father): the Prophet said, "Your stay among the nations before you is like the time between the ʿAṣr prayer and the setting of the sun." [alusi]

### WHY GOD SWEARS BY TIME

- `103:1.12` · lesson · Reasons offered: time's turning states point to the Maker (Qurṭubī); it holds every kind of wonder, hence "father of wonders" (Ibn ʿAbbās, in Alūsī); it holds a lesson for the one who looks (Baghawī); in it come hardship and ease, war and peace, health and sickness, good deeds and bad (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [qurtubi, alusi, baghawi, uthaymeen]
- `103:1.13` · lesson · The oath also clears time of blame: loss is a quality of man, not of time. Alūsī quotes "they blame time and time has no fault," and notes the objection that this use of ʿaṣr is not obvious. [alusi]
- `103:1.14` · lesson · Time bears witness too: the rise and fall of individuals and nations shows that only the four acts bring real success, and everyone who abandoned them lost (Maʿārif). [maarif]

### WHAT AN OATH BY ʿAṢR MEANS IN LAW

- `103:1.15` · ruling · Swearing not to speak to a man "ʿaṣran": Mālik held a year (the most said of it, in keeping with his strictness in oaths); al-Shāfiʿī, an hour unless he intended more. Ibn al-ʿArabī sides with al-Shāfiʿī, adding that an Arab swearer is asked what he meant, and any meaning the word can carry is accepted. [qurtubi]

### A WEAK STORY THE SOURCES WARN AGAINST

- `103:1.16` · history · A story told by "the Imām": a woman confessed to adultery, killing her newborn in a vinegar jar and selling the vinegar, and was told the real fault was neglecting the ʿAṣr prayer. Alūsī says the hadith imams do not rely on such reports and warns against following it. [alusi]

### A VARIANT READING OF THE FIRST WORD

- `103:1.17` · language · Sallām read "wa-l-ʿaṣiri" with a kasra on the ṣād. [qurtubi@2]

## Verse 103:2

> Surely humanity is in ˹grave˺ loss

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### WHO THE HUMAN BEING IS

- `103:2.1` · athar · The disbeliever is meant (Ibn ʿAbbās, in Abū Ṣāliḥ's narration); al-Ḍaḥḥāk reports from him a group of polytheists — al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib and al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd Yaghūth. Baghawī: said to mean the disbeliever, since the believers are excepted. [qurtubi, baghawi, abbas]
- `103:2.2` · language · Or all of humankind: the definite article covers every human, which the exception signals (Alūsī). Ibn ʿUthaymīn: "man" here is the species, as the test "every" in place of "the" shows; Qurṭubī also gives "the human kind"; Jalālayn: "man, the generic". [alusi, uthaymeen, qurtubi, jalalayn]
- `103:2.3` · language · Verse 2 answers the oath: Qurṭubī calls it the answer to the oath; the study draft says vv. 2–3 are the response to v. 1, like 95:5–6. [qurtubi, initial]

### HOW THE SOURCES GLOSS LOSS

- `103:2.4` · language · Khusr glossed as loss or deprivation, destruction (al-Akhfash), punishment (al-Farrāʾ, citing 65:9, "and the outcome of its affair was loss"), evil (Ibn Zayd), decrease. Ṭabarī: destruction and decline; Ibn Kathīr: loss and destruction; Baghawī: loss and decline of the capital of one's self and lifespan, spent in sins. [qurtubi, tabari, kathir, baghawi]
- `103:2.5` · lesson · Alūsī: loss in their trading and efforts, spending their lives on pursuits that bring no benefit in the Hereafter and may even harm them. Jalālayn: loss "in all his bargaining". [alusi, jalalayn]
- `103:2.6` · language · The indefinite "khusr" is for magnification ("a great loss") or for kinds ("a sort of loss beyond what man knows") (Alūsī). [alusi]
- `103:2.7` · lesson · Degrees of loss (Saʿdī): absolute loss, losing this world and the next, missing bliss and earning hell; or loss in some respects only. Hence God made loss general for every human except those with four qualities. [saadi]
- `103:2.8` · athar · Ibn ʿAbbās: the loss and torment of losing one's family and one's place in Paradise. [abbas]

### AGE AS A KIND OF LOSS

- `103:2.9` · athar · Ibrāhīm: a man who lives long and grows old declines in strength and regresses, except the believers, for whom the rewards of their youthful deeds are written (Qurṭubī; Baghawī from Ibn ʿAwn). Ibn ʿAbbās: loss through the decline of works in old age and their ending at death. Parallel: 95:4–6. [qurtubi, baghawi, abbas]

### HOW THE QURʾĀN SPEAKS OF LOSS

- `103:2.10` · quran · Loss means losing this world and the Hereafter (22:11); the losers lose their souls and their families on the Day of Resurrection (39:15); disbelief only adds to the disbelievers' loss (35:39). The study draft adds that those who misuse their time are in constant loss. [initial]

### TIME AS A CAPITAL THAT MELTS

- `103:2.11` · lesson · Maʿārif: all human states unfold within time; life is capital shrinking breath by breath. Invested in good works it brings profit, in evil works a loss of capital and the burden of sins, in neither works both profit and capital are lost. An old scholar learned the verse from an ice-seller whose stock melted if he was careless. [maarif]
- `103:2.12` · hadith · "Every person goes out in the morning and sells his soul, either freeing it or destroying it" — given by Maʿārif as a Prophetic report, with no collection named. [maarif]
- `103:2.13` · quran · 61:10: the Qurʾān itself calls faith and righteous deed a trade that saves from a painful punishment (Maʿārif). [maarif]

### LOSS THAT SURROUNDS ON EVERY SIDE

- `103:2.14` · language · "In loss" rather than "a loser": the preposition means containment — man is immersed in loss, surrounded on every side. The statement carries three emphases: the oath, "inna" and the lām (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [uthaymeen]

### THE WORDS READ DIFFERENTLY

- `103:2.15` · language · ʿAlī read "By the ʿaṣr and the misfortunes of time; man is in loss, and he is in it until the end of time" (Ṭabarī via ʿAmr dhī Murr; Qatādah, Qurṭubī, Alūsī). Ibn Masʿūd's version (via Maymūn ibn Mihrān) adds "except those who believe". Qurṭubī: the sound reading is the community's. [tabari, qurtubi, alusi@3]
- `103:2.16` · language · Al-Aʿraj, Ṭalḥah and ʿĪsā al-Thaqafī read khusur with a ḍamma (Hārūn from Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim); Qurṭubī: both forms follow vowel harmony, like ʿusr and ʿusur. [qurtubi]

## Verse 103:3

> except those who have faith, do good, and urge each other to the truth, and urge each other to perseverance

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### THE EXCEPTION AND ITS EXTENT

- `103:3.1` · language · The exception is taken out of "man", since "man" means "people" (Qurṭubī, on the sound view). Mujāhid (in Ṭabarī): the believers are excepted from man because man carries the sense of a plural. Ibn ʿUthaymīn: the whole species is in loss except those with the four qualities. [qurtubi, tabari, uthaymeen]
- `103:3.2` · lesson · Alūsī: "those who believe" covers everyone with the described qualities — not only ʿAlī and Salmān al-Fārisī, as Ibn ʿAbbās's limiting mention might suggest. They are first among them; likewise limiting the loser to Abū Jahl is only the obvious case. [alusi]
- `103:3.3` · lesson · Alūsī: they are in a trade that never fails — they sold the cheap, passing thing and bought the precious, lasting one, exchanging the lasting good deeds for the passing ones of morning and evening. [alusi]

### NAMING THE FOUR AFTER THE COMPANIONS

- `103:3.4` · hadith · Ubayy ibn Kaʿb asked the Prophet the sūrah's meaning: the oath is by the end of the day; "man is in loss" — Abū Jahl; "those who believe" — Abū Bakr; "righteous deeds" — ʿUmar; "counsel to truth" — ʿUthmān; "counsel to patience" — ʿAlī. Ibn ʿAbbās said so from the pulpit (Qurṭubī). No collection is named. [qurtubi]

### FAITH WITHOUT DOUBT, AND ITS SIX PILLARS

- `103:3.5` · hadith · Faith, in Ibn ʿUthaymīn's words, is belief with no doubt or wavering in what the Prophet told Jibrīl: "that you believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and that you believe in the decree, its good and its bad." Reported by Muslim from Ibn ʿUmar. [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.6` · ruling · Three classes of people regarding faith: the sincere believer with no doubt, the denying disbeliever, the hesitant. Only the first escapes loss (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.7` · ruling · Ibn ʿUthaymīn spells out the pillars: belief in God's existence, names, attributes and lordship; in the angels, created of light, with duties — Jibrīl revelation, Mīkāʾīl rain and plants, Isrāfīl the trumpet, Mālik the Fire, Riḍwān Paradise — and others not named. [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.8` · hadith · Reports Ibn ʿUthaymīn cites: no space of four fingers in the sky without an angel prostrating (al-Tirmidhī, Abū Dharr); people gathered barefoot, naked, uncircumcised (al-Bukhārī, Muslim, Ibn ʿAbbās), and ʿĀʾishah's question whether they will look at each other, "the matter is graver" (al-Bukhārī, Muslim). [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.9` · hadith · More of his reports: questioning in the grave (Abū Dāwūd, al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib); the grave as a garden of Paradise or a pit of the Fire (al-Ṭabarānī in al-Awsaṭ, Abū Hurayrah); the Pen told to write what is and will be (al-Tirmidhī, ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit). [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.10` · quran · 40:78: of the messengers, some God told us of and some He did not, so we believe in the named ones individually and the rest in general (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [uthaymeen]

### RIGHTEOUS DEEDS AND THE TWO CONDITIONS OF ACCEPTANCE

- `103:3.11` · language · Righteous deeds are works, not belief of the heart alone: prayer, zakāh, fasting, pilgrimage, kindness to parents, keeping kinship (Ibn ʿUthaymīn); the duties laid on the Companions (Qurṭubī); all inward and outward good, owed to God and to people, obligatory or recommended (Saʿdī); obedience (Ibn ʿAbbās). [uthaymeen, qurtubi, saadi, abbas]
- `103:3.12` · ruling · A deed is righteous only with sincerity to God and following the Messenger; a deed not done for God alone is rejected (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.13` · hadith · "I am the most self-sufficient of partners; whoever does a deed in which he associates another with Me, I leave him and his association" — a hadith qudsī in Muslim, from Abū Hurayrah. [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.14` · hadith · "Whoever does a deed that is not in accordance with our command, it is rejected" — al-Bukhārī and Muslim, from ʿĀʾishah. [uthaymeen]

### WHAT COUNSEL TO TRUTH MEANS

- `103:3.15` · athar · Meanings given for "truth": the Qurʾān (Qatādah; al-Ḥasan in Ṭabarī: "the truth is the Book of Allah"); tawḥīd (Ibn ʿAbbās, via al-Ḍaḥḥāk); God Himself (al-Suddī); faith and tawḥīd (Muqātil, in Baghawī). [tabari, qurtubi, baghawi]
- `103:3.16` · language · Tawāṣī means to charge, urge and love one another (Qurṭubī); Maʿārif: waṣiyyah is strong, effective advice about the best course, also a testament — truth and patience are two chapters of one testament. [qurtubi, maarif]
- `103:3.17` · lesson · Truth as the whole of faith and deed: obedience and avoiding the forbidden (Ibn Kathīr); faith and righteous deed, urged on others (Saʿdī); the sharia — warning a brother who neglects a duty or does a forbidden thing (Ibn ʿUthaymīn); the established, undeniable good in believing in God and following His books and messengers (Alūsī). [kathir, saadi, uthaymeen, alusi]
- `103:3.18` · lesson · Maʿārif: truth is correct faith and good deeds and patience is abstaining from sin; or truth is the articles of faith and patience all good action. Ibn Taymiyyah: two things hold a person back — doubts, answered by counsel to truth (knowledge), and desires, answered by counsel to patience (practice). [maarif]
- `103:3.19` · quran · The study draft: truth may also be what one says at death to those left behind, to die only in submission (3:102); and the pair of counsels parallels belief and righteous deed — knowing truth from falsehood and keeping practice despite obstacles. [initial]

### PATIENCE IN THREE KINDS

- `103:3.20` · language · What counsel to patience covers: obedience and refraining from disobedience (Qatādah and al-Ḥasan in Ṭabarī; Jalālayn); performing the obligations and upholding God's command (Baghawī); enduring the plots and harm of those who harm people for commanding good and forbidding evil (Ibn Kathīr); misfortunes as well (Ibn ʿAbbās). [tabari, jalalayn, baghawi, kathir, abbas]
- `103:3.21` · ruling · Three kinds of patience: in obedience, from disobedience, and under God's painful decrees (Saʿdī; Ibn ʿUthaymīn). Ibn ʿUthaymīn's cases: laziness over congregational prayer, stinginess over zakāh, the gaze in the market, the loss of wealth or a child. [saadi, uthaymeen]
- `103:3.22` · hadith · "Tell her to be patient and to seek reward, for to Allah belongs what He took and what He gave" — the Prophet to his daughter whose child was dying (al-Bukhārī, Muslim, from Usāmah ibn Zayd). [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.23` · quran · 22:11: one who worships God "on an edge", content if good befalls him and turning back if a trial does, losing this world and the next — Ibn ʿUthaymīn's warning of where failing the third kind of patience can lead. [uthaymeen]
- `103:3.24` · lesson · Alūsī: patience is part of "truth" but named again to stress it; or truth is the rank of worship (doing what pleases God) and patience the rank of servitude (contentment with what God does), receiving all that comes from Him beautifully, inwardly and outwardly. [alusi]
- `103:3.25` · language · Maʿārif: ṣabr is from "to withhold oneself and bind oneself", binding oneself to righteous deeds and abstaining from sins. [maarif]
- `103:3.26` · quran · 3:200, "be patient, vie in patience", and 90:17 on exhorting to patience: in the study draft, al-Rāzī reads it as encouraging one another to keep religious practice in the face of difficulty and opposition. [initial]

### THE DUTY TO CALL OTHERS

- `103:3.27` · ruling · Maʿārif: urging other Muslims to faith and good deeds is as necessary as one's own submission; without sincere effort one's own deeds do not suffice for salvation, above all toward one's wife, children and family. [maarif]
- `103:3.28` · lesson · Alūsī: the sūrah urges enjoining good and forbidding evil, and loving for one's brother what one loves for oneself. [alusi]

### AN OBJECTION FROM THE MUʿTAZILAH ANSWERED

- `103:3.29` · ruling · Some Muʿtazilah argued that a major-sin committer stays in the Fire forever, since only believers who do righteous deeds are excepted. Alūsī's good reply: it shows only that the rest are in loss — eternal for one who dies a disbeliever, entry to the Fire for an unforgiven sinner, lost high ranks if forgiven. [alusi]

### READINGS OF THE LAST WORDS

- `103:3.30` · language · Sallām, Hārūn and Ibn Mūsā, from Abū ʿAmr, read "wa-l-ʿaṣiri" and "bi-ṣ-ṣabiri" with a kasra. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah: allowed only when pausing, carrying the vowel over; the author of al-Lawāmiḥ: a widespread dialect, not irregular. [alusi]
