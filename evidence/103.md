## Introduction

### WHERE IT CAME DOWN

- `103:0.1` · history · Alūsī: Makkan in the view of Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn al-Zubayr and the majority; Madinan in the view of Mujāhid, Qatādah and Muqātil. Its verses are three "without disagreement". [alusi¶2]
- `103:0.2` · history · Qurṭubī: "It is Makkan"; Qatādah said Madinan, "and it was reported from Ibn ʿAbbās" — so Ibn ʿAbbās is reported for both places, since Alūsī and Baghawī give him for Makkah. Three verses. [qurtubi¶1, alusi¶2, baghawi¶1]
- `103:0.3` · athar · Baghawī's note: Ibn Mardawayh, from Ibn ʿAbbās, "Sūrat wa-l-ʿaṣr was revealed at Makkah" (cited to al-Durr al-Manthūr 8/621). Saʿdī says only "It is Makkan". [baghawi¶1, saadi¶1]

### AL-SHĀFIʿĪ ON ITS SUFFICIENCY

- `103:0.4` · lesson · Al-Shāfiʿī: "If the people were to ponder on this sūrah, it would be sufficient for them" (Ibn Kathīr); Maʿārif words it "enough for their guidance". [kathir¶7, maarif¶2]
- `103:0.5` · lesson · Alūsī's wording, "narrated from al-Shāfiʿī": had nothing but this sūrah been revealed it would have sufficed people, because it takes in all the sciences of the Qurʾān — for all its brevity it gathered what it gathered. [alusi¶2]
- `103:0.6` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn quotes al-Shāfiʿī ("had God sent down no proof but this sūrah it would have sufficed them") and explains: sufficed as admonition, not as legislation — no purification, prayer, zakāh, pilgrimage or fasting is in it — since every sane person knows he is in loss unless he has the four qualities and will try to gain them. [uthaymeen¶35]
- `103:0.7` · lesson · Maʿārif: a concise but comprehensive sūrah that, in three verses, outlines a complete way of human life on the Islamic worldview. [maarif¶2]

### THE COMPANIONS' PRACTICE OF RECITING IT

- `103:0.8` · athar · Ibn Kathīr: al-Ṭabarānī recorded from ʿAbdullāh ibn Ḥiṣn Abī Madīnah that when two Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ met, they did not part until one had recited Sūrat al-ʿAṣr in full to the other and one had given the other the greeting of peace. [kathir¶6]
- `103:0.9` · athar · Maʿārif gives the same practice from "ʿUbaydullāh ibn Ḥiṣn", transmitted by al-Ṭabarānī — the first name differs from Ibn Kathīr's "ʿAbdullāh". [maarif¶2]
- `103:0.10` · athar · Alūsī: al-Ṭabarānī (al-Awsaṭ) and al-Bayhaqī (al-Shuʿab) report it from Abū Ḥudhayfah, "who had companionship" with the Prophet: two Companions, when they met, did not part until one recited "wa-l-ʿaṣr" to the other, and then one greeted the other. [alusi¶3]

### MUSAYLIMAH'S IMITATION AND ʿAMR'S ANSWER

- `103:0.11` · history · Ibn Kathīr: "they have mentioned" that ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ visited Musaylimah the Liar after the Prophet's commission and before ʿAmr accepted Islam. Musaylimah asked what had been revealed to "your friend"; ʿAmr said a short, concise sūrah, and recited this one. [kathir¶1-3]
- `103:0.12` · history · Musaylimah thought a while, then said something like it had come to him: "O wabr, O wabr! You are only two ears and a chest, and the rest of you is digging and burrowing." ʿAmr answered: "By God, you know that I know you are lying." [kathir¶4]
- `103:0.13` · history · Ibn Kathīr saw that Abū Bakr al-Kharāʾiṭī gave a chain for part of the story, or something close to its sense, in volume two of Masāwiʾ al-Akhlāq. The wabr is a small cat-like animal whose ears and torso are its largest parts and the rest ugly; Musaylimah meant his nonsense to set against the Qurʾān, yet it could not convince even the idol-worshipper of that day. [kathir¶5]

### WHERE THE SŪRAH STANDS AND WHAT IT GATHERS

- `103:0.14` · lesson · Alūsī: it points to the state of the one whom rivalry in accumulation did not distract, and so was placed after Sūrat al-Takāthur. [alusi¶3]
- `103:0.15` · lesson · Maʿārif, quoting Ibn Kathīr: God swears by Time and says mankind is in loss, excepting four qualities — faith, righteous deeds, advising one another to truth, advising one another to patience; the only path of salvation in both worlds. The first two concern one's own betterment, the last two the guidance of others. [maarif¶3]
- `103:0.16` · lesson · Saʿdī: by the first two a servant perfects himself, by the last two he perfects others, and by completing all four he is safe from loss and wins the great profit. [saadi¶3]
- `103:0.17` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: God confirmed, with an oath reinforced by "inna" and the lām, that all the children of Adam are in loss, which surrounds them on every side, except those with the four qualities. [uthaymeen¶34]

## Verse 103:1

> By the ˹passage of˺ time

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial
**Set aside:** tabari¶1 (heading that quotes the whole sūrah), tabari¶8 (heading for the next clause), kathir¶9 (the basmalah in English), kathir¶10-11 (the sūrah quoted again), kathir¶13-14 (the clause quoted as a heading), kathir¶20 (colophon), maarif¶1 (heading), maarif¶4 (heading), maarif¶20 (heading), uthaymeen¶1 (opening praises), uthaymeen¶2 (the lecture's setting: the 99th weekly open-door meeting, Thursday 14 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 1416 AH), uthaymeen¶3 (the verses quoted and the opening question), uthaymeen¶36 (closing prayer)

### THE MEANINGS OF AL-ʿAṢR

- `103:1.1` · language · Time itself (al-dahr): Ibn ʿAbbās (Qurṭubī, Baghawī, Alūsī). Ṭabarī reports "some said: an oath by time — al-ʿaṣr is al-dahr". Qurṭubī adds that ʿaṣr is like dahr and quotes a line in which a day of passion is a month and a month an age. [qurtubi¶4-5, baghawi¶3, alusi¶12, tabari¶2]
- `103:1.2` · language · Ṭabarī's own judgement: God swore by al-ʿaṣr, a name for time — evening, night and day — and the name singles out none of these; whatever it covers is within the oath. [tabari¶6]
- `103:1.3` · language · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: this is the soundest view. God swore by time for the changes of states, the turning of affairs and the rotation of days among people, seen now and told of from the past; its seasons vary — hardship and ease, war and peace, health and sickness, good deed and bad. [uthaymeen¶5]
- `103:1.4` · language · Saʿdī: al-ʿaṣr is night and day, the place of the servants' deeds. Ibn Kathīr: the time in which the movements of Adam's children occur, good or evil — he calls this the popular view. [saadi¶3, kathir¶12]
- `103:1.5` · language · Jalālayn gives "By Time!" first, then, as alternatives, the period from the sun's decline to sunset, or the afternoon prayer. [jalalayn¶1]

### A PART OF THE DAY

- `103:1.6` · athar · Ibn ʿAbbās: "al-ʿaṣr is an hour of the hours of the day" (Ṭabarī, by a chain through ʿAlī, Abū Ṣāliḥ, Muʿāwiyah and ʿAlī). Ṭabarī files it with the reports for "ʿaṣr is time", though it names a part of the day. [tabari¶4]
- `103:1.7` · athar · Al-Ḥasan: "it is the evening (al-ʿashiyy)" (Ṭabarī, by Ibn ʿAbd al-Aʿlā, Ibn Thawr, Maʿmar). Qurṭubī: the ʿashiyy is from the sun's decline to its setting, said by al-Ḥasan and Qatādah; Baghawī's note cites ʿAbd al-Razzāq's Tafsīr (2/394) and Fatḥ al-Bārī (8/729). [tabari¶5, qurtubi¶10, baghawi¶3]
- `103:1.8` · athar · Qatādah: the last hour of the hours of the day (Qurṭubī, Baghawī); Baghawī's note says ʿAbd al-Razzāq has it without "last". [qurtubi¶12, baghawi¶3]
- `103:1.9` · athar · Zayd ibn Aslam, through Mālik: "it is the evening" (Ibn Kathīr, who still calls the first view the popular one). [kathir¶12]
- `103:1.10` · language · Qurṭubī's line for this sense: "Set out with us, ʿAmr — the ʿaṣr has grown short; in the first outing is the gain and the reward." [qurtubi¶10-11]
- `103:1.11` · lesson · Alūsī: Qatādah is reported as saying al-ʿaṣr is al-ʿashiyy, which God swore by as He swore by al-ḍuḥā, for the signs of power in both. [alusi¶9]

### DAY AND NIGHT, MORNING AND EVENING: THE TWO ʿAṢRS

- `103:1.12` · language · Ibn Kaysān: al-ʿaṣr is night and day, together called "the two ʿaṣrs" (Baghawī); al-Zajjāj: al-ʿaṣr is the day, and also the night (Alūsī). [baghawi¶3, alusi¶10]
- `103:1.13` · language · Qurṭubī: "some said, night and day", with Ḥumayd ibn Thawr's line — the two ʿaṣrs, a day and a night, will not wait when they seek to overtake what they aim at. Alūsī quotes the same line. [qurtubi¶6-7, alusi¶10-11]
- `103:1.14` · language · The two ʿaṣrs are also morning and evening: Qurṭubī's line "I put him off the two ʿaṣrs till he tires of me", explained as "if he came at the start of the day I promised him its end"; Alūsī calls them bukrah and ʿashiyyah, "the two cool times". [qurtubi¶8-10, alusi¶11]
- `103:1.15` · language · Alūsī's consequence: on this view and the one before it, the oath is by one of two things, not one fixed in advance. [alusi¶11]

### THE ʿAṢR PRAYER AS THE THING SWORN BY

- `103:1.16` · athar · Muqātil: God swore by the ʿaṣr prayer, for its merit, since it is the middle prayer (Qurṭubī, Baghawī; Alūsī: "according to the majority"). [qurtubi¶12, baghawi¶3, alusi¶4]
- `103:1.17` · athar · Ibn ʿAbbās (Tanwīr): "Allah swears by the ʿAṣr prayer", with "the hardships of time" as another gloss; Jalālayn offers the afternoon prayer as an alternative. [abbas¶1, jalalayn¶1]
- `103:1.18` · language · Qurṭubī on usage: "the call was made for the ʿaṣr" means for the ʿaṣr prayer, and "I prayed the ʿaṣr" means the ʿaṣr prayer — ʿaṣr is used for the prayer itself. [qurtubi¶12]
- `103:1.19` · hadith · "The middle prayer is the ʿaṣr prayer" — Qurṭubī: "in the authentic report", referring back to his discussion at Sūrat al-Baqarah (vol. 3 p. 210); no collection named. [qurtubi¶12]
- `103:1.20` · hadith · "They kept us from the middle prayer, the ʿaṣr prayer" — Alūsī's proof that it is the middle prayer for the majority; no collection named. [alusi¶4]
- `103:1.21` · athar · Alūsī adds Ḥafṣah's codex, which reads "and the middle prayer, the ʿaṣr prayer". [alusi¶5]
- `103:1.22` · quran · The study draft ties this to 2:238, the "middle prayer", while noting that verse is read in many ways, and says many judge the ʿaṣr prayer the likeliest sense here (citing Bḍ, Q, Z); it derives ʿaṣr from ʿaṣara, "to squeeze", for time that is fading fast. [initial¶1]
- `103:1.23` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: the end of the day is its best part, and the ʿaṣr prayer is called the middle prayer in the sense of "best" (al-fuḍlā), as the Prophet ﷺ named it. [uthaymeen¶4]
- `103:1.24` · hadith · "Whoever misses the ʿaṣr prayer, it is as though his family and his wealth were taken from him" — Alūsī, "in the hadith"; no collection named. [alusi¶6]
- `103:1.25` · lesson · Alūsī: the prayer is singled out because the duty is hardest then — people hurry about their trade and earnings at the end of the day and are busy with livelihood. [alusi¶9]
- `103:1.26` · history · Alūsī: or the oath is by the time of that prayer — for the merit of the prayer, or because Ādam, father of mankind, was created in it on a Friday; he says Qatādah took this view. [alusi¶9]

### THE ERA OF THE PROPHET AS THE THING SWORN BY

- `103:1.27` · athar · Qurṭubī: an oath by the ʿaṣr of the Prophet ﷺ, for its merit through the renewal of prophethood in it; the study draft gives the same view, citing Q. [qurtubi¶12, initial¶1]
- `103:1.28` · lesson · Alūsī: "the ʿaṣr of prophethood" — the time of his life ﷺ, the noblest of ages through the Prophet's honour; or his lifetime and all that follows to the Day of Resurrection, whose measure against past time is that of the ʿaṣr hour in a day. [alusi¶11]
- `103:1.29` · hadith · Alūsī's support, from al-Bukhārī (Sālim ibn ʿAbdillāh, from his father): the Prophet ﷺ said, "Your stay among the nations before you is only like the time between the ʿaṣr prayer and the setting of the sun." [alusi¶11]
- `103:1.30` · lesson · Alūsī: its honour is that it is the time of the Prophet and of his community, "the best community brought forth to mankind"; its coming late does not harm it, as a spearhead's coming after the shaft's ends does not harm it, nor a blossom's coming after the branch-ends. [alusi¶12]
- `103:1.31` · lesson · The study draft: the sūrah can be read as concerning both the individual's life and the human species'; by traditional belief the fall of humanity continues as the historical cycle unfolds, each generation a degree below the one before. [initial¶1]

### "BY THE LORD OF THE ʿAṢR"

- `103:1.32` · language · Qurṭubī: the sense may be "by the Lord of the ʿaṣr"; Baghawī gives the same and adds "and likewise in its like", that is, oaths of this form elsewhere. [qurtubi¶12, baghawi¶3]

### WHY GOD SWEARS BY TIME

- `103:1.33` · lesson · Qurṭubī: for what time holds of alerting to the turning and changing of states, and the sign it gives of the Maker. [qurtubi¶6]
- `103:1.34` · lesson · Baghawī: because it holds a lesson for the one who looks. [baghawi¶3]
- `103:1.35` · lesson · Ibn ʿAbbās (in Alūsī): it is time, and God swore by it because it holds every kind of wonder — hence it is called "father of wonder". [alusi¶12]
- `103:1.36` · lesson · Alūsī: it is as though God, by the oath, recalls the blessings time holds and their opposites, to alert man, who is ready for loss and for happiness. [alusi¶12]
- `103:1.37` · lesson · Alūsī: the oath also honours time by denying it any loss or share in events, against those who ascribe events to it; that loss is then ascribed to man shows it is his attribute, not time's — as the line says, "they blame time, and the fault is not in it but in its people". An objection followed: using ʿaṣr in this sense is not apparent. [alusi¶12-13]
- `103:1.38` · lesson · Maʿārif: an oath must stand in some relation to its subject; commentators generally say all man's conditions — growth, movements, actions, morals — take place within time. [maarif¶5]
- `103:1.39` · lesson · Maʿārif's second reason: time as history bears witness — look at the causes of the rise and decline of individuals and nations and only these four acts secure real success; those who abandoned them suffered great loss, as recorded events show. [maarif¶14]

### WHAT AN OATH BY ʿAṢR MEANS IN LAW

- `103:1.40` · ruling · Qurṭubī's second issue: Mālik said that whoever swears not to speak to a man "ʿaṣran" does not speak to him for a year. [qurtubi¶12]
- `103:1.41` · ruling · Ibn al-ʿArabī (in Qurṭubī): Mālik took a year because it is the most that has been said of the word, in keeping with his strictness about meaning in oaths; al-Shāfiʿī said the oath is kept with an hour unless the swearer had an intention. [qurtubi¶12]
- `103:1.42` · ruling · Ibn al-ʿArabī sides with al-Shāfiʿī ("and I say so"), adding that an Arab swearer is asked what he meant and an explanation the word can bear is accepted, "unless it be the least"; and on Mālik's school too, it is taken as he explains it. [qurtubi¶12]

### A WEAK STORY THE SOURCES WARN AGAINST

- `103:1.43` · history · Alūsī: "it is related" that a woman went crying in the lanes of Madīnah for the Messenger of God ﷺ and told him: her husband was away, she committed adultery, bore a child, threw it into a jar of vinegar where it died, and sold the vinegar — was there repentance? He said: stoning for the adultery, Hell for the killing, a major sin for the vinegar — "but I thought you had left the ʿaṣr prayer". [alusi¶7-8]
- `103:1.44` · lesson · Alūsī: "the Imām" mentioned it — "and by my life he is an imām in relating the like of this, on which the hadith imams do not rely; so beware of following him." [alusi¶9]

### ʿALĪ'S READING: "AND THE MISFORTUNES OF TIME"

- `103:1.45` · athar · ʿAmr dhū Murr: "I heard ʿAlī read: wa-l-ʿaṣri wa-nawāʾibi l-dahr; inna l-insāna la-fī khusr; wa-innahu fīhi ilā ākhiri l-dahr" (Ṭabarī, by Ibn ʿAbd al-Aʿlā, Abū Nuʿaym, Isrāʾīl, Abū Isḥāq). [tabari¶11]
- `103:1.46` · athar · A second chain for ʿAlī's reading (Abū Kurayb, Wakīʿ, Sufyān, Abū Isḥāq, ʿAmr dhū Murr), giving "wa-l-ʿaṣri wa-nawāʾibi l-dahr, inna l-insāna la-fī khusr" without the closing clause. [tabari¶13]
- `103:1.47` · athar · Qurṭubī: ʿAlī used to read it with "and the misfortunes of time"; Alūsī: reported by ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd, Ibn Jarīr, Ibn al-Mundhir and others from ʿAlī. [qurtubi@2¶1, alusi@3¶7-8]

### SALLĀM'S KASRA ON THE FIRST WORD

- `103:1.48` · language · Sallām read "wa-l-ʿaṣiri" with a kasra on the ṣād (Qurṭubī); Alūsī names Sallām, Hārūn and Ibn Mūsā, from Abū ʿAmr, reading so, and "bi-l-ṣabiri" likewise. [qurtubi@2¶1, alusi@3¶5]

## Verse 103:2

> Surely humanity is in ˹grave˺ loss

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### THE ANSWER TO THE OATH, AND WHO "MAN" IS

- `103:2.1` · language · Qurṭubī: "this is the answer to the oath". The study draft: vv. 2–3 answer the oath of v. 1 and are close in meaning to 95:5–6 ("Then We cast him to the lowest of the low, save those who believe and perform righteous deeds; for theirs shall be a reward unceasing"). Ibn ʿUthaymīn: God swore on the state of man — that he is in loss. [qurtubi¶1, initial¶1, uthaymeen¶6-7]
- `103:2.2` · athar · The unbeliever is meant: Ibn ʿAbbās, in Abū Ṣāliḥ's narration (Qurṭubī); Baghawī: "it was said he meant the unbeliever", proved by the exception of the believers; Ibn ʿAbbās (Tanwīr): "the disbelieving man"; the study draft: disbelievers (Bg, Q). [qurtubi¶1, baghawi¶4, abbas¶1, initial¶1]
- `103:2.3` · athar · Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, from Ibn ʿAbbās: a group of polytheists — al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ibn Asad ibn ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā and al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd Yaghūth. [qurtubi¶1]
- `103:2.4` · language · Or the whole kind of mankind: Qurṭubī ("the genus of people"; at v. 3, "since it means al-nās, on the sound view"); Jalālayn: "al-insān, the generic"; Saʿdī: every human is a loser. [qurtubi¶1, qurtubi@3¶1, jalalayn¶1, saadi¶3]
- `103:2.5` · language · Alūsī: the definite article is for totality (istighrāq), as the exception shows. Mujāhid (in Ṭabarī): believers are excepted from al-insān because it carries the sense of a plural, not a single person. [alusi¶2, tabari¶14]
- `103:2.6` · language · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: "man" is general because the kind is meant; the sign is that "every" can stand where the article stands — "every human is in loss" would carry the same sense. [uthaymeen¶6]
- `103:2.7` · lesson · The study draft: "mankind" can be read as the disbelievers or as humanity in general (Q); and, seen against "the declining day", as humanity in the later days of the historical cycle. [initial¶1]

### WHAT KHUSR MEANS

- `103:2.8` · language · Ṭabarī: the son of Adam is in destruction (halakah) and loss (nuqṣān); Ibn Kathīr: loss and destruction; Baghawī: khusrān and nuqṣān. [tabari¶9, kathir¶12, baghawi¶4]
- `103:2.9` · language · Qurṭubī's glosses: ghabn (being cheated); al-Akhfash — destruction; al-Farrāʾ — punishment, as in 65:9, "and the outcome of its affair was loss"; Ibn Zayd — evil; or decrease — "the meaning is close". [qurtubi¶1]
- `103:2.10` · language · Baghawī: khusrān is the loss of a man's capital, his self and his lifespan, through sins — his two greatest assets. [baghawi¶4]
- `103:2.11` · lesson · Alūsī: loss in their trades and efforts, spending their lives on pursuits that do not profit them in the Hereafter and may even harm them when they reach the sāhirah. [alusi¶1]
- `103:2.12` · language · Jalālayn: "in a state of loss in all his bargaining". [jalalayn¶1]
- `103:2.13` · language · Alūsī: khusr is indefinite — said to be for magnification ("a great loss"), or for kinds ("a sort of loss beyond what man knows"). [alusi¶2]
- `103:2.14` · lesson · Saʿdī: al-khāsir is the opposite of the winner; loss has many degrees — absolute, as of one who lost this world and the next, missed bliss and deserved Hell, or loss in some respects only; hence God made loss general to every human except those with four qualities. [saadi¶3]
- `103:2.15` · athar · Ibn ʿAbbās (Tanwīr): loss and torment through losing his family and his place in Paradise; "it is also said": loss through the decrease of his works in old age and their cessation after death. [abbas¶1]
- `103:2.16` · language · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: loss and decrease in all his states, in this world and the next, except those God excepted. "In loss", not "a loser": "fī" means containment — man is immersed in loss, surrounded on every side — and the sentence carries three emphases: the oath, "inna" and the lām. [uthaymeen¶7]

### LOSS IN THE QURʾĀN'S OWN WORDS

- `103:2.17` · quran · The study draft: the Qurʾān's loss is losing this world and the Hereafter (22:11); 35:39 — disbelief only adds to the disbelievers' loss; 39:15 — "the losers are those who lose their souls and their families on the Day of Resurrection". The losers lose what they value most in this life, not only material things, and in the next. [initial¶1]
- `103:2.18` · lesson · The study draft: spiritually, loss is losing faith and attachment to God; and if ʿaṣr is time, v. 2 says people who do not use their time properly are in constant loss. [initial¶1]

### TIME AS A CAPITAL THAT MELTS

- `103:2.19` · lesson · Maʿārif: man loses the capital of his existence — hours, days, months and years pass quickly, spiritual and material potentialities decline, abilities fade — like a man from whose great capital a portion is taken each day against his will. This is life's nature: continual loss. [maarif¶5]
- `103:2.20` · language · Maʿārif quotes a verse: "Your life comprises a few breaths that can be counted; when one is sent out, a part of your life has diminished." [maarif¶6-7]
- `103:2.21` · lesson · Maʿārif: God gave man the capital of his life to invest; wisely invested in good works its returns have no limit; unwisely in evil, he loses even the capital and takes on sins; not invested at all, he loses both profit and capital. [maarif¶8]
- `103:2.22` · hadith · Maʿārif's Prophetic support: "Every man goes out in the morning and sells his soul, either freeing it or destroying it" — the passage gives the Arabic and its sense, and names no collection. [maarif¶8-10]
- `103:2.23` · quran · 61:10 — the Qurʾān itself calls faith and righteous deed a trade: "shall I tell you about a trade that saves you from a painful punishment?" [maarif¶11-12]
- `103:2.24` · lesson · Maʿārif: since time is the capital, man is the trader, and the capital flows each minute, so he must be wise and agile. An old scholar said he learned the verse from an ice-seller whose whole stock would melt if he were careless a moment; the oath by time points to a melting capital, and the only escape is to value every moment and spend it on the four acts. [maarif¶13]

### ʿALĪ'S CLOSING CLAUSE: UNTIL THE END OF TIME

- `103:2.25` · athar · ʿAlī read: "inna l-insāna la-fī khusr wa-innahu fīhi ilā ākhiri l-dahr" — "and he is in it until the end of time" (Ṭabarī). [tabari¶10]
- `103:2.26` · athar · Qatādah: "in some readings: wa-innahu fīhi ilā ākhiri l-dahr" (Ṭabarī, by Bishr, Yazīd, Saʿīd). [tabari¶12]
- `103:2.27` · athar · Ibrāhīm: "our reading is: wa-l-ʿaṣri inna l-insāna la-fī khusr, wa-innahu fī ākhiri l-dahr". Qurṭubī: the sound reading is what the community and the codices have; the refutation of those who contradict the codex of ʿUthmān — that such wording is not Qurʾān recited — is in his introduction (vol. 1 p. 80). [qurtubi¶1]
- `103:2.28` · athar · Maymūn ibn Mihrān read "…wa-innahu la-fīhi ilā ākhiri l-dahr, illā alladhīna āmanū" and said it was Ibn Masʿūd's reading (ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd; Ibn Abī Dāwūd in al-Maṣāḥif, per Alūsī). [alusi@3¶9]

### OLD AGE AS A KIND OF LOSS

- `103:2.29` · athar · Ibrāhīm: when a man is given long life and grows old he is in decrease, weakness and regress, except the believers, for whom the rewards of what they did in youth are written; like 95:4–5, "We created man in the best of forms, then reduced him to the lowest of the low" (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶1]
- `103:2.30` · athar · Baghawī gives the same through Ibn ʿAwn from Ibrāhīm (the believers are credited with their good deeds of youth and health) and cites 95:4–6, "except those who believe and do righteous deeds". [baghawi¶6]

### THE READING "KHUSUR"

- `103:2.31` · language · Al-Aʿraj, Ṭalḥah and ʿĪsā al-Thaqafī read khusur with a ḍamma on the sīn (Hārūn from Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim); Qurṭubī: both follow vowel-harmony (ittibāʿ), like ʿusr and ʿusur. [qurtubi¶1]

## Verse 103:3

> except those who have faith, do good, and urge each other to the truth, and urge each other to perseverance

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### THE EXCEPTION AND ITS EXTENT

- `103:3.1` · language · Qurṭubī: "illā alladhīna āmanū" is an exception taken out of al-insān, since it means "people", on the sound view. [qurtubi¶1]
- `103:3.2` · athar · Mujāhid (in Ṭabarī): except those who believed in God and affirmed His oneness, acknowledged His unity and obedience, did righteous deeds, discharged what His obligations required and avoided His forbidden acts — believers are excepted from al-insān because it carries the sense of a plural. [tabari¶14]
- `103:3.3` · lesson · Baghawī: "they are not in loss"; Jalālayn: "they are not in a state of loss". [baghawi¶5, jalalayn¶1]
- `103:3.4` · lesson · Alūsī: they are in a trade that never fails — they sold the perishing and base and bought the lasting and precious, exchanging "the lasting good deeds" for the passing ones of morning and evening: "what a profitable bargain". [alusi¶1]
- `103:3.5` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: God excepts those who believe in their hearts and do righteous deeds with their limbs. [kathir¶15]
- `103:3.6` · lesson · Alūsī: "those who believe" covers everyone with the described attributes — not only ʿAlī and Salmān al-Fārisī, as Ibn ʿAbbās's limiting mention may suggest (both are included first); so too his naming Abū Jahl alone as the loser is the obvious case. This clause is "the account of how they perfect themselves". [alusi¶2]
- `103:3.7` · lesson · Saʿdī: the four qualities — faith in what God commanded belief in, which is not without knowledge (a branch of it, complete only through it); righteous deed — all good acts, outward and inward, owed to God and to His servants, obligatory and recommended; mutual counsel to the truth, which is faith and righteous deed; and mutual counsel to patience on obedience, against disobedience, and under God's painful decrees. [saadi¶3]

### THE REPORT NAMING THE COMPANIONS

- `103:3.8` · hadith · Ubayy ibn Kaʿb: "I recited 'wa-l-ʿaṣr' to the Messenger of God ﷺ and asked its tafsīr. He said: an oath from God — your Lord swore by the end of the day; 'man is in loss' — Abū Jahl; 'except those who believe' — Abū Bakr; 'and do righteous deeds' — ʿUmar; 'and urge one another to the truth' — ʿUthmān; 'and urge one another to patience' — ʿAlī." Qurṭubī adds that Ibn ʿAbbās preached it so from the pulpit, a statement stopping at him (mawqūf); he gives no chain or grading, and no collection is named. [qurtubi¶2]
- `103:3.9` · athar · Qurṭubī: "wa-ʿamilū l-ṣāliḥāt" — they did the obligations laid on them, and they are the Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ. [qurtubi¶2]
- `103:3.10` · language · Qurṭubī: "tawāṣaw" means they loved one another, enjoined one another and urged one another on. [qurtubi¶2]

### FAITH WITHOUT DOUBT, AND ITS SIX PILLARS

- `103:3.11` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: God excepted those with four qualities; the first is faith with no doubt or wavering, in what the Messenger set out when Jibrīl asked him about faith. [uthaymeen¶8]
- `103:3.12` · hadith · "That you believe in God, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and that you believe in the decree, its good and its evil" — Muslim (8/1), from Ibn ʿUmar; Ibn ʿUthaymīn: believe in them as if you saw them with your own eyes. [uthaymeen¶8]
- `103:3.13` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: people stand in three classes — the sincere believer with no doubt, the denying disbeliever, the hesitant; only the first is saved. [uthaymeen¶9-13]
- `103:3.14` · lesson · Faith in God covers His existence, names, attributes, divinity and lordship; faith in the angels — an unseen world created from light, with tasks, some known, some not. [uthaymeen¶13]
- `103:3.15` · lesson · The known tasks: Jibrīl, revelation to the prophets and messengers; Mīkāʾīl, rain and plants; Isrāfīl, the blowing of the Trumpet; Mālik, the Fire; Riḍwān, Paradise. [uthaymeen¶13]
- `103:3.16` · hadith · "There is no place of four fingers in the sky without an angel standing, bowing or prostrating to God" — al-Tirmidhī (2312), from Abū Dharr, whose fuller wording has the Prophet ﷺ say "I see what you do not see… the sky groans and has a right to groan"; "I wish I were a tree cut down" is Abū Dharr's own saying (note citing Musnad Aḥmad 21516). [uthaymeen¶13]
- `103:3.17` · quran · 40:78 — "among them are those We have told you about, and those We have not": we believe in the books God sent down, in the messengers He named by name, and in the rest in general. [uthaymeen¶14]
- `103:3.18` · hadith · On the Last Day people come out of their graves "barefoot, naked, uncircumcised, buhm" — al-Bukhārī (3349) and Muslim (2860), from Ibn ʿAbbās, with 21:104 ("as We began the first creation We shall repeat it"); "buhm" is in Aḥmad (16042), from ʿAbdullāh ibn Unays. Ibn ʿUthaymīn: ḥufāh — without sandals; ʿurāh — without clothes; ghurl — uncircumcised; buhm — without wealth. [uthaymeen¶15]
- `103:3.19` · hadith · ʿĀʾishah asked whether men and women would look at one another; the Prophet ﷺ said, "The matter is graver than that" (al-Bukhārī 6527, Muslim 2859) — each is busy with himself. [uthaymeen¶15]
- `103:3.20` · hadith · Shaykh al-Islām: faith in the Last Day includes all the Prophet reported of what follows death — the trial of the grave, when two angels ask the dead about his Lord, religion and prophet (Abū Dāwūd 4753, from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib). [uthaymeen¶16]
- `103:3.21` · hadith · "The grave is a garden of the gardens of Paradise or a pit of the pits of the Fire" — al-Ṭabarānī in al-Awsaṭ (8613), from Abū Hurayrah; faith also covers Paradise and the Fire — all that relates to the Last Day. [uthaymeen¶16]
- `103:3.22` · hadith · The decree: "God created the Pen and said to it: Write… what will be until the Day of Resurrection" — al-Tirmidhī (2155), from ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit; faith in the decree is that God decreed everything. [uthaymeen¶17]
- `103:3.23` · lesson · So "those who believe" includes the six pillars the Messenger set out. [uthaymeen¶18]

### RIGHTEOUS DEEDS AND THE TWO CONDITIONS OF ACCEPTANCE

- `103:3.24` · language · "They did the righteous deeds": prayer, zakāh, fasting, pilgrimage, dutifulness to parents, upholding kinship — not stopping at the heart's belief but acting and producing (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [uthaymeen¶19]
- `103:3.25` · language · Qurṭubī: the obligations laid on them; Ibn ʿAbbās (Tanwīr): obedience done privately between them and their Lord; Saʿdī: all outward and inward good, owed to God and to His servants, obligatory or recommended. [qurtubi¶2, abbas¶1, saadi¶3]
- `103:3.26` · ruling · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: righteous deeds combine two things — sincerity to God and following the Messenger ﷺ; a deed not purely for God is rejected. [uthaymeen¶20]
- `103:3.27` · hadith · "I am the most self-sufficient of partners; whoever does a deed in which he associates another with Me, I leave him and his association" — a ḥadīth qudsī, Muslim (2985), from Abū Hurayrah; so prayer, charity, study or kinship done to show off is rejected though righteous in appearance. [uthaymeen¶20]
- `103:3.28` · hadith · "Whoever does a deed that is not in accordance with our command, it is rejected" — al-Bukhārī (2697, similar) and Muslim (1718), from ʿĀʾishah; a deed the Messenger did not do is not accepted even if sincere. [uthaymeen¶21]
- `103:3.29` · ruling · So the righteous deed is the one that combines two descriptions: sincerity to God, and following the Messenger ﷺ. [uthaymeen¶22]

### WHAT AL-ḤAQQ IS

- `103:3.30` · athar · "The truth is the Book of God": Qatādah (Ṭabarī, by Bishr, Yazīd, Saʿīd) and al-Ḥasan, by two chains (Ibn ʿAbd al-Aʿlā, Ibn Thawr, Maʿmar; and ʿImrān ibn Bakkār al-Kalāʿī, Khaṭṭāb ibn ʿUthmān, Abū Rawḥ al-Sakūnī — "a Ḥimṣī I met in Armenia"). [tabari¶20-22]
- `103:3.31` · athar · The Qurʾān: al-Ḥasan and Qatādah (Baghawī; Qatādah in Qurṭubī); the study draft names it too (Bg, Q, Ṭs). [baghawi¶5, qurtubi¶3, initial¶1]
- `103:3.32` · athar · Tawḥīd: al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās (Qurṭubī); Muqātil: faith and tawḥīd (Baghawī); Ibn ʿAbbās (Tanwīr): belief in God's oneness, "and it is said, the Qurʾān"; Jalālayn: faith; the study draft: faith and professing divine unity (Bg, Ṭs). [qurtubi¶3, baghawi¶5, abbas¶1, jalalayn¶1, initial¶1]
- `103:3.33` · athar · Al-Suddī: al-ḥaqq here is God; the study draft: truth can mean God (Q). [qurtubi¶3, initial¶1]
- `103:3.34` · lesson · Ṭabarī: they enjoined one another to hold to acting by what God sent down in His Book — His commands — and to avoid what He forbade there; "the people of interpretation said as we have said". [tabari¶17-18]
- `103:3.35` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: the truth is the sharīʿah — if one sees his brother neglecting a duty he tells him, "My brother, benefit yourself, do the duty"; if he sees him doing a forbidden thing, "avoid the forbidden" — benefiting themselves and others. [uthaymeen¶23]
- `103:3.36` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: to do acts of obedience and avoid the forbidden; Saʿdī: faith and righteous deed, urged on others and made desirable. [kathir¶17, saadi¶3]
- `103:3.37` · lesson · Alūsī: the established thing that cannot be denied and whose good traces do not vanish in either abode — all good: faith in God and following His books and messengers in every creed and deed; the account of how they perfect others. [alusi¶3]
- `103:3.38` · lesson · Maʿārif: truth is the package of correct faith and good deeds and patience is abstaining from all sin (counsel to truth enjoins the good, counsel to patience forbids evil); or truth is the articles of faith and patience all good action and abstention from evil; believers must adopt both and advise others strongly and effectively, creating a healthy atmosphere around them. [maarif¶17-18]
- `103:3.39` · language · Maʿārif: tawāṣī comes from waṣiyyah, to advise someone strongly and effectively about the best course, also a will or testament; truth and patience are two chapters of one testament. [maarif¶16-17]
- `103:3.40` · quran · The study draft: truth may also be what one says at death to those left behind — "die not except in submission" (3:102; Ṭs). [initial¶1]

### WHAT PATIENCE COVERS

- `103:3.41` · athar · "Patience is obedience to God": Qatādah (Ṭabarī, by Bishr, Yazīd, Saʿīd) and al-Ḥasan, by two chains (ʿImrān, Khaṭṭāb, Abū Rawḥ; and Ibn ʿAbd al-Aʿlā, Ibn Thawr, Maʿmar). [tabari¶28-30]
- `103:3.42` · lesson · Ṭabarī: they enjoined one another to patience in acting by obedience to God; "the people of interpretation said as we have said". [tabari¶25-26]
- `103:3.43` · language · Obedience and refraining from disobedience: Qurṭubī ("patience in obedience to God and patience against His disobedience"; he refers back to an earlier discussion, p. 71), Jalālayn; Baghawī: patience in performing obligations and establishing God's command; Ibn ʿAbbās (Tanwīr): in performing obligations, avoiding transgressions and enduring misfortunes, "because the believers are unlike the disbelievers". [qurtubi¶4, jalalayn¶1, baghawi¶6, abbas¶1]
- `103:3.44` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: patience under the plots, evils and harms of those who harm people because they command them to good and forbid them evil. [kathir¶19]
- `103:3.45` · lesson · Alūsī: patience against the sins the soul longs for by human nature, on the hard acts of obedience, and under the calamities God tests His servants with. [alusi¶4]
- `103:3.46` · lesson · Alūsī: patience is part of the truth and is named after it, repeating the preposition and verb, to show special care; or the first is the rank of worship (doing what pleases God) and the second the rank of servitude (contentment with what God did) — patience is not mere restraint but receiving what comes from God with grace and contentment, inwardly and outwardly. [alusi¶4]
- `103:3.47` · language · Maʿārif: ṣabr originally means to withhold and bind oneself — binding oneself to righteous deeds and abstaining from sins. [maarif¶18]
- `103:3.48` · lesson · Three kinds: patience on obedience to God, against what He forbade, and under His decrees (Saʿdī, Ibn ʿUthaymīn); Ibn ʿUthaymīn defines patience as holding the soul back from what ought not to be done. [saadi¶3, uthaymeen¶24+32]
- `103:3.49` · lesson · Examples of patience in obedience (Ibn ʿUthaymīn): the man lazy about prayer in congregation who says "I pray at home"; the man who hesitates over a large zakāh; and 2:45 on prayer — "indeed it is hard except for the humble" — hence they counsel one another "do not tire, do not be lazy". [uthaymeen¶25-27]
- `103:3.50` · lesson · Patience against disobedience: forbidden earnings by ribā, cheating, lying or deceit; and the gaze that follows women in the market. [uthaymeen¶28-29]
- `103:3.51` · lesson · Patience under decrees: illness, loss of wealth, loss of loved ones — "this is decreed; impatience helps nothing; suppose it had never been" (the lost million; "did you not come from your mother's womb without clothes?"); for a child's death: suppose he had never been created. [uthaymeen¶30-31]
- `103:3.52` · hadith · The Prophet ﷺ, of his daughter whose child was dying: "Command her to be patient and to seek reward, for to God belongs what He took and what He gave" — al-Bukhārī (7448), Muslim (923), from Usāmah ibn Zayd; the fuller wording adds "and everything with Him has an appointed term". [uthaymeen¶31]
- `103:3.53` · lesson · Which kind is hardest differs: some find obedience heavy, some leaving sin, some bear both and cannot bear misfortune — it may reach apostasy. [uthaymeen¶33]
- `103:3.54` · quran · 22:11 — "among people is one who worships God on an edge: if good touches him he is content; if a trial strikes him he turns on his face; he has lost this world and the Hereafter; that is the clear loss" (Ibn ʿUthaymīn, on failing the third patience). [uthaymeen¶33]
- `103:3.55` · quran · The study draft: 3:200, "be patient, vie in patience"; exhorting to patience (cf. 90:17) is read as encouraging one another to keep religious practice under difficulty and opposition (R); the pair of counsels parallels belief and righteous deeds (Sh) and, by extension, all one must know (truth from falsehood) and do. [initial¶1]
- `103:3.56` · lesson · The study draft: righteous deeds arise from faith and are always joined with it; more broadly the sūrah stresses clinging to truth and patience in a world spiritually darkened, and at times in crisis for a person. [initial¶1]

### THE TWO CAUSES OF NEGLECT AND THEIR REMEDIES

- `103:3.57` · lesson · Maʿārif quotes Ibn Taymiyyah (from one of his monographs): two things hold a person back from faith and righteous deeds — doubts about the true faith, which confuse and harm deeds, and selfish desires, which lead one astray though he believes he should do good. The verse remedies both: counsel to truth (reforming others in knowledge) for the first, counsel to patience (giving up base desires) for the second — improving Muslims' knowledge and their practical life. [maarif¶19]
- `103:3.58` · lesson · Maʿārif: the first two principles (faith, righteous deeds) concern one's moral and spiritual growth and need no elaboration; the last two do. [maarif¶15]

### THE DUTY TO CALL OTHERS

- `103:3.59` · ruling · Maʿārif: calling other Muslims to faith and good deeds is as necessary as one's own submission; without sincere effort to invite others one's own good deeds do not suffice for salvation, above all toward one's wife, children and family; many learned people are lax in this. [maarif¶21]
- `103:3.60` · lesson · Alūsī: the sūrah urges enjoining good and forbidding evil, and that one love for his brother what he loves for himself. [alusi¶9]

### AN OBJECTION FROM THE MUʿTAZILAH ANSWERED

- `103:3.61` · lesson · Some Muʿtazilah argued from this sūrah that the committer of a major sin stays in the Fire forever, because only those who believe and do righteous deeds are excepted from loss. [alusi¶9]
- `103:3.62` · lesson · Alūsī's reply: the verse shows only that the non-excepted are in loss, not that they abide in the Fire; loss is general — abiding for one who dies an unbeliever, entering the Fire for one who dies disobedient and unforgiven, missing the high ranks if forgiven — "a good answer". Al-Māturīdī's strained efforts on this are in his Taʾwīlāt. [alusi¶9]

### READINGS OF THE LAST WORDS

- `103:3.63` · language · Sallām, Hārūn and Ibn Mūsā, from Abū ʿAmr, read "wa-l-ʿaṣiri" and "bi-l-ṣabiri" with a kasra; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah: allowed only in pause, by carrying the vowel over; a partial vowel (ishmām) from Abū ʿAmr likewise only in pause. [alusi¶5]
- `103:3.64` · language · The author of al-Lawāmiḥ: ʿĪsā of Baṣrah read "bi-l-ṣabir" by carrying the rā's vowel to the bāʾ, to avoid a half-vowel in pause or two silent letters — a widespread dialect, not irregular, marking iʿrāb. Alūsī (from al-Baḥr) cites the line "I am Jarīr, my kunyah Abū ʿAmir; I strike with the sword and Saʿd is in the ʿaṣir". [alusi¶5-7]
