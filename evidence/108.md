## Introduction

### WHERE IT CAME, AND WHAT IT IS CALLED

- `108:0.1` · history · Makkan per Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Kalbī, Muqātil; Madinan per al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrimah, Mujāhid, Qatādah (Qurṭubī). Alūsī: Makkan is the majority view, but al-Nawawī prefers Madinan. Three verses. [qurtubi, alusi, baghawi]
- `108:0.2` · language · Also called Sūrat al-Naḥr, "the sūrah of sacrifice" (al-Biqāʿī, in Alūsī). [alusi]

### THE REPORT OF ITS REVELATION

- `108:0.3` · hadith · Anas: the Prophet dozed, woke smiling, said a sūrah had just been revealed, recited it and explained al-Kawthar. Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, al-Nasāʾī (Ibn Kathīr); Aḥmad, al-Bayhaqī (Alūsī). [kathir, alusi, qurtubi]
- `108:0.4` · occasion · The sūrah answers the taunt that the Prophet was "abtar", cut off, once his sons had died — the persons named are under verse 3. [qurtubi@3, baghawi@3, maarif, initial@3, alusi@3, uthaymeen@3]

### THE SHAPE OF THE SŪRAH

- `108:0.5` · lesson · Gift, thanks, rebuttal: God reminds the Prophet of His favour (v. 1), commands thanks by prayer and sacrifice (v. 2), and answers his hater (v. 3) (Saʿdī, Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [saadi, uthaymeen]

## Verse 108:1

> Indeed, We have granted you ˹O Prophet˺ abundant goodness

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### WHAT AL-KAWTHAR MEANS

- `108:1.1` · language · Kawthar is a fawʿal form from kathra, "abundance": Arabs call anything great in number, measure or worth kawthar (Qurṭubī, Baghawī); "much good" (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [qurtubi, baghawi, uthaymeen]
- `108:1.2` · athar · Ibn ʿAbbās (via Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, in al-Bukhārī and Ibn Kathīr): it is the good God gave His Messenger, the river being part of it. Also Jalālayn, Saʿdī. [kathir, initial, abbas, jalalayn, saadi]
- `108:1.3` · language · Sixteen views in all (Qurṭubī); beyond the river and pool: the Qurʾān (al-Ḥasan), prophethood, intercession, the five prayers, the shahādah (Hilāl ibn Yasāf), abundant offspring. [qurtubi, alusi, initial]
- `108:1.4` · lesson · Ṭabarī prefers the river, "described as abundant for its great worth", because the reports from the Messenger run unbroken. [tabari]

### THE RIVER IN PARADISE

- `108:1.5` · hadith · Ibn ʿUmar from the Prophet: banks of gold, course over pearls and rubies, soil sweeter than musk, water sweeter than honey and whiter than snow — ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ (al-Tirmidhī, in Qurṭubī). [qurtubi, tabari]
- `108:1.6` · hadith · Anas: the Prophet saw a river with banks of hollowed-pearl domes; Jibrīl said, "This is al-Kawthar" (al-Bukhārī and Muslim, in Ibn Kathīr); the study draft places it on his ascent. [kathir, initial]

### THE POOL AT THE GATHERING

- `108:1.7` · hadith · "A river my Lord promised me with much good; a pool my community will come to on the Day of Resurrection, its vessels as many as the stars" — Muslim, from Anas (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi]
- `108:1.8` · lesson · Two channels flow from the river into the pool at the gathering (Maʿārif, Ibn ʿUthaymīn). Saʿdī: a month long and wide, whiter than milk; one drink and never thirst again. [maarif, uthaymeen, saadi]
- `108:1.9` · hadith · Every prophet has a pool, and they will vie over whose has more visitors — al-Tirmidhī, ḥasan gharīb (Alūsī). [alusi]
- `108:1.10` · lesson · Maʿārif: some will be turned away from the pool — those who later turned back from Islam, or were hypocrites. [maarif]

### THE GOOD IN THIS LIFE AND THE NEXT

- `108:1.11` · lesson · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: abundant good in both worlds — a great community guided (the guide to good is like its doer), the Praised Station, the great intercession. [uthaymeen]

### THE NŪN READING

- `108:1.12` · language · Al-Ḥasan and Ṭalḥah read "anṭaynāka" with a nūn, reported from Umm Salamah from the Prophet — a dialect form of "gave" (Qurṭubī, Alūsī). [qurtubi, alusi]

## Verse 108:2

> So pray and sacrifice to your Lord ˹alone˺

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### THE COMMAND TO PRAY

- `108:2.1` · athar · The obligatory prayer: al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās; the five prayers, the pillar of worship (Ibn al-ʿArabī); most take it so (study draft). Ibn ʿUthaymīn: every prayer — obligatory, voluntary, ʿĪd, Friday. [qurtubi, initial, uthaymeen]
- `108:2.2` · athar · Or the ʿĪd prayer on the day of sacrifice: Qatādah, ʿAṭāʾ, ʿIkrimah (Qurṭubī, Baghawī, Ṭabarī); Jalālayn; Ibn ʿAbbās's last gloss. [qurtubi, baghawi, tabari, jalalayn, abbas]
- `108:2.3` · athar · The dawn prayer at Muzdalifah and slaughtering at Minā: Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḥakam. Saʿīd also: revealed at al-Ḥudaybiyyah, when the Prophet was barred from the House. [qurtubi, baghawi, tabari]
- `108:2.4` · lesson · Why "pray" and not "give thanks": prayer gathers every kind of thanks (Alūsī). Saʿdī: the two acts are the best worship — humility of heart and limbs, and giving up cherished wealth. [alusi, saadi]

### THE COMMAND TO SACRIFICE

- `108:2.5` · language · Naḥr: slaughter of camels by stabbing the throat, named because Arabs sacrificed camels (Maʿārif); Ibn ʿUthaymīn adds that camels benefit the poor most. [maarif, uthaymeen]
- `108:2.6` · athar · It means to sacrifice: Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAṭāʾ, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrimah, Qatādah and many of the Salaf (Ibn Kathīr, Maʿārif, Ṭabarī). [kathir, maarif, tabari]
- `108:2.7` · athar · Anas: the Prophet used to sacrifice and then pray, and was told to pray first (Qurṭubī, Ṭabarī); most hold the prayer must precede the sacrifice for it to be accepted (study draft). [qurtubi, tabari, initial]
- `108:2.8` · hadith · Ibn ʿUthaymīn: on pilgrimage the Prophet gave a hundred camels, slaughtered sixty-three himself, left the rest to ʿAlī, gave away all but a piece of each, cooked and ate (Muslim, from Jābir). [uthaymeen]

### THE HANDS AT THE CHEST

- `108:2.9` · athar · Another reading of "wanḥar": hand on hand in prayer (ʿAlī, al-Shaʿbī, Ibn ʿAbbās), or raise the hands at the opening takbīr (Baghawī's note: unsound; Ibn Kathīr: munkar). [tabari, baghawi, abbas, maarif]

### FOR GOD ALONE

- `108:2.10` · lesson · Ṭabarī's choice: make all your prayer and sacrifice purely for your Lord, not idols, in thanks for the gift — no reason to single out one prayer or sacrifice. [tabari]
- `108:2.11` · athar · Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb: some people prayed and sacrificed to others than God, so he was told to do both for God alone (Baghawī, Ṭabarī). Ibn Kathīr: the opposite of the idolaters' way. [baghawi, tabari, kathir]
- `108:2.12` · quran · 6:162, "my prayer, my sacrifice, my life and my death are for God", is joined here by Ibn Kathīr and Maʿārif; Ibn Kathīr adds 6:121. [kathir, maarif]

## Verse 108:3

> Only the one who hates you is truly cut off ˹from any goodness˺

**Sources with text:** tabari, qurtubi, baghawi, kathir, alusi, jalalayn, abbas, saadi, uthaymeen, maarif, initial

### WHO THE HATER WAS

- `108:3.1` · occasion · Most reports: al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, who called the Prophet "abtar" after his son by Khadījah died (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Qatādah; Qurṭubī, Baghawī, Jalālayn). [qurtubi, baghawi, tabari, kathir, jalalayn, abbas]
- `108:3.2` · occasion · Others named: ʿUqbah ibn Abī Muʿayṭ (Shimr ibn ʿAṭiyyah); Abū Jahl when Ibrāhīm died (ʿIkrimah from Ibn ʿAbbās); Abū Lahab (ʿAṭāʾ); a group of Quraysh. [qurtubi, tabari, kathir, alusi]
- `108:3.3` · occasion · Al-Bazzār from Ibn ʿAbbās: Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf came to Makkah, the Quraysh called the Prophet cut off and asked who was better; he said "you". Ibn Kathīr: the chain is authentic. [kathir, maarif, initial, baghawi]

### WHAT "ABTAR" MEANS

- `108:3.4` · language · From batara, "to cut": one whose male line has ended — Arabs said it when sons died and only daughters remained (Qurṭubī); a cut-off tail, then one with no posterity or good name (Alūsī). [qurtubi, alusi, initial, uthaymeen]
- `108:3.5` · language · Shāniʾ is one who hates: "enemy" (Baghawī, from Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Bukhārī); "hater, traducer" (Maʿārif); shanaʾān is hatred (Ibn ʿUthaymīn). [baghawi, maarif, uthaymeen]
- `108:3.6` · language · Abtar: the lesser, the baser, cut off at the root (Baghawī, Ṭabarī); cut off from every good, in deed and memory (Saʿdī, Ibn ʿUthaymīn). Alūsī rejects Qatādah's "despised". [baghawi, tabari, saadi, uthaymeen, alusi]
- `108:3.7` · language · Alūsī: shāniʾ is read as continuing or past so the verse does not condemn Companions who hated the Prophet before they believed; Ibn ʿAbbās read "shanīk". [alusi]

### THE ANSWER TO THE TAUNT

- `108:3.8` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: they thought in ignorance that his remembrance would end with his sons — instead God keeps his name before all and his law binding on all until the Day of Judgment. [kathir]
- `108:3.9` · lesson · Maʿārif: Kawthar includes a multitude of children — his offspring continue, as spiritual father he has the largest community, his name is called five times a day, while his enemies' children are forgotten. [maarif]
- `108:3.10` · lesson · Alūsī: his lineage, good name and the traces of his merit last to the Day of Resurrection; the children of his daughters count as his offspring. [alusi]
- `108:3.11` · lesson · The study draft: true legacy lies in belief and deeds, not in children (26:88–89). Ṭabarī: the verse describes anyone who hates him, though it came down about one man. [initial, tabari]

### THE LIMIT ON HATRED

- `108:3.12` · quran · 5:2 and 5:8: hatred of a people must not lead one to wrong them or to abandon justice (Ibn ʿUthaymīn on shanaʾān). [uthaymeen]
