### SIXTEEN ARGUMENTS FOR COUNTING IT IN AL-FĀTIḤAH

- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī: al-Rāzī lengthened the discussion and gave sixteen arguments that the basmalah is a verse of al-Fātiḥah; Alūsī, a Ḥanafī, sets out to answer them and to support his school. [alusi¶4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · First argument — al-Shāfiʿī, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abū Mulaykah (so printed), from Umm Salamah: the Prophet recited Fātiḥat al-Kitāb counting "bismillāh…" a verse, "al-ḥamdu li-llāh…" a verse, "al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm" a verse, "mālik yawm al-dīn" a verse, "iyyāka…" a verse, "ihdinā…" a verse and the last verse a verse — "an explicit text". [alusi¶4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Second — Saʿīd al-Maqburī, from his father, from Abū Hurayrah: "Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is seven verses, the first of them 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm'." [alusi¶4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Third — al-Thaʿlabī, with his chain, from Abū Burdah from his father: the Prophet said, "Shall I tell you of a verse that came down to no one after Sulaymān ibn Dāwūd but me? … With what do you open the Qurʾān when you open the prayer?" — "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm" — "That is it." [alusi¶5.1]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Fourth — al-Thaʿlabī, from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad from his father, from Jābir: the Prophet asked, "How do you speak when you stand for prayer?" — "I say 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn'" — "Say 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm'." [alusi¶5.1-5.2]
- `1:1.0` · athar · More from al-Thaʿlabī's chains: Umm Salamah — the Prophet used to recite "bismillāh…" then "al-ḥamdu li-llāh…"; ʿAlī opened a sūrah in prayer with the basmalah and said whoever leaves reciting it has fallen short; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, from Ibn ʿAbbās, on "seven of the oft-repeated" (15:87): it is al-Fātiḥah — "and where is the seventh?" — "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm". [alusi¶5.2-5.3]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Abū Hurayrah (al-Thaʿlabī): "When you recite Umm al-Qurʾān do not leave out 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm', for it is one of its verses." [alusi¶5.3]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Abū Hurayrah (al-Thaʿlabī): the ḥadīth qudsī in a form that counts the basmalah — when the servant says "bismillāh…" God says "My servant has glorified Me"; "al-ḥamdu…" — "praised Me"; "al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm" — "extolled Me"; "mālik yawm al-dīn" — "My servant has entrusted to Me"; "iyyāka…" — "between Me and My servant"; "ihdinā…" — "for My servant, and he shall have what he asks". [alusi¶5.3]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Abū Hurayrah (al-Thaʿlabī): a man entered, prayed, took refuge and said "al-ḥamdu li-llāh…" without the basmalah; the Prophet said, "You have cut your prayer off. Did you not know that 'bismillāh…' is part of al-ḥamd? Whoever leaves it leaves a verse of it; whoever leaves a verse has cut off his prayer, for there is no prayer without it; so whoever leaves a verse, his prayer is void." [alusi¶5.3-5.4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Ṭalḥah ibn ʿUbaydillāh (al-Thaʿlabī): "Whoever leaves 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm' has left a verse of the Book of God." [alusi¶5.4]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Fifth — reciting it is obligatory at the start of al-Fātiḥah, so it must be a verse of it: "Read in the name of your Lord" (96:1) — the bāʾ cannot be idle, since every letter of God's speech has a point, so the sense is "read, beginning with the name of your Lord"; the command means obligation and is not established outside recitation in prayer, so it is established there, to keep the text from being left idle. [alusi¶6]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Sixth — it is written in the codex's script and nothing that is not Qurʾān is: they banned writing sūrah names in the codex and marks at the tenths and fifths, to keep non-Qurʾān from mixing with the Qurʾān; so had it not been Qurʾān they would not have written it so. [alusi¶7]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Seventh — the Muslims agree that what lies between the two covers is God's word, and the basmalah lies between them, so it must be counted of it. [alusi¶8]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Eighth — most hold al-Fātiḥah has seven verses; al-Shāfiʿī counts the basmalah, Abū Ḥanīfah does not and counts "ṣirāṭa alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim" as a verse, a weak view; so the verses reach seven only by counting the basmalah as a full verse. [alusi¶9]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ninth — reciting it before al-Fātiḥah is obligatory: Abū Ḥanīfah grants that reciting it is better, so the Prophet apparently recited it and we must follow him (7:158), and once it is obligatory it belongs to the sūrah, for no one separates the two. And "every matter of importance not begun with God's name is cut off (abtar)": the greatest deed after faith is prayer, so al-Fātiḥah without it makes the prayer a cut-off deed — a word meaning utmost deficiency, used of the hateful unbeliever — and all who accept this call such a prayer void. [alusi¶10]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Tenth — the Prophet asked Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, "What is the greatest verse in the Qurʾān?" and Ubayy said "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm", which the Prophet confirmed; so this much is a complete verse — and it is not complete in al-Naml, so it must be elsewhere, and only al-Fātiḥah remains. [alusi¶11]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Eleventh — Anas: Muʿāwiyah came to Madīnah and led an audible prayer, reciting Umm al-Qurʾān without the basmalah; afterwards the Muhājirūn and Anṣār called from every side, "Did you forget? Where is 'bismillāh…' when you opened the recitation?" — and he repeated the prayer and recited it aloud. [alusi¶12]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Twelfth — every prophet began good works with God's name: Nūḥ, "in the name of God be its course" (11:41); Sulaymān, "In the name of God … do not exalt yourselves against me" (27:30-31); so it is obligatory on our Prophet ("follow their guidance", 6:90), hence on us ("follow him"), hence a verse of al-Fātiḥah. [alusi¶13]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Thirteenth — God is eternal and everything else created, so by rational fitness His mention comes before all else, and that precedence occurs only if the basmalah is recited first; what the believers see as good is good with God; so reciting it is obligatory and, none distinguishing, it is a verse of al-Fātiḥah. [alusi¶14]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Fourteenth — it is certainly Qurʾān in al-Naml, and it is seen repeated in the Qurʾān's script, so it is Qurʾān — as "Woe that Day to the deniers" and "Which of your Lord's favours will you deny?", also repeated, are counted Qurʾān. [alusi¶15]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Fifteenth — the report that the Prophet wrote "bismika Allāhumma" shows every part of the formula is Qurʾān and that it is established in it; were it removable despite this and its fame, removing other verses would be easier, which would slander the Qurʾān. [alusi¶16]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Sixteenth — it is established by mass transmission that God sent this phrase to Muḥammad and he ordered it written in the codex's script; the dispute is only whether reciting it is obligatory and whether one in ritual impurity may touch it; affirming those rulings is the safer course — "leave what makes you doubt for what does not". [alusi¶17]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī: "and it is nothing": part is answered, part is no proof against the Ḥanafīs, since the sound view of their school is that the basmalah is an independent verse of the Qurʾān even if not of al-Fātiḥah itself, and many of them make its recitation obligatory in the prayer; al-Zaylaʿī (Sharḥ al-Kanz): the sounder view is that it is obligatory; al-Zāhidī, from al-Mujtabā: sound that it is obligatory in every rakʿah where recitation is required — the sound narration from Abū Ḥanīfah. [alusi¶17]
