### ISM STANDING FOR THE VERBAL NOUN "TASMIYAH"

- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ṭabarī's second objection: how can "bismillāh" mean "by naming God" when ism is a noun and tasmiyah is the verbal noun of sammaytu? Answer: the Arabs often bring verbal nouns out on other patterns — "akramtu fulānan karāmatan" (the pattern of afʿaltu is ifʿāl), "ahantuhu hawānan", "kallamtuhu kalāman" (the pattern of faʿʿaltu is tafʿīl). [tabari¶9-10]
- `1:1.0` · language · His lines: al-Quṭāmī — "Is it ingratitude after you turned death from me and after your giving (ʿaṭāʾika) a hundred pasturing camels?", ʿaṭāʾ for iʿṭāʾ (his editor adds that he said it to Zufar ibn al-Ḥārith, who had captured him, freed him and given him a hundred camels); another — "in my long hoping (ṭūlī) for you I was an Ashʿab", ṭūlī for iṭālatī; and al-Ḥārith ibn Khālid — "your misfortune (muṣābakum) in a man who gave the greeting was wrong", muṣāb for iṣābah. [tabari¶11-16]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · So since the Arabs often bring verbal nouns out on other patterns, and often on the pattern of nouns, what Ṭabarī says of "bismillāh" is clear: at the start of an act or speech it means "I begin by naming God before my act or speech", and at the start of Qurʾān recitation "I recite beginning with naming God"; "ism" is put in the place of "tasmiyah" as kalām for takhlīm and ʿaṭāʾ for iʿṭāʾ. [tabari¶17-18]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Ṭabarī: there is no disagreement among the scholars of the Ummah that one who says "bi-llāh" and not "bismillāh" when slaughtering an animal has left the prescribed words — which shows "bismillāh" does not mean "bi-llāh" as the one who says the "name of God" in "bismillāh…" is God Himself claims; had it been so, the one who said "bi-llāh" would have said the prescribed words. [tabari¶23]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ṭabarī: this is not the place to expand on whether the name is the named, other than it, or an attribute of it — that would lengthen the book; it is the place to say whether the "ism" attached to God is a noun or a verbal noun in the sense of tasmiyah. [tabari¶24]
- `1:1.0` · language · Ṭabarī: so they did with "sammā yusammī tasmiyah": they brought out for this four-letter verb a verbal noun on the pattern of an event-noun, "ism", saying "sammā yusammī ismān" for "tasmiyah"; thus "kalām" for "takhlīm" and "ism" for "tasmiyah"; what is attached to His name in "bismillāh" and the like is therefore a verbal noun with the sense of "tasmiyah", not the event-noun of the naming act. The editor's note adds the derivation from "samawtu l-shayʾ" — the three-letter verb dropped, its verbal noun "sumuw" kept, the final wāw dropped and an alif added at the start — and praises this as the finest explanation, which the scholars missed. [tabari¶24-26]
