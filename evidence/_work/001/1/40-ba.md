### THE BĀʾ AND THE VERB LEFT UNSAID

- `1:1.0` · language · Baghawī: bāʾ is a particle that puts the next word in the genitive, like min and ʿan; what it attaches to is dropped because the speech shows it — "I begin in the name of God", or "say: in the name of God". [baghawi¶6]

- `1:1.0` · language · Maʿārif: bāʾ has several senses in Arabic, three fitting here — contiguity, seeking aid, and seeking blessing — so the phrase means "with the name of God", "with the help of the name of God", or "with the barakah of the name of God". [maarif¶16, maarif¶19-22]
- `1:1.0` · language · The study draft: "bi" may imply a verb at the start — "I begin" or "I seek help in the Name of God" (R, Ṭb) — or a noun, "the beginning of the discussion is in the Name of God" (R); or the phrase may be read as following the verse, "In the Name of God I begin" (R). [initial¶2]
- `1:1.0` · language · Maʿārif: in each sense the phrase is incomplete until the work begun is named, so a suitable verb is understood — "I begin or recite with the name of God" — and propriety puts that verb after the phrase so that one really begins with His name; the bāʾ must still come before the name, as Arabic requires. [maarif¶23]
