### THE VERB "BASMALA" AND ITS KIN

- `1:1.0` · language · Qurṭubī, seventh issue: al-Māwardī says one who says "bismillāh" is called "mubasmil", a coined word that appears in verse — ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿah: "Laylā basmalat in the morning I met her; how sweet is that beloved who says bismillāh". [qurtubi¶8.3, qurtubi¶9]
- `1:1.0` · language · Qurṭubī: the well-known form among the lexicographers (Yaʿqūb ibn al-Sikkīt, al-Muṭarriz, al-Thaʿālibī) is "basmala" — he said "bismillāh" — so "you have said the basmalah much"; and so ḥawqala (lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwata illā bi-llāh), hallala (lā ilāha illā llāh), sabḥala (subḥāna llāh), ḥamdala (al-ḥamdu li-llāh), ḥayṣala (ḥayya ʿalā l-ṣalāh), jaʿfala (juʿiltu fidāka), ṭabqala (aṭāla llāhu baqāʾaka), damʿaza (adāma llāhu ʿizzaka) and ḥayfala (ḥayya ʿalā l-falāḥ). [qurtubi¶10]
