### THE ALIF DROPPED AND THE BĀʾ LENGTHENED

- `1:1.0` · language · The alif of "ism" is dropped for lightness and frequency of use and the bāʾ is lengthened — al-Qutaybī: so that the opening of God's Book is a magnified letter; ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz told his scribes, "Lengthen the bāʾ, show the sīn, space them and round the mīm", in honour of God's Book. [baghawi¶6]
- `1:1.0` · language · Or, when they dropped the alif they gave its length to the bāʾ to show it is missing; where the alif is written, as in "Read in the name of your Lord" (96:1), the bāʾ returns to its ordinary shape; and the alif is not dropped when ism is added to other than God, or after a particle other than bāʾ. [baghawi¶6]

- `1:1.0` · language · Maʿārif: ordinary Arabic writing joins the bāʾ to an alif ("bi-ismi"), but the ʿUthmānic codex, by the consensus of the Companions, drops the alif and joins the bāʾ to the sīn so that the bāʾ looks like part of "ism" and one begins, in effect, with the name; the alif stays in other combinations — as in "Read in the name of your Lord" (96:1) — the peculiarity of "bismillāh" alone. [maarif¶23-24]
- `1:1.0` · language · Qurṭubī, thirteenth issue: "bismillāh" is written without an alif, the bāʾ of contiguity making it unnecessary in speech and script through frequent use — unlike "Read in the name of your Lord" (96:1), where the alif stays, being less used (the printed text says it is dropped there, which the codex does not do); they differ on dropping it before "al-Raḥmān" and "al-Qāhir": al-Kisāʾī and Saʿīd al-Akhfash drop it; Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb drops it only in "bismillāh", where use is frequent. [qurtubi¶13]
