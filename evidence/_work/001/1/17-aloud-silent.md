### ALOUD OR SILENT: THE REPORTS AND THE VIEWS

- `1:1.0` · hadith · Ibn ʿAbbās: "The Prophet did not recite the basmalah aloud until he died" (Alūsī). [alusi¶19.10]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Muslim, from Anas: "I prayed behind the Prophet, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and did not hear any of them recite 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm'" — Alūsī: this denies hearing it, not reciting it, for Anas elsewhere says "they did not recite it aloud" (Aḥmad and al-Nasāʾī, on the conditions of the two shaykhs). [alusi¶19.10-19.11]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Ṭabarānī, with a chain, from Anas: the Prophet recited "bismillāh…" silently, and so did Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī. [alusi¶19.11]
- `1:1.0` · athar · ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mughaffal: his father heard him say "bismillāh…" and said, "My son, beware of innovation in Islam — I prayed behind the Messenger and behind Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and they began the recitation with 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn'; so when you pray say 'al-ḥamdu li-llāh…'" — that is, say it aloud and the basmalah silently; Alūsī does not accept that the report is weak. [alusi¶19.11]
- `1:1.0` · history · Silent: al-Thawrī, Ibn al-Mubārak, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Zubayr, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, al-Ḥasan ibn Abī l-Ḥasan, al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī, Qatādah, ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, al-Aʿmash, al-Zuhrī, Mujāhid, Aḥmad and many others. [alusi¶19.11-19.12]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Of the reports of aloud recitation, only Ibn ʿAbbās's, in al-Shāfiʿī, is sound — "the Messenger used to recite 'bismillāh…' aloud"; Alūsī says it clashes with Ibn ʿAbbās's other report, or means that he sometimes did so to show it is recited, as ʿUmar said the thanāʾ aloud to teach and the takbīr is aloud to announce; "until he died" qualifies the negated thing, not the negation, so the two do not collide. [alusi¶19.12-19.13]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Some memorisers say every explicit hadith for aloud recitation has a criticism in its chain; al-Dāraquṭnī wrote a book on it, and when a Mālikī adjured him to say which was sound he said none was sound for aloud recitation. [alusi¶19.13]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Saying that Anas's reports are six conflicting ones — now aloud, now silent, from fear of the Umayyads who opposed ʿAlī, whose school was aloud — does not harm the Ḥanafīs: at conflict the stronger chain is preferred, and here it is the one that agrees with them, on the conditions of the two shaykhs; accusing the dissenting narrator of lying about Anas is lighter than accusing Anas, a Companion, of lying about the Messenger and his foremost Companions. [alusi¶19.13-19.14]
