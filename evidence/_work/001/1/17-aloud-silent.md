### ALOUD OR SILENT: THE REPORTS AND THE VIEWS

- `1:1.0` · hadith · Ibn ʿAbbās: "The Prophet did not recite the basmalah aloud until he died" (Alūsī). [alusi¶19.10]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Muslim, from Anas: "I prayed behind the Prophet, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and did not hear any of them recite 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm'" — Alūsī: this denies hearing it, not reciting it, for Anas elsewhere says "they did not recite it aloud" (Aḥmad and al-Nasāʾī, on the conditions of the two shaykhs). [alusi¶19.10-19.11]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Ṭabarānī, with a chain, from Anas: the Prophet recited "bismillāh…" silently, and so did Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī. [alusi¶19.11]
- `1:1.0` · athar · ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mughaffal: his father heard him say "bismillāh…" and said, "My son, beware of innovation in Islam — I prayed behind the Messenger and behind Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and they began the recitation with 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn'; so when you pray say 'al-ḥamdu li-llāh…'" — that is, say it aloud and the basmalah silently; Alūsī does not accept that the report is weak. [alusi¶19.11]
- `1:1.0` · history · Silent: al-Thawrī, Ibn al-Mubārak, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Zubayr, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, al-Ḥasan ibn Abī l-Ḥasan, al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī, Qatādah, ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, al-Aʿmash, al-Zuhrī, Mujāhid, Aḥmad and many others. [alusi¶19.11-19.12]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Of the reports of aloud recitation, only Ibn ʿAbbās's, in al-Shāfiʿī, is sound — "the Messenger used to recite 'bismillāh…' aloud"; Alūsī says it clashes with Ibn ʿAbbās's other report, or means that he sometimes did so to show it is recited, as ʿUmar said the thanāʾ aloud to teach and the takbīr is aloud to announce; "until he died" qualifies the negated thing, not the negation, so the two do not collide. [alusi¶19.12-19.13]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Some memorisers say every explicit hadith for aloud recitation has a criticism in its chain; al-Dāraquṭnī wrote a book on it, and when a Mālikī adjured him to say which was sound he said none was sound for aloud recitation. [alusi¶19.13]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Saying that Anas's reports are six conflicting ones — now aloud, now silent, from fear of the Umayyads who opposed ʿAlī, whose school was aloud — does not harm the Ḥanafīs: at conflict the stronger chain is preferred, and here it is the one that agrees with them, on the conditions of the two shaykhs; accusing the dissenting narrator of lying about Anas is lighter than accusing Anas, a Companion, of lying about the Messenger and his foremost Companions. [alusi¶19.13-19.14]

- `1:1.0` · ruling · Ibn Kathīr: those who do not count the basmalah part of al-Fātiḥah say it is not said aloud; among those who count it part of every sūrah but the ninth some, like al-Shāfiʿī, say it is recited aloud with al-Fātiḥah, as do many of the Companions, the Tābiʿūn and the imams of the Salaf and later times. [kathir¶1.35]
- `1:1.0` · history · Aloud, in Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr's and al-Bayhaqī's accounts: Abū Hurayrah, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Muʿāwiyah, ʿUmar and ʿAlī; al-Khaṭīb reports it also of the four caliphs, though the report from them is contradicted. [kathir¶1.35]
- `1:1.0` · history · The Tābiʿūn Ibn Kathīr lists for aloud: Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿIkrimah, Abū Qilābah, al-Zuhrī, ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥasan and his son Muḥammad, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, Mujāhid, Sālim, Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī, Abū Bakr ibn Muḥammad ibn ʿAmr ibn Ḥazm, Abū Wāʾil, Ibn Sīrīn, Muḥammad ibn al-Munkadir, ʿAlī ibn ʿAbdillāh ibn ʿAbbās and his son Muḥammad, Nāfiʿ the freedman of Ibn ʿUmar, Zayd ibn Aslam, ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, al-Azraq ibn Qays, Ḥabīb ibn Abī Thābit, Abū l-Shaʿthāʾ, Makḥūl and ʿAbdullāh ibn Maʿqil ibn Muqarrin; al-Bayhaqī adds ʿAbdullāh ibn Ṣafwān and Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥanafiyyah, and Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr adds ʿAmr ibn Dīnār. [kathir¶1.36]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · The two works attribute some of the same names to opposite practices: Alūsī lists al-Zuhrī, Mujāhid, ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, Ibn al-Mubārak and others for silent recitation, Ibn Kathīr lists al-Zuhrī, Mujāhid and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz for aloud — the map records both and does not decide between them. [kathir¶1.36, alusi¶19.11-19.12]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · The ground of those who recite it aloud: since the basmalah is part of al-Fātiḥah, it is recited aloud like the rest of it (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.37]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Nasāʾī's Sunan, Ibn Ḥibbān and Ibn Khuzaymah's Ṣaḥīḥs, al-Ḥākim's Mustadrak: Abū Hurayrah once prayed reciting the basmalah aloud and afterwards said "Among you I perform the prayer closest to the Messenger of God's"; al-Dāraquṭnī, al-Khaṭīb and al-Bayhaqī graded it ṣaḥīḥ. [kathir¶1.37]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Bukhārī: Anas was asked about the Prophet's recitation and said "it was unhurried", and demonstrated by lengthening "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm". [kathir¶1.37]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Aḥmad's Musnad, Abū Dāwūd's Sunan, Ibn Ḥibbān's Ṣaḥīḥ and al-Ḥākim's Mustadrak, Umm Salamah: the Prophet used to set apart each verse in his recitation — "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm", "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn", "al-raḥmāni l-raḥīm", "mālik yawmi l-dīn"; al-Dāraquṭnī graded its chain ṣaḥīḥ. [kathir¶1.37-1.38]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Shāfiʿī and al-Ḥākim (Mustadrak): Muʿāwiyah led the prayer in Madīnah without reciting the basmalah; the Muhājirūn present criticised it, and at the next prayer he recited it aloud. [kathir¶1.38]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: these hadiths suffice as proof for reciting it aloud; the opposing evidences and the analysis of the narrations' weaknesses he does not wish to take up now. [kathir¶1.38]
- `1:1.0` · history · Others hold it is not recited aloud in the prayer — the established practice of the four caliphs, of ʿAbdullāh ibn Mughaffal and of several Tābiʿūn and later scholars; also the school of Abū Ḥanīfah, al-Thawrī and Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.38]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Mālik held it is recited neither aloud nor silently, on Muslim's report from ʿĀʾishah that the Prophet began the prayer with the takbīr and then "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn" (Ibn Abī Ḥātim 1:12), and the Two Ṣaḥīḥs from Anas — "I prayed behind the Prophet, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and they began with 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn'"; Muslim adds "and they did not mention 'bismillāh…', at the start or the end of the recitation", and the Sunan have a like report from ʿAbdullāh ibn Mughaffal. [kathir¶1.39]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: these are the views of respected imams, and they agree that the prayer of one who recites al-Fātiḥah aloud or silently is valid. [kathir¶1.39]
