### WHICH VERB IS UNDERSTOOD

- `1:1.0` · language · Ibn Jarīr (al-Ṭabarī) estimates "atlū", "I recite", since the one who recites the basmalah is reciting; so every doer understands the specific verb of the act he begins with it; this is of the Qurʾānic meanings and necessary in ordinary usage; the speaker is not one particular person, so each intends himself by the pronoun, and its coming before this reader's recitation — indeed before his existence — does no harm (Alūsī). [alusi¶32]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Al-Farrāʾ, by choice, and reported from Ibn ʿAbbās: the understood verb is an imperative to the servants — "say", "begin" — so that the speaker of the spoken and of the understood are the same; God put the naming first to urge His servants to do it, which suits teaching. [alusi¶32]
- `1:1.0` · language · The grammarians estimate a general verb such as "abtadiʾ", "I begin", and support it: the verb of beginning can be estimated in every basmalah, the verb of reciting not, and the general is better to estimate — as they estimate "kawn" and "istiqrār" for a preposition-phrase as predicate, adjective, circumstantial or relative clause wherever it occurs. [alusi¶32]
- `1:1.0` · language · And it is enough for the basmalah's purpose, occurring as a beginning, so it fits the place: if you estimate "aqraʾu" you estimate "I begin by reciting", since what falls in the middle of reciting is also reciting, and the basmalah is not prescribed there; the verb of beginning is evident in "every matter of importance not begun with 'bismillāh' is cut off", while in "Read in the name of your Lord" the verb of reading is evident because the main thing is the reading, with no regard to its beginning — which is not so in the basmalah. [alusi¶32-33]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī prefers the Imām's (Ibn Jarīr's): it fits and aims more closely and covers more, for it requires that the whole recitation take place accompanied by the basmalah with God's name sought for all of it, which estimating "abtadiʾ" does not address. [alusi¶34.1]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · His replies: the grammarians' "kawn" and "istiqrār" are only an illustration, like Zayd and ʿAmr, not a particular governing verb; and "abtadiʾ" is not more general than reading here, since it means beginning the reading, which is narrower than reading — reading covers first, middle and last — so this is not the "kawn" and "istiqrār" of the earlier case; and the claim that "abtadiʾ" is general, treated like an intransitive with the context showing the reading is what is begun, is plainly flawed. [alusi¶34.1-34.2]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · That "beginning" suffices for the aim is not conceded — reading is closer and wider; one begins with the basmalah by the deed without needing an estimate, as one who begins prayer with the opening takbīr needs none to be a beginner, though he needs its blessing and its reaching all he does; the hadith does not say every important matter in which one never says or estimates "bismillāh"; keeping to a hadith's wording suits writers, not God's speech; and the dispute is only about preferring a general verb (abdaʾu, ashraʿu), not "aqraʾu" over "abdaʾu" — so these arguments leave the rule of courtesy and the point at issue. [alusi¶34.2-34.3]

- `1:1.0` · language · Some estimate a noun, "ibtidāʾī" ("my beginning"), which adds an omission, the predicate also having to be understood, so that three words are understood; that a noun sentence shows permanence is offset by the present verb's showing renewed continuity, which suits the place; but the difference between the basmalah's sentence and the "al-ḥamd" sentence remains, a matter Alūsī thinks easy. [alusi¶35]
- `1:1.0` · language · Ibn ʿArabī, "the greatest shaykh", made the phrase the predicate of an understood subject, "the beginning of the world and its appearing", since the cause of its existence is the divine names, which hold sway over it — rather than attaching it to what follows, since God is praised only by His names; an allusion not to be judged by the surface or bound by the rules; Alūsī finds objecting to it unfair. [alusi¶35]
- `1:1.0` · language · Qurṭubī, twelfth issue: they also differ on what the bāʾ conveys — a command, "begin with the name of God" (the reciters), or a report, "I began with the name of God" (al-Zajjāj); "bismillāh" is accusative either way; or the sense is "my beginning is in the name of God" with "bismillāh" the nominative predicate; or the predicate is omitted — "my beginning is fixed in the name of God" — and "bismillāh" is governed by "fixed", like "Zayd is in the house", as in "when he saw it fixed (mustaqirran) with him" (27:40), "with him" being accusative, reported from the Baṣran grammarians; or "my beginning with the name of God exists", the phrase accusative by the verbal noun "ibtidāʾī". [qurtubi¶13]
