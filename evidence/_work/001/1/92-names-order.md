### WHY ALLĀH COMES FIRST, THEN AL-RAḤMĀN, THEN AL-RAḤĪM

- `1:1.0` · language · Ṭabarī's question: why does God put "Allāh" before "al-Raḥmān", and "al-Raḥmān" before "al-Raḥīm"? Because the Arabs, when they report of someone, put his name first and then follow with his attributes, and that is what the rule requires — the name before its description — so that the hearer knows of whom the report is. [tabari¶108-109]
- `1:1.0` · language · God has names He forbade creation to take, keeping them for Himself — Allāh, al-Raḥmān, al-Khāliq — and names He allowed them to give one another — al-Raḥīm, al-Samīʿ, al-Baṣīr, al-Karīm and the like; so His own names should come first, that the hearer know to whom praise and glorification are directed, followed by names others may bear once the hearer knows of whom the following meanings are said. [tabari¶109]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · So He began with "Allāh", since divinity belongs to no one else in any respect — neither by being so named nor by meaning, "Allāh" meaning the worshipped, and none being worshipped but Him; and calling oneself by it is forbidden, even if the namer intends only what one means who is named "Saʿīd" while wretched or "Ḥasan" while ugly (Ṭabarī). [tabari¶109]
- `1:1.0` · quran · Ṭabarī: God says in more than one verse "Is there a god with God?", deeming it monstrous in one who admits it, and in specifying Himself by "Allāh" and "al-Raḥmān" — "Say: call upon Allāh or call upon al-Raḥmān, whichever you call, His are the most beautiful names" (17:110) — then puts "al-Raḥmān" second, since He also forbade creation to bear it, though some creatures may deserve some of its meanings: many below God may be described by some attributes of mercy, but none may deserve any share of divinity — so "al-Raḥmān" follows "Allāh". [tabari¶110]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · As for "al-Raḥīm", others may be described by it, and mercy is among His attributes; so it falls in the place of the adjectives that follow the names. This is the reason for putting "Allāh" before "al-Raḥmān" and "al-Raḥmān" before "al-Raḥīm" — which the editor calls among the best arguments ever made. [tabari¶111]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said of "al-Raḥmān" as Ṭabarī does, that it is one of the names God forbade His servants to take (no. 150: Muḥammad ibn Bashshār, Ḥammād ibn Masʿadah, ʿAwf, al-Ḥasan: "al-Raḥmān is a forbidden name"); the editor: Ibn Kathīr and al-Suyūṭī quote it and ʿAwf ibn Abī Jamīlah is trustworthy and firm. [tabari¶112-113]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ṭabarī: the consensus of the Ummah that none of the people may be named by it spares us citing al-Ḥasan and others for what he says. [tabari¶114]
