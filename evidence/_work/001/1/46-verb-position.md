### BEFORE OR AFTER THE NAME: WHERE THE VERB STANDS

- `1:1.0` · language · Many hold the verb is better estimated after the name: God's name precedes the act in essence, so it should precede it in mention, which points to the demonstration from cause, nobler than the demonstration from effect — hence a gnostic's "I saw nothing but I saw God before it"; the infant mind is weaned from what is not God by the sweetness of this name, without the bitterness of createdness; and the blessing sought is full of importance (Alūsī). [alusi¶35]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Even if putting the verb first cuts the root of the claim to power, answering those who make it, that suits the rank of messengership; and it shows the secret of putting the verb first in the first verse revealed, since that station was prophethood — no refutation and no conveying in it: "each place has its saying", eloquence being the fit of speech to the situation. [alusi¶35]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Minds clashed over justifying restriction here — some claimed all three kinds — but Alūsī estimates the verb first, as most do, and finds estimating it last "delayed from the precinct of verification": after the bāʾ, no one who knows the bāʾ says it. [alusi¶35]
- `1:1.0` · language · After "ism" it would separate, even in thought, the two terms of an annexation, where they require omission; after "Allāh" it would separate adjective from described, and between the two adjectives "the tear widens"; after the whole, a subtle deficiency shows — the sentence ties the ruling to what suggests causality, "the Raḥmān, the Raḥīm" being the reason for the recitation bound to God's name, and a verb put after its effect, with its cause before it, suggests exclusivity whose ground is not apparent. [alusi¶35-36]
- `1:1.0` · language · Estimating the verb first, the original order, avoids this and still gives specification — "I recite, seeking help or blessing by the name of God, the Raḥmān, the Raḥīm, because He is the Raḥmān, the Raḥīm": removing the cause removes the effect in rhetorical speech when no other cause appears, especially for one who holds the implication of an attribute, which signals that one without it is outside the circle. [alusi¶36]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī's allusive reading: putting the originated first in thought and omitting it in mention, nothing independent appearing but the eternal Name, hints at the precedence of the entities fixed in knowledge, though not over God's existence, His precedence being absolute — "everything perishes but His face" (28:88); one of the great said "I saw nothing but I saw God in it", without incarnation, counted more perfect than the first saying; those who put the verb last had in mind "in the name of God be its course" (11:41) and "in Your name, my Lord, I lay my side" and made it run their course. [alusi¶36]
