### ISM: THE NAME IS THE NAMED

- `1:1.0` · language · Baghawī: the name is the named — its very self: God says "We give you good news of a boy whose name is Yaḥyā" (19:7) and then calls the name, "O Yaḥyā"; "You worship besides Him only names you have named" (12:40) means the persons worshipped; and "Glorify the name of your Lord" (87:1). [baghawi¶7]
- `1:1.0` · language · The word is also used for the naming itself, and more often so than for the named; asked what God's naming Himself means, the answer is that it teaches the servants how to open their recitation. [baghawi¶7]

- `1:1.0` · language · Maʿārif: the word "ism" has many lexical and intellectual nuances which an average reader need not know; it is rendered in English as "name". [maarif¶17]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī: there has been long contention whether the name is the named or other — the Ashʿarīs say the same, the Muʿtazilah other; the most skilled were bewildered in defining the point at issue; al-Rāzī (al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr) called it akin to futility and offered a view he thought subtle, which al-Shihāb spared refuting; al-Sayyid tried in Sharḥ al-Mawāqif without completing it; Ibn al-Sīd wrote a separate treatise against al-Suhaylī's view; al-Shihāb said it was not yet settled; Alūsī then offers something of his own, hoping it is accepted — an editor's note says the author's discussion is abridged for its length. [alusi¶49-50]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī, eighteenth issue — what the dispute over the root is worth: one who says ism comes from height says God has never ceased to be described, before creation, after it and at its end, and creatures have no effect on His names or attributes — the Ahl al-Sunnah's view; one who says it comes from simah says God, in eternity, had no name or attribute, creatures made names and attributes for Him, and when He brings them to nothing He will be without name or attribute — the Muʿtazilah's view, against the Ummah's consensus and "a greater error than saying His speech is created — exalted is God above that!" [qurtubi¶24]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Nineteenth: from that dispute arose the question of name and named; the people of truth, as the Qāḍī Abū Bakr ibn al-Ṭayyib reports, hold the name is the named — Ibn Fūrak approved it, and so held Abū ʿUbaydah and Sībawayh: when one says "God is knowing" the words point to the Essence described as knowing, so the name, "being knowing", is the named itself; likewise "God is Creator" — the Creator is the Lord and the name itself; so for them the name is the named itself, without distinction. [qurtubi¶24]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār (in Qurṭubī): innovators who deny the attributes claim the names refer only to the Essence, so they say the name is other than the named; those who affirm the attributes hold that the names refer to meanings that are the Essence's attributes, which differ from the expressions — the expressions being the names in their view; more will come in al-Baqarah and al-Aʿrāf. [qurtubi¶25]
