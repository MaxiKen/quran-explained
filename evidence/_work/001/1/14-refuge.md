### SEEKING REFUGE BEFORE RECITING

- `1:1.0` · ruling · Maʿārif: before reciting the Qurʾān it is a sunnah to say "aʿūdhu bi-llāhi mina l-shayṭāni l-rajīm" and then the basmalah, and during recitation to say the basmalah at the start of each sūrah except al-Barāʾah (al-Tawbah). [maarif¶12-14]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · 16:98: "When you recite the Qurʾān, seek refuge in God from Satan the rejected". Maʿārif: by the consensus of the Ummah the taʿawwudh is a sunnah before recitation, in the prayer or outside it (Sharḥ al-Munyah), and is peculiar to Qurʾān recitation — for other tasks one says the basmalah only (ʿĀlamgīrī). [maarif¶32-33]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Begin recitation with both; during it repeat the basmalah but not the taʿawwudh at the next sūrah, except al-Barāʾah — if met in the course of recitation, no basmalah before it; if one begins the Qurʾān with it, say both (ʿĀlamgīriyyah, from al-Muḥīṭ). [maarif¶34]

- `1:1.0` · quran · Ibn Kathīr: three verses tell how to deal with the satanic enemy: 7:199-200 ("show forgiveness, enjoin what is good, turn from the ignorant; and if an evil whisper from Satan comes to you, seek refuge in God"), 23:96-98 ("repel evil with what is better … and say: My Lord, I seek refuge in You from the promptings of the devils, and that they come near me") and 41:34-36 — "these are the only three verses that carry this meaning". [kathir¶1.16-1.18]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · God commanded leniency toward the human enemy, so that his softened nature may make him an ally; and seeking refuge against the satanic enemy, because the devil does not relent when treated kindly — he seeks only the destruction of Adam's son, from his old bitter hatred of man's father, Adam (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.18]
- `1:1.0` · quran · Satan's enmity: "let not Satan tempt you as he drove your parents out of Paradise" (7:27); "Satan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy" (35:6); "will you take him and his offspring as protectors rather than Me?" (18:50); and his oath "By Your might I will mislead them all, except Your chosen servants" (38:82-83) — he lied to Adam that he wished to advise him, so how will he treat us after that vow? [kathir¶1.18-1.19]
- `1:1.0` · quran · 16:98-100: "when you recite the Qurʾān, seek refuge in God from Satan the outcast — he has no power over those who believe and rely on their Lord; his power is only over those who take him as a patron and those who join partners with God". [kathir¶1.20]
- `1:1.0` · language · "When you recite" means before you recite; so too 5:6, "when you intend to stand for prayer, wash your faces", meaning before standing, as the hadiths mentioned show (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.20-1.21]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Aḥmad, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: when the Prophet stood to pray at night he began with the takbīr, then "Subḥānaka llāhumma wa-bi-ḥamdika, wa-tabāraka smuka, wa-taʿālā jadduka, wa-lā ilāha ghayruka", then "lā ilāha illā llāh" three times, then "aʿūdhu bi-llāhi l-samīʿi l-ʿalīmi mina l-shayṭāni l-rajīm, min hamzihi wa-nafkhihi wa-nafthihi"; the four Sunan record it, and al-Tirmidhī calls it the best known report on the subject. [kathir¶1.21]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah, Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim from his father: the Prophet began the prayer with "Allāhu akbaru kabīran" three times, "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi kathīran" three times, "subḥāna llāhi bukratan wa-aṣīlan" three times, then "O God, I seek refuge in You from the cursed Satan, from his hamz, nafkh and nafth"; ʿAmr said hamz is asphyxiation, nafkh arrogance, nafth poetry. [kathir¶1.22]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Ibn Mājah, Ibn Masʿūd: the same prayer for refuge, with hamz explained as death, nafkh as arrogance and nafth as poetry. [kathir¶1.22]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Seeking refuge in anger — Abū Yaʿlā al-Mawṣilī's Musnad, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb: two men quarrelled before the Prophet and one's nose swelled with anger; he said "I know words that, if he said them, what he feels would go: 'aʿūdhu bi-llāhi mina l-shayṭāni l-rajīm'"; al-Nasāʾī also records it in al-Yawm wa-l-Laylah. [kathir¶1.23]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Bukhārī, Sulaymān ibn Ṣurad: two men disputed before the Prophet and one cursed the other, his face red with anger; the Prophet said the same words; told of it, the man answered "I am not insane"; Muslim, Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī also record it, and there are many other hadiths on seeking refuge, in the books of supplication. [kathir¶1.23-1.24]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Ibn Kathīr: most scholars hold that seeking refuge, in the prayer and when reciting the Qurʾān, is recommended and not required, so omitting it is no sin; but al-Rāzī reported ʿAṭāʾ ibn Abī Rabāḥ that it is required in the prayer and when reading the Qurʾān. [kathir¶1.24]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Al-Rāzī's support for ʿAṭāʾ: the plain sense of "so seek refuge" is a command that must be carried out; the Prophet always said it; it wards off Satan's evil, which is needed, and what is needed to fulfil an obligation is itself obligatory; and "I seek refuge with God from the cursed devil" is enough (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.24-1.25]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Its virtues: it cleanses the mouth of the foul speech it has indulged in and prepares it to recite God's speech; it seeks God's help and admits His ability to do all things; it affirms the servant's meekness and weakness before his inner enemy, whom God alone, who created him, can repel — an enemy who accepts no kindness, unlike the human one, as three verses show (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.25]
- `1:1.0` · quran · 17:65, "My servants — you have no authority over them; your Lord suffices as a guardian" (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.26]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: believers killed by the human enemy die as martyrs, but those who fall to the inner enemy, Satan, become bandits; believers beaten by the visible enemy, the unbelievers, gain a reward, while those beaten by the inner enemy earn a sin and go astray; since Satan sees man where man cannot see him, believers should seek refuge from him with the One whom Satan cannot see — seeking refuge is a form of drawing near to God and taking refuge in Him from the evil of every evil creature. [kathir¶1.26]
- `1:1.0` · language · What it means: "I seek refuge with God from the cursed Satan, that he be kept from affecting my religious or worldly affairs, from hindering me in what I was commanded or luring me into what I was forbidden"; only God can stop Satan's evil from touching Adam's son. That is why God allowed gentleness with the human devil, whose softened nature may make him leave his evil, but required us to seek refuge in Him from Satan, who takes no bribe and is moved by no kindness, being pure evil — only his Creator can stop him (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.27]
- `1:1.0` · quran · Ibn Kathīr says this meaning is repeated in only three verses and sets them out again: 7:199, on dealing with people ("show forgiveness, enjoin what is good, turn from the foolish"), then 7:200 ("if an evil whisper comes to you from Satan, seek refuge in God"); 23:96-98 in al-Muʾminūn; and 41:34-36 in al-Sajdah. [kathir¶1.27-1.29]
