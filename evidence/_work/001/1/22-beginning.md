### BEGINNING EVERY ACT WITH THE NAME OF GOD

- `1:1.0` · hadith · The study draft: pious Muslims use the basmalah, or its short form, to consecrate all licit actions, since in the Qurʾānic view all things should be done for God's sake and in His Name; the Prophet said, "Any important matter not begun with the Name of God shall be cut off" (Ṭb). [initial¶2]
- `1:1.0` · history · Maʿārif: in the Jāhiliyyah people began everything with the names of their idols; to end that, the first verse Jibrīl brought — "Read in the name of your Lord" (96:1) — commanded him to begin with the name of God. [maarif¶4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Maʿārif: the Prophet said no important work receives God's blessing unless begun with His name; by another hadith, shutting the door, putting out the lamp and covering a vessel are done with the basmalah; the Qurʾān and the hadith instruct it for eating, drinking, wuḍūʾ and mounting or dismounting a carriage (Qurṭubī); no collection is named. [maarif¶6-8]
- `1:1.0` · language · The study draft: "In the Name of God" is also understood as "I began in the Name of God, so you too begin" — as if God said, "I began through My Name, was united with My Name, and commenced in My Name, so begin through My Name…" (My). [initial¶2]

- `1:1.0` · ruling · Ibn Kathīr: the basmalah is recommended before any act — before a khuṭbah, before entering the privy (there is a hadith for it), and before wuḍūʾ: "There is no wuḍūʾ for one who has not mentioned God's name over it" (Aḥmad and the Sunan, from Abū Hurayrah, Saʿīd ibn Zayd and Abū Saʿīd; ḥasan). [kathir¶1.41]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Before eating — Muslim: the Prophet said to ʿUmar ibn Abī Salamah, a boy in his care, "Say 'bismillāh', eat with your right hand and eat from what is nearest you"; some scholars hold the basmalah before eating obligatory. [kathir¶1.41]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Before intercourse — the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, Ibn ʿAbbās: "If one of you, when he comes to his wife, says 'In the name of God; O God, keep Satan from us and keep Satan from what You grant us', then if a child is decreed between them Satan will never harm it." [kathir¶1.41-1.42]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ṭabarī: God taught His Prophet to put His beautiful names before all his acts and told him to describe Him by them before all his important concerns, and made what He taught him a sunnah for all creation, a path they follow — so they open their speech, their letters, books and needs with it, until what shows of the speaker's "bismillāh" makes up for what stays hidden of his intent, which is omitted. [tabari¶3]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ṭabarī: servants are commanded to begin their affairs by naming God, not by reporting His greatness and attributes — as they are commanded to name Him over slaughtered animals and game, at eating and drinking and in all their acts, and on opening the recitation of God's revelation and the head of their letters and books. [tabari¶22]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Qurṭubī, eighth issue: the Law urges the basmalah at the start of every act — eating, drinking, slaughtering, intercourse, purification, sailing — "eat of that over which God's name has been mentioned" (6:118), "embark in it; in the name of God be its course and its mooring" (11:41). [qurtubi¶10]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · The Prophet: "Shut your door and mention God's name; put out your lamp and mention God's name; cover your vessel and mention God's name; tie your waterskin and mention God's name"; "if one of you, when he intends to come to his wife, says 'In the name of God; O God, keep Satan away from us and keep Satan away from what You provide us', and a child is decreed, Satan will never harm it" (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶10-11]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · To ʿUmar ibn Abī Salamah: "Boy, name God, eat with your right hand and eat from what is near you"; "Satan makes food lawful to himself unless God's name is mentioned over it"; "Whoever has not slaughtered, let him slaughter in the name of God." [qurtubi¶11]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · ʿUthmān ibn Abī l-ʿĀṣ complained of a pain he had felt in his body since he became Muslim; the Prophet said, "Put your hand on the place that hurts and say 'bismillāh' three times, then seven times 'I seek refuge in God's might and power from the evil of what I find and fear'"; Qurṭubī: all this is established in the Ṣaḥīḥ. [qurtubi¶11]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Ibn Mājah and al-Tirmidhī: "The screen between the jinn and the nakedness of Adam's sons, when one enters the privy, is to say 'bismillāh'"; al-Dāraquṭnī, from ʿĀʾishah: when the Prophet touched his purification water he named God and then poured water over his hands. [qurtubi¶11]
