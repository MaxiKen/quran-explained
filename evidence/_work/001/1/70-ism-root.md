### THE ROOT OF ISM

- `1:1.0` · language · Al-Mubarrad (Baṣrans): ism comes from sumuw, loftiness — it rises over its meaning and the meaning sits beneath it; Thaʿlab (Kūfans): from wasm or simah, a mark. Baghawī judges the first sounder: its diminutive is sumayy, where a wasm-word would give wusaym (like wuʿayd from waʿd), and its verb is sammaytu, not wasamtu. [baghawi¶8]

- `1:1.0` · language · Alūsī: for the Baṣrans ism is one of ten nouns whose beginnings are built on a silent letter — ibn, ibnah, ibnum, ism, ist, ithnān, ithnatān, imruʾ, imraʾah, aymun Allāh (eleven if ibnum counts); pronounced, they take a hamzah, since beginning with a silent letter other than a prolongation is ugly; ism has lost its last letter, like "yad". [alusi¶45]
- `1:1.0` · language · Its origin is sumuw: the wāw dropped for lightness, frequent use and the succession of vowels, the sīn made silent and the mīm voweled, a hamzat al-waṣl brought, its form "ifʿ"; its plural asmāʾ, its diminutive sumayy and its verb sammaytu — not awsām, wasīm, wasamtu — bear it out; derived from sumuw like ʿuluww, because by indicating what it names it lifts it from the lowland of hiddenness to the peak of clarity (Alūsī). [alusi¶46]
- `1:1.0` · language · The Kūfans: from simah, a mark, since it marks its named; origin wasm, the wāw dropped and replaced by a hamzat al-waṣl, its form "iʿl"; objected: the hamzah is not known to enter a word that lost its first letter; an extra change is more analogical than having no precedent; a replacement is intended for itself while a hamzat al-waṣl is incidental — "joining the lizard and the fish". So it was said: neither dropping nor replacing — the wāw turned to hamzah, as in iʿāʾ and ishāḥ, and frequent use made it a hamzat al-waṣl, cut in verse by necessity; the first view is preferred for the two witnesses. [alusi¶47]
- `1:1.0` · language · Ism has ten dialects "with eight" more, a verse from the author's grandfather listing ism, simāt, samā, wasm, simah and samāʾ with three vowels on its first letter. [alusi¶48-49]
- `1:1.0` · language · Qurṭubī, fifteenth issue: "ism" has the form "ifʿ", the dropped letter being the wāw — it is from samawtu — its plural asmāʾ and diminutive sumayy; its estimated origin is said to be fiʿl or fuʿl; al-Jawharī: asmāʾ is the plural of this pattern, like jidhʿ and ajdhāʿ or qufl and aqfāl, whose forms are known only by hearing. [qurtubi¶15]
- `1:1.0` · language · Four dialects — ism with kasrah, usm with ḍammah (Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā: who gives the alif ḍammah takes it from "samawtu asmū", who gives it kasrah from "sammaytu asmī"), and sim and sum, with lines of verse for each (one from an Arab giving a boy a blessed "sum", another with "simuh", another "bi-smi lladhī fī kulli sūratin simuh"); the sīn is silent in "bi-smi" by an irregular change, its alif is a joining alif, and poets may cut it by necessity, as al-Aḥwaṣ does. [qurtubi¶15-23]
- `1:1.0` · language · Qurṭubī, sixteenth issue: the Arabs make the relative adjective "sumawī", or "ismī" if one wishes, leaving the word as it is; its plural is asmāʾ and the plural of asmāʾ is asāmin; al-Farrāʾ reports "I commend you to the asmāwāt of God". [qurtubi¶24]
- `1:1.0` · language · Seventeenth: the Baṣrans derive "ism" from sumuw, height — so called because its owner is as one raised by it; or because the name lifts its named above others; or because it rose by its strength over the two other parts of speech, particle and verb, noun being stronger by agreement as the base — three sayings; the Kūfans from simah, mark, since a name is a mark of what it is set for, its origin "wasm". The first is sounder: the diminutive is sumayy and the plural asmāʾ, and plural and diminutive return things to their origins, so one does not say "wasīm" or "awsām" (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶24]
