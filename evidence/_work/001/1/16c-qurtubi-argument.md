### QURṬUBĪ'S CASE THAT IT IS NOT A VERSE OUTSIDE AL-NAML

- `1:1.0` · lesson · Fifth issue, Qurṭubī: the sound one of these views is Mālik's, because the Qurʾān is not established by single reports but by decisive mass transmission about which there is no disagreement; Ibn al-ʿArabī: "it is enough to show it is not Qurʾān that people differ over it, and the Qurʾān is not differed over". [qurtubi¶7.2]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · And the sound reports, with no flaw in them, indicate that the basmalah is not a verse of al-Fātiḥah or any other sūrah except al-Naml alone. [qurtubi¶7.3]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Muslim, Abū Hurayrah: the ḥadīth qudsī "I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant in two halves…", with its replies to "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn", "al-raḥmāni l-raḥīm", "mālik yawmi l-dīn" ("glorified Me", once "entrusted to Me"), "iyyāka naʿbudu…" ("between Me and My servant") and the last three verses ("for My servant") — quoted by Qurṭubī as his proof. [qurtubi¶7.3]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī reads it: "I divided the prayer" means al-Fātiḥah, called prayer because prayer is not valid without it; the first three verses He made His own, exclusive to Him, and the Muslims do not differ over them; the fourth He placed between Himself and His servant, since it holds the servant's self-abasement and his seeking His help, which implies magnifying God; then three verses complete the seven. [qurtubi¶7.4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · What shows the last are three verses is "these are for My servant" — Mālik's report — not "these two"; so "anʿamta ʿalayhim" is a verse; Ibn Bukayr, from Mālik: "anʿamta ʿalayhim" is a verse, then the seventh to the end. [qurtubi¶7.4]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · So by this division, and the Prophet's words to Ubayy, "How do you recite when you open the prayer?" — and Ubayy's "I recited 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn' to its end" — it is established that the basmalah is not a verse of it. [qurtubi¶7.5]
- `1:1.0` · history · So counted the people of Madīnah, Shām and Baṣrah and most reciters, "anʿamta ʿalayhim" being a verse; so Qatādah reports from Abū Naḍrah from Abū Hurayrah, "the sixth verse is 'anʿamta ʿalayhim'"; the Kūfan reciters and jurists counted the basmalah and did not count "anʿamta ʿalayhim" (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶7.5]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · The objection — it is in the codex, written in its script and transmitted as in al-Naml, by mass transmission — Qurṭubī grants and answers: that is because it is Qurʾān, or because it separates the sūrahs ("we did not know the end of a sūrah until 'bismillāh…' came down", Abū Dāwūd), or for blessing, as the Ummah agreed to write it at the head of books and letters — all possible. [qurtubi¶7.6]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Al-Jurayrī: al-Ḥasan was asked about "bismillāh…" and said "at the head of letters"; and al-Ḥasan also said it came down nowhere in the Qurʾān except in "Ṭā Sīn" (al-Naml) — "It is from Sulaymān and it is 'bismillāh…'". Qurṭubī: the decisive point is that the Qurʾān is not proved by reasoning and inference but by decisive mass transmission known by necessity. [qurtubi¶7.6]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Al-Shāfiʿī's own wavering about it at the head of each sūrah shows it is not a verse of every sūrah; and to "a group reported it as Qurʾān, and al-Dāraquṭnī gathered that in a part he graded sound" Qurṭubī answers that he does not deny the reports, but has firmly established opposing ones, from trustworthy imams and sure jurists. [qurtubi¶7.7]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Muslim, ʿĀʾishah: the Prophet opened the prayer with the takbīr and the recitation with "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn"; Muslim, Anas: "I prayed behind the Prophet, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar and they opened with 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn', not mentioning 'bismillāh…' at the start of the recitation or its end." [qurtubi¶7.7]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī: his school is favoured by a great reason — the Prophet's mosque at Madīnah, through all the ages from the Messenger to Mālik's time, nobody ever recited the basmalah in it, in following the Sunnah, which refutes the other side's hadiths; though his colleagues liked reciting it in voluntary prayer and read the reports of reciting it at the start as leeway; Mālik: no harm in reciting it in voluntary prayer, or by one who reviews the Qurʾān by reciting it through. [qurtubi¶7.8]
