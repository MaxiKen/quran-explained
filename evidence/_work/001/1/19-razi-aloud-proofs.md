### AL-RĀZĪ'S SIX PROOFS FOR RECITING IT ALOUD

- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī wonders at al-Rāzī: how can he allow the possibility of accusation and yet report the Madinans' objection to the master of the Umayyad kings in that ugly wording and at that high place — why did they not fear, keep silent and make peace? — and more, he gives six proofs for aloud recitation that are, Alūsī says, "more hidden than non-existence". [alusi¶19.14]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · First proof: the basmalah is of the sūrah, so its rule is the sūrah's, aloud or silent. Reply: refuted above; and even granting it is of the sūrah, what prevents part being said silently and part aloud, as the Prophet did — "so follow him"? Perhaps the secret is that of aloud and silent rakʿahs in one prayer; or that the state of the one receiving the Qurʾān was seclusion first and manifestation second; and the ḥadīth qudsī "I was a hidden treasure…" shows a greater secret. [alusi¶19.14]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Second: it is praise and magnification, so it must be announced — "remember God as you remember your fathers, or with deeper remembrance" (2:200). Reply: most of what the prayer contains is like that — is all of it recited aloud? [alusi¶19.14-20]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Third: reciting God's remembrance aloud shows pride in it and indifference to its denier, which reason approves and so does law — al-Rāzī adds that this proof is strong in his mind and rooted in his reason, and no words of opponents will remove it. Reply: as before; and the noble may be hidden — "obscurity is no shame for a man of majesty; the Night of Qadr is hidden and it is the best of nights"; and would that one knew whether the Prophet's tasbīḥ in bowing and prostration was faulty, so he hid it, or good, so he announced it. [alusi¶20-24]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Fourth: al-Shāfiʿī's report from Anas that Muʿāwiyah prayed with the Madinans without the basmalah and the Muhājirūn and Anṣār objected. Reply: the opponents reject it; or it was not recited aloud, and they knew by some signs — "and the one who saw is not like the one who heard". [alusi¶24]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Fifth: al-Bayhaqī from Abū Hurayrah — the Prophet recited it aloud in prayer; the same is reported from ʿUmar and his son, Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr; from ʿAlī it is mass-transmitted, and "whoever follows ʿAlī in his religion is guided". Reply: opposing reports contradict it; if aloud is conceded it was sometimes, for a purpose, and Alūsī's reports also contradict the attribution to ʿUmar, ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās; Ahl al-Sunnah deny tawātur from ʿAlī — the Shīʿah claim it and recite aloud in silent and loud prayers alike, and one who acted on all they claim is mass-transmitted from the Commander would disbelieve; "whoever follows ʿAlī is guided" is conceded if what ʿAlī did is established, which is far off. [alusi¶25]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Ahl al-Sunnah put first what the two shaykhs held, and if they differ, what al-Ṣiddīq held, as the Prophet narrowed it to him step by step: "My Companions are like the stars — whichever you follow you will be guided"; "Hold to my sunnah and the sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs after me"; "Follow the two after me, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar"; "If you do not find me, go to Abū Bakr." [alusi¶25]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Sixth: the basmalah is attached to an implied verb ("with the help of the name of God they began"), and hearing it alerts the mind that there is no turning from disobedience but by God's protection and no strength for obedience but by His success, that nothing good completes unless begun with God's remembrance, and announcing it is enjoining the good. Reply: besides the poverty of that estimate and that no one holds it, understanding "enjoining good" from this sentence needs thought which, spent on "iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn", would yield many times more; the rest of what he writes serves only to thicken the page and frighten weak students with armies of ink. [alusi¶26]
