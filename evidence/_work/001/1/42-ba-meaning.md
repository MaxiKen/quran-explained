### THE BĀʾ: HELP OR ACCOMPANIMENT

- `1:1.0` · language · Alūsī: the bāʾ is for seeking help (istiʿānah), accompaniment (muṣāḥabah), contiguity, elevation, superfluous, or an oath-particle; the last four are "nothing", though some verses lend them faint support, and the dispute is over which of the first two is preferable. [alusi¶27.1]
- `1:1.0` · language · Al-Bayḍāwī's words suggest help is preferable: it gives the name a greater part in the act, as though the act could not occur or exist without God's name — "not without charm". [alusi¶27.1]
- `1:1.0` · language · Al-Zamakhsharī's words suggest accompaniment, supported by eleven points. (1) It is commoner in use than the bāʾ of help, especially with meanings and kindred acts. (2) Seeking blessing by God's name is courtesy and honour toward Him, whereas an instrument is a thing used and not sought for itself. (3) The idolaters began with their gods' names to seek blessing, so they should be answered in kind. (4) It shows better that every part of the act touches God's name. [alusi¶27.1]
- `1:1.0` · language · (5) It fits the hadith "the naming of God is in the heart of every Muslim, whether he says it or not". (6) Seeking blessing by the name is an obvious sense anyone grasps, while the instrument sense needs fine thought, and the name is an instrument only because it brings one to the act through its blessing — so it returns to blessing. (7) Making the name an instrument for reciting al-Fātiḥah does not suit those who hold the basmalah part of the sūrah. (8) "In the name of God with whose name nothing harms" supports it. [alusi¶27.1-27.2]
- `1:1.0` · language · (9) The instrument sense treats what exists as non-existent — the name is present in the recitation. (10) It would make the name like the pen, which is not read, and it is read. (11) It is concise, a little wording for much meaning, by the estimate "seeking blessing" as a circumstantial clause, which shows the doer's state, and every act of nearness to God needs His help, so the state indicates more. [alusi¶27.2-27.3]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī himself holds help preferable, nearly necessary: it has courtesy, humility and the showing of servitude that a claim of accompaniment lacks; it hints from the first instant at dropping power and strength and denying the servants' independent ability and effect, opening the door of mercy and winning the treasure "no power or strength but by God"; it suits "You alone we ask for help" better; and in "Read in the name of your Lord" (96:1) it is nearly required, as the fullest answer to the Prophet's "I am not a reader". [alusi¶27.3]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · His reply to (1) and (2): proving it commoner is "picking thorns"; that an instrument is demeaned is a delusion from illustrating instruments with sensed things — not every help by an instrument is demeaning, "istaʿantu bi-llāh" is sound and found in the law ("seek help in God and be patient", 7:128); restricting help to instruments is itself doubtful, since help may be by instrument and by power; and if conceded, nothing stops the allusion here that as He is the aim in Himself He is the aim incidentally, there being no power or strength but by Him. [alusi¶27.3-27.4]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · To (3) and (4): the idolaters are nearer to seeking help from their gods, who were their go-betweens to God and most like instruments; an instrument must be present in every part to the end of the act, but one thing accompanying another does not necessarily touch all its parts; and the hadith about the naming in every Muslim's heart fits help better, for it shows the servant disclaiming his power and affirming it for God — a creed every Muslim holds in his heart. [alusi¶28-29]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · To (5) and (6): if "accompaniment means seeking blessing" is meant, it is plainly false; if blessing is understood from indication, the same is claimed for help when instrumentality is meant, since legal reckoning depends on it; that blessing is evident to everyone is not conceded as special to accompaniment, and the restriction in (6) is denied. [alusi¶30]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · To (7): what a thing is opened with may be part of it — al-Fātiḥah opens the Qurʾān and is part of it — and if conceded, it opens relative to what follows (al-Shihāb), which does not harm the Ḥanafī. [alusi¶30]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · To (8): "In the name of God with whose name nothing harms" means "I do this seeking help of the name of God, with whose mention nothing harms me — whoever seeks help at His threshold He helps, whoever takes refuge at His door He guards"; Alūsī finds that far, and to the objection that accompaniment needs something already present while the recitation has not yet happened, answers that the accompaniment here is not sensed, and that denying harm's company implies the company of benefit and blessing — keeping whispering from the reciter, with great reward — a mere reassurance, of which the Ḥanafīs have plenty. [alusi¶31]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · To (9) to (11): treating the existent as non-existent is for an embellishment not required here — the name is likened, from the believer's certainty in what the Sunnah brings, to something sensed: books exist by the pen and are absent without it — then expressed as a derivative metaphor in the particle; the likeness to the pen has its own state, not hidden; and seeking blessing is not conceded to be the meaning of accompaniment or its concomitant, but is known from outside — accompanying His name yields it, as does seeking help by it, which has subtleties besides. [alusi¶31-32]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī suggests, though it is far, that al-Zamakhsharī did not choose "help" because of Muʿtazilī whisperings of the servant's independence in his act, which he and his followers held and which Alūsī will refute. [alusi¶32]
