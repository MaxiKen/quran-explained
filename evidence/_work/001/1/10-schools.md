### THE BASMALAH AS A VERSE: WHAT THE SCHOOLS HOLD

- `1:1.0` · ruling · Baghawī: the qurrāʾ of Madīnah and Baṣrah and the jurists of Kūfah hold that it is not part of al-Fātiḥah or of any other sūrah, and that it is said at the start for blessing and good omen. [baghawi¶12]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · The qurrāʾ of Makkah and Kūfah and most Ḥijāzī jurists: part of al-Fātiḥah but not of the other sūrahs, written to separate them. [baghawi¶12]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · A group — al-Thawrī, Ibn al-Mubārak, al-Shāfiʿī: part of al-Fātiḥah and of every sūrah except al-Tawbah, because it is written in the codex in the script of the Qurʾān. [baghawi¶12]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · All agree that al-Fātiḥah has seven verses; for those who count the basmalah it is the first and the last begins "ṣirāṭa alladhīna"; for those who do not, the first is "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn" and the last begins "ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim" (Baghawī). [baghawi¶13]
- `1:1.0` · athar · For counting it: Saʿīd ibn Jubayr recited al-Fātiḥah to Ibn Jurayj's father and counted "bismillāh…" as the seventh verse, saying he had recited it so to Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn ʿAbbās added "so He stored it up for you and brought it out to no one before you". Baghawī's chain runs through al-Shāfiʿī, ʿAbd al-Majīd and Ibn Jurayj; his note cites al-Shāfiʿī's Musnad and his own Sharḥ al-Sunnah. [baghawi¶13]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Against counting it: Anas ibn Mālik — "I stood behind Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and none of them recited 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm' when he opened the prayer" (Mālik in the Muwaṭṭaʾ; Muslim 399). [baghawi¶14]

- `1:1.0` · ruling · Maʿārif: the Muslims agree that the basmalah is a verse of the Qurʾān as part of al-Naml and that it is written at the head of every sūrah but al-Tawbah; they differ whether it is an integral part of al-Fātiḥah or of all sūrahs. [maarif¶2]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Abū Ḥanīfah: it is an integral part of no sūrah except al-Naml; it is an independent verse revealed to be placed at the beginning of each sūrah to separate and distinguish it from the next (Maʿārif). [maarif¶2]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · The study draft: all but one of the 114 sūrahs begin with it (it is also in 27:30), and there is a debate whether it is a verse of each sūrah or a sign that a new sūrah begins. [initial¶1]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq, in the study draft: the basmalah is "the greatest verse in the Book of God"; on this Shiite scholars all hold it a verse of the Fātiḥah and of every sūrah that begins with it (Ṭb, Ṭs); others say it is a verse, though separate from the sūrahs it begins (IK). [initial¶1]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Ibn Kathīr: the scholars agree it is part of a verse in al-Naml; they differ whether it is a separate verse before every sūrah, or a verse or part of a verse inside each sūrah that opens with it; similar statements are attributed to ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās and others, and al-Dāraquṭnī has a hadith from Abū Hurayrah supporting the one in Ibn Khuzaymah. [kathir¶1.34]
- `1:1.0` · history · That it is a verse of every sūrah but al-Barāʾah: attributed to the Companions Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn al-Zubayr, Abū Hurayrah and ʿAlī; the Tābiʿūn ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Makḥūl and al-Zuhrī; and ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak, al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad (in one report), Isḥāq ibn Rāhwayh and Abū ʿUbayd al-Qāsim ibn Sallām. Mālik, Abū Ḥanīfah and their followers: not a verse in al-Fātiḥah or any sūrah. Dāwūd: a separate verse at the start of every sūrah, not part of it — also attributed to Aḥmad (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.34]
- `1:1.0` · athar · Qurṭubī, fourth issue: Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq is reported to have said "the basmalah is the crowns of the sūrahs" — Qurṭubī: this shows it is not a verse of al-Fātiḥah or of any other sūrah. [qurtubi¶5.3]
- `1:1.0` · ruling · Qurṭubī states the views: first, not a verse of al-Fātiḥah or any other sūrah — Mālik; second, a verse of every sūrah — ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak; third, al-Shāfiʿī: a verse in al-Fātiḥah, wavering over the other sūrahs — once "a verse of each", once "a verse only in al-Fātiḥah" — and none disputes that it is a verse of the Qurʾān in al-Naml. [qurtubi¶5.3-7.1]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Al-Shāfiʿī's proof, al-Dāraquṭnī's report through Abū Bakr al-Ḥanafī, ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd ibn Jaʿfar, Nūḥ ibn Abī Bilāl and Saʿīd al-Maqburī from Abū Hurayrah: "When you recite 'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn' recite 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm'; it is Umm al-Qurʾān, Umm al-Kitāb and the seven oft-repeated, and 'bismillāh…' is one of its verses." The editor notes the manuscripts garble it and ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd's kunyah is Abū l-Faḍl or Abū Ḥafṣ, not Abū Bakr, who narrates from him. [qurtubi¶7.1]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd ibn Jaʿfar raised this hadith to the Prophet; Aḥmad, Yaḥyā ibn Saʿīd and Yaḥyā ibn Maʿīn declared him trustworthy, Abū Ḥātim said "his place is truthfulness", and al-Thawrī used to put him down and attack him; Nūḥ ibn Abī Bilāl is trustworthy and well known (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶7.2]
- `1:1.0` · hadith · Ibn al-Mubārak's proof, one with al-Shāfiʿī's — Muslim, Anas: the Prophet dozed among them, raised his head smiling and said "A sūrah has just come down to me", and recited "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm: Innā aʿṭaynāka l-Kawthar…" (given in full at Sūrat al-Kawthar). [qurtubi¶7.2]
