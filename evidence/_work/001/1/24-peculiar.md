### WHETHER THE FORMULA BELONGS TO THIS COMMUNITY ALONE

- `1:1.0` · language · Maʿārif: al-Suyūṭī says all the divine books begin with the basmalah; other scholars hold that "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm" is peculiar to the Qurʾān and Muḥammad's followers. The two agree if all books share beginning with God's name while these exact words are the Qurʾān's: reports say the Prophet used to say "bismika Allāhumma" until the verse came down and then adopted these words, and the practice was fixed by his command, act or approval (Qurṭubī, Rūḥ al-Maʿānī). [maarif¶5]

- `1:1.0` · history · Alūsī: scholars differed whether it is peculiar to this community; Abū Bakr al-Tūnisī reported the consensus of the scholars of every religion that God opened every book with it, and al-Suyūṭī (through al-Sirminī, "the responsibility is his") narrated that "bismillāh al-raḥmān al-raḥīm" opens every book. [alusi¶2.1]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · That narrator held it peculiar to this community because the Prophet wrote "bismika Allāhumma" until 11:41 came, then "bismillāh", then, after 17:110, "bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm", then the verse of al-Naml; and because it is said the meanings of the books are in the Qurʾān, its meanings in al-Fātiḥah, its in the basmalah and the basmalah's in the bāʾ — so were it in the earlier books it would have been ordered written first and the Qurʾān's meanings would be in every book (Alūsī). [alusi¶2.1]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Alūsī's reply: ordering it in stages does not imply it was absent — it may only show knowledge of it was absent then; what is peculiar to the Qurʾān is the Arabic wording in this order, and the heavenly books are not Arabic (against al-Ghayṭī), what the Qurʾān holds of them being translated; perhaps these very words give the comprehension of all meanings, so they are not in other books even if a basmalah is; and the first proof, on its face, shows it is not peculiar. [alusi¶2.1-2.2]
