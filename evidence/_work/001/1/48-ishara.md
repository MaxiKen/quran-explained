### ALLUSIVE READINGS OF THE BĀʾ AND ITS DOT

- `1:1.0` · lesson · Some say, by allusion: the bāʾ takes a kasrah to teach approach to God and attachment to His names by brokenness, submission and servitude's abasement — no kind of knowledge is reached but by a kind of lowliness; as Ibn al-Fāriḍ hints, "If you were for me a lowering from the dot of the bāʾ, you would be raised to what you never reached by any trick". Lowering is the opposite of raising: whom the sight of servitude's lowliness lowers, destiny raises to witness the might of lordship — a raising gained by pure divine gift, not by trick; whoever descends in order to rise has a flawed descent and an unaccepted effort. [alusi¶38-40]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · That is specific to the basmalah's bāʾ and cannot hold for the genitive bāʾ in general. Alūsī's own secret: the bāʾ is the second rank after the simple bare alif that precedes all ranks — the alif alluding to True Existence, the bāʾ to His attributes, which the dot of being manifested; hence al-Shiblī, asked "Are you al-Shiblī?", said "I am the dot beneath the bāʾ", and Ibn ʿArabī's verses say the bāʾ is considered by the gnostic al-Shiblī and in its little dot is a reminder for the heart. [alusi¶41-43]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · The attributes are of beauty or of majesty, and beauty comes first — "My mercy outstripped My anger" — and the genitive bāʾ alludes to it, being the go-between in annexation and outpouring, so kasrah and lowering the wing suit it; starting with it hastens the glad news and hints that the pivot is mercy, as the Prophet said none enters Paradise by his deed, "not even I, unless God wraps me in His mercy"; God was gradual in showing it — hinting with the bāʾ, pointing with "Allāh", stating it fully with "al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm". [alusi¶44]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · Or the bāʾ alludes to the Muḥammadan reality, the first determination — "the first thing God created was the light of your Prophet, O Jābir" — through which the outpouring came, "were it not for you I would not have created the spheres"; mercy being his dominant attribute, especially toward the believers (21:107, 9:128), kasrah fits what points to his rank; starting with it hints at the attribute of him to whom the Book came, the caller to God, and with the one called to described as al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm it gives complete longing and great encouragement; the Qurʾān describes him step by step until "you are of a great character" (68:4), the hint sufficing here since the traces had not yet appeared — "the first of rain is a drop, then it pours". [alusi¶44]
- `1:1.0` · lesson · No sūrah but the Lord opened it with a hint at his state, honouring him and giving good news to whoever listens and witnesses; since majesty is plain in Sūrat Barāʾah, He left the basmalah's hint and used a fatḥah-voweled bāʾ for the change of state and the lowering of the veil over beauty's brides, yet without leaving the hint to the Muḥammadan reality wholly; Alūsī will say no more here, for fear of the veiled — "behind it is a great secret". [alusi¶44]
