### MĀLIK OR MALIK: THE TWO READINGS

- `1:4.0` · language · Baghawī: ʿĀṣim, al-Kisāʾī and Yaʿqūb read "mālik"; the others "malik". [baghawi¶20]
- `1:4.0` · language · Some say the two are one in meaning, like farihīn/fārihīn and ḥadhirīn/ḥādhirīn — the Lord, as in "rabb of the house and its owner"; or the one able to bring things from non-being into being, which none can but God. [baghawi¶20]
- `1:4.0` · language · Abū ʿUbaydah prefers mālik as wider and more comprehensive: one is mālik of a slave, a bird, the beasts, but not malik of them; and one is never mālik of a thing without owning it, while he may be malik of what he does not own. [baghawi¶20]
- `1:4.0` · language · Others prefer malik: every king is an owner, not every owner a king; and it agrees better with the rest of the Qurʾān — "exalted is God, the True King" (20:114), "the Holy King" (59:23). [baghawi¶20]
