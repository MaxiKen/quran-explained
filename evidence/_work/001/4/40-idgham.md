### ABŪ ʿAMR AND THE MERGED MĪM

- `1:4.0` · language · Abū ʿAmr reads "al-raḥīmi mālik" merging the mīm into the mīm, as he merges any two letters of one kind or one point of articulation, voweled or silent, with set exceptions (a doubled, nunated or deficient first letter, a fatḥah-voweled one, a tā' of address after a silent letter); Ḥamzah agrees with him in some merged words ("bayyata ṭāʾifah", "wa-l-ṣāffāti ṣaffan…"), al-Kisāʾī and Ḥamzah in the "small" merging of a silent letter into a voweled one, with their own exceptions (Baghawī).
