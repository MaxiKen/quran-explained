### ḤAMD, SHUKR AND THE LĀM OF "LI-LLĀH"

- `1:2.0` · language · Baghawī: "al-ḥamdu li-llāh" is worded as a report — that the one who deserves praise is God — and teaches creation to say it ("say: al-ḥamdu li-llāh"). [baghawi¶16]
- `1:2.0` · language · Ḥamd is thanks for a favour or praise for good qualities — "I praised so-and-so for what he did for me" and "for his knowledge and courage"; shukr is only for a favour; so ḥamd is wider: every ḥāmid is a shākir, not every shākir a ḥāmid. [baghawi¶16]
- `1:2.0` · language · Or ḥamd is by the tongue, in words, and shukr by the limbs, in deeds — "say: praise be to God, who has taken no son" (17:111); "work, O family of Dāwūd, in gratitude" (34:13). [baghawi¶16]
- `1:2.0` · language · The lām of "li-llāh" is for entitlement (istiḥqāq), as in "the house is Zayd's". [baghawi¶17]
