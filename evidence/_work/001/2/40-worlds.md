### WHO THE WORLDS ARE

- `1:2.0` · language · Baghawī: al-ʿālamīn is the plural of ʿālam, which has no singular of its own wording; it comes from ʿilm or ʿalāmah, "sign" — they are so named for the visible trace of craftsmanship in them. [baghawi¶19]
- `1:2.0` · athar · Ibn ʿAbbās: jinn and humans, because they are the ones addressed — "that he may be a warner to the worlds" (25:1). [baghawi¶19]
- `1:2.0` · athar · Qatādah, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan: all created things — as in Pharaoh's "And what is the Lord of the worlds?" answered "Lord of the heavens and the earth and what is between them" (26:23-24). [baghawi¶19]
- `1:2.0` · athar · Abū ʿUbaydah: four nations — angels, humans, jinn and devils; animals are not called ʿālam, having no intellect. [baghawi¶19]
- `1:2.0` · athar · Their number: Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab, a thousand worlds, 600 in the sea and 400 on land; Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, 80,000, half in each; Wahb, 18,000 of which this world is one, its inhabited part among the ruin like a tent in a desert; Kaʿb al-Aḥbār: none counts them but God (74:31). [baghawi¶19]
