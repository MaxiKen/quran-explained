### WHAT THE DIVIDED-PRAYER REPORT MEANS

- `1:0.0` · hadith · Ibn Kathīr: in al-Nasāʾī's wording of the ḥadīth qudsī the replies are "praised Me", "extolled Me" and, at "mālik yawm al-dīn", "glorified Me" or "entrusted all matters to Me", then "between Me and My servant", then "for My servant"; Muslim and al-Nasāʾī both have "half of it is for Me and half for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked". [kathir¶1.12-1.13]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Ibn Kathīr: the report uses "prayer" for reciting the Qurʾān — here al-Fātiḥah — as in "do not be loud in your prayer nor silent in it" (17:110), meaning your recitation, as the Ṣaḥīḥ relates from Ibn ʿAbbās; the division shows how important reciting the Qurʾān is in the prayer, one of its greatest pillars, so "prayer" is used though only a part, the recitation, is meant. [kathir¶1.14]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Likewise "recitation" is used where the prayer is meant — "and the dawn recitation of the Qurʾān; the dawn recitation is witnessed" (17:78) means the Fajr prayer, which the angels of the night and the day attend, as the Two Ṣaḥīḥs record. [kathir¶1.14-1.15]
