### MUST AL-FĀTIḤAH BE READ IN EVERY RAKʿAH

- `1:0.0` · ruling · Qurṭubī, fifth issue: Mālik and his colleagues say it is required of the imam and the one praying alone in every rakʿah; Ibn Khuwayzmindād: Mālik never varied that one who forgot it in a rakʿah of a two-rakʿah prayer has no valid prayer. [qurtubi¶60.2]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · His view varied for one who forgot it in a rakʿah of a four- or three-rakʿah prayer: sometimes he repeats the prayer, sometimes he prostrates for forgetfulness (Ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥakam's report from Mālik); Ibn Khuwayzmindād: or he repeats that rakʿah and prostrates after the salām; Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: the sound saying is to cancel that rakʿah and bring another in its place, like one who dropped a prostration by mistake — Ibn al-Qāsim's choice. [qurtubi¶60.3]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, most Baṣrans and al-Mughīrah ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Makhzūmī: if he reads Umm al-Qurʾān once in the prayer, it suffices and he need not repeat, since it is a prayer in which he read it — "no prayer for one who does not read Umm al-Qurʾān" — and this one did. Qurṭubī: it may also mean "in every rakʿah", which is the sound view, or "in most rakʿahs" — which is the cause of the disagreement. [qurtubi¶60.3-60.4]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Abū Ḥanīfah, al-Thawrī and al-Awzāʿī: if he leaves it deliberately through the whole prayer and reads something else, it suffices (al-Awzāʿī's reports differ); Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan: the least is three verses or one long verse like the verse on debt; Muḥammad: he allows ijtihād in the size of a verse and of a meaningful word like "al-ḥamdu li-llāh", not in a letter that is not speech. [qurtubi¶60.4]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Al-Ṭabarī: he reads Umm al-Qurʾān in every rakʿah, and if he does not, nothing suffices but the like of it from the Qurʾān in number of verses and letters; Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: this has no sense, since the text singles it out for the ruling, and it is impossible that one bound to it should leave it, able to read it, and bring a substitute — he must bring it, as with all fixed obligations of worship. [qurtubi¶60.4]

- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī, eleventh issue, Ibn al-ʿArabī: when the Prophet said "no prayer for one who does not read Fātiḥat al-Kitāb", people differed whether the negation means completeness and perfection or validity, and rulings differed with the examiner's position; since the more famous and stronger basis is that the negation is general, the stronger of Mālik's reports is that one who does not read al-Fātiḥah has no valid prayer; and one who takes "do that in all your prayer" to cover every rakʿah must repeat the reading as he repeats bowing and prostration. [qurtubi¶63.1]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Twelfth: what he has given refutes the Kūfans' saying that al-Fātiḥah is not singled out and is like any other verses — the Prophet singled it out, he being the one who makes clear God's intent in "establish the prayer". [qurtubi¶63.1]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Abū Dāwūd, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: "We were ordered to read Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and what is easy", showing that the Prophet's "read what is easy for you of the Qurʾān" to the Bedouin means what is beyond al-Fātiḥah, the explanation of "read what is easy of it" (73:20); Muslim, ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit: "No prayer for one who does not read Umm al-Qurʾān" — in one report adding "and more". [qurtubi¶63.2]
- `1:0.0` · language · "It is khidāj, three times, incomplete" means not sufficient, by the evidence cited; khidāj is deficiency and invalidity — al-Akhfash: khadajat al-nāqah when she casts her young not complete, and akhdajat when she casts it before its time though formed fully; reasoning requires that with the deficiency the prayer is not valid, since it is a prayer not completed, and one who leaves it incomplete must repeat it as commanded (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶63.2]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Whoever claims it is valid while admitting its deficiency owes proof (Qurṭubī). Thirteenth: it is reported from Mālik that reading is not obligatory in any prayer, and al-Shāfiʿī said so in Iraq of one who forgot it, then went back on it in Egypt: the prayer of one who can read al-Fātiḥah is not valid without it, nor with a letter short — if he did not read it, or dropped a letter, he repeats his prayer even if he read something else. [qurtubi¶63.3]
- `1:0.0` · athar · Qurṭubī calls that the sound view, and as for what is reported of ʿUmar — that he prayed al-Maghrib without reading, was told, asked "how were the bowing and prostration?", was told "good" and said "then no harm" — it is a hadith of objectionable wording and broken chain, narrated by Ibrāhīm ibn al-Ḥārith al-Taymī from ʿUmar and once through Abū Salamah; Mālik had it in the Muwaṭṭaʾ (in some narrators, not Yaḥyā's) and threw it out of his book late, saying it is not what is practised, since the Prophet said "every prayer in which Umm al-Qurʾān is not read is khidāj". [qurtubi¶63.4]
- `1:0.0` · athar · The sound report is that ʿUmar repeated that prayer — Yaḥyā ibn Yaḥyā al-Naysābūrī, through Abū Muʿāwiyah, al-Aʿmash, Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī and Hammām ibn al-Ḥārith: ʿUmar forgot the reading in al-Maghrib and repeated the prayer with them; Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: a connected report which Hammām witnessed, narrated in several ways; Ashhab, from Mālik, asked about one who forgot the reading: "I deny that ʿUmar did it … people see ʿUmar do this in al-Maghrib and no one says tasbīḥ to him! I think he who does this should repeat the prayer." [qurtubi¶63.4-63.5]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Fourteenth: the scholars agree there is no prayer without reading, and that nothing is fixed beyond al-Fātiḥah, though they like only one sūrah with it, as most reported of the Prophet; Mālik: the Sunnah is Umm al-Qurʾān and a sūrah in the first two rakʿahs and al-Fātiḥah in the last two; al-Awzāʿī: if he reads another instead of al-Fātiḥah it suffices, but if he forgets to read in three rakʿahs he repeats. [qurtubi¶63.5]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Al-Thawrī: al-Fātiḥah and a sūrah in the first two; in the last two he may glorify or read, and if he does neither the prayer is valid — also Abū Ḥanīfah and the Kūfans; Ibn al-Mundhir: from ʿAlī, "read in the first two and glorify in the last two", and so al-Nakhaʿī; Sufyān: if he does not read in three rakʿahs he repeats, as one rakʿah's reading does not suffice, and so for forgetting in a dawn rakʿah. [qurtubi¶63.5-64.1]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Abū Thawr, like the Egyptian al-Shāfiʿī and the Shāfiʿīs: no prayer suffices without reading al-Fātiḥah in every rakʿah; Ibn Khuwayzmindād the Mālikī: reading it is obligatory with us in every rakʿah — "and this is the sound view". [qurtubi¶64.1]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Muslim, Abū Qatādah: the Prophet led them, reading in Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr in the first two rakʿahs Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and two sūrahs, letting them hear a verse now and then, lengthening the first rakʿah of Ẓuhr and shortening the second, and so in Ṣubḥ; in one report "and he reads in the last two rakʿahs Fātiḥat al-Kitāb" — Qurṭubī: an explicit text and sound hadith for Mālik's view that it is required in every rakʿah, against whoever refuses; the proof is in the Sunnah, not in what opposes it. [qurtubi¶64.1-64.2]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Fifteenth: the majority hold that reading beyond al-Fātiḥah is not obligatory — Muslim, Abū Hurayrah: "In every prayer there is reading; what the Prophet let us hear we let you hear and what he hid we hid; whoever reads Umm al-Qurʾān, it suffices him, and whoever adds, it is better" — and in al-Bukhārī, "if you add, it is good". [qurtubi¶64.2]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Many scholars refused to leave the sūrah, in need or not — ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, Khawwāt ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, Abū Wāʾil, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās and others: no prayer for one who does not read in it Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and something with it; some set two verses, some one, some no limit; all requiring that one learn what is easy of the Qurʾān with al-Fātiḥah, by ʿUbādah's and Abū Saʿīd's hadiths; al-Mudawwanah has, from ʿUmar through one who heard him, "a prayer in which one does not read Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and something with it is not valid"; Mālik's school has three views on the sūrah — sunnah, virtue, obligatory. [qurtubi¶64.3]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Sixteenth: one who, after his utmost effort, cannot learn al-Fātiḥah or anything of the Qurʾān must remember God in the place of reading — takbīr, tahlīl, taḥmīd, tasbīḥ, tamjīd or "lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwata illā bi-llāh" — praying alone or, behind an imam, in what the imam reads silently. [qurtubi¶65]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Abū Dāwūd and others, ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Awfā: a man told the Prophet "I cannot take anything of the Qurʾān; teach me what will suffice"; he said, "Say: subḥāna llāh, wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāh, wa-lā ilāha illā llāh, wa-llāhu akbar, wa-lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwata illā bi-llāh"; the man said "That is for God — what is for me?" — "Say: O God, have mercy on me, give me well-being, guide me and provide for me." [qurtubi¶65]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Seventeenth: if he cannot even manage these words he must not give up the prayer with the imam as far as he can, the imam bearing it for him, God willing, and he must always strive to learn Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and more until death comes while he is striving and God excuses him. Eighteenth: a non-Arab who cannot speak Arabic has the Arabic supplication translated into the language he understands for his prayer, which suffices, God willing. [qurtubi¶65]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Nineteenth: the prayer of one who reads in Persian though he is good at Arabic is not valid, in the majority's view; Abū Ḥanīfah: reading in Persian suffices even if he is good at Arabic, the aim being the meaning; Ibn al-Mundhir: it does not suffice, as against what God commanded, what the Prophet taught and the Muslim communities — "and we know of no one who agrees with him". [qurtubi¶65]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Twentieth: one who began the prayer as commanded not knowing the reading, and then knew it during the prayer — as when he heard someone read it and it stuck in his memory — does not restart, since what passed was done as commanded and there is no ground to void it (said in Ibn Saḥnūn's book). [qurtubi¶65-66]
