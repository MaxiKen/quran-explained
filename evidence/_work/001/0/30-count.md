### HOW MANY VERSES, WORDS AND LETTERS

- `1:0.0` · ruling · Ibn Kathīr: there is no disagreement that al-Fātiḥah has seven verses; according to most Kūfan reciters, a group of the Companions, the Tābiʿūn and a number of later scholars, the basmalah is a separate verse at its beginning — he promises to return to this. [kathir¶1.3]
- `1:0.0` · history · The scholars say al-Fātiḥah has twenty-five words and one hundred and thirteen letters (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.3]

- `1:0.0` · ruling · Qurṭubī, first issue of the chapter on revelation and rulings: the Ummah agree al-Fātiḥah is seven verses, except what is reported from Ḥusayn al-Juʿfī — six — and from ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd, who counted "iyyāka naʿbudu" a verse for eight; both are anomalous, and 15:87 and "I divided the prayer" refute them; and the Ummah agree it is Qurʾān. [qurtubi¶59.1]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · The objection that were it Qurʾān Ibn Masʿūd would have put it in his codex, as he did not the two refuge sūrahs — the answer (Abū Bakr al-Anbārī, with his chain to Ibrāhīm): asked why he did not write Fātiḥat al-Kitāb in his codex, Ibn Masʿūd said, "Had I written it I would have written it with every sūrah"; Abū Bakr: he meant every rakʿah is to open with Umm al-Qurʾān before the sūrah that follows, so he left it out to be brief, trusting the Muslims' memory of it, and placed it nowhere lest he have to write it with every sūrah. [qurtubi¶59.1-59.2]
