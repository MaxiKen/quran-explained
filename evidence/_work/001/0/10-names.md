### THE NAMES OF THE SŪRAH

- `1:0.0` · language · Baghawī: it has three well-known names — Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, Umm al-Qurʾān and al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī. [baghawi¶2]
- `1:0.0` · language · Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, "the Opener of the Book", because God opened the Qurʾān with it (Baghawī). [baghawi¶3]
- `1:0.0` · language · Umm al-Qurʾān and Umm al-Kitāb, because it is the root of the Qurʾān, which began from it; the "umm" of a thing is its root — as Makkah is Umm al-Qurā, the root of the lands, the earth being spread out from beneath it (Baghawī). [baghawi¶3]
- `1:0.0` · language · Or because it is the introduction and leader of the sūrahs after it: its writing begins the codex and its recitation begins the prayer (Baghawī). [baghawi¶3]
- `1:0.0` · language · Al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī, "the seven oft-repeated": seven verses by the agreement of the scholars, and "repeated" because it is said again in every rakʿah of the prayer (Baghawī). [baghawi¶3]
- `1:0.0` · athar · Mujāhid: it is called mathānī because God set it aside (istathnāhā) for this community and stored it up for them (Baghawī). [baghawi¶3]

- `1:0.0` · language · Ibn Kathīr: it is called al-Fātiḥah, "the Opener of the Book", the sūrah with which prayers are begun; and Umm al-Kitāb, "the Mother of the Book", according to the majority of the scholars. [kathir¶1.1]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Al-Tirmidhī, graded ṣaḥīḥ, from Abū Hurayrah: the Prophet said "al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn is Umm al-Qurʾān, Umm al-Kitāb, the seven oft-repeated and the great Qurʾān". [kathir¶1.1]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · It is also called al-Ḥamd and al-Ṣalāh, because the Prophet said his Lord said "I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant in two halves"; and al-Ṣalāh because reciting it is a condition for the prayer's validity (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.1-1.2]
- `1:0.0` · language · It is also called al-Shifāʾ, "the Cure", and al-Ruqyah, "the remedy", for the story, in the Ṣaḥīḥ, of the Companion who used it on a poisoned tribal chief (Ibn Kathīr; the story is told under its own head below). [kathir¶1.2]
- `1:0.0` · language · Why Umm al-Kitāb: al-Bukhārī, at the start of the Book of Tafsīr, "because the Qurʾān starts with it and the prayer is begun by reciting it"; it was also said because it holds the meanings of the whole Qurʾān. [kathir¶1.4]
- `1:0.0` · language · Ibn Jarīr: the Arabs call every comprehensive thing that holds several particular parts an "umm" — the skin around the brain is "umm al-raʾs", the flag that gathers the ranks of an army an "umm"; Makkah was called Umm al-Qurā because it is the grandest and the leader of the villages, and it was said that the earth was made starting from Makkah. [kathir¶1.4]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Aḥmad, from Abū Hurayrah: the Prophet said of Umm al-Qurʾān, "It is Umm al-Qurʾān, the seven oft-repeated and the great Qurʾān"; Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī has it as "Umm al-Qurʾān, Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, the seven oft-repeated". [kathir¶1.4-1.5]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī, fourth issue: it has twelve names — (1) al-Ṣalāh, "the Prayer", from the ḥadīth qudsī "I divided the prayer between Me and My servant"; (2) al-Ḥamd, because it mentions praise, as one says Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, al-Anfāl, al-Tawbah. [qurtubi¶39-40]
- `1:0.0` · language · (3) Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, "without disagreement among the scholars", because recitation of the Qurʾān is opened with it in speech, writing in the codex is opened with it in script, and the prayers are opened with it. [qurtubi¶41]
- `1:0.0` · athar · (4) Umm al-Kitāb is disputed: the majority allow it; Anas, al-Ḥasan and Ibn Sīrīn disliked it — al-Ḥasan: Umm al-Kitāb is the lawful and unlawful, "verses clear in meaning, they are the Mother of the Book" (3:7); Anas and Ibn Sīrīn: it is the name of the Preserved Tablet, "and it is in the Mother of the Book with Us" (43:4). [qurtubi¶42]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · (5) Umm al-Qurʾān is disputed likewise — the majority allow it, Anas and Ibn Sīrīn disliked it — and the sound hadiths refute both dislikes: al-Tirmidhī, Abū Hurayrah, "al-ḥamdu li-llāh is Umm al-Qurʾān, Umm al-Kitāb and the seven oft-repeated" — ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ; and al-Bukhārī says it was called Umm al-Kitāb because writing the codices begins with it and recitation in prayer begins with it. [qurtubi¶43]
- `1:0.0` · language · Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar: Umm al-Qurā is Makkah, Umm Khurāsān is Marw, Umm al-Qurʾān is Sūrat al-Ḥamd; it was said it is so called because it is the Qurʾān's first and holds all its sciences — as Makkah is Umm al-Qurā, being the first of the earth, from which it was spread, and a mother is "umm" as the root of offspring, and the earth is "umm" in Umayyah ibn Abī l-Ṣalt's line ("the earth was our refuge and our mother; in it are our graves and in it we are born"); and a war banner is an "umm", going first with the army following it. [qurtubi¶43-45]
- `1:0.0` · language · The origin of umm is ummah, hence the plural ummahāt ("your mothers", 4:23); ummāt without the hāʾ is also said, as in a line of verse; and it was said ummahāt is for people and ummāt for animals (Ibn Fāris, al-Mujmal). [qurtubi¶45-47]
- `1:0.0` · language · (6) al-Mathānī, because it is repeated in every rakʿah; or because it was set aside (istuthniyat) for this community and sent down to none before it, stored for it. [qurtubi¶48]
- `1:0.0` · language · (7) al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm, for holding all the sciences of the Qurʾān: praise of God with the attributes of His perfection and majesty; the command to acts of worship and sincerity in them and the admission of inability to do any without His help; the supplication for guidance to the straight path and for the sufficing of the states of the covenant-breakers; and the exposition of the end of the deniers. [qurtubi¶49]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · (8) al-Shifāʾ: al-Dārimī, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī — "Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is a cure from every poison"; the editor notes that al-Dārimī's Musnad has, from ʿAbd al-Malik ibn ʿUmayr, "in Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is a cure for every disease". [qurtubi¶50]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · (9) al-Ruqyah, established in Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī's hadith, where the Prophet said to the man who recited over the chief of the tribe "What made you know it is a ruqyah?" and he answered "O Messenger of God, something was cast into my heart"; the imams record it and it is given in full later. [qurtubi¶51]
- `1:0.0` · athar · (10) al-Asās, "the foundation": a man complained to al-Shaʿbī of a pain in the side, and he said "Take to the foundation of the Qurʾān, Fātiḥat al-Kitāb — I heard Ibn ʿAbbās say everything has a foundation: of the world, Makkah, since it was spread from it; of the heavens, ʿArībā, the seventh heaven; of the earth, ʿAjībā, the lowest seventh earth; of the gardens, Jannat ʿAdn, their navel, on which Paradise is founded; of the Fire, Jahannam, its lowest seventh level; of creation, Ādam; of the prophets, Nūḥ; of the Israelites, Yaʿqūb; of the books, the Qurʾān; of the Qurʾān, al-Fātiḥah; of al-Fātiḥah, 'bismillāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm' — so when you are ill, hold to al-Fātiḥah and you will be healed." [qurtubi¶52]
- `1:0.0` · language · (11) al-Wāfiyah, "the complete", said Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, because it cannot be halved or cut short — were one to read half of any other sūrah in one rakʿah and the other half in the next it would suffice, but were al-Fātiḥah halved over two rakʿahs it would not. [qurtubi¶53]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · (12) al-Kāfiyah, "the sufficient", said Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr, because it suffices in place of others and nothing else suffices in place of it, on the report from Muḥammad ibn Khallād al-Iskandarānī — the Prophet said "Umm al-Qurʾān is a substitute for the others and none of the others is a substitute for it" (no collection named). [qurtubi¶54]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī, sixth and seventh issues: calling it al-mathānī and Umm al-Kitāb does not stop others being so called — "a Book consistent, paired (mathānī)" (39:23), since reports are repeated in it; the seven long sūrahs are also called mathānī, since obligations and stories are repeated in them — Ibn ʿAbbās: the Prophet was given "seven of the mathānī", the seven long ones (al-Nasāʾī): six from al-Baqarah to al-Aʿrāf, the seventh disputed — Yūnus, or al-Anfāl with al-Tawbah (Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr); a line of Aʿshā Hamdān: "enter the mosque and call on your Lord and study these mathānī and the ṭuwal". [qurtubi¶55-56]
- `1:0.0` · language · Mathānī is the plural of mathnā, the one that comes after the first; ṭuwal the plural of aṭwal; al-Anfāl is counted among the mathānī because it follows the ṭuwal in length; or they are the sūrahs whose verses exceed the mufaṣṣal and fall short of the miʾīn, the miʾūn being those with more than a hundred verses each (Qurṭubī; he will say more at Sūrat al-Ḥijr). [qurtubi¶57]
