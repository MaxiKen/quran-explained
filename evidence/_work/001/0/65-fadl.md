### IS ONE SŪRAH OR VERSE BETTER THAN ANOTHER

- `1:0.0` · lesson · Some scholars took this hadith as evidence that some verses and sūrahs are more excellent than others (Ibn Kathīr). [kathir¶1.9]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī, second issue: the scholars differed on preferring some sūrahs and verses over others, and some of God's beautiful names over others; some say none is better, since all is God's speech, and so are His names — the view of al-Ashʿarī, al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr ibn al-Ṭayyib, Abū Ḥātim ibn Ḥibbān al-Bustī and a group of jurists, reported in sense from Mālik; Yaḥyā ibn Yaḥyā: preferring some of the Qurʾān over other is an error; so Mālik disliked repeating a sūrah, or reciting it again and again, to the exclusion of others. [qurtubi¶38.4]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · From Mālik on "We bring one better than it or like it" (2:106): "a clear one in place of an abrogated one" — Ibn Kinānah has all this from Mālik; their argument: "better" implies a deficiency in the lesser, and in essence all is one, God's speech, in which there is no deficiency. [qurtubi¶38.5]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Al-Bustī: the sense of "nothing like Umm al-Qurʾān in the Torah or the Gospel" is that God does not give the reader of the Torah and Gospel the reward He gives the reader of Umm al-Qurʾān, since by His favour He preferred this community over others and gave it more reward for reading His speech; "the greatest sūrah" means in reward, not that part of the Qurʾān is better than another. [qurtubi¶38.5]

- `1:0.0` · lesson · The other side (Qurṭubī): some hold preference is real — what 2:163 ("your God is one God; there is no god but He, al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm"), Āyat al-Kursī, the end of al-Ḥashr and Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ hold of proofs of His oneness and attributes is not found in "Perish the hands of Abū Lahab" (111:1) and its like; but the preference lies in wondrous meanings and their abundance, not in the attribute (the speech being God's) — "and this is the truth". [qurtubi¶38.6]
- `1:0.0` · history · Among those who prefer: Isḥāq ibn Rāhwayh and other scholars and theologians; it is the choice of al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr ibn al-ʿArabī and Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār, on the hadith of Abū Saʿīd ibn al-Muʿallā and that of Ubayy, to whom the Prophet said "Which verse with you in God's Book is greatest?" and he said "Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa l-ḥayyu l-qayyūm" (2:255). [qurtubi¶38.6]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · The Prophet struck Ubayy's chest and said, "May knowledge be pleasant to you, Abū l-Mundhir" — al-Bukhārī and Muslim; Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār: "I wonder at one who mentions disagreement with these texts." [qurtubi¶38.7]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Ibn al-ʿArabī: "God did not reveal in the Torah, the Gospel or the Qurʾān its like" is silent about the other books, the scrolls and the Psalms, because these named are the best of them, and what is best of the best is best of all — as "Zayd is the best of the scholars" makes him best of people; in al-Fātiḥah are attributes found in no other sūrah, so it was said the whole Qurʾān is in it — it is twenty-five words holding all the sciences of the Qurʾān. [qurtubi¶38.7]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī: of its honour is that God divided it between Himself and His servant, no act of nearness is valid without it and no deed reaches its reward; so it became "the Mother of the great Qurʾān", as "Say: He is God, One" equals a third of the Qurʾān, the Qurʾān being tawḥīd, rulings and admonition and al-Ikhlāṣ holding all of tawḥīd — and by this sense the Prophet's words to Ubayy are explained. [qurtubi¶38.8-39]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · It was the greatest verse because it is all tawḥīd, as "the best of what I and the prophets before me have said: there is no god but God alone, without partner" became the best dhikr, being words that hold all knowledge of tawḥīd; al-Fātiḥah gathered tawḥīd, worship, admonition and reminder — not far from God's power (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶39]
