### THE FOLLOWER'S RECITATION BEHIND THE IMAM

- `1:0.0` · ruling · Qurṭubī, sixth to eighth issues: if the follower catches the imam bowing, the imam bears his recitation — they agree he says the takbīr, bows and reads nothing; if he catches him standing he reads. No one should leave reciting behind the imam in a silent prayer; if he does he has done wrong, but nothing is due on him, per Mālik and his colleagues. [qurtubi¶60.5]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · When the imam recites aloud, the well-known view of Mālik is that there is no reading of al-Fātiḥah or anything else, by "when the Qurʾān is recited, listen and be silent" (7:204), "Why am I contended with in the Qurʾān?", "when he recites, be silent" and "whoever has an imam, the imam's recitation is his recitation". [qurtubi¶60.5]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Al-Shāfiʿī (al-Buwayṭī's report) and Aḥmad: no one's prayer suffices until he reads Fātiḥat al-Kitāb in every rakʿah, imam or follower, whether the imam recites aloud or silently; in Iraq al-Shāfiʿī said the follower reads when the imam is silent and not when he is aloud, like Mālik's well-known view; in Egypt he gave two sayings for the loud prayers — read, or be satisfied with the imam's recitation (Ibn al-Mundhir). [qurtubi¶61.1]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Ibn Wahb, Ashhab, Ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥakam, Ibn Ḥabīb and the Kūfans: the follower reads nothing, aloud or silent, by "the imam's recitation is his recitation", which is general, and Jābir's "whoever prays a rakʿah without reading Umm al-Qurʾān has not prayed, unless behind an imam". [qurtubi¶61.1]
- `1:0.0` · ruling · Qurṭubī, ninth issue: the sound one is al-Shāfiʿī's and Aḥmad's, and Mālik's other saying — al-Fātiḥah is required in every rakʿah of everyone — by "no prayer for one who does not read in it Fātiḥat al-Kitāb" and "whoever prays a prayer without Umm al-Qurʾān, it is khidāj" (three times); Abū Hurayrah: the Messenger told me to proclaim "no prayer except with reading Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and more" (Abū Dāwūd); as one rakʿah's bowing or prostration cannot stand for another's, so one rakʿah's reading cannot stand for another's. [qurtubi¶61.2]
- `1:0.0` · history · So held ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAwn, Ayyūb al-Sakhtiyānī, Abū Thawr and other Shāfiʿīs, Dāwūd ibn ʿAlī, Makḥūl, and it is reported from al-Awzāʿī; reported also that ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū Hurayrah, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī, ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, ʿUthmān ibn Abī l-ʿĀṣ and Khawwāt ibn Jubayr said "no prayer except with Fātiḥat al-Kitāb"; so too Ibn ʿUmar and al-Awzāʿī's well-known view — "these Companions are the models" — all requiring it in every rakʿah. [qurtubi¶61.2-61.3]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Ibn Mājah's Sunan has what removes the disagreement (Abū Kurayb, Muḥammad ibn Fuḍayl; Suwayd ibn Saʿīd, ʿAlī ibn Mushir; Abū Sufyān al-Saʿdī; Abū Naḍrah; Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī): "There is no prayer for one who does not read in every rakʿah 'al-ḥamdu li-llāh' and a sūrah, in an obligatory prayer or other"; Muslim, Abū Hurayrah: the Prophet to the man he taught prayer, "and do that in all your prayer". [qurtubi¶61.3-62.1]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · Abū Dāwūd, Nāfiʿ ibn Maḥmūd ibn al-Rabīʿ al-Anṣārī: ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit was late for the dawn prayer and Abū Nuʿaym the muʾadhdhin led; ʿUbādah arrived and stood behind him while Abū Nuʿaym recited aloud, and read Umm al-Qurʾān; asked about it he said "Yes — the Messenger led us in a loud prayer and it was confused for him; when he finished he turned to us: 'Do you read when I recite aloud?' Some said 'we do'; he said 'Do not — I say, why am I contended with in the Qurʾān? So read nothing of the Qurʾān when I recite aloud except Umm al-Qurʾān'" — "an explicit text about the follower" (Qurṭubī). [qurtubi¶62.1-62.2]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Al-Tirmidhī records it from Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq in the same sense and says ḥasan; the practice is on it for recitation behind the imam among most of the Companions and Tābiʿūn — Mālik ibn Anas, Ibn al-Mubārak, al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad and Isḥāq; al-Dāraquṭnī: a good chain with trustworthy men; Maḥmūd ibn al-Rabīʿ lived at Īliyāʾ (Jerusalem) and Abū Nuʿaym was the first to call the adhān in Bayt al-Maqdis. [qurtubi¶62.2]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Against its chain: ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq says al-Bukhārī does not mention Nāfiʿ ibn Maḥmūd in his Tārīkh nor does Ibn Abī Ḥātim, and al-Bukhārī and Muslim record nothing from him; Abū ʿUmar (Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr) calls him unknown. [qurtubi¶62.3]
- `1:0.0` · athar · Al-Dāraquṭnī, Yazīd ibn Sharīk: "I asked ʿUmar about reading behind the imam and he told me to read; I said even if you are the imam? — even if I am; even if you recite aloud? — even if I recite aloud" — al-Dāraquṭnī: a sound chain. [qurtubi¶62.3]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · From Jābir, the Prophet: "The imam is a guarantor, so do as he does" — Abū Ḥātim: this is sound for one who holds reading behind the imam, and so Abū Hurayrah the Persian ruled that he read it to himself, then cited the ḥadīth qudsī of the divided prayer. [qurtubi¶62.3]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī, tenth issue: the first group's "and when he recites, be silent" (Muslim, from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī, in Jarīr's version from Sulaymān from Qatādah) — al-Dāraquṭnī: Sulaymān al-Taymī was not followed in this wording from Qatādah, and the memorisers among Qatādah's pupils — Shuʿbah, Hishām, Saʿīd ibn Abī ʿArūbah, Hammām, Abū ʿAwānah, Maʿmar, ʿAdī ibn Abī ʿUmārah — did not give it, their agreement showing his slip; a follow-up from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿĀmir from Qatādah is not strong (al-Qaṭṭān left him); Abū Dāwūd also records the addition from Abū Hurayrah and says it is not preserved; ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq: Muslim graded Abū Hurayrah's hadith, "it is sound to me". [qurtubi¶62.4]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · Qurṭubī: that Muslim put it in his book from Abū Mūsā's hadith, though not agreed upon, shows he held it sound; Aḥmad and Ibn al-Mundhir graded it sound; as for 7:204, it came down at Makkah and the ban on speech in prayer at Madīnah (Zayd ibn Arqam), so it is no proof — it was aimed at the polytheists (Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab); al-Dāraquṭnī reports from Abū Hurayrah that it came down about raising the voice behind the Messenger in prayer, and calls ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿĀmir weak. [qurtubi¶62.5]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · "Why am I contended with in the Qurʾān?" — Mālik records it from Ibn Shihāb from Ibn Ukaymah al-Laythī (his name ʿAmr per Mālik, others ʿĀmir, Yazīd, ʿUmārah or ʿAbbād; kunyah Abū l-Walīd; died 101 aged seventy-nine; al-Zuhrī narrates only this one report from him; trustworthy); its sense: do not recite aloud when I recite aloud, for that is contending, pulling and confusion — "read within yourselves", as ʿUbādah's hadith, ʿUmar's ruling and Abū Hurayrah's, who narrated both, show; had the prohibition been understood wholly he would not have ruled otherwise; and al-Zuhrī's "so the people stopped reciting with the Messenger in the loud prayers" means al-Ḥamd, as explained. [qurtubi¶62.6]
- `1:0.0` · hadith · "Whoever has an imam, the imam's recitation is his recitation" is a weak hadith — raised by al-Ḥasan ibn ʿUmārah (abandoned) and Abū Ḥanīfah (weak, says Qurṭubī; his editor notes Ibn Ḥajar and Ibn Khallikān record no weakness in hadith for him, only Ibn Saʿd does), both from Mūsā ibn Abī ʿĀʾishah from ʿAbdullāh ibn Shaddād from Jābir; al-Dāraquṭnī: Sufyān al-Thawrī, Shuʿbah, Isrāʾīl, Sharīk, Abū Khālid al-Dālānī, Abū l-Aḥwaṣ, Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, Jarīr and others have it mursal from ʿAbdullāh ibn Shaddād from the Prophet, which is correct. [qurtubi¶62.7]
- `1:0.0` · athar · Jābir's "whoever prays a rakʿah without reading Umm al-Qurʾān has not prayed, unless behind an imam": Mālik gives it as Jābir's own saying, through Wahb ibn Kaysān; Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, the tafsīr author, gave it from Mālik through Wahb from Jābir from the Prophet, but the correct form is Jābir's own, as in the Muwaṭṭaʾ. [qurtubi¶62.7]
- `1:0.0` · lesson · The fiqh in it (Qurṭubī): voiding a rakʿah in which Umm al-Qurʾān is not read, which bears out Ibn al-Qāsim's view and his report from Mālik — cancel the rakʿah and build on another, the one praying not counting a rakʿah in which he did not read Fātiḥat al-Kitāb; and that the imam's recitation counts for those behind him, which is Jābir's school and was opposed by others. [qurtubi¶62.8]
