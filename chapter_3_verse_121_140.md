## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:121

> ˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when you left your home in the early morning to position the believers in the battlefield. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Commentary:**

### Preparing for Uḥud

This verse recalls the Prophet ﷺ leaving Madinah early to arrange the believers for the Battle of Uḥud. The event was not random or disorganized. The Prophet consulted, assessed the situation, selected positions, and placed people according to a military plan. Reliance upon Allah includes responsible preparation.

The verse preserves the human dimension of the battle. The believers had to make decisions, manage fear, and obey instructions. Divine assistance did not remove the need for discipline.

### Leadership and Consultation

The Prophet ﷺ was the best of creation and received revelation, yet he consulted his Companions in worldly matters. Consultation, *shūrā*, is not a sign of weak leadership. It allows experience to be heard, encourages shared responsibility, and helps a community commit to a difficult decision.

Once a decision was made, the Prophet ﷺ proceeded with trust in Allah. The Qur’an later commands consultation followed by resolve and reliance (3:159).

### The Battlefield as a Test

Uḥud would expose both strength and weakness. The believers included sincere companions, people who struggled, and hypocrites who withdrew. A battlefield reveals obedience under pressure, but ordinary life also contains tests of trust, patience, and discipline.

The verse should not make war appear glamorous. It presents a community facing an armed threat and receiving moral instruction within that reality. The purpose is to learn faith and responsibility, not to glorify bloodshed.

### Allah Hears and Knows

Allah is All-Hearing of the consultations, prayers, promises, and excuses spoken before the battle. He is All-Knowing of intentions and future choices. He knows who seeks Allah’s pleasure, who fears people, and who obeys only while success is visible.

This gives comfort to sincere effort and warning to hidden disobedience. The battlefield may conceal motives from people, but not from Allah.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:121–129 on Uḥud, consultation, divine aid, and the Prophet’s responsibility.
- Qur’an 3:159 on consultation, resolve, and reliance upon Allah.
- Qur’an 8:45–46 on steadfastness, remembrance, and avoiding dispute in battle.
- Qur’an 22:39–40 on fighting as a response to persecution and aggression.
- Qur’an 9:105 and 67:13–14 on Allah’s knowledge of outward and hidden actions.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on Uḥud, the Prophet’s leadership, and consultation with the Companions.
- For the historical setting, *shūrā*, battlefield discipline, and Allah’s hearing and knowledge, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:121.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:122

> ˹Remember˺ when two groups among you ˹believers˺ were about to cower, then Allah reassured them. So in Allah let the believers put their trust.

**Commentary:**

### Fear before the Battle

The verse acknowledges that two groups among the believers nearly lost courage. Classical tafsīr identifies them as groups from the Anṣār who felt the danger of the situation, while also noting that Allah strengthened them and they did not abandon the community. The Qur’an records their moment of weakness without erasing their later faithfulness.

This honesty is important. Believers can feel fear without being hypocrites. The moral test is whether fear becomes abandonment of duty or is overcome through trust and support.

### Allah Reassured Them

Allah strengthened the two groups so that they remained committed. Reassurance may come through revelation, companionship, clear leadership, prayer, or a renewed understanding of Allah’s promise. It does not always remove fear immediately; it gives the servant enough courage to act rightly despite fear.

A community should therefore support people who are anxious rather than humiliating them. Good leadership strengthens resolve through truth and compassion.

### Trust in Allah

The verse concludes, “In Allah let the believers put their trust.” *Tawakkul* means relying upon Allah while taking lawful means. The Prophet ﷺ arranged the army, and the Companions prepared themselves, but their hearts did not depend upon numbers or weapons alone.

Trust is not fatalism. A person plans, seeks counsel, trains, treats illness, and protects people, then entrusts the result to Allah. He also accepts that outcomes may contain wisdom he cannot see.

### Fear and Faith

Faith does not mean the absence of natural emotion. The prophets and companions experienced fear, grief, and uncertainty. Faith gives these emotions a proper direction. Fear can lead to prayer, caution, and discipline rather than panic or betrayal.

The believer asks Allah to strengthen the heart and to prevent temporary fear from becoming a permanent surrender to falsehood.

- Qur’an 3:159 and 65:3 on consultation, resolve, and reliance upon Allah.
- Qur’an 9:40 on the Prophet’s reassurance during the Hijrah.
- Qur’an 2:153 and 16:127 on patience and seeking help from Allah.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the Companions’ courage, fear, and reliance in battle.
- For the two groups, reassurance, *tawakkul*, and the difference between fear and abandonment, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:122.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:123

> Indeed, Allah made you victorious at Badr when you were ˹vastly˺ outnumbered. So be mindful of Allah, perhaps you will be grateful.

**Commentary:**

### Victory at Badr

Allah reminds the believers of Badr, where the Muslims were greatly outnumbered yet received victory. The reminder is designed to strengthen them after the hardship of Uḥud. Their strength does not come from numbers alone; it comes from Allah, who supported them when their material resources were limited.

Badr was a real battle requiring preparation, courage, and sacrifice. Attributing victory to Allah does not erase the efforts of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions. It places those efforts within dependence upon the One who grants their result.

### Taqwā after Victory

The command to be mindful of Allah comes after victory. Success can be more dangerous than defeat if it produces pride, injustice, or carelessness. Taqwā protects the victor from becoming like the arrogant enemy he defeated.

A community should maintain worship, justice, and restraint when it gains power. Victory does not make every action lawful, and political success is not proof that Allah approves every motive.

### Gratitude

The verse says “perhaps you will be grateful.” Gratitude includes recognizing Allah’s favour, praising Him, obeying Him, and using success to protect people rather than exploit them. It also includes remembering the sacrifices of others without turning history into self-glorification.

The believer is grateful for victory by remaining humble and by caring for those affected by conflict.

### Badr and Uḥud

The contrast between Badr and Uḥud teaches that divine support is connected to obedience and that events are tests. Badr did not give the believers permission to assume they could never lose; Uḥud did not prove that Allah had abandoned them. Both events educated the community.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:124

> ˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when you said to the believers, “Is it not enough that your Lord will send down a reinforcement of three thousand angels for your aid?”

**Commentary:**

### Divine Reassurance at Badr

The verse recalls the Prophet’s reassurance to the believers at Badr: Allah would reinforce them with three thousand angels. The number conveyed strength to a small and anxious community, but the verse’s central point is not military arithmetic. It is that Allah can support His servants through means beyond ordinary calculation.

The angels were not independent powers. They were created servants acting under Allah’s command. Their presence strengthened the believers’ hearts and demonstrated divine support.

### Encouragement, Not Complacency

The Prophet’s question—“Is it not enough?”—was meant to reassure, not to encourage passivity. The believers still had to stand, obey, and fight within the limits of justice. Divine promise does not cancel preparation or turn a person’s duty into a spectator event.

The Qur’an later says that victory comes only from Allah, the Almighty and Wise (3:126). The angels were a means and a sign; Allah was the giver of victory.

### Faith in the Unseen

Belief in angels is part of Islamic faith. They belong to the unseen world, but Allah reveals their existence and functions. The believer accepts what revelation establishes without inventing unnecessary details about their physical movements or military methods.

This is a lesson in how to approach unseen matters: affirm the revelation, avoid denial, and avoid speculation beyond evidence.

### Reassurance for Fearful Hearts

The immediate benefit of the reinforcement was psychological as well as strategic. The believers needed courage and tranquillity. Allah often supports servants through inner calm, good counsel, loyal companions, and unexpected openings. Not every form of divine help appears as a visible miracle.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:125

> Most certainly, if you ˹believers˺ are firm and mindful ˹of Allah˺ and the enemy launches a sudden attack on you, Allah will reinforce you with five thousand angels designated ˹for battle˺.

**Commentary:**

### Divine Help Has Conditions

The verse describes Allah’s reinforcement with five thousand angels under specific conditions: the believers remain firm, practise taqwā, and respond to a sudden attack. The conditions teach that divine support is not a slogan attached to every desire. It is connected to obedience, discipline, and a just cause.

Firmness, *ṣabr*, means holding the line of faith and duty under fear. Taqwā means not violating Allah’s limits even while facing an enemy. A community that abandons justice cannot claim divine support simply because it uses religious language.

### Sudden Attack and Readiness

The reference to a sudden attack shows that the believers were facing a real tactical danger. Islam recognizes the need for readiness and defence. Preparation includes training, consultation, intelligence, lawful organization, and protection of civilians.

Readiness is not aggression. It allows a community to protect life and prevent surprise from producing unnecessary harm.

### Angels Assigned for Battle

The angels are described as designated or marked. Classical tafsīr discusses their identified ranks and the ways they supported the believers. Muslims affirm the unseen aid because revelation establishes it, while avoiding invented details that Allah and His Messenger did not provide.

The angels remain servants of Allah. Their involvement magnifies Allah’s power rather than giving them independent control over victory.

### Faith and Material Means

The verse joins spiritual qualities to practical circumstances. Firmness and taqwā do not replace strategy, just as strategy does not replace prayer. The believer uses means while depending upon Allah for the outcome.

The same balance applies outside battle. Students study and pray, patients seek treatment and supplicate, and communities plan while trusting Allah.

### Not a Promise of Automatic Victory

The verse should not be used to claim that every group calling itself Muslim will be given miraculous military victory. Uḥud itself shows that believers can suffer when they dispute or disobey. Divine aid is governed by Allah’s wisdom and does not excuse misconduct.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:126

> Allah ordained this ˹reinforcement˺ only as good news for you and reassurance for your hearts. And victory comes only from Allah—the Almighty, All-Wise—

**Commentary:**

### The Purpose of the Reinforcement

Allah explains that the angelic reinforcement was good news and reassurance for the believers’ hearts. The event strengthened their faith and courage, but it did not make the angels independent causes of victory. The help came from Allah.

This teaches believers to recognize means without worshipping them. Human effort, an army, medicine, wealth, or a teacher can be a means, but the outcome remains in Allah’s hand.

### Tranquillity in Fear

The heart can become unstable when danger is near. Allah grants *sakīnah*, tranquillity, through remembrance, truthful leadership, companionship, and signs of His support. Tranquillity does not mean the absence of fear; it enables the believer to act correctly while fear exists.

The Prophet ﷺ strengthened the community through prayer and clear instruction. Emotional reassurance was joined to practical preparation.

### Victory Belongs to Allah

Victory comes only from Allah, *al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*. Allah’s might means that no force can defeat what He ultimately chooses to preserve. His wisdom means that victory may take forms and occur at times people do not expect.

Sometimes victory is military success; sometimes it is survival of faith, protection of the innocent, moral clarity, or the eventual exposure of falsehood. A worldly setback does not prove that Allah has abandoned the believers.

### The Almighty and All-Wise

The two divine names balance one another. Allah has absolute power, but His actions are not arbitrary. He gives support according to wisdom, tests communities, and may delay visible victory for reasons that become clear only later.

Believers should therefore ask for help without dictating to Allah the exact form of the answer.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:127

> to destroy a group of the disbelievers and humble the rest, causing them to withdraw in disappointment.

**Commentary:**

### The Outcome of Badr

This verse continues the description of Allah’s aid at Badr. A group of the hostile force was destroyed, while the rest withdrew in disappointment. The reference is to an armed confrontation in which the opposing force had come to attack and persecute the Muslim community.

The verse should be read with the rules governing combat. It is not a general command to harm every person who disbelieves. The Qur’an distinguishes combatants from noncombatants and commands believers not to transgress.

### Justice in Conflict

The destruction of a group in battle was a consequence within a specific conflict, not a celebration of human suffering. Islamic ethics require legitimate authority, just cause, proportionality, protection of civilians, and honouring treaties. The Prophet ﷺ prohibited killing women, children, and those not participating in combat.

Believers must never use historical battle verses to justify terrorism, murder, or vigilantism.

### Humbled Power

The powerful force that expected victory withdrew in disappointment. This shows that worldly confidence is not the same as security. A community may possess weapons and numbers while lacking justice and divine support.

The believers, however, were not entitled to arrogance. Victory was a test of whether they would remain obedient, protect captives, and use power responsibly.

### Allah’s Wisdom in Victory

The phrase “Allah may destroy a group” is not an invitation for people to claim certainty about every military event. Allah’s wisdom governs history, while human beings remain responsible for the means they choose. They must pursue peace when possible and avoid turning religion into a pretext for domination.

### Disappointment and Repentance

The enemy’s withdrawal in disappointment may have opened a later opportunity for reflection. A military defeat does not remove a person’s chance to repent while alive. Allah can guide someone who once opposed the believers, as occurred with people who later entered Islam.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:128

> You ˹O Prophet˺ have no say in the matter.[156] It is up to Allah to turn to them in mercy or punish them, for indeed they are wrongdoers.

**Commentary:**

### Allah Alone Judges Hearts

Allah reminds the Prophet ﷺ that the final matter belongs to Him. The Prophet is a messenger, teacher, judge, and leader, but he does not control who receives final forgiveness or punishment. Divine judgement is not delegated to any human being.

The verse was revealed in the context of severe opposition and the suffering of believers. It corrected any assumption that the Prophet could determine the final fate of particular enemies through personal desire or anger.

### Mercy or Punishment

Allah may turn to a wrongdoer in mercy through repentance, guidance, and reform, or punish him for persistent wrongdoing. The two possibilities show that the door of repentance remains open while a person lives, even after serious harm.

Mercy does not mean ignoring victims. A repentant wrongdoer must stop the harm and restore rights where possible. Punishment, when deserved and lawfully applied, is not a denial of divine mercy.

### The Prophet’s Humanity

The verse protects tawḥīd by distinguishing the Prophet’s authority from Allah’s. Muhammad ﷺ conveys revelation and rules by it, but he does not own the unseen or possess independent power to forgive sins. Muslims love and obey him without worshipping him.

This also teaches leaders to recognize their limits. No imam, scholar, ruler, or parent can guarantee another person’s salvation or permanently declare that Allah’s mercy is closed.

### Justice and Intercession

The verse does not deny the intercession Allah may grant the Prophet ﷺ. Intercession occurs only by Allah’s permission and according to His wisdom (20:109). It cannot be demanded as an independent power or used to escape repentance and justice.

### A Lesson in Anger

When believers suffer, they may feel that an opponent deserves only punishment. This verse teaches them to leave ultimate judgement to Allah while pursuing lawful justice. A person who later repents may become a believer and a source of good.

- Qur’an 39:53–54 on the open door of mercy before death.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:129

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### Absolute Ownership and Judgement

Everything in the heavens and earth belongs to Allah, so the decision to forgive or punish belongs to Him alone. No created being can claim independent authority over the ultimate fate of another person. Human beings may apply justice within lawful limits, but final judgement is Allah’s right.

This statement follows the reminder to the Prophet ﷺ that he has no final say in the matter. Even the greatest messenger is Allah’s servant before Allah’s sovereignty.

### Forgiveness and Punishment with Wisdom

Allah forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills. His will is not arbitrary; it is joined to perfect knowledge and wisdom. He knows the sincerity of repentance, the gravity of wrongdoing, the evidence that reached a person, and the rights violated.

Forgiveness may be granted directly or through repentance, good deeds, intercession by permission, and mercy beyond what the servant deserves. Punishment may be deserved because of disbelief, oppression, or persistent sin. Allah never punishes unjustly.

### Divine Mercy

The verse ends with *al-Ghafūr al-Raḥīm*, the All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. The warning about punishment is therefore enclosed by hope. The servant should not despair after sin, and he should not treat Allah’s mercy as a reason to persist.

The Prophet ﷺ described Allah’s mercy as greater than a mother’s mercy for her child, in an authentic hadith. This encourages sincere return while preserving reverent fear.

### Mercy and Human Justice

Believers should imitate mercy in their own dealings without cancelling justice. They forgive when forgiveness reforms and protects, and they seek accountability when continued harm would result. They do not claim to know the unseen or take final vengeance into their own hands.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:130

> O believers! Do not consume interest, multiplying it many times over. And be mindful of Allah, so you may prosper.

**Commentary:**

### The Prohibition of Ribā

Allah forbids believers from consuming *ribā*, an unjust increase tied to a debt or exchange. The verse mentions multiplication because pre-Islamic lenders would often tell a debtor to pay immediately or accept an increase for more time. A debt could grow until the original obligation became impossible to escape.

The prohibition is not limited to one dramatic form of compounding. The Qur’an and Sunnah establish broader rules concerning unlawful increases in loans and certain unequal exchanges. Detailed applications to modern financial products require qualified scholarship and careful examination of the actual contract.

### Debt and Exploitation

Ribā turns a person’s need into a source of guaranteed extraction. The lender gains regardless of whether the borrower’s business succeeds or fails, while the borrower carries the risk and the growing burden. This can deepen poverty, destroy families, and concentrate wealth.

Islam encourages lending as assistance and trade as a lawful exchange of risk and benefit. A creditor may seek repayment of the principal and may show patience or forgive part of a debt. He may not use necessity as a basis for oppression.

### Ribā and Trade Are Not the Same

The Qur’an distinguishes lawful trade from ribā (2:275). Trade involves an exchange of goods, services, or productive investment with permissible risk. Ribā involves an unlawful guaranteed increase attached to a debt or specific unequal exchange.

Not every profit, fee, salary, or investment return is ribā, and not every transaction labelled “finance” is automatically lawful. The substance of the contract matters more than its marketing language.

### Taqwā and Prosperity

Allah joins the prohibition to taqwā and prosperity. True prosperity is not merely the growth of money; it is lawful provision, social stability, blessing, and success in the Hereafter. Wealth gained through oppression may appear to increase while losing blessing and producing accountability.

Taqwā makes a person careful about contracts, transparent in business, and compassionate toward debtors.

### Mercy toward Debtors

The Qur’an commands creditors to give a debtor in difficulty more time and praises forgiving the debt altogether (2:280). This is not economic weakness. It is a system that recognizes human vulnerability and prevents wealth from becoming a weapon.

The Prophet ﷺ praised the person who was easy in buying, selling, and demanding repayment, in reports recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:275–280 and 4:161 on the prohibition of ribā, lawful trade, and kindness to debtors.
- Qur’an 30:39 on the difference between ribā and charity.
- Qur’an 59:7 on preventing wealth from circulating only among the rich.
- Qur’an 5:1 and 17:34 on contracts and obligations.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith condemning the one who consumes ribā, gives it, records it, or witnesses it; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, reports on leniency in repayment.
- For *ribā*, *aḍʿāfan muḍāʿafah*, debt exploitation, lawful trade, and financial taqwā, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:130.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:131

> Guard yourselves against the Fire prepared for the disbelievers.

**Commentary:**

### A Warning after Financial Law

This verse follows the prohibition of ribā and tells believers to guard themselves from the Fire. The connection shows that financial ethics are part of faith. Islam does not separate worship from how people earn, lend, borrow, and treat the vulnerable.

The Fire is described as prepared for disbelievers, but the warning also tells believers to avoid the sins that lead toward it. A person should not feel safe merely because he carries a Muslim name while deliberately persisting in oppression and refusing repentance.

### Guarding Oneself

The Arabic command, *wa-ttaqū al-nār*, means to place a protection between oneself and the Fire. The protection is faith, obedience, repentance, lawful earning, and Allah’s mercy. It includes leaving ribā, returning unlawful gain, and reforming systems that exploit debtors.

Taqwā is active. A person identifies harmful practices, seeks reliable knowledge, changes his contracts, and asks Allah for forgiveness.

### The Fire Is Real

The Qur’an speaks of the Fire as a real consequence, not merely a symbol for psychological regret. Belief in it protects the heart from treating injustice as a game with no final result. The person who benefits from exploitation may escape worldly courts, but he cannot escape Allah’s knowledge.

This warning should be conveyed with seriousness and wisdom, not used to make casual declarations about individuals’ final fate.

### Hope and Fear

The verse comes within a passage that also mentions Allah’s forgiveness, mercy, and Paradise. Fear of the Fire is balanced by hope in Allah. The believer does not despair after sin; he leaves it, repents, and repairs harm.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that Allah’s mercy exceeds His wrath, while the Qur’an repeatedly warns against security from punishment. Both truths are necessary for a balanced heart.

### Social Protection

Guarding against the Fire is not only individual. Communities should prevent predatory lending, provide debt relief, educate people about contracts, and protect those in need. Economic injustice can become institutional, so moral responsibility includes reforming systems as well as personal transactions.

- Qur’an 3:133 on hastening toward forgiveness and Paradise.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on hope and return after sin.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:132

> Obey Allah and the Messenger, so you may be shown mercy.

**Commentary:**

### Obedience and Mercy

Allah joins obedience to Him with obedience to the Messenger ﷺ. The Prophet conveys, explains, and embodies Allah’s guidance, so following him is part of following Allah. Obedience is the path through which the servant seeks mercy, not a way to earn independence from mercy.

The verse follows warnings about ribā and the Fire. Mercy is therefore connected to practical obedience: leaving oppression, fulfilling rights, and reforming conduct.

### The Messenger’s Sunnah

Obedience to the Messenger includes accepting his authentic teachings, following his worship, and imitating his character. His Sunnah clarifies how to pray, give charity, conduct trade, reconcile people, and apply justice.

This requires learning which reports are authentic and understanding them in context. Following the Sunnah is not the same as turning every cultural detail into an obligation or rejecting legitimate scholarly differences.

### Obedience Is Not to Sin

Islamic obedience does not mean obeying a created being in disobedience to Allah. The Prophet ﷺ taught that obedience is only in what is right. Leaders, parents, teachers, and institutions must be obeyed in lawful matters, but no one can make ribā, oppression, or falsehood righteous by commanding it.

This principle protects both religion and conscience.

### Mercy as a Result

Allah’s mercy includes forgiveness, guidance, protection from sin, strength to obey, and reward in the Hereafter. It also includes the Prophet’s teaching, through which believers can know how to live. A person who obeys with sincerity may still make mistakes, but he returns to Allah rather than defending his error.

### Obedience and Social Ethics

The verse is not limited to private rituals. Obedience requires honest contracts, fair treatment of debtors, compassion, family responsibility, and the protection of life and dignity. The Messenger ﷺ embodied mercy in these relationships.

A person who performs rituals while systematically exploiting people should ask whether he has understood obedience.

- Qur’an 3:31–32 and 4:59–80 on following and obeying Allah and the Messenger.
- Qur’an 24:51–54 on the response of believers when called to Allah and His Messenger.
- Qur’an 59:7 on accepting what the Messenger gives and avoiding what he forbids.
- Qur’an 16:43 and 33:21 on learning from and following the Messenger.
- Qur’an 5:2 and 5:8 on obedience expressed through justice and cooperation in good.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith “Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah” and the principle that obedience is only in what is right.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:133

> And hasten towards forgiveness from your Lord and a Paradise as vast as the heavens and the earth, prepared for those mindful ˹of Allah˺.

**Commentary:**

### Hasten to Forgiveness

Allah calls believers to hasten toward forgiveness and Paradise. The command, *sāriʿū*, encourages urgency in repentance and good deeds. Life is short and opportunities change, so a person should not postpone prayer, restitution, charity, or reconciliation.

Hastening does not mean acting recklessly or without knowledge. It means not delaying a clear good out of laziness, pride, or attachment to worldly comfort.

### Forgiveness as a Destination

Forgiveness is mentioned before Paradise because the servant needs Allah’s pardon before he can hope for the final reward. Sin creates a barrier, while repentance, good deeds, and divine mercy remove it. The believer seeks forgiveness continually, even after good deeds, because he knows his worship is imperfect.

### The Vastness of Paradise

Paradise is described as vast as the heavens and earth. The expression conveys unimaginable breadth and abundance, not a limitation on Paradise’s actual extent. The unseen cannot be measured by ordinary human comparison.

The image also answers the narrowness of worldly life. A person may feel restricted by poverty, persecution, grief, or social injustice, but Allah has prepared a spacious and secure home for those who remain mindful.

### Prepared for the Mindful

Paradise is prepared for *al-muttaqūn*, those who cultivate taqwā. Taqwā includes faith, obedience, repentance, justice, and restraint from sin. It is not a claim of perfection. The mindful fall but return, and they do not persist proudly in wrongdoing.

### A Community that Hurries toward Good

The plural command invites collective effort. Communities should hasten to forgive, settle debts, feed the hungry, educate children, protect the vulnerable, and establish justice. Paradise is not pursued through private spirituality that ignores public rights.

- Qur’an 2:148 and 5:48 on racing toward good deeds.
- Qur’an 57:21 on racing toward forgiveness and Paradise.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on forgiveness and immediate return to Allah.
- Qur’an 9:72 and 76:11–22 on Paradise, divine pleasure, and the reward of the righteous.
- Qur’an 51:15–18 on the qualities of the mindful.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on repentance, the shortness of life, and the vastness of Paradise.
- For *sāriʿū*, forgiveness, the vastness of Paradise, and *muttaqūn*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:133.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:134

> ˹They are˺ those who donate in prosperity and adversity, control their anger, and pardon others. And Allah loves the good-doers.

**Commentary:**

### Spending in Every Circumstance

The first quality of the mindful is generosity in prosperity and adversity. The prosperous give from abundance, while those facing difficulty give according to their means. The verse does not require a person to neglect essential needs; it praises a heart that remains open even when circumstances are not comfortable.

Spending includes zakāh, voluntary charity, family support, debt relief, hospitality, time, and service. The value of the gift is measured by sincerity and sacrifice, not only by its monetary size.

### Controlling Anger

“Control their anger” translates *al-kāẓimīn al-ghayẓ*. The image is of containing anger rather than allowing it to spill into abuse. Anger is a natural emotion, and Islam does not demand that a person never feel it. It teaches him to govern speech and action when angry.

The Prophet ﷺ said that the strong person is not the one who overcomes others physically but the one who controls himself when angry, in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Practical means include silence, changing posture, leaving the situation, making wuḍūʾ, and delaying a response.

### Pardon with Wisdom

The righteous pardon people. Pardon, *ʿafw*, means releasing a claim of personal revenge. It is a virtue when it produces reform, protects dignity, and does not expose others to continuing harm. Islam does not require a victim to remain in an abusive situation or to surrender lawful rights.

Justice and pardon can coexist. A person may report a crime, seek compensation, or establish a boundary while refusing to nourish hatred or seek unlawful revenge.

### Allah Loves the Excellent

Allah loves the *muḥsinūn*, those who do good with excellence. *Iḥsān* includes doing what is right beautifully, generously, and with awareness that Allah sees. The qualities in this verse—charity, anger control, and pardon—show that excellence is social as well as devotional.

### A Remedy for Conflict

These qualities are especially relevant to communities after disagreement. Generosity reduces scarcity, anger control prevents escalation, and pardon repairs relationships. A community that combines them can transform conflict into reconciliation without surrendering truth.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:177 and 2:261–274 on generosity and charity.
- Qur’an 59:9 and 64:16 on preferring others and spending according to ability.
- Qur’an 41:34 and 42:37–43 on responding to evil, anger, patience, and pardon.
- Qur’an 24:22 on pardoning and overlooking.
- Qur’an 5:13 and 3:134 on Allah’s love for those who do good.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith on controlling anger and the hadith of the powerful person who forgives.
- For *infāq*, *kāẓim al-ghayẓ*, *ʿafw*, *iḥsān*, and the balance between pardon and protection from harm, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:134.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:135

> ˹They are˺ those who, upon committing an evil deed or wronging themselves, remember Allah and seek forgiveness and do not knowingly persist in sin—and who forgives sins except Allah?

**Commentary:**

### The Righteous Also Make Mistakes

The righteous are not described as people who never sin. They are described as people who remember Allah after sinning, seek forgiveness, and do not knowingly persist. This protects believers from perfectionism and despair. Human weakness is real, but it must be met with repentance rather than excuses.

An evil deed, *fāḥishah*, may include a grave indecency, while wronging oneself includes every sin that damages the soul. All sin ultimately harms the person who commits it, even if the harm is temporarily hidden.

### Remembering Allah after Sin

Sin often produces forgetfulness: the person loses awareness of Allah, consequence, and the rights of others. Remembering Allah restores moral clarity. The repentant servant admits what happened, feels regret, stops the wrongdoing, and asks Allah for forgiveness.

Remembrance is not merely repeating words while continuing the same harm. *Istighfār* must be joined to a sincere change of direction.

### Do Not Persist Knowingly

The phrase “do not knowingly persist” does not mean that a person who relapses after sincere repentance has no hope. It means that he does not decide to continue sin while treating repentance as a licence. If he falls again, he must return again, without becoming comfortable with disobedience.

Persistence can be shown by defending the sin, refusing to stop, harming others without concern, or postponing repentance indefinitely.

### Who Forgives except Allah?

The rhetorical question directs the heart to Allah as the ultimate forgiver. People may forgive one another, but Allah alone removes sin from the record and grants acceptance. This is why no human priest or intermediary owns forgiveness.

When another person’s right is involved, seeking Allah’s forgiveness does not remove the obligation to repair the harm. The rights of people must be returned or addressed with justice.

### A Practical Repentance

Sincere repentance includes stopping the sin, regretting it, resolving not to return, and restoring the rights of others. If the sin involves public misinformation, the person should correct it; if it involves stolen property, he should return it; if it involves abuse, he must stop and accept lawful accountability.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:110 and 25:70 on forgiveness after repentance and reform.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on not despairing of Allah’s mercy.
- Qur’an 2:222 and 66:8 on Allah’s love for those who repent and sincere repentance.
- Qur’an 17:32 and 24:21 on avoiding the approaches and temptations of sin.
- Qur’an 4:17–18 on repentance before death and the danger of delay.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith “Every son of Adam sins, and the best of sinners are those who repent,” and reports on Allah’s joy at repentance.
- For *fāḥishah*, *ẓulm al-nafs*, *dhikr*, *istighfār*, and the prohibition of knowingly persisting in sin, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:135.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:136

> Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord and Gardens under which rivers flow, staying there forever. How excellent is the reward for those who work ˹righteousness˺!

**Commentary:**

### Forgiveness before Reward

The reward of the righteous begins with forgiveness. Before entering Paradise, the servant needs Allah to pardon shortcomings and cleanse the record. This order teaches that even good deeds are imperfect and that salvation depends upon Allah’s mercy.

The preceding verse described repentance after sin. Allah does not merely erase the guilt; He grants a lasting reward to those who return and reform.

### The Gardens

The Gardens of Paradise are described with rivers flowing beneath them. The image conveys life, purity, abundance, and security. The Qur’an uses familiar images to give human beings a glimpse of an unseen reality whose full beauty surpasses their imagination.

The greatest delight of Paradise is Allah’s pleasure and the removal of every fear and grief. Physical descriptions are true, but they do not exhaust the reality of the reward.

### Everlasting Residence

“Staying there forever” contrasts with worldly life, where every home and relationship is temporary. The righteous do not fear eviction, death, illness, or loss. The permanence of the reward gives courage to sacrifice temporary pleasure for obedience.

### Working Righteousness

The verse praises those who “work righteousness.” Righteousness is active. It includes faith, prayer, charity, truthfulness, patience, anger control, pardon, and repentance. It is not achieved by wishing for a good outcome while neglecting responsibility.

The phrase also indicates that good deeds must be done sincerely and lawfully. A person cannot seek Paradise through actions that Allah forbids.

### Hope for the Sinful

The description gives hope to people who have sinned but repent. Their past does not have to define their final outcome. What matters is returning to Allah, repairing harm, and continuing to act righteously.

- Qur’an 2:25 and 18:30–31 on gardens, rivers, and no lost reward.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:137

> Similar situations came to pass before you, so travel throughout the land and see the fate of the deniers.

**Commentary:**

### History Repeats Its Patterns

Allah reminds the believers that similar situations occurred before them. Communities experienced victory and defeat, power and weakness, guidance and rejection. History is not random in the Qur’anic view; it reveals recurring moral patterns.

The believers’ setback at Uḥud should not make them think that hardship proves falsehood. Earlier communities were also tested, and the final outcome belonged to those who remained truthful and patient.

### Travel and Observe

The command to travel through the land and observe the fate of the deniers invites thoughtful historical reflection. Ruins, abandoned power centres, and records of fallen civilizations can teach humility when people ask why those societies rose and fell.

The purpose is not tourism or the collection of ruins without moral reflection. It is to observe how arrogance, oppression, rejection, and corruption eventually destroy the strength people thought permanent.

### The Sunnah of Allah in History

Allah’s recurring patterns include the testing of believers, the delay of punishment, the exposure of hypocrisy, and the eventual failure of oppression. These patterns do not mean that every event is simple or that believers always triumph quickly. They mean that no injustice escapes the divine order.

A community should study history honestly, including its own mistakes, rather than using history only to flatter itself.

### Learning from Uḥud

The believers had suffered because some disputed and disobeyed. This verse places their experience alongside earlier events so that they learn rather than collapse into grief. A setback can become a school in obedience, leadership, consultation, and patience.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:138

> This[157] is an insight to humanity —a guide and a lesson to the God-fearing.

**Commentary:**

### Insight for Humanity

The Qur’an describes the preceding history as *bayān*, a clear explanation and insight for humanity. It makes moral patterns visible: the consequences of arrogance, the need for patience, the danger of division, and the certainty of Allah’s justice.

Insight is more than information. It is the ability to see beneath events and respond wisely. The same battle can appear as a defeat to one person and as a lesson in obedience to another.

### Guidance

The Qur’an is *hudā*, guidance. It does not merely describe what happened; it directs people toward what they should believe and do. The account of Uḥud guides believers to patience, consultation, unity, repentance, and trust in Allah.

Guidance requires a willing heart. A person can read history without changing, but the mindful person asks how the revelation applies to his own choices.

### A Lesson for the God-Fearing

The verse calls the account a lesson for the *muttaqūn*. Taqwā enables a person to learn from events rather than treating them as entertainment or proof of his own superiority. The mindful person sees the failures of earlier communities as warnings for himself.

### The Qur’an’s Educational Method

The Qur’an teaches through doctrine, story, command, warning, and reflection. It does not isolate theology from history. The believers learn about Allah’s power while learning about their own disobedience and need for reform.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:139

> Do not falter or grieve, for you will have the upper hand, if you are ˹true˺ believers.

**Commentary:**

### Encouragement after Setback

After the hardship of Uḥud, Allah tells the believers not to weaken or grieve. Their temporary loss does not mean that truth has failed or that Allah has abandoned them. They are to recover courage, examine their mistakes, and continue faithfully.

The command does not deny pain. Grief after injury and the death of companions is natural. It prevents grief from becoming hopelessness and prevents setback from becoming surrender.

### The Condition of True Belief

The promise of having the upper hand is conditional: “if you are true believers.” Faith includes obedience, patience, repentance, unity, and reliance upon Allah. A community cannot claim the promise while abandoning the qualities that faith requires.

The upper hand may include moral clarity, perseverance, protection of the message, and eventual success, not only immediate military victory. Believers may suffer while still being honoured before Allah.

### Uḥud and Resilience

Uḥud teaches resilience rather than invulnerability. The believers had made mistakes, but Allah taught them and forgave them. A community’s strength is shown by its ability to acknowledge failure, repair discipline, and return to obedience.

True resilience is not pretending nothing happened. It is learning without becoming paralysed by shame.

### Do Not Be Arrogant

Having the upper hand is not permission to humiliate others or violate justice. Victory is another test. The believers must remain merciful, honour treaties, protect noncombatants, and give people opportunities for repentance.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:140

> If you have suffered injuries ˹at Uḥud˺, they suffered similarly ˹at Badr˺. We alternate these days ˹of victory and defeat˺ among people so that Allah may reveal the ˹true˺ believers, choose martyrs from among you—and Allah does not like the wrongdoers—

**Commentary:**

### Loss Is Not the Whole Story

Allah reminds the believers that their enemy had also suffered at Badr. The pain at Uḥud was not unique to them, and history does not move in a straight line of uninterrupted victory. Both sides experience changing circumstances, but the moral meaning of the events is not the same.

The reminder does not trivialize the wounds or deaths of the believers. It places them within a larger education about faith, obedience, and the temporary nature of worldly outcomes.

### Alternating Days

Allah alternates days of victory and defeat among people. The alternation prevents one group from imagining that worldly success proves permanent superiority. It also tests gratitude in victory and patience in defeat.

A community should use victory to serve and defeat to reform. Neither condition excuses injustice. Worldly power is temporary, while the final judgement remains with Allah.

### Revealing True Believers

The word “reveal” means that trials make qualities visible. Uḥud exposed sincerity, hypocrisy, fear, greed, obedience, and repentance. The trial did not give Allah new knowledge; He already knows the unseen. It revealed the reality to the people themselves and distinguished conduct in the public record of history.

A person may claim courage before danger arrives. The trial shows whether the claim is real.

### Choosing Martyrs

Allah chooses martyrs from among the believers. Martyrdom is an honour when a person dies sincerely in a legitimate cause and according to Allah’s conditions. It cannot be claimed merely by dying in violence, nor can individuals confidently declare the final status of another person without evidence.

The Qur’an later consoles the families of those killed in Allah’s cause by affirming that they are alive with their Lord and provided for (3:169–171). Their loss is real in the world, but their reward is greater with Allah.

### Allah Does Not Love Wrongdoers

The verse closes by rejecting wrongdoing. The believers’ setback was connected to disobedience and internal weakness, but Allah did not wrong them. He taught them through consequence and pardoned them. Likewise, the enemy’s wrongdoing does not become righteous because it achieved temporary success.

The verse calls every side to examine justice, obedience, and moral purpose rather than relying on victory as proof of virtue.

- Qur’an 8:42–44 on Badr and the alternation of perception and circumstance.

- Qur’an 3:169–171 on martyrs alive with their Lord and receiving His bounty.
