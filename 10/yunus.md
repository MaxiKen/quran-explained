# Sūrah 10 — Yūnus (Jonah)

*109 verses; predominantly Makkan. Maʿārif notes reports that three verses were revealed after the Hijrah, while al-Qurṭubī records differing reports about particular exceptions. The sūrah is named for the prophet Jonah, whose people are recalled later as the people who believed and were spared (10:98) **[Maʿārif] [Qurṭubī]**.*

**Sources cited inline:** **[Ṭabarī]** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* · **[Qurṭubī]** *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* · **[Ibn Kathīr]** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* · **[Jalālayn]** *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* · **[Saʿdī]** *Taysīr al-Karīm al-Raḥmān* · **[Maʿārif]** *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* · **[Study Quran]** *The Study Quran* (2015).

**The sūrah.** Its opening joins the mysterious letters to the wisdom of revelation, then answers the claim that a human messenger is astonishing. It turns to the ordered heavens and earth as signs of the Creator and of the Return, contrasts heedlessness with faith, and proceeds through the histories of earlier messengers before its account of Jonah’s people. Maʿārif presents its central themes as God’s Oneness, the Messenger’s mission, and the Hereafter, supported by creation and by admonitory history **[Maʿārif]**.

---

## 10:1

*“Alif Lam Ra. These are the verses of the wise Book.”*

**Meaning.** *Alif Lam Ra* belongs to the Qurʾān’s disconnected opening letters. Al-Ṭabarī preserves interpretations attributed to early authorities, including “I am God; I see” and the letters of the divine name *al-Raḥmān*, without treating any as a conclusive explanation **[Ṭabarī]**. Al-Jalālayn leaves their intended meaning to God and takes “these” as the signs of the Qurʾān, the wise Book whose verses make guidance clear **[Jalālayn]**. As-Saʿdī emphasizes that the Book gathers wisdom, rulings, and proofs of religious truths; its hearers are called to receive and follow it **[Saʿdī]**.

**Reflection.** The opening places both the limits and the confidence of interpretation side by side: not every sign is exhausted by human explanation, yet the Book’s guidance is meant to be understood and obeyed.

## 10:2

*“Is it a marvel for mankind that We have sent revelation to a man from among them: ‘Warn mankind and give glad tidings to those who believe that they have a station of sincerity with their Lord’? The disbelievers say, ‘This is indeed a manifest sorcerer.’”*

**Meaning.** The question rebukes those who found it strange that revelation should come through a human being from their own community. Al-Ṭabarī explains the warning as notice of the consequence of disobedience; the glad tidings promise believers a sure reward for sincere faith and good works **[Ṭabarī] [Saʿdī]**. Al-Qurṭubī and The Study Quran report the Quraysh’s derision that God would send no more than an ordinary man—identified in the report as the orphan of Abū Ṭālib—as His messenger **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**. “A station of sincerity” (*qadam ṣidq*) is an honorable standing or secure reward with God, not a claim to worldly status **[Study Quran]**. The charge of sorcery turns rejection of the message into an accusation against its bearer.

**Context.** The objection is part of a wider demand that a messenger be other than human; Maʿārif answers that a messenger must share the nature of those to whom he is sent **[Maʿārif]**.

**Reflection.** The verse pairs warning with hope: the messenger calls all to account, but also announces that faith has a promised future with God.

## 10:3

*“Surely your Lord is God, Who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then established Himself over the Throne, directing the affair. There is no intercessor except after His leave. That is God, your Lord, so worship Him. Will you not remember?”*

**Meaning.** The Creator alone governs what He has made. Al-Ṭabarī explains that creation and the ordering of affairs belong to God without partner; al-Qurṭubī glosses “directing the affair” as decreeing and carrying matters through **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī]**. The verse’s reference to God’s establishment over the Throne is affirmed without likening it to created things **[Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr]**. No intercessor acts independently: intercession occurs only with God’s permission, a rejection of the claim that idols can secure it **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**. Since He is both Lord and Creator, worship is due to Him alone **[Maʿārif]**.

**Reflection.** The appeal “Will you not remember?” asks people to let the visible order of the world lead beyond itself—to the One who sustains it and to whom worship belongs.

## 10:4

*“To Him is your return, all together—a true promise from God. He originates creation, then brings it back, that He may justly reward those who believe and do righteous deeds. As for those who disbelieve, theirs is a drink of boiling liquid and a painful punishment for their disbelief.”*

**Meaning.** Everyone returns to God; resurrection is not a second, unrelated act but the completion of the creation He began. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr connect the return with the Day of Judgment, when deeds are recompensed; the promise is true and none escapes it **[Ṭabarī] [Ibn Kathīr]**. The Study Quran relates the pairing of origination and return to the Qurʾānic language of the Origin and the Return **[Study Quran]**. Believers who act righteously receive their just reward, whereas the passage names boiling drink and painful punishment for those who deny the truth **[Jalālayn]**.

**Belief.** God’s power to originate life grounds belief in resurrection; the verse joins that belief to moral accountability.

**Reflection.** The certainty of return gives present choices lasting weight: the Creator is also the Judge to whom all people come back.

## 10:5

*“He it is Who made the sun a radiance and the moon a light, and determined for it stations, that you might know the number of years and the reckoning of time. God did not create this except in truth; He expounds the signs for a people who know.”*

**Meaning.** The sun and moon are distinct signs, and their ordered courses make time measurable. Al-Qurṭubī notes the wording’s distinction between *ḍiyāʾ* (radiance) and *nūr* (light); The Study Quran likewise notes the moon’s reflected luminosity **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**. Al-Ṭabarī explains the stations as measured positions that the moon neither exceeds nor falls short of **[Ṭabarī]**. Al-Jalālayn counts twenty-eight lunar stations, through which the months and years can be reckoned **[Jalālayn]**. “In truth” denies that this ordered creation is purposeless; its signs invite knowledge of the Maker and His wisdom **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**.

**Reflection.** The regularity that makes calendars possible is also a lesson: human knowledge depends on a world whose rhythms are not random.

## 10:6

*“Surely in the alternation of night and day, and in whatever God has created in the heavens and the earth, are signs for a people who are reverent.”*

**Meaning.** Night gives way to day and day to night; the changing sequence, together with the creatures of the heavens and earth, points to the power and lordship of God **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn]**. Al-Qurṭubī records the report that when the people of Mecca asked for a sign, they were directed to consider the works of creation **[Qurṭubī]**. As-Saʿdī distinguishes the response of knowledge, which recognizes how creation indicates its Maker, from *taqwā*, which moves the heart toward good and away from evil **[Saʿdī]**. The Study Quran adds that the passing of night and day reminds the reverent of the world’s transience and of its eternal Source **[Study Quran]**.

**Reflection.** Contemplation becomes more than observation when it changes how one lives: recognition of the signs should awaken reverence and gratitude **[Maʿārif]**.

## 10:7

*“Indeed, those who do not anticipate meeting Us, and are content with the life of this world and secure in it, and are heedless of Our signs—”*

**Meaning.** The verse describes people who do not expect to stand before God and have made worldly life their final concern. Al-Ṭabarī says they take its adornments in place of the Hereafter and turn away from the proofs of God’s Oneness **[Ṭabarī]**. As-Saʿdī stresses that they have made this passing life the end of their aims, rather than a passage from which to prepare for the lasting life **[Saʿdī]**. Al-Qurṭubī notes that *yarjūn* can carry the sense of hoping for or fearing an encounter: the heedless neither look for the promised reward nor fear the reckoning **[Qurṭubī]**. The Study Quran likewise understands the “meeting” as the Resurrection and the hope of recompense and mercy **[Study Quran]**.

**Reflection.** The criticism is not of ordinary life itself, but of treating it as ultimate and becoming inattentive to the signs that point beyond it.

## 10:8

*“It is they whose refuge shall be the Fire for what they used to earn.”*

**Meaning.** This completes the warning begun in the previous verse: the settled heedlessness described there has an end. Al-Ṭabarī explains that those who ignore God’s proofs and reject the Hereafter meet the Fire because of what they have done **[Ṭabarī]**. Al-Jalālayn specifies idolatry and disobedience among the deeds named by the verse **[Jalālayn]**. Ibn Kathīr reports al-Ḥasan’s description of people who beautified and praised worldly life until they were satisfied with it, while neglecting both the signs in creation and God’s commands **[Ibn Kathīr]**.

**Belief.** The passage attributes judgment to deeds and denial, not to an arbitrary fate; the warning follows the refusal to heed revelation.

**Reflection.** The verse invites its hearer to examine what present habits are preparing: comfort that eclipses accountability cannot provide a lasting refuge **[Maʿārif]**.

## 10:9

*“Truly those who believe and do righteous deeds, their Lord guides them by their faith; rivers run beneath them in Gardens of bliss.”*

**Meaning.** Faith and righteous action appear together: belief is lived out through good works, including the work of the heart and the limbs, done sincerely **[Saʿdī]**. “Guides them by their faith” has several complementary explanations. Al-Ṭabarī understands God to guide them to Paradise; al-Qurṭubī also records that their guidance is increased; The Study Quran mentions increased guidance, a light by which they are led on the Day of Resurrection, and guidance to the Garden as a reward **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**. The rivers and Gardens portray a lasting reward, while Maʿārif emphasizes that the fortunate recognize God’s signs and answer them with belief and good conduct **[Maʿārif]**.

**Reflection.** Faith is both the path by which a person is guided and the root from which righteous action grows **[Ibn Kathīr]**.

## 10:10

*“Their supplication therein shall be, ‘Glory be to You, O God!’ Their greeting therein shall be, ‘Peace,’ and the conclusion of their supplication shall be, ‘Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds!’”*

**Meaning.** The inhabitants of Paradise are described by a speech that begins in glorification, includes peace, and ends in praise. Al-Qurṭubī records that *daʿwā* can mean their supplication or request; they may ask in the language of glorification and conclude with praise, and their greeting of peace may be from God, the angels, or one another **[Qurṭubī]**. Al-Jalālayn similarly understands their prayer as asking for what they desire, which is granted, and their final words as praise of God **[Jalālayn]**. The Study Quran notes that commentators also take the supplication as magnification and praise, and describes peace as the greeting heard from God and the angels **[Study Quran]**. Maʿārif explains the apparent paradox: in Paradise, glorification itself becomes the language of the believers’ supplication **[Maʿārif]**.

**Reflection.** The passage imagines fulfillment without anxiety: desire is joined to reverence, and every blessing returns its recipients to gratitude **[Ibn Kathīr]**.
