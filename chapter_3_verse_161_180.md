## Verse 161

Verse 161 says: *It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war. And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment. Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged.*

In simple terms, the verse clears the prophets of the violation of the trust: a prophet does not unlawfully take from the spoils of war; whoever does so will bear it on the Day of Judgment — then every soul will be repaid in full for what it did, and none will be wronged.

The verse turns from the shura and the trust to the doctrine of the *ghulul* — the misappropriation of the spoils. Ibn Kathir: "It is not for any Prophet to illegally take a part of the booty, and whosoever deceives his companions over the booty, he shall bring forth on the Day of Resurrection that which he took." The verse's *wa ma kana li-nabiyyin an yaghulla* — and it is not for a Prophet to misappropriate — is the exoneration; the *wa man yaghlul ya'ti bima ghalla yawma al-qiyamah* — and whoever misappropriates will come with what he misappropriated on the Day of Resurrection — is the recompense; and the *thumma tuwaffa kullu nafsin ma kasabat wa hum la yuzlamun* — then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned and they will not be wronged — is the justice.

"It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war" — *wa ma kana li-nabiyyin an yaghulla* — and it is not for a Prophet to commit ghulul, to breach the trust. The *ghulul* — the misappropriation, the treachery with the spoils — is the violation: the *yaghulla* — he misappropriates — the *ghulul* — the taking of the trust. Ibn Kathir: Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid and Al-Hasan — "It is not for a Prophet to breach the trust." The verse's *ma kana li-nabiyyin* — it is not for a Prophet — is the categorical: the prophecy is incompatible with the *ghulul*; the *li-nabiyyin* — for a Prophet — the absolute of the office — the *ghulul* is the breach of the prophecy's character. Ibn Jarir/Ibn 'Abbas: the verse was revealed in connection with a red robe missing from the spoils of Badr — some said the Messenger might have taken it; "This Ayah exonerates the Messenger of Allah of all types of deceit and treachery, be it returning what was entrusted with him, dividing the spoils of war, etc." (Ibn Kathir).

"And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment" — *wa man yaghlul ya'ti bima ghalla yawma al-qiyamah* — and whoever misappropriates will come with what he misappropriated on the Day of Resurrection. The *yaghlul* — misappropriates — and the *ya'ti bima ghalla* — will come with what he took — the *ghulul* carried: the misappropriated thing produced on the Day — the *ya'ti'* — the coming — with the *ghalla* — the taken — the burden of the Day. Ibn Kathir: the verse "contains a stern warning and threat against Ghulul" — and the hadiths that prohibit such practice. The verse's *yawma al-qiyamah* — the Day of Resurrection — is the disclosure: the *ghulul* is brought to the Day — the misappropriated wealth, the breached trust — the *ya'ti* — the coming with — the burden of the account.

"Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged" — *thumma tuwaffa kullu nafsin ma kasabat wa hum la yuzlamun* — then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned, and they will not be wronged. The *tawfi'* — the full payment — the *kull nafs* — every soul — the *ma kasabat* — what it earned — the *waf* — the complete; the *la yuzlamun* — they will not be wronged — the justice. Ibn Kathir: "Then every person shall be paid in full what he has earned, and they shall not be dealt with unjustly." The verse's *tuwaffa* — is paid in full — is the exactness: the account is the full — the *kasb* — the earning — the *tawfi'* — the complete payment; and the *la yuzlamun* — not wronged — is the justice of the accounting: no soul is wronged — the *yaghull* — the misappropriator — brought with the *ghalla* — and the *tawfi'* — the full — the justice of the Day.

The hadiths of the warning, per Ibn Kathir: Imam Ahmad/Abu Malik Al-Ashja'i — "The worst Ghulul with Allah is a yard of land... when you find two neighbors in a land or home and one of them illegally acquires a yard of his neighbor's land. When he does, he will be tied with it from the seven earths until the Day of Resurrection." Imam Ahmad/Abu Humayd As-Sa'idi — the Zakah collector Ibn Al-Lutbiyyah returned and said, "This (portion) is for you and this has been given to me as a gift" — the Prophet stood on the Minbar: "What is the matter with a man whom we appoint to collect Zakah... By Him in Whose Hand my life is, whoever takes anything from the resources of the Zakah (unlawfully), he will carry it on his neck on the Day of Resurrection; if it be a camel, it will be grunting; if a cow, it will be mooing; and if a sheep, it will be bleating." — and raised his hands till the whiteness of his armpits, saying three times, "O Allah! Haven't I conveyed Your Message."

For the reader, the verse is the exoneration of the prophets and the warning of the trust. The believer is taught: the *ghulul* — the misappropriation — is the breach of the prophecy's character: the *ma kana li-nabiyyin* — not for a Prophet — the categorical of the office; and the *yaghlul* — whoever misappropriates — the *ya'ti bima ghalla* — the coming with the taken — the burden of the Day. The verse's *tuwaffa kull nafs* — the full payment of every soul — is the justice: the *kasb* — the earned — the *tawfi'* — the complete — and the *la yuzlamun* — the not-wronged — the justice of the account. And the *ghulal* — the misappropriated — the *ya'ti* — the coming with — the recompense of the breach. Verse 178 says: *Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment.*

In simple terms, the verse warns the disbelievers: do not think that Our delaying of punishment is good for them — We grant them respite only so they may increase in sin — and a humiliating punishment awaits them.

The verse is the warning against the respite's misinterpretation. Ibn Kathir: "the postponing of the punishment is not good for them; it is only so they may increase in sinfulness — a disgraceful torment." The verse's *wa la yahsabanna alladhina kafaru annama numli lahum khayrun li-anfusihim* — let not those who disbelieved think that what We grant them is good for their selves — is the thought; the *innama numli lahum liyazdadu ithman* — We only grant them respite so they may increase in sin — is the purpose; and the *wa lahum 'adhabun muhin* — and for them is a humiliating punishment — is the punishment.

"Do not let the disbelievers think that We delaying punishment is good for them" — *wa la yahsabanna alladhina kafaru annama numli lahum khayrun li-anfusihim* — and let not those who disbelieved think that what We give them (respite) is good for their selves. The *hasb* — the thinking, the supposing — is the negation: the *la yahsabanna* — let them not think — the *numli* — We give respite, We delay — the *khayr* — the good — the *li-anfusihim* — for their selves — the thought refuted. Ibn Kathir: "the postponing of the punishment is not good for them." The verse's *numli* — We give respite — is the delay: the *imla'* — the granting, the lengthening — the *khayr* — the good — supposed — the *li-anfus* — for the selves — the thought of the good — refuted — the delay — not the good — the *hashb* — the thinking — denied.

"We give them respite only to let them increase in sin" — *innama numli lahum liyazdadu ithman* — We only grant them respite so they may increase in sin. The *innama* — only — is the restriction: the *numli* — We grant — the *liyazdadu* — so they increase — the *ithm* — the sin — the *ziyadah* — the increase — the purpose of the delay. Ibn Kathir: "It is only so they may increase in sinfulness" — the parallels: 23:55–56, 68:44, 9:85. The verse's *innama* — only — is the restriction: the respite — not the good — the *liyazdadu* — to increase — the *ithm* — the sin — the delay's purpose — the increase — of the sin — the *imla'* — the grant — the growth — of the defiance. Al-Jalalayn's parallel: "We grant them respite only so that they increase in their sins."

"and they will suffer a humiliating punishment" — *wa lahum 'adhabun muhin* — and for them is a humiliating punishment. The *'adhab* — the punishment — is the recompense: the *muhin* — the humiliating, the disgraceful — the *lahum* — for them — the *'adhab* — the torment — of the increased. Ibn Kathir: "a disgraceful torment." The verse's *muhin* — the humiliating — is the quality: the *'adhab* — the punishment — the *muhin* — the degrading — the *ithm* — the sin — increased — the *'adhab* — the humiliating — the recompense.

The verse's context: the respite and the increase (178) — the *numli* — the grant — the *liyazdadu* — the increase — the *ithm* — the sin — the *'adhab muhin* — the humiliating — the warning of the delay. Ibn Kathir's frame: the *imla'* — the postponement — the *khayr* — the good — mistaken — the *ithma* — the sin — increased — and the *'adhab* — the humiliation — the parallels of the delay's purpose.

## Verse 179

Verse 179 says: *Allah would not leave the believers in the condition you were in, until He distinguished the good from the evil ˹among you˺. Nor would Allah ˹directly˺ reveal to you the unseen, but He chooses whoever He wills as a messenger. So believe in Allah and His messengers. And if you are faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺, you will receive a great reward.*

In simple terms, the verse states the divine method: Allah will not leave the believers in the state you are now until He distinguishes the good from the bad; and He does not disclose the unseen to you, but He chooses from His messengers whoever He wills — so believe in Allah and His messengers, and if you believe and fear Allah, you will have a great reward.

The verse is the doctrine of the distinguishing and the unseen. Ibn Kathir: "Allah will not leave the believers in the state in which you are now until He distinguishes the wicked from the good" — He allows a calamity to happen and during it His friend becomes known and His enemy exposed; "Nor will Allah disclose to you the secrets of the Unseen" — you do not have access to Allah's knowledge of His creation to distinguish believer from hypocrite except by the signs He uncovers; "but Allah chooses of His Messengers whom He wills" — (72:26–27) reveals the Unseen to the chosen Messenger; "So believe in Allah and His Messengers... and if you believe and fear Allah, then for you there is a great reward." The verse's *ma kana Allahu liyadhara al-mu'minina 'ala ma antum 'alayhi hatta yamiza al-khabitha mina al-tayyib* — Allah would not leave the believers in the state you are on until He distinguishes the bad from the good — is the distinguishing; the *wa ma kana Allahu liyutli'akum 'ala al-ghayb* — and Allah would not disclose to you the unseen — is the unseen; the *wa lakinna Allaha yajtabi min rusulihi man yasha'* — but Allah chooses of His messengers whom He wills — is the choosing; and the *fa'minu billahi wa rusulihi* — so believe in Allah and His messengers — is the command.

"Allah would not leave the believers as you are ˹now˺ until He distinguishes the good from the bad" — *ma kana Allahu liyadhara al-mu'minina 'ala ma antum 'alayhi hatta yamiza al-khabitha mina al-tayyib* — Allah was not to leave the believers in the state you are on until He distinguishes the bad from the good. The *tamyiz* — the distinguishing — is the method: the *yamiza* — He distinguishes — the *khabith* — the bad — from the *tayyib* — the good — the separating — of the two. Ibn Kathir: "He allows a calamity to happen and during it His friend becomes known and His enemy exposed, the patient believer recognized and the sinful hypocrite revealed." The verse's *al-khabitha mina al-tayyib* — the bad from the good — is the two: the *khabith* — the wicked — the *tayyib* — the good — the *miza* — the sifting, the distinguishing — the *tamyiz* — the separation — of the two. The verse's *'ala ma antum 'alayhi* — on the state you are on — is the present: the *hatta* — until — the *yamiza* — the distinguishing — the state of the believers — the uncertainty — the *tamyiz* — the clarity — of the day. Mujahid: distinguished between them during the day of Uhud; Qatadah: in Jihad and Hijrah (Ibn Kathir).

"Nor would Allah disclose to you what is unseen" — *wa ma kana Allahu liyutli'akum 'ala al-ghayb* — and Allah was not to disclose to you the unseen. The *itla'* — the disclosure, the informing — is the negation: the *liyutli'akum* — to disclose to you — the *ghayb* — the unseen — the not-disclosure — to the people. Ibn Kathir: "you do not have access to Allah's knowledge of His creation to distinguish believer from hypocrite except by the signs He uncovers to you." The verse's *ghayb* — the unseen — is the hidden: the *itla'* — the informing — negated — the *ghayb* — the unseen — not disclosed — the *tamyiz* — the distinguishing — by the signs — not the unseen.

"But Allah chooses whoever He wills from among His messengers" — *wa lakinna Allaha yajtabi min rusulihi man yasha'* — but Allah chooses of His messengers whom He wills. The *ijtiba'* — the choosing, the selection — is the divine: the *yajtabi* — He selects — the *min rusulihi* — from His messengers — the *man yasha'* — whom He wills — the choice of the divine. Ibn Kathir: "Allah chooses of His Messengers whom He wills" — the reference to 72:26–27: "He is the Knower of the Unseen, and He does not disclose His Unseen to anyone — except a Messenger whom He has chosen." The verse's *yajtabi* — He selects — is the choice: the *rusul* — the messengers — the *man yasha'* — whom He wills — the *ijtiba'* — the selection — of the divine — the *ghayb* — the unseen — disclosed — to the chosen.

"So believe in Allah and His messengers. And if you believe and are mindful ˹of Allah˺, you will have a mighty reward" — *fa'minu billahi wa rusulihi wa in tu'minu wa tattaqu falakum ajrun 'azim* — so believe in Allah and His messengers, and if you believe and fear (Him), then for you is a great reward. The *iman* — the belief — and the *taqwa* — the fear — are the two: the *aminu billah wa rusulihi* — believe in Allah and His messengers — the *in tu'minu wa tattaqu* — if you believe and fear — the *ajr 'azim* — the great reward. Ibn Kathir: "So believe in Allah and His Messengers... and if you believe and fear Allah, then for you there is a great reward." The verse's *ajr 'azim* — the mighty reward — is the wage: the *iman* — the belief — and the *taqwa* — the mindfulness — the *ajr* — the reward — the *'azim* — the mighty.

The verse's context: the *tamyiz* — the distinguishing — the *ghayb* — the unseen — the *ijtiba'* — the selection — and the *iman* — the belief — the *ajr* — the reward. Ibn Kathir's frame: the *musibah* of Uhud — the *tamyiz* — the distinction — of the believers and the hypocrites — the *ghayb* — the unseen — not disclosed — the *ijtiba'* — the chosen messenger — the *iman* — the belief — and the *ajr* — the reward.

For the reader, the verse is the distinguishing and the unseen. The believer is taught: the *ma kana Allahu liyadhara* — the not-leaving — the believers in the present state — until the *yamiza* — the distinguishing — the *khabith* from the *tayyib* — the bad from the good — the *tamyiz* — the sifting — of the day (Uhud); and the *liyutli'akum 'ala al-ghayb* — the not-disclosure of the unseen — the *ghayb* — not known — the signs — the method — and the *yajtabi min rusulihi* — the chosen messenger — the *ijtiba'* — the selection — the *ghayb* — revealed — to the chosen. And the *aminu billah wa rusulihi* — the belief — the *tu'minu wa tattaqu* — the faith and the fear — the *ajr 'azim* — the mighty reward. The believer's program: the *iman* — the belief — and the *taqwa* — the mindfulness — the *tamyiz* — the distinguishing — through the signs — not the unseen — the *ijtiba'* — the selection — of the messengers — the *ajr 'azim* — the reward of the faith and the fear.

## Verse 180

Verse 180 says: *And do not let those who ˹greedily˺ withhold Allah’s bounties think it is good for them—in fact, it is bad for them! They will be leashed ˹by their necks˺ on the Day of Judgment with whatever ˹wealth˺ they used to withhold. And Allah is the ˹sole˺ inheritor of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do.*

In simple terms, the verse warns the miserly: do not think that the hoarding of Allah's bounty is good for them — rather, it is bad; on the Day of Judgment, what they hoarded will be collared around their necks; to Allah alone belongs the inheritance of the heavens and the earth — and He is All-Aware of what you do.

The verse is the censure of the stinginess and the warning of the collar. Ibn Kathir: "the miser should not think that collecting money benefits him — it harms him in his religion and worldly affairs"; "the things that they were stingy with shall be tied to their necks like a collar on the Day of Resurrection"; "To Allah belongs the inheritance of the heavens and the earth" — spend of that whereof He has made you trustees (57:7) — "Allah is Well-Acquainted with all that you do." The verse's *wala yahsabanna alladhina yabkhaluna bima atahumu Allahu min fadlihi huwa khayran lahum* — and let not those who are stingy with what Allah gave them of His bounty think it is good for them — is the thought; the *bal huwa sharrun lahum* — rather, it is evil for them — is the correction; the *sayutawwaquna ma bakhiłu bihi yawma al-qiyamah* — they will be collared with what they were stingy with on the Day of Resurrection — is the collar; the *walillahi mithathu al-samawati wa al-ard* — and to Allah is the inheritance of the heavens and the earth — is the ownership; and the *wa Allahu bima ta'maluna khabir* — and Allah is All-Aware of what you do — is the awareness.

"Do not let those who are stingy with what Allah has granted them out of His bounty think that it is good for them" — *wala yahsabanna alladhina yabkhaluna bima atahumu Allahu min fadlihi huwa khayran lahum* — let not those who are stingy with what Allah gave them of His bounty think it is good for them. The *bukhl* — the stinginess, the miserliness — is the state: the *yabkhalun* — they are stingy — the *bima atahum* — with what He gave them — the *min fadlih* — of His bounty — the *khayr* — the good — supposed — the *hashb* — the thinking — denied. Ibn Kathir: "the miser should not think that collecting money benefits him — it harms him in his religion and worldly affairs." The verse's *hayr* — good — is the supposed: the *la yahsabanna* — let them not think — the *khayr* — the good — the *lahum* — for them — the *bukhl* — the hoarding — mistaken — the *khayr* — the good — denied.

"Rather, it is bad for them" — *bal huwa sharrun lahum* — rather, it is evil for them. The *bal* — rather — is the correction: the *sharr* — the evil — the *lahum* — for them — the *sharr* — the harm — of the hoarding — the correction of the thought. Ibn Kathir: "it harms him in his religion and worldly affairs." The verse's *sharr* — the evil — is the truth: the *khayr* — the good — denied — the *sharr* — the evil — affirmed — the *bukhl* — the hoarding — the harm — to the selves.

"On the Day of Judgment, they will be collared with what they were stingy with" — *sayutawwaquna ma bakhiłu bihi yawma al-qiyamah* — they will be collared with what they were stingy with on the Day of Resurrection. The *tawq* — the collar — is the recompense: the *yutawwaqun* — they will be collared — the *ma bakhiłu* — what they were stingy with — the *yawm al-qiyamah* — the Day of Resurrection — the collar of the hoard. Ibn Kathir: "the things that they were stingy with shall be tied to their necks like a collar on the Day of Resurrection." The verse's *tawq* — the collar — is the burden: the *yutawwaqun* — they will be collared — the *bukhila bihi* — the hoarded — the *yawm al-qiyamah* — the Day — the collar — of the wealth — the burden.

The hadiths of the collar: Al-Bukhari/Abu Hurayrah — "Whoever Allah makes wealthy and he does not pay the Zakah due on his wealth, then on the Day of Resurrection his wealth will be made in the likeness of a bald-headed poisonous male snake with two black spots over the eyes. The snake will encircle his neck and bite his cheeks and proclaim, 'I am your wealth, I am your treasure'" (Al-Bukhari; also Ibn Hibban's Sahih). Imam Ahmad/'Abdullah — the snake follows the one who does not pay Zakah — "I am your treasure" — (At-Tirmidhi: hasan sahih; An-Nasa'i; Ibn Majah).

"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the inheritance of the heavens and the earth" — *walillahi mithathu al-samawati wa al-ard* — and to Allah is the inheritance of the heavens and the earth. The *mithath* — the inheritance — is the ownership: the *lillah* — to Allah — the *samawat wa al-ard* — the heavens and the earth — the *mithath* — the inheritance — of the divine. Ibn Kathir: "spend of that whereof He has made you trustees" (57:7). The verse's *mithath* — the inheritance — is the return: the *samawat* and the *ard* — the heavens and the earth — the *lillah* — to Allah — the heritage — the divine ownership — the wealth — the trust.

"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do" — *wa Allahu bima ta'maluna khabir* — and Allah is All-Aware of what you do. The *khabir* — the All-Aware, the All-Knowing — is the awareness: the *bima ta'malun* — of what you do — the deeds — aware — the *khabir* — the All-Aware — the works — seen. Ibn Kathir: "Allah is Well-Acquainted with all that you do" — "with your intentions and what your hearts conceal." The verse's *khabir* — the All-Aware — is the witness: the *ta'malun* — you do — the *bima* — with what — the deeds — the *khabir* — the knowledge — of the works.

The verse's context: the *bukhl* — the stinginess — and the *tawq* — the collar — the *khayr* and the *sharr* — the good and the evil — the *mithath* — the inheritance — and the *khabir* — the All-Aware. Ibn Kathir's frame: "The Censure of Selfishness, and Warning Against it" — the hoarding of the bounty — the harm of the religion and the world — the collar of the Day — the ownership of the divine — the awareness of the deeds.

For the reader, the verse is the censure of the stinginess and the warning of the collar. The believer is taught: the *la yahsabanna* — the not-thinking — the *khayr* — the good — of the *bukhl* — the hoarding — denied; the *bal sharr* — rather evil — the *sharr* — the harm — of the hoard — to the religion and the world. And the *yutawwaqun* — the collared — with the *bakhil bihi* — the hoarded — the *yawm al-qiyamah* — the Day — the collar — the snake — the "I am your wealth" — the burden of the hoard. And the *mithath* — the inheritance — the *lillah* — to Allah — the heavens and the earth — the ownership — the *trust* — the spending of the bounty — and the *khabir* — the All-Aware — of the deeds. The believer's program: the *bukhl* — the stinginess — avoided — the *zakat* — the purification — of the wealth — the *mithath* — the inheritance — the divine — the *infaq* — the spending — the *khabir* — the All-Aware — the deeds — seen — the *yutawwaqun* — the collar — avoided — the *khayr* — the good — of the *infaq* — the charity.

- Ibn Kathir on 3:179 — "Allah will not leave the believers in the state in which you are now until He distinguishes the wicked from the good": He allows a calamity to happen and during it His friend becomes known and His enemy exposed, the patient believer recognized and the sinful hypocrite revealed; refers to Uhud — Allah tested the believers, making known the faith, endurance, patience, firmness and obedience, and exposed the hypocrites in their defiance, reverting from Jihad, and the treachery; Mujahid: distinguished between them during the day of Uhud; Qatadah: in Jihad and Hijrah; "Nor will Allah disclose to you the secrets of the Unseen" — you do not have access to Allah's knowledge of His creation to distinguish believer from hypocrite except by the signs He uncovers; "but Allah chooses of His Messengers whom He wills" (72:26-27) — reveals the Unseen to the chosen Messenger; "So believe in Allah and His Messengers... and if you believe and fear Allah, then for you there is a great reward." https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-176-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir and Al-Jalalayn on 3:180 — "The Censure of Selfishness, and Warning Against it": the miser should not think that collecting money benefits him — it harms him in his religion and worldly affairs; "the things that they were stingy with shall be tied to their necks like a collar on the Day of Resurrection"; Al-Bukhari/Abu Hurayrah: "Whoever Allah makes wealthy and he does not pay the Zakah due on his wealth, then on the Day of Resurrection his wealth will be made in the likeness of a bald-headed poisonous male snake with two black spots over the eyes. The snake will encircle his neck and bite his cheeks and proclaim, 'I am your wealth, I am your treasure'" (Al-Bukhari; Ibn Hibban's Sahih); Imam Ahmad/'Abdullah — the snake follows the one who does not pay Zakah — "I am your treasure" (at-Tirmidhi: hasan sahih; An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah); "And to Allah belongs the inheritance of the heavens and the earth" — spend of that whereof He has made you trustees (57:7) — so it benefits you on the Day of Return; "Allah is Well-Acquainted with all that you do" — with your intentions and what your hearts conceal. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-180-sora-3.html
