## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:161

> It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war. And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment. Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged.

**Commentary:**

### Prophetic Integrity

Allah declares that it is impossible for a prophet to betray the public trust by concealing something from the spoils of war. The word *yaghull* refers to *ghulūl*: secretly taking, withholding, or misappropriating property entrusted to the community. Prophets are the most trustworthy of people, and revelation protects their public integrity.

The verse also refutes rumours that arose when some spoils were missing. A setback must not lead believers to accuse their messenger without evidence.

### Public Wealth Is a Trust

Although the immediate context is war booty, the principle is broad. Public funds, charitable donations, organisational property, state resources, and another person’s possessions are all trusts. Concealing a share, manipulating records, or taking what has not been assigned is betrayal.

A person may not justify corruption by claiming that the amount is small or that everyone else does it. What is hidden from people is not hidden from Allah.

### Accountability in the Hereafter

The one who misappropriates will come with what he took on the Day of Judgment. This imagery makes the hidden act publicly visible and shows that unlawful gain becomes a burden. Wealth obtained through betrayal does not disappear from the moral record when it disappears from the hand.

The believer should return property, seek forgiveness, and repair harm rather than relying on private regret alone.

### No Soul Is Wronged

Every soul will be paid in full for what it has done. Allah’s judgement is exact: neither a victim’s loss nor a perpetrator’s hidden act is overlooked. The verse gives confidence to those whose rights were taken and warning to those who abuse authority.

Divine justice includes both reward and punishment, and Allah does not punish one person for another’s deed.

### Leadership and Trust

The verse is especially important for leaders. Religious or political office does not create an exemption from accountability; it increases responsibility. A leader must separate personal property from public resources, disclose decisions honestly, and ensure that distribution is fair.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:152 and 3:161 on the Uḥud setback, spoils, obedience, and the prohibition of betrayal.
- Qur’an 4:58 and 8:27 on returning trusts and not betraying Allah, the Messenger, or entrusted responsibilities.
- Qur’an 2:188 on consuming wealth wrongfully and bribing authorities.
- Qur’an 59:7 on fair distribution of public wealth.
- Qur’an 99:7–8 and 6:164 on the complete record and individual accountability.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports warning against *ghulūl*, betrayal of public property, and taking even a small item from communal spoils.
- For *ghulūl*, prophetic trustworthiness, public responsibility, and the Day of Resurrection, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:161.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:162

> Are those who seek Allah’s pleasure like those who deserve Allah’s wrath? Hell is their home. What an evil destination!

**Commentary:**

### Two Opposite Paths

The verse places two groups side by side: those who pursue Allah’s pleasure and those who earn His wrath. They cannot be equal, even if they appear similar in wealth, influence, or outward circumstances. The decisive difference is the direction of the heart and the deeds that follow it.

Seeking Allah’s pleasure is *ibtighāʾ riḍwān Allāh*: directing worship, obedience, sacrifice, and moral effort toward His acceptance. It is not merely wanting a favourable worldly outcome.

### Allah’s Pleasure and Human Approval

People often choose actions to gain praise, avoid criticism, or preserve status. The verse teaches believers to ask a deeper question: will this action please Allah? Human approval changes quickly, but Allah’s judgement is true and lasting.

Seeking divine pleasure does not mean ignoring people’s rights. Allah is pleased by justice, honesty, mercy, and fulfilment of trusts—not by private claims of piety that harm others.

### Deserving Wrath

Allah’s wrath is deserved by persistent rejection, hypocrisy, betrayal, and deliberate wrongdoing after truth becomes clear. The verse should produce fear in the believer, but not despair. The door of repentance remains open during life, and sincere repentance transforms the servant’s direction.

A person must not declare a specific individual destined for Hell merely because of an outward mistake. Final judgement belongs to Allah, who knows knowledge, intention, opportunity, and repentance.

### Hell as a Destination

Hell is described as their home and an evil destination. This is a warning that choices have consequences. The world is not the final court in which injustice is always corrected; the Hereafter completes the moral order.

### The Context of Uḥud

In the surrounding passage, the distinction includes those who obeyed Allah and His Messenger and those who pursued worldly interests or spread hypocrisy. The verse calls the community to measure success by Allah’s pleasure, not simply by military victory or material gain.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:161–163 on betrayal, divine judgement, Allah’s pleasure, and differing ranks.
- Qur’an 9:72 on Allah’s pleasure as greater than the delights of Paradise.
- Qur’an 48:29 on the signs of believers and Allah’s pleasure with them.
- Qur’an 98:8 on the reward of those who fear Allah and seek His pleasure.
- Qur’an 5:80 and 4:93 on actions that lead to Allah’s wrath and punishment.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 and 25:70 on repentance and the transformation of sins into good deeds.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on sincerity, seeking Allah’s face, and the danger of showing off.
- For *riḍwān*, *sakhaṭ*, divine pleasure, wrath, and the contrast between worldly appearance and final destiny, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:162.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:163

> They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in the sight of Allah. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do.

**Commentary:**

### Degrees before Allah

The two paths mentioned in the previous verse are not equal, and the people within them are not all identical. They have varying degrees before Allah. Some believers exceed others in faith, sincerity, knowledge, patience, sacrifice, and good character; wrongdoing and rejection also have differing levels of seriousness.

The verse directs attention away from superficial measures of rank. Wealth, ancestry, office, and public fame do not determine a person’s standing with Allah.

### Faith Produces Action

Degrees are connected to what people do. Faith is not an empty claim, and good works are not independent of belief. A person’s rank rises through sincere obedience, repentance, service, and perseverance.

This should encourage excellence without producing arrogance. The one who is given more knowledge or opportunity has greater responsibility, not permission to despise others.

### Allah Sees the Whole Deed

Allah is All-Seeing of what they do. He sees the outward action and knows the inner intention, the opportunity available, the difficulty faced, and the harm or benefit produced. Human beings can rank people by visible achievement, but Allah’s evaluation is complete.

A small deed done sincerely may outweigh a celebrated deed performed for reputation.

### Justice and Hope

The verse establishes justice: no one is placed in a degree without wisdom. It also gives hope: a person can rise through repentance and sustained good action. Yesterday’s weakness does not prevent tomorrow’s excellence.

### The Community’s Responsibility

Muslims should honour genuine scholarship, sacrifice, and service, while avoiding class systems based on wealth or lineage. Communities should make room for different forms of goodness: worship, teaching, earning lawfully, caring for family, defending the vulnerable, and reconciling people.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:162–163 on Allah’s pleasure, wrath, and varying degrees.
- Qur’an 6:132 and 46:19 on degrees according to deeds.
- Qur’an 17:21 on the superiority of some over others in the Hereafter and the need not to envy worldly differences.
- Qur’an 58:11 on Allah raising believers and those given knowledge in rank.
- Qur’an 49:13 on honour being measured by *taqwā*, not ancestry.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on repentance and rising after wrongdoing.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on levels of faith, sincerity, and the different ranks of people in Paradise.
- For *darajāt*, *baṣīr*, rank by deeds, intention, and responsibility, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:163.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:164

> Indeed, Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom. For indeed they had previously been clearly astray.

**Commentary:**

### The Greatest Favour

Allah identifies the Messenger ﷺ as a great favour to the believers. Guidance is not something humanity can manufacture through power, philosophy, or social custom. Allah sent revelation and a messenger who embodied it, explained it, and taught people how to live by it.

The favour is especially great because it leads to eternal salvation, not merely to temporary prosperity.

### From Among Them

The Prophet ﷺ was from among the people he addressed. They knew his language, ancestry, character, and life before prophethood. His humanity made his example accessible, while his revelation made the source of guidance divine.

A messenger from among a people can be understood and followed in ordinary life: worship, family relationships, trade, leadership, patience, and hardship.

### Reciting the Revelations

The first task mentioned is reciting Allah’s signs, *āyāt*. The Qur’an must be heard, recited, understood, and allowed to challenge the heart. Recitation is not only sound; it is communication of guidance and evidence.

The believer returns to revelation when cultural habits, personal desire, or social pressure conflict with Allah’s command.

### Purification

The Prophet ﷺ purifies people through *tazkiyah*: cleansing belief from shirk, character from arrogance and envy, and conduct from injustice and sin. Purification is both removal and growth. A heart is cleansed of corruption and cultivated with sincerity, patience, gratitude, and mercy.

Knowledge without purification can become a tool of pride, while spiritual feeling without knowledge can become misguidance.

### The Book and Wisdom

The Book is the revealed Qur’an. Wisdom, *ḥikmah*, includes sound understanding, correct application, and the Prophetic Sunnah that explains and embodies revelation. The Prophet ﷺ taught the verses and demonstrated their meaning through practice.

This is why Islamic learning requires both textual knowledge and ethical formation.

### From Clear Misguidance to Guidance

Before revelation, the Arabs were in manifest error: idolatry, tribal injustice, exploitation of the weak, and moral confusion were widespread. The verse does not deny every good trait they possessed; it shows that good customs without sound worship and justice cannot provide complete guidance.

The transformation began through revelation, purification, teaching, and lived example.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:129 and 2:151 on the Messenger’s tasks of recitation, purification, teaching, and wisdom.
- Qur’an 62:2 on the same Prophetic mission among the unlettered.
- Qur’an 33:21 on the Prophet ﷺ as an excellent example.
- Qur’an 16:43–44 on the Messenger explaining revelation.
- Qur’an 59:7 on accepting what the Messenger gives and avoiding what he forbids.
- Qur’an 91:9–10 on purification and corruption of the soul.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the Prophet’s character, teaching, and his statement that he was sent to complete noble character.
- For *mann*, *rasūl*, *āyāt*, *tazkiyah*, *kitāb*, and *ḥikmah*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:164.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:165

> Why is it when you suffered casualties ˹at Uḥud˺—although you had made your enemy suffer twice as much ˹at Badr˺—you protested, “How could this be?”? Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “It is because of your disobedience.” Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything.

**Commentary:**

### The Question after Uḥud

The believers struggled to understand how they could suffer casualties at Uḥud after receiving victory at Badr. The Qur’an does not dismiss their pain, but it corrects their assumption that divine support means that a community will never experience a setback.

The answer points to their disobedience: some abandoned the position assigned to them and disputed about the command. The loss had a moral and practical cause.

### Comparing Badr and Uḥud

At Badr, the believers had inflicted twice the casualties they later suffered at Uḥud. The comparison teaches that previous victory is not a permanent entitlement. A community cannot rely on yesterday’s success while neglecting today’s obedience and discipline.

Favour should produce gratitude and greater responsibility, not complacency.

### Divine Power and Human Responsibility

Allah is Most Capable of everything. He could have granted victory despite the disobedience, but He allowed the consequences to unfold as a lesson. His power does not cancel human responsibility, and human causes do not operate independently of His will.

The verse therefore avoids two errors: attributing everything only to human strength, or using divine decree to deny the consequences of wrongdoing.

### A Setback Can Be Instruction

The setback exposed weaknesses in obedience, leadership, intention, and emotional control. It forced the believers to distinguish between following Allah and following immediate expectation. A loss can become a means of reform when the community asks what it did wrong rather than merely blaming the enemy.

### Not Every Victim Is Personally Guilty

The verse addresses the community’s disobedience; it should not be used to accuse every wounded person or martyr of individual sin. Innocent people can suffer from the consequences of collective decisions. Allah knows each person’s intention and will reward sacrifice and patience.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:121–152 on the planning of Uḥud, the archers’ command, and the turning point.
- Qur’an 3:140 and 8:25 on alternating days and the danger of collective trials.
- Qur’an 8:26 and 8:41 on Badr, Allah’s support, and the distribution of spoils.
- Qur’an 8:53 on Allah not changing a blessing until people change what is within themselves.
- Qur’an 42:30 on calamity connected to what people’s hands have earned, together with Allah’s pardon.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on Badr, Uḥud, and the archers’ disobedience.
- For *muṣībah*, *aṣāba*, twice the casualties, disobedience, divine capability, and collective responsibility, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:165.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:166

> So what you suffered on the day the two armies met was by Allah’s Will, so that He might distinguish the ˹true˺ believers

**Commentary:**

### Uḥud under Allah’s Will

The casualties and confusion at Uḥud occurred by Allah’s permission. This does not mean that Allah loves disobedience or that the people were forced to abandon the command. It means that no event escapes His knowledge, power, and wisdom.

The believers acted through real choices, and Allah allowed those choices and their consequences to become part of the test.

### Distinguishing True Believers

The trial distinguished true believers by making their responses visible. Some remained obedient, some returned after fleeing, some repented, and some exposed hypocrisy. Faith was not measured by confident speech before the battle but by conduct under pressure.

Allah’s purpose was not to discover something unknown to Him. The event established evidence in the world and taught the community what qualities it needed to cultivate.

### Divine Wisdom in Pain

A painful event can carry purposes beyond what the sufferers immediately see: correction, purification, exposure of hypocrisy, elevation of the patient, and protection from arrogance. Believers do not claim to know every hidden wisdom, but they trust that Allah’s wisdom is never unjust.

This trust does not eliminate grief or the duty to prevent future harm.

### Lessons for Communities

Communities should examine themselves after crisis. Who remained truthful? Who served the wounded? Who spread rumours? Who took responsibility? Who returned to Allah? Such questions can turn collective pain into moral clarity.

A community that refuses self-examination may repeat its failures even after reciting the story of Uḥud.

### The Continuation of the Verse

The next verse completes the contrast by mentioning those who were hypocritical. The two verses together show that the same event can strengthen sincere believers and expose concealed hypocrisy.

- Qur’an 64:11 and 57:22–23 on decree and the believer’s response to calamity.
- Qur’an 8:25 on a trial that may affect a community because of wrongdoing.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on Uḥud and the conduct of the believers during hardship.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:167

> and expose the hypocrites. When it was said to them, “Come fight in the cause of Allah or ˹at least˺ defend yourselves,” they replied, “If we had known there was fighting, we would have definitely gone with you.” They were closer to disbelief than to belief on that day—for saying with their mouths what was not in their hearts. Allah is All-Knowing of what they hide.

**Commentary:**

### Hypocrisy Exposed by Crisis

The trial of Uḥud exposed the hypocrites. They had presented themselves as part of the community, but when sacrifice became real they withdrew and then offered a false excuse. Crisis does not make Allah aware of something previously hidden; it reveals the reality of a person’s claim through action.

The leader of the hypocrites, ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy, withdrew with a group before the battle. Their departure weakened the believers but also separated insincere loyalty from sincere commitment.

### Fight or Defend

The invitation was to fight in Allah’s cause or at least defend the community. This wording shows that participation could take more than one form. Not everyone had to be a frontline fighter; supporting legitimate defence and protecting the community were also meaningful responsibilities.

The verse concerns a specific hostile military situation. It is not a command to force people into unlawful warfare or to condemn those with genuine exemptions.

### False Excuses

They claimed that they would have gone if they had known fighting was involved, although the circumstances made the danger clear. Their words were an evasion rather than an honest assessment. Hypocrisy often uses technically plausible language to conceal a heart that has already chosen self-preservation over truth.

Believers should speak honestly about ability, fear, family responsibilities, and legitimate limits rather than inventing excuses.

### Closer to Disbelief

They were closer to disbelief than belief on that day because their outward claim contradicted their inner attitude. This warns against treating affiliation, slogans, or public religious identity as proof of sincere faith.

It does not give ordinary Muslims permission to declare individuals hypocrites based on one mistake. Allah knows the heart, and specific judgements require revelation or clear evidence.

### Allah Knows What Is Hidden

Allah’s knowledge reaches motives, private calculations, and concealed resentment. A person may deceive a community for a time, but he cannot deceive Allah. This truth calls for sincerity and protects victims from thinking that injustice will remain hidden forever.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:166–168 on Uḥud exposing hypocrisy and the speech of those who stayed behind.
- Qur’an 9:67 and 63:1–3 on the characteristics and claims of hypocrites.
- Qur’an 2:8–10 and 4:142 on hidden disbelief and deceptive religious appearance.
- Qur’an 4:95 and 9:91 on legitimate exemptions and the difference between inability and hypocrisy.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports before harming people.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith describing signs of hypocrisy: lying, breaking promises, and betraying trusts.
- For *nifāq*, *yuʿallim*, *qitāl*, defence, hypocrisy under trial, and Allah’s knowledge of the hidden, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:167.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:168

> Those who sat at home, saying about their brothers, “Had they listened to us, they would not have been killed.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Try not to die[161] if what you say is true!”

**Commentary:**

### The Blame of Those Who Stayed Behind

The verse records the words of those who remained behind and then blamed the believers who were killed. Their statement was not compassionate mourning; it was an attempt to present cowardice as superior wisdom and to undermine those who answered the call of the Messenger ﷺ.

The Qur’an distinguishes these blame-givers from people who stayed behind for genuine reasons such as illness, disability, age, or family duty.

### The Rhetorical Challenge

“Try not to die if what you say is true!” is a rhetorical challenge. If remaining at home gives a person control over death, as they claimed, then let them prevent their own death. They cannot, because life and death are in Allah’s hands.

The statement is not an invitation to seek danger. It exposes the false certainty with which the hypocrites judged a complex event.

### Rejecting Counterfactual Arrogance

People often imagine that they would have avoided every harm if others had followed their advice. Sometimes mistakes really can be prevented through better planning, and communities should learn from them. But claiming complete control over the unseen produces arrogance and cruelty toward victims.

The correct response combines practical learning with humility before Allah’s decree.

### Brothers despite Failure

The wording refers to the slain as their brothers, showing that communal ties and inherited claims can remain outwardly while the heart is corrupted by hypocrisy. True brotherhood, however, is demonstrated by sincere support, mercy, and honouring the sacrifice of others.

### Consolation for the Bereaved

The verse helps the believers understand that the dead were not worthless or foolish because they died. Allah knows their intentions and will judge them with perfect justice. The living must not let hostile commentary turn grief into shame.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:156–158 and 3:166–168 on death, decree, Uḥud, and the speech of hypocrites.
- Qur’an 4:78 and 62:8 on the inevitability of the appointed term.
- Qur’an 57:22–23 and 64:11 on calamity, decree, and avoiding destructive regret.
- Qur’an 9:91 and 48:17 on genuine exemptions from fighting.
- Qur’an 3:169–171 on the life, joy, and provision of those killed in Allah’s cause.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith teaching that a believer should pursue what benefits him, seek Allah’s help, and not surrender to “if only” after an event has occurred.
- For *qaʿadū*, *akh*, the rhetorical challenge, *ajal*, and the difference between legitimate absence and hypocritical blame, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:168.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:169

> Never think of those martyred in the cause of Allah as dead. In fact, they are alive with their Lord, well provided for—

**Commentary:**

### The Reality beyond Sight

Allah forbids believers from thinking of those killed in His cause as dead in the ordinary sense. Their bodies have left worldly life, but their souls possess a special life with their Lord and receive provision. This is a reality of the unseen, not something measured by ordinary physical observation.

The verse gives honour and comfort to the martyrs’ families and corrects the assumption that death is annihilation.

### What Martyrdom Means

The phrase “in the cause of Allah” requires sincerity and a legitimate cause. A person does not become a martyr merely by dying violently or by giving himself a religious label. Suicide, terrorism, aggression, and the killing of innocents cannot be sanctified by this verse.

Allah knows who truly sought His pleasure and acted within His limits. Muslims may hope for martyrdom for a person whose outward life was righteous, but final certainty about individuals belongs to Allah unless revelation specifies it.

### Provision in the Intermediate Life

The martyrs’ provision belongs to the life of *barzakh*, the intermediate realm between death and resurrection. It is real, but its nature is not identical to worldly life. The believer affirms what revelation teaches without inventing details that Allah has not disclosed.

### Honour without Worship

Honouring martyrs does not mean worshipping them, praying to them, or claiming that they control worldly affairs. Their life is “with their Lord”; Allah remains the giver and the one worshipped. Their honour should encourage obedience to Allah, not the creation of practices contrary to tawḥīd.

### Courage and Consolation

The verse gives courage to those defending justice and consolation to those who lose loved ones in sincere service. The sacrifice is not wasted. Allah preserves its reward and grants the martyr a life and provision greater than worldly observers can see.

- Qur’an 4:29 and 5:32 on the sanctity of life and the prohibition of unlawful killing.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:170

> rejoicing in Allah’s bounties and being delighted for those yet to join them. There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.

**Commentary:**

### The Martyrs’ Joy

This verse continues the description of the martyrs. They rejoice in what Allah has granted them and are delighted for the believers who have not yet joined them. Their joy is not selfish; they wish goodness for their brothers and sisters who remain in the world.

The unseen life of the martyrs is one of honour, provision, safety, and happiness—not the loss or emptiness imagined by those who measure reality only through the senses.

### No Fear and No Grief

Fear concerns what lies ahead, while grief concerns what has passed or what was left behind. Allah removes both from the martyrs in their honoured state. They have no fear of the future and no grief over the worldly life they left, because Allah has shown them a reality greater than it.

For living believers, the verse points toward the goal of faith: security from ultimate loss through Allah’s mercy.

### Love for Those Who Follow

The martyrs’ delight for those yet to join them shows the continuing bond of faith. Righteous people do not want Paradise to be a private achievement; they hope for the salvation and honour of other believers.

This should shape the living community. It should invite people toward Allah with mercy, support families of the deceased, and avoid treating religious success as a competition for status.

### Hope without Presumption

The verse does not authorize anyone to claim certainty about the unseen condition of a specific person without evidence. It gives a general promise for those truly martyred in Allah’s cause. Believers combine hope with humility and pray for Allah’s acceptance.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:171

> They are joyful for receiving Allah’s grace and bounty, and that Allah does not deny the reward of the believers.

**Commentary:**

### Grace and Bounty

The martyrs rejoice in Allah’s *niʿmah* and *faḍl*: His grace, blessing, generosity, and favour. Their reward is not earned as a debt that limits Allah; it is granted through His generosity to sincere faith and sacrifice.

The language teaches believers to attribute success to Allah while still taking responsibility for obedience. Every good outcome is both a result of effort and a gift from the Lord who enabled the effort.

### No Good Deed Is Lost

Allah does not deny the reward of believers. The sacrifice of the martyrs is not erased by the apparent defeat at Uḥud, and the righteous action of any believer is not lost because people overlook it. Allah preserves intention, effort, patience, and hidden service.

This is a powerful consolation for people whose good work receives no public recognition.

### Reward Beyond the Battlefield

The verse begins with martyrs but ends with all believers. Its principle extends to every sincere act: prayer, charity, caring for parents, raising children, seeking knowledge, supporting the oppressed, and patiently resisting sin. The scale of the deed may differ, but Allah’s justice and generosity encompass it.

### Joy Grounded in Allah

The martyrs’ joy is not based on temporary praise, revenge, or worldly victory. It is grounded in Allah’s acceptance and generosity. This teaches believers to measure success by nearness to Allah rather than by the applause of people.

### Encouragement after Loss

The passage follows a painful account of defeat and death. It redirects the community from measuring Uḥud only by casualties to seeing the eternal reward of sincere believers. Loss in the world does not necessarily mean loss before Allah.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:172

> ˹As for˺ those who responded to the call of Allah and His Messenger after their injury,[162] those of them who did good and were mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have a great reward.

**Commentary:**

### Returning despite Injury

After Uḥud, the Prophet ﷺ called the believers to pursue the enemy even though they were wounded and exhausted. Those who responded demonstrated that the setback had not destroyed their faith. They were willing to obey, within their ability, rather than allow pain and fear to determine the future of the community.

Classical reports identify this expedition with Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad. Its purpose was to show the Quraysh that the Muslims still possessed strength and to deter another attack.

### Obedience after Failure

The phrase “after their injury” is significant. The believers had made mistakes and suffered consequences, yet they did not use the failure as a reason to withdraw permanently. They accepted correction and returned to obedience.

Repentance is proven by changed conduct. A person who says he regrets wrongdoing but refuses every opportunity to reform has not completed the work of repentance.

### Goodness and God-consciousness

Allah promises a great reward to those who did good and were mindful of Him. *Iḥsān* gives action excellence, while *taqwā* restrains it from corruption. Courage without taqwā can become aggression; piety without action can become passivity.

### Responsible Courage

The verse does not glorify reckless behaviour or require the wounded to ignore medical needs and legitimate weakness. The expedition was under prophetic leadership and served a genuine protective purpose. Courage is measured by obedience and responsibility, not by needless exposure to harm.

### A Community Can Recover

The response after Uḥud shows how a community rebuilds after crisis: acknowledge the mistake, obey sound leadership, protect the community, and refuse to let an enemy define its spirit. Wounds may remain, but they do not have to become permanent defeat.

- Qur’an 3:159–160 on consultation, decision, and trust.
- Qur’an 8:45–46 on obedience, remembrance, and firmness.
- Qur’an 9:119 and 33:23 on truthfulness and fulfilment of a covenant with Allah.
- Qur’an 48:29 on mercy, strength, and the believers’ steadfastness.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:173

> Those who were warned, “Your enemies have mobilized their forces against you, so fear them,” the warning only made them grow stronger in faith and they replied, “Allah ˹alone˺ is sufficient ˹as an aid˺ for us and ˹He˺ is the best Protector.”

**Commentary:**

### Fear-Based Propaganda

The believers were warned that their enemies had gathered a great force against them. The message was designed to paralyse them and make them abandon their responsibility. Instead, the warning increased their faith because it directed their hearts more completely to Allah.

This does not mean that information about danger should be ignored. A believer verifies reports, assesses risk, and prepares wisely. He refuses, however, to let fear become his master.

### “Allah Is Sufficient for Us”

Their response, *ḥasbunā Allāhu wa niʿma al-wakīl*, means that Allah is sufficient as the One upon whom we rely and the best Disposer of affairs. It is a declaration of tawḥīd and *tawakkul*: no created power is independent of Allah, and no outcome is outside His control.

The phrase is not a slogan for avoiding effort. It is a heart that remains anchored in Allah while the body takes lawful means.

### Faith Becomes Stronger under Pressure

Faith grows when a person responds to fear with remembrance, truth, and obedience. The same threat that makes one person collapse can make another more sincere. This is not because the believer enjoys danger, but because he sees danger within Allah’s knowledge and power.

### The Best Protector

Allah is the best Protector, *wakīl*. Human beings may provide advice, security, and companionship, but they cannot guarantee the future. Allah protects faith, guides decisions, and grants the outcome that carries ultimate good.

### Courage without Recklessness

Trusting Allah does not mean entering a situation without preparation or exposing others to unnecessary harm. The believers had a leader, a community, information, and a legitimate purpose. Tawakkul joins spiritual courage with practical wisdom.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:172–175 on responding after injury, the warning of a large enemy force, and divine protection.
- Qur’an 3:160 and 8:40 on Allah as the ultimate Helper and Protector.
- Qur’an 9:51 and 65:3 on trust in Allah’s decree and reliance upon Him.
- Qur’an 8:45–46 on remembrance, firmness, and obedience in danger.
- Qur’an 2:249 on a small group overcoming fear through patience and certainty.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:174

> So they returned with Allah’s favours and grace, suffering no harm. For they sought to please Allah. And surely Allah is ˹the˺ Lord of infinite bounty.

**Commentary:**

### Returning with Favour

The believers answered the call after Uḥud and returned with Allah’s favours and grace. Allah protected them from the enemy’s renewed attack and strengthened their standing. Their return itself was an act of courage and recovery after injury.

The verse shows that obedience may bring a reward different from the one first expected. They went out prepared for danger but returned without suffering the harm they feared.

### Grace and Bounty

“Favour” and “grace” emphasize that the outcome was not merely the result of human planning. The believers prepared and obeyed, while Allah granted safety, strength, and benefit beyond their ability.

The Lord of infinite bounty can reward a servant through visible success or through protection from an unseen disaster.

### Seeking Allah’s Pleasure

Their motive was to please Allah, not to display courage or repair their reputation before people. Sincere intention transforms difficult action into worship. The believer should ask whether he would still do the right thing if no one praised him.

### No Harm Does Not Mean No Hardship

The phrase “suffering no harm” does not mean that every righteous effort will be physically comfortable. The believers had already been wounded at Uḥud. It means Allah prevented the particular harm their enemies intended in that expedition and granted them a favourable outcome.

A believer must not assume that hardship proves disapproval or that ease always proves acceptance.

### Recovery after Setback

The passage moves from defeat to renewed obedience, then to safety and bounty. This is a model for spiritual and communal recovery. A setback can be followed by courageous action, sincere reliance, and unexpected relief.

- Qur’an 2:286 and 65:2–3 on relief, provision, and reliance upon Allah.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:175

> That ˹warning˺ was only ˹from˺ Satan, trying to prompt you to fear his followers.[163] So do not fear them; fear Me if you are ˹true˺ believers.

**Commentary:**

### The Source of the Intimidation

The warning that the enemy had gathered overwhelming forces was a satanic attempt to frighten the believers through the power of Satan’s followers. Satan uses alarming speech, exaggeration, rumours, and imagined consequences to make people surrender before a danger has even arrived.

The verse does not say that every report of danger is false. It teaches believers to examine the intention and effect of fear-producing messages.

### Fear Allah Rather than People

“Do not fear them; fear Me” does not mean that believers feel no natural fear. It means that fear of people must not make them disobey Allah. Fear of Allah, *khashyah*, is reverence that leads to obedience, justice, and careful conduct.

Fear of created beings becomes blameworthy when it causes betrayal, oppression, abandonment of obligations, or acceptance of falsehood.

### Courage and Discernment

A believer must distinguish between prudent caution and paralysis. He can avoid unnecessary danger, seek protection, and make a strategic retreat when wisdom requires it. He cannot let intimidation decide whether truth and justice will be upheld.

Courage is therefore not blind aggression; it is freedom from servitude to human threats.

### Faith Tested by Fear

The verse ends by connecting this attitude to true belief. Faith becomes visible when a person must choose between Allah’s command and the approval or threat of people. The heart that knows Allah’s greatness can resist manipulation without becoming reckless.

### Modern Forms of Intimidation

Satan’s method can appear through propaganda, sectarian incitement, online misinformation, political coercion, or the fear of social exclusion. Muslims should verify information, avoid spreading panic, and remember that Allah’s judgement is more important than the crowd’s reaction.

- Qur’an 3:172–175 on the enemy warning, increased faith, and Allah’s protection.

- Qur’an 2:150 and 5:44 on not fearing people at the expense of Allah’s commands.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying alarming reports before acting upon them.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:176

> ˹O Prophet!˺ Do not grieve for those who race to disbelieve—surely they will not harm Allah in the least. It is Allah’s Will to disallow them a share in the Hereafter, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment.

**Commentary:**

### The Prophet’s Concern

The Prophet ﷺ grieved when people hurried toward disbelief and rejected guidance. His sorrow came from mercy and his desire for their salvation. Allah consoles him by reminding him that their rejection cannot harm Allah or reduce His authority.

The instruction does not make the Prophet hard-hearted. It teaches him not to let people’s choices destroy his resolve or cause him to doubt the truth of his mission.

### Racing toward Disbelief

The phrase describes people who hasten into rejection, opposition, and hypocrisy despite clear guidance. Their speed reveals a determined direction. The Qur’an repeatedly invites people to repent, so the warning concerns persistence rather than a moment of confusion followed by return.

### Allah Is Independent

Disbelief cannot harm Allah. He is *al-Ghaniyy*, free of need, while creation depends upon Him. Human rejection may harm the rejecters and damage societies, but it cannot diminish Allah’s perfection, kingdom, or truth.

This protects believers from imagining that the success of a falsehood makes it true or that opposition can defeat revelation.

### Losing the Hereafter

The denial of a share in the Hereafter is a consequence of choosing disbelief and refusing repentance. Allah’s will is wise and just; it is not arbitrary. Those who reject guidance cannot demand the reward of those who accept and live by it while continuing to reject its source.

The verse should motivate the living to repent, not encourage them to pronounce final judgements over particular people.

### Consolation for Teachers and Reformers

Anyone who calls people to truth may feel grief when advice is rejected. This verse teaches him to offer guidance sincerely, use wisdom, and leave hearts to Allah. He is responsible for conveying the truth, not for controlling another person’s choice.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:177

> Those who trade belief for disbelief will never harm Allah in the least, and they will suffer a painful punishment.

**Commentary:**

### A Destructive Exchange

The verse describes abandoning faith as a trade: a person gives up the priceless truth of belief and takes disbelief in exchange. The metaphor emphasizes the irrationality and loss of the transaction. The rejecter may gain temporary approval, safety, wealth, or power, but has exchanged eternal good for something worthless by comparison.

### Disbelief Does Not Harm Allah

The loss belongs entirely to the person who rejects. Allah is not diminished by disbelief, just as the light of the sun is not diminished when someone closes his eyes. The community may be hurt by injustice and corruption, but Allah’s dominion remains perfect.

### Faith Is a Trust

Faith, *īmān*, includes recognition, acceptance, love, and obedience. It is not a decorative identity that can be surrendered whenever pressure increases. The verse calls believers to protect faith through knowledge and practice, while also inviting those who have left the path to return.

### The Painful Consequence

The punishment is painful because the exchange involved conscious loss and persistent rejection. The Qur’an’s warnings are meant to awaken the heart before death, not to remove hope from one who sincerely repents.

A person who has committed disbelief or serious sin should return to Allah rather than conclude that return is impossible.

### The Worldly Price of Compromise

People may trade religious principles for employment, influence, popularity, or political advantage. Not every worldly compromise is disbelief, and Islam permits wisdom and lawful cooperation. The danger is consciously rejecting truth or violating clear obligations merely to obtain temporary benefit.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:178

> Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment.

**Commentary:**

### Respite Is Not Approval

Allah warns the disbelievers not to interpret extended life, wealth, or power as proof that He approves of them. Time is a gift and a test. When it is used to deepen rejection and wrongdoing, it becomes an opportunity for the person to accumulate sin rather than a sign of success.

The same principle applies to the apparent prosperity of unjust individuals and societies.

### The Danger of Delay

A person who repeatedly postpones repentance may become more comfortable with sin. Habits strengthen, excuses multiply, and the heart loses sensitivity. Longer life can therefore increase a person’s responsibility because more opportunities to return were placed before him.

Yet as long as a person lives, repentance remains possible. The verse warns against complacency; it does not close the door of mercy.

### Humiliating Punishment

The punishment is humiliating because those who claimed honour through wealth, lineage, or power will discover that such status cannot protect them. Their public confidence will not survive the exposure of truth in the Hereafter.

The Qur’an directs believers to seek honour through obedience rather than worldly dominance.

### Do Not Envy Wrongdoers

Believers should not envy a person merely because he is prosperous or lives long. Nor should they rejoice in another person’s death or assume that every hardship is punishment. Allah may give time to a wrongdoer as a test, a warning, or an opportunity for repentance; only Allah knows the final judgement.

### Responsible Use of Time

The verse invites self-examination. Health, opportunity, knowledge, and free time should be used for repentance and good works. A believer asks not only how long he has lived, but what his life has produced.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:179

> Allah would not leave the believers in the condition you were in, until He distinguished the good from the evil ˹among you˺. Nor would Allah ˹directly˺ reveal to you the unseen,[164] but He chooses whoever He wills as a messenger. So believe in Allah and His messengers. And if you are faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺, you will receive a great reward.

**Commentary:**

### A Community Is Not Left Unexamined

Allah would not leave the believers in an indistinguishable condition indefinitely. Through revelation, trials, and the consequences of choices, He separates the sincere from the hypocritical and the sound from the corrupt. Uḥud was one of the events through which hidden realities became visible.

The purpose is not to satisfy curiosity about other people. It is to purify the community and teach it whom to trust, how to reform, and how to recognize qualities of faith.

### Good and Evil Are Distinguished

The distinction between *ṭayyib* and *khabīth* includes sincere faith and hypocrisy, truthful speech and deception, obedience and betrayal. These qualities can exist within a community, and a person should begin by examining himself rather than eagerly labelling others.

Trials reveal what a person serves when comfort, reputation, and safety are threatened.

### The Unseen Belongs to Revelation

Allah does not directly disclose the unseen to everyone. Knowledge of *al-ghayb* belongs to Him. He chooses messengers and reveals to them what He wills, so that revelation can guide humanity. Claims of fortune-tellers, spirits, secret powers, or unrestricted personal access to the unseen have no place in tawḥīd.

The Prophet ﷺ knew only what Allah revealed, not everything hidden in creation.

### Belief in Allah and His Messengers

The proper response is to believe in Allah and all His messengers, without accepting one and rejecting another. Faith in revelation protects the community from both blind suspicion and invented spiritual authority.

### Faith and God-Consciousness

The verse ends with faith and *taqwā*. Believing in unseen revelation must produce obedience, restraint, honesty, and hope. A person cannot claim certainty about the unseen while neglecting the obligations Allah has made clear.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:180

> And do not let those who ˹greedily˺ withhold Allah’s bounties think it is good for them—in fact, it is bad for them! They will be leashed ˹by their necks˺ on the Day of Judgment with whatever ˹wealth˺ they used to withhold. And Allah is the ˹sole˺ inheritor of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

**Commentary:**

### Wealth Is a Divine Bounty

Allah’s bounties include wealth, knowledge, health, time, influence, and ability. The verse especially warns those who greedily withhold wealth that Allah has entrusted to them. Their possession is temporary, while their duty to use it justly is lasting.

Islam does not condemn lawful ownership or responsible saving. It condemns greed, refusal of obligatory charity, denial of people’s rights, and treating wealth as a private absolute beyond accountability.

### Withholding Becomes a Burden

The image of wealth being placed around the neck on the Day of Judgment shows that what a person hoarded will become a source of humiliation and punishment. The object he treated as security becomes a burden he cannot escape.

The image also reverses worldly assumptions: wealth does not belong to a person absolutely merely because it is in his possession. He will answer how he earned it, how he used it, and what rights he withheld.

### Zakat and Social Responsibility

The verse includes the obligation of *zakāh*, but its moral reach is broader. A Muslim must fulfil zakāh, pay debts, honour contracts, support dependants, and assist the needy according to ability. Wealth should circulate through lawful trade, charity, family support, and public benefit rather than becoming a tool of domination.

Charity is not a favour that gives the wealthy superiority over the poor; it is a right and responsibility established by Allah.

### Allah Is the Inheritor

Allah is the sole inheritor of the heavens and the earth. Every human owner eventually leaves what he possesses, while Allah remains. Remembering this turns ownership into stewardship, *amānah*.

A person may enjoy wealth lawfully, but he should know that it is temporary and that its real owner can reclaim it at any time.

### All-Aware of Deeds

Allah is All-Aware of what people do. He knows the amount withheld, the excuse given, the person harmed, and the private motive of the hoarder. He also knows the quiet generosity that people fail to notice.
