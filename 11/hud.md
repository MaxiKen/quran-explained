# Sūrah 11 — Hūd

*123 verses; Meccan in the verdict of al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrimah, ʿAṭāʾ and Jābir, while Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatādah
excepted one verse, "Establish prayer at the two ends of the day" (11:114) [Qurṭubī]. It is named for the
prophet Hūd, whose encounter with ʿĀd it carries (11:50–60). Abū Bakr said, "O Messenger of God, you have
grown old!" and he ﷺ answered, "Hūd, al-Wāqiʿah, al-Mursalāt, ʿAmma yatasāʾalūn and Idhā sh-shamsu
kuwwirat have made me old" — Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan gharīb — and in the narration of Abū Juḥayfah:
"Hūd and its sisters" [Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr]. Al-Qurṭubī explains the greying: terror withers the soul and
dries the body's moisture, and the hair whitens as green crops whiten when their watering fails; this sūrah
recounts the nations and the striking of God's sudden punishment, so that when the people of certainty
recite it, His seizing of His enemies appears to their hearts. Al-Dārimī records from Kaʿb: "Recite Sūrah
Hūd on Friday" [Qurṭubī].*

**Sources cited inline:** **[Ṭabarī]** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* · **[Qurṭubī]** *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* ·
**[Ibn Kathīr]** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* · **[Jalālayn]** *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* · **[Saʿdī]** *Taysīr
al-Karīm al-Raḥmān* · **[Maʿārif]** *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* · **[Study Quran]** *The Study Quran* (2015).

**The sūrah.** Its opening declares the Book's perfection and detailing (11:1) and its whole programme —
worship of God alone, seeking forgiveness and repentance (11:2–4) — then it marches through the destroyed
nations: Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Ibrāhīm, Lūṭ, Shuʿayb and Mūsā, and closes with the command to stand firm
(11:112). **[Maʿārif]** observes that among the sūrahs it is unique in describing the coming of several kinds
of mass punishment triggered by divine displeasure, and the horrendous happenings of the fateful Day of
Judgment and its outcome in individual reward and punishment — the awe of which is what aged the Prophet ﷺ.

---

## 11:1

*"Alif Lām Rā. A Book whose verses have been perfected, then detailed — from One Wise, All-Aware."*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** *Alif Lām Rā* is one of the opening letters; the dispute of the people of
interpretation over it has already been recorded with its proofs, so there is no need to repeat it here.
"A Book" is raised on the sense of "this is a Book," namely the Qurʾān that God sent down upon His prophet
Muḥammad ﷺ. As for "whose verses have been perfected, then detailed": al-Ḥasan said, perfected by command
and prohibition, then detailed by reward and punishment — that is, by promise and threat. **[Jalālayn]** God
knows best what He intends by these letters; this is a Book whose verses have been made clear through
marvellous arrangement and wonderful meanings, then detailed and expounded through rulings, stories and
admonitions — "from One Wise, All-Aware," that is, from God. **[Ibn Kathīr]** "Perfected then detailed"
means perfect in wording, detailed in meaning: the Book is complete in form and in sense; the earlier
discussion of the letters at the beginning of al-Baqarah suffices without repetition. **[Saʿdī]** This is a
great Book and a noble revelation: its verses are made firm and beautifully executed — truthful in their
reports, just in their commands and prohibitions, eloquent in their wording, radiant in their meanings —
and then they are distinguished and clarified with the highest kind of clarification, from One Wise who
places everything in its proper place and One All-Aware of the outward and the inward; and when its
perfecting and detailing are from the Wise, the All-Aware, one need not ask after its majesty, its perfect
wisdom and its encompassing mercy.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** The finest word on "perfected" (*uḥkimat*) is Qatādah's: its verses were all
made firm, containing no defect and no falsehood — *iḥkām* is the guarding of speech from corruption, and
the Qurʾān is arranged in an arrangement that no contradiction or flaw can reach. Ibn ʿAbbās said: no Book
abrogates it, unlike the Torah and the Gospel. "Then detailed": by promise and threat, reward and
punishment; Qatādah said, God secured it against falsehood, then detailed it with the lawful and the
forbidden. Mujāhid: it was perfected as a whole, then explained verse by verse with every proof needed of
God's oneness, prophethood and resurrection. It was also said: it was gathered in the Preserved Tablet,
then detailed in the sending-down; and: it was sent down portion by portion so that it might be pondered.
ʿIkrimah read *fuṣilat* (without shadda), "judged with truth." "From One Wise" — He who perfects all
affairs; "All-Aware" — of everything that comes to be and everything that does not. The grammarians also
disputed the sūrah's name: al-Khalīl and Sībawayh declined "Hūd" without tanwīn, as a proper name of the
sūrah, while ʿĪsā b. ʿUmar allowed it with tanwīn; Sībawayh's proof is that one says "this is
ar-Raḥmān," meaning "the sūrah of ar-Raḥmān."

**Belief.** **[Maʿārif]** The isolated letters are a secret between God and His Messenger ﷺ; others were not
informed of their meaning and are even restrained from busying themselves with it. *Muḥkam* comes from
*iḥkām*: a balancing of meaningful speech so correct that no likelihood of error or disorder remains in
word or meaning. Ibn ʿAbbās took "perfected" in contrast to "abrogated": the earlier Books were abrogated,
but this Book — with which the very chain of prophets and revelations ended — will not be abrogated until
the Last Day (internal abrogation of some Qurʾānic verses by the Qurʾān itself does not contradict this).
"Detailed" also means that the sections on beliefs, worship, dealings, social life and morals are separated
from one another and clarified; and it is said that the whole Qurʾān was already embedded in the Preserved
Tablet, then revealed in installments as conditions required, so that preserving it and acting on it would
be made easy. It comes from One Wise and All-Aware whose considerations of wisdom cannot be encompassed and
who knows every particle of this universe as it is and as it shall be — unlike human lawmakers, however
intelligent, whose reason and vision are cordoned by a limited frame of reference.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** **[Qurṭubī]** Abū Bakr said, "O Messenger of God, you have grown old!" and he ﷺ said,
"Hūd, al-Wāqiʿah, al-Mursalāt, ʿAmma yatasāʾalūn and Idhā sh-shamsu kuwwirat have made me old" (Tirmidhī
graded it ḥasan gharīb); in the narration of Abū Juḥayfah, "Hūd and its sisters." Qurṭubī explains: terror
distills the soul and dries up the body's moisture, beneath every hair is a spring, and when terror dries
the springs the hair withers and whitens, as green crops whiten when their watering is cut off; since this
sūrah recounts the nations and the striking of God's sudden punishment, the people of certainty, when they
recite it, see upon their hearts His dominion and His seizing of His enemies — and God tempers His mercy to
them in those moments so that they can bear to recite His speech. Some said what aged the Prophet ﷺ of
this sūrah was "So stand firm as you are commanded" (11:112). Yazīd b. Abān said: I saw the Messenger of
God ﷺ in a dream and recited Sūrah Hūd to him; when I finished he said, "O Yazīd, this is recitation — but
where is the weeping?" **[Ibn Kathīr]** records the same greying ḥadīth from Ibn ʿAbbās, adding the variant
"Hūd and its sisters."

**Reflection.** When a Book is perfected and detailed by the Wise, the All-Aware, the servant's part is not
to sit in judgement over it but to submit to it; and if recitation leaves the eye dry, the heart has not
yet tasted the awe that made the Messenger of God ﷺ grow old.

## 11:2

*"That you worship none but God. Truly I am to you from Him a warner and a bearer of good tidings."*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** The verses are "detailed" with this: that you worship God alone, without partner,
and cast off the idols and rivals. Then God tells His prophet: say, O Muḥammad, to the people, "I am to you
from God a warner who warns you of His punishment for disobeying Him and worshipping idols, and a bearer
of good tidings of abundant reward for obeying Him and devoting worship sincerely to Him." **[Jalālayn]**
Worship none but God; truly I am to you from Him a warner of chastisement if you disbelieve, and a bearer
of good tidings of reward if you believe. **[Ibn Kathīr]** ties the call to the message of every messenger:
"We sent no messenger before you but We revealed to him: there is no god but I, so worship Me" (21:25), and
"We sent among every nation a messenger: worship God and shun false gods" (16:36); and he recalls the
authentic report that the Messenger of God ﷺ ascended aṣ-Ṣafā and called out to his kinsmen of Quraysh,
and when they gathered he said, "O Quraysh, if I told you cavalry were about to fall on you at dawn, would
you believe me?" They said, "We have never found you to be a liar." He said, "Then I am a warner to you
before a severe punishment."

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ took the lām of *an lā taʿbudū* to mean "with": the
Book was perfected then detailed *with* "worship none but God"; al-Zajjāj took it purposively, "so that
you worship none but God." It was also said the entire speech is God's, so that God Himself is the warner
against worshipping other than Him, as He says, "God warns you of Himself" (3:28). **[Maʿārif]** notes that
*nadhīr* is not applied to a fear-inspiring enemy or beast, but to one who, out of love and affection,
warns another and saves him from what harms him in this world or the next — the office of warning here is
itself an office of mercy. **[Saʿdī]** God sent down His Book precisely for this: that religion be made
sincere entirely to Him and none of His creatures be associated with Him; the Messenger is a warner to the
presumptuous in disobedience of the punishment of this world and the next, and a bearer of good tidings to
the obedient of the reward of this world and the next.

**Cross-references.** **[Study Quran]** This is one of several passages calling the Prophet a bearer of
glad tidings and a warner (5:19; 7:188; 10:2; 17:105; 25:56; 33:45; 34:28; 35:24; 48:8), and elsewhere the
same two offices belong to all the prophets (2:213; 4:165; 6:48; 18:56).

**Reflection.** All the perfecting and detailing of the Book is gathered into a single purpose — that none
but God be worshipped — and the one who delivers it warns out of love, like a watchman calling out before
the raid at dawn.

## 11:3

*"And seek forgiveness of your Lord, then repent to Him, and He will grant you good enjoyment for a term
appointed and give every possessor of excellence his due. But if you turn away, I fear for you the
punishment of a great Day."*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** "Seek forgiveness of your Lord": act, O people, with deeds by which your Lord is
pleased with you, so that He covers over the grave sins you committed in worshipping idols and setting up
rivals. "Then repent to Him": return to your Lord with pure devotion after renouncing the rivals and
disavowing their worship. For this reason He said "then repent," not "and repent": seeking forgiveness is
from the idolatry they were dwelling in, while repentance is the return to obeying God, and no deed is for
God until shirk is abandoned — so He commanded repentance after seeking forgiveness because the idolaters
imagined that in much of what they did they were already obeying God. "He will grant you good enjoyment for
a term appointed": if you do this, He will spread this world out for you, provide you of its adornment, and
respire your terms until the time He has decreed for your death. **[Jalālayn]** Ask forgiveness of your
Lord for your idolatry, then return to Him through acts of obedience; the fair enjoyment is pleasant living
and abundant provision until death; "every possessor of excellence" will be given, in the Hereafter, the
due of his excellence; *tawallaw* drops one of the two tāʾs of *tatawallaw*; the awful day is the Day of
Resurrection. **[Ibn Kathīr]** I command you to seek forgiveness for previous sins and to turn to God from
future sins and abide by that; the "good enjoyment" is this worldly life, and "He will give every possessor
of excellence his excellence" refers to the Hereafter, according to Qatādah — like "We shall surely grant
him a good life" (16:97). The threat is severe for whoever turns away from God's commands and rejects His
messengers: the punishment will afflict him on the Day of Resurrection with no escape. **[Saʿdī]** Seek
forgiveness for what has issued from you of sins, then repent for what remains of your lives, returning
from what God dislikes to what He loves and pleases Him; then the verse states what follows upon seeking
forgiveness and repentance.

**Rulings.** **[Maʿārif]** Forgiveness (*maghfirah*) concerns past sins, repentance (*tawbah*) the resolve
never to come near them again; a true repentance is no more than being ashamed of past sins, praying that
they be forgiven, and resolving firmly not to repeat them. Some righteous elders said that seeking
forgiveness by tongue alone, without firm resolution and the arrangements to implement it, is "the
repentance of liars" — as the poet said, "Sin laughs at my style of seeking forgiveness" (both cited via
Qurṭubī). Most commentators take the "good enjoyment" for the enjoyments of this world: expanded
sustenance, easy means of comfort, protection from calamities and punishments, available until death
removes them — while the eternal comforts follow in the next life. Sahl b. ʿAbdullāh said the meaning of
"good enjoyment" is that one's attention bypasses the created and stays fixed on the Creator; other men of
God said it is contentment with what is at hand and no worry for what is not.

**Cross-references.** **[Study Quran]** Shunning idols is itself a seeking of forgiveness for ascribing
partners to God, and devoting oneself exclusively to Him is the form of repentance; the verse is usually
read to mean there is no forgiveness unless the servant actually repents (R). The good enjoyment is
sustenance and blessing in this world (JJ, Kl), or the life of faith itself, in which believers hope in God
and rest content with His decree while disbelievers can only rejoice in this world's good things; the term
appointed is death, and the bounty of every possessor of merit is the reward of the Hereafter, commensurate
with the merit of his obedience (Z) — see 8:4. The punishment of a great Day is the chastisement of the
Resurrection, and for the Makkans also the days of their great defeats, such as Badr (Kl). **[Maʿārif]**
links the promise to "Whoever works righteousness, male or female, while a believer — We shall surely grant
him a good life" (16:97) and to the address to the seekers of forgiveness in Sūrah Nūḥ: "He will send the
sky upon you in showers, and aid you with wealth and sons, and appoint for you gardens and rivers"
(71:11–12).

**Reflection.** Repentance is not a single utterance but a re-direction of a life: forgiveness for what has
passed, return for what remains — and the fruits are a good provision here and the due of excellence there.

## 11:4

*"Unto God is your return, and He has power over all things."*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** To God, O people, is your homecoming and your destination; so beware His
punishment if you turn away from what I call you to of repentance from worshipping idols — for if you
perish in your shirk before repenting, He will make you abide in the fire of Gehenna. "He has power over
all things": He is able to revive you after your death and to punish you for setting up idols, and for
whatever else He wills for you and for others. **[Jalālayn]** Unto God is your return, and He has power
over all things, including reward and chastisement. **[Ibn Kathīr]** Your return is to God on the Day of
Judgment, and He is able to do whatever He wishes — goodness towards His allies or vengeance upon His
enemies, including repeating the creation on the Day of Resurrection; this clause instils fear, as the
preceding instilled hope. **[Saʿdī]** "He has power over all things" stands like a proof of the revival of
the dead: He is powerful over all things and the resurrection of the dead is among them; He has informed of
it, and He is the most truthful of speakers, so its occurrence is necessary by reason and by revelation
alike. **[Maʿārif]** Let man do what he elects and live as he fancies: ultimately, once dead, he returns to
the One who is powerful over everything.

**Cross-references.** **[Study Quran]** The return to God is spoken of in the same fashion throughout the
Qurʾān: 3:55; 5:48, 105; 6:60, 164; 10:4, 23; 29:8; 31:15; 39:7.

**Reflection.** Every road, whichever way a person turns his face, ends at God; and since the One to whom
all return has power over all things, the only reasonable fear is His, and the only reasonable hope is in
Him.

## 11:5

*"Behold, they fold up their breasts to hide from Him. Behold, when they cover themselves with their
garments, He knows what they conceal and what they disclose. Truly He knows what is within the breasts."*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** The reciters differed over the reading: the cities read *yathnūna ṣudūrahum*, on
the measure of *yafʿalūna* from *thanaytu*, with the breasts in the accusative. Among those who read it
so, some said it describes a hypocrite who, when he passed by the Messenger of God ﷺ, threw his garment
over his face, bent his back and lowered his head — ʿAbdullāh b. Shaddād b. al-Hād related this of the
hypocrites, that they did it so the Prophet ﷺ would not see them and summon them to faith. **[Saʿdī]** God
reports the ignorance of the idolaters and the depth of their error: they bend their breasts so that their
breasts might stand as a veil between God's knowledge of their states and His sight of them; and God
exposes the mistake — at the very moment they cover themselves with their garments, in the most concealed
of states, He knows them, and knows what they keep secret of words and deeds and what they publish, and
more than that: He knows what is within the breasts of intentions, whisperings and thoughts never spoken, in
secret or aloud — how then can your state be hidden from Him when you fold your breasts to hide from Him?
It may also mean that the deniers, out of the intensity of their turning away from the Messenger, hunch
down when they see him, lest he see them and make them hear his call — and is there anything beyond such
aversion? **[Maʿārif]** The verse refutes the hypocrites and disbelievers who camouflage their hostility:
their hearts blaze with malice while they keep a cool front, find covers for their true identity and
intention, and imagine no one will discover who they really are — but God knows what they bring into the
open and what they hide behind clothes, covers and curtains, for He is aware of what lies in the hearts.

**Context.** **[Jalālayn]** al-Bukhārī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that it was revealed concerning people who
were embarrassed to withdraw to relieve nature, or to have intercourse with their wives, without clothes,
exposed to the heaven; it is also said it concerns the hypocrites — at the moment they wrap themselves in
their garments God knows what they hide and what they proclaim, so their concealment is of no avail.
**[Qurṭubī]** gives the range: the verse reports the enmity of the idolaters toward the Prophet ﷺ and the
believers, and their supposition that their states are hidden from God. Ibn ʿAbbās said they conceal the
hatred in their breasts and show its opposite, and it was revealed about al-Akhnas b. Sharīq, a man of
sweet speech who met the Messenger of God ﷺ with what pleased him while his heart was folded upon what
grieved him. Mujāhid: it is doubt and misgiving; al-Ḥasan: they fold them upon the disbelief within.
Others: it is the hypocrites who said, "If we bolt our doors, cover ourselves with our garments and fold
our breasts upon enmity to Muḥammad, who will know of us?" A group of Muslims also used to practise
asceticism by covering their bodies and never exposing them under the sky, until God made plain that true
devotion is what the hearts hold of belief and what the limbs show of word and deed. Ibn ʿAbbās also
reported the reading *tathnawwī ṣudūruhum*, in the measure of *tanṭawī*, explaining that they would not
consort with their wives or relieve themselves while facing the open heaven. **[Study Quran]** likewise
reports it of al-Akhnas b. Sharīq, very eloquent and pleasing in speech to the Prophet while inwardly
harbouring hatred toward him; others see it more generally of the Makkan idolaters' concealed enmity, and
take "they cover themselves with their garments" as aversion to hearing the Qurʾān — casting their garments
over their heads so as not to hear — as in 71:7.

**Reflection.** Whoever supposes that walls, garments or folded breasts can hide him from God has not yet
grasped that He is aware of the breasts themselves — concealment is a delusion that only postpones the
account.
