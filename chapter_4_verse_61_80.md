## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:61

> When it is said to them, “Come to Allah’s revelations and to the Messenger,” you see the hypocrites turn away from you stubbornly.

**Commentary:**

### The Call to Divine Judgement

The hypocrites are invited to return to Allah’s revelations and to the Messenger ﷺ, but they turn away stubbornly. The invitation is not merely to hear a sermon; it is to let revelation govern belief, conduct, and disputes. Their refusal exposes that their claim of faith is conditional upon receiving the result they prefer.

### Hypocrisy Revealed through Resistance

Hypocrisy, *nifāq*, is a conflict between outward profession and inward rejection. A person may attend religious gatherings, use religious language, or claim loyalty, yet resist revelation whenever it limits his wealth, status, or revenge. The test is not whether a person has questions, but whether he is willing to submit to truth after it becomes clear.

### The Messenger’s Role

The Messenger ﷺ is the authorised interpreter and judge of Allah’s revelation. Turning to him is not worshipping a human being; it is obeying the one sent by Allah. His Sunnah explains the Qur’an and shows how its principles operate in life.

### Stubborn Turning Away

The description of turning away stubbornly indicates deliberate refusal, not inability, confusion, or a sincere request for clarification. A believer may ask questions, seek evidence, and consult scholars while remaining humble before revelation. Stubbornness appears when a person refuses every answer because submission itself is unwelcome.

### A Warning for Believers

Muslims should not read this verse only as a description of others. They should ask whether they accept Allah’s guidance in worship, family rights, financial dealings, and justice, or whether they invoke religion only when it supports their desires.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:59–65 on returning disputes to Allah and the Messenger and the test of sincere belief.
- Qur’an 24:48–51 on the hypocrites’ refusal and the believers’ response to divine judgement.
- Qur’an 33:36 on submitting when Allah and His Messenger decide a matter.
- Qur’an 59:7 on accepting what the Messenger gives and avoiding what he forbids.
- Qur’an 2:8–10 and 63:1–3 on the speech and inward condition of hypocrites.
- Qur’an 16:43–44 on the Messenger explaining revelation.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on obedience to the Prophet ﷺ, hypocrisy, and accepting his judgement.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:62

> How ˹horrible˺ will it be if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands have done, then they come to you swearing by Allah, “We intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation.”

**Commentary:**

### Consequences Bring Them Back

The verse imagines a disaster striking the hypocrites because of what their hands have done. Their actions—rejecting just judgement, supporting false authority, and harming the community—produce consequences. When the results become painful, they return to the Prophet ﷺ and present a favourable explanation of their motives.

### Goodwill as an Excuse

They swear that they intended only goodwill and reconciliation. Reconciliation is genuinely praiseworthy, but a noble label cannot purify a dishonest method. A person may claim that corruption, deception, or betrayal was meant to produce peace; Allah judges the actual conduct and its effects.

### The Difference between Excuse and Repentance

An excuse tries to escape responsibility without changing the behaviour. Repentance admits the wrong, stops it, seeks forgiveness, and repairs the harm. The following verse instructs the Prophet to address their hearts, showing that their explanations required moral correction rather than simple acceptance.

### Disasters and Self-Examination

The verse should not be misused to declare that every disaster is punishment for the victims. Calamities can be tests, warnings, consequences of choices, or events whose wisdom is hidden. Allah alone knows the full reality. Individuals and communities should nevertheless examine whether their conduct contributed to preventable harm.

### Truthful Reconciliation

Genuine *iṣlāḥ*, reconciliation, requires truth, justice, accountability, and restoration of rights. Peace built on silencing victims or concealing wrongdoing is not the reconciliation praised by Allah.

- Qur’an 30:41 and 42:30 on harm caused by human actions, alongside Allah’s pardon.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 and 25:70 on repentance, reform, and returning to Allah.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports and not accepting convenient claims without evidence.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on sincere repentance, reconciling disputes, and the accountability of those who claim good intentions while harming others.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:63

> ˹Only˺ Allah knows what is in their hearts. So turn away from them, caution them, and give them advice that will shake their very souls.

**Commentary:**

### Allah Knows the Hidden Heart

Allah alone fully knows what is in the hearts. Human beings may observe words and conduct, but they cannot see every motive or private calculation. This protects against reckless declarations of hypocrisy while affirming that Allah’s judgement reaches what people conceal.

### Three Responses to Hypocrisy

The Prophet ﷺ is instructed to turn away from them, caution them, and address them with penetrating advice. Turning away can mean not allowing their excuses to control the community or rewarding manipulation with excessive attention. Caution means making the consequences clear. Advice that reaches the soul seeks reform rather than humiliation.

### Counsel Must Be Effective

The expression “advice that will shake their very souls” translates a form of *qawl balīgh*: speech that reaches its intended effect. Effective counsel is truthful, timely, direct, and suited to the listener. It exposes the danger of hypocrisy, reminds the person of Allah, and opens a path to repentance.

Harshness for its own sake is not the same as strong advice.

### Accountability and Mercy

The verse does not instruct believers to ignore hypocrisy or allow it to harm others. Nor does it encourage public cruelty. A community may set boundaries, protect people, and hold wrongdoers accountable while still hoping for their repentance.

### Advice before Consequence

The command to counsel shows that warning is part of mercy. People should be told what is wrong and what change is required before consequences become final. A leader who punishes without explanation may create fear without reform; a leader who only advises without protecting others may enable harm.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 63:1–4 on the speech and inner disease of hypocrites.
- Qur’an 66:8 on sincere repentance and reform.
- Qur’an 16:125 and 20:44 on advising with wisdom and gentle but effective speech.
- Qur’an 49:6 and 24:4 on evidence before accusation and harm.
- Qur’an 42:37 and 3:134 on forgiveness, restraint, and controlling anger.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on sincere counsel, correcting wrongdoing, and combining mercy with protection from harm.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:64

> We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah’s Will. If only those ˹hypocrites˺ came to you ˹O Prophet˺—after wronging themselves—seeking Allah’s forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness, they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### The Purpose of Messengers

Allah sent messengers to be obeyed by His permission. Their role is not merely to deliver inspiring information; they convey commands, explain worship, judge disputes, and model submission. Obedience to the Messenger ﷺ is obedience to Allah’s revealed instruction, not independent worship of a human being.

### Wronging Oneself

Sin is described as wronging oneself because the sinner damages his own faith, character, and future while also possibly harming others. Hypocrisy adds a deeper injury: the person presents loyalty while secretly resisting guidance.

Recognition of wrong is the beginning of repair.

### The Path of Repentance

The verse presents a path: come to the Messenger, seek Allah’s forgiveness, and accept the Messenger’s prayer for forgiveness. In the Prophet’s lifetime, this meant going to him, admitting the wrong, and returning to obedience. The essential elements remain after his death: sincere repentance to Allah, following his Sunnah, and restoring rights.

The Messenger is not an independent forgiver. Allah is the One who accepts repentance and shows mercy.

### Repentance Is More Than Words

The hypocrites could not merely repeat a formula while continuing betrayal. True *tawbah* includes regret, abandoning the wrong, resolving not to return, and correcting harm. If the sin involved another person, the right must be returned or the victim’s forgiveness sought where appropriate.

### Mercy for the Returning Servant

Allah is ever Accepting of repentance, *Tawwāb*, and Most Merciful. The verse gives hope to people who have wronged themselves and shows that the door remains open when they return sincerely.

- Qur’an 39:53–54 and 4:110 on forgiveness and returning to Allah after sin.
- Qur’an 9:102–105 on confession, repentance, charity, and reform.
- Qur’an 63:5–6 on refusing the invitation to seek forgiveness.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:65

> But no! By your Lord, they will never be ˹true˺ believers until they accept you ˹O Prophet˺ as the judge in their disputes, and find no resistance within themselves against your decision and submit wholeheartedly.

**Commentary:**

### A Solemn Standard of Faith

Allah swears by the Lord of the Prophet ﷺ that true faith requires accepting the Messenger as judge in disputes. The issue is not merely acknowledging that he existed; it is accepting his authority to explain Allah’s guidance and establish justice.

This verse is especially relevant when a ruling conflicts with personal benefit, tribal loyalty, or financial interest.

### No Inner Resistance to Truth

The believers are required to find no constricting resistance within themselves against the Messenger’s decision and to submit wholeheartedly. This does not mean that a person never feels sadness, fear, or the difficulty of giving up a preference. It means that he does not treat personal desire as superior to Allah’s revealed judgement.

### Submission Is Not Blindness

Accepting the Messenger’s authority does not mean abandoning evidence or allowing anyone to claim prophetic authority after Muhammad ﷺ. It means returning disputes to the Qur’an and authentic Sunnah, understood through reliable knowledge and justice. A person must distinguish the Prophet’s guidance from the unsupported claims of later individuals.

### Justice in Disputes

The context includes conflicts over property, betrayal, family rights, and public decisions. Submission to the Messenger means telling the truth, accepting a fair process, and relinquishing an unjust advantage. Religious language cannot be used to manipulate a court or silence the harmed party.

### The Heart and the Law

Islam seeks both outward compliance and inward acceptance. A person may obey a lawful ruling while still feeling pain or needing time to understand it; the verse condemns arrogant rejection, not involuntary emotion. Faith grows when the heart asks Allah for contentment and follows truth despite the self’s resistance.

- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on justice, testimony, and judging fairly.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:66

> If We had commanded them to sacrifice themselves or abandon their homes, none would have obeyed except for a few. Had they done what they were advised to do,[209] it would have certainly been far better for them and more reassuring,

**Commentary:**

### The Weight of Obedience

Allah points out that if the hypocrites had been commanded to sacrifice themselves or leave their homes, only a few would have obeyed. The example highlights the distance between claiming faith and accepting a costly command. It is not a command to commit suicide; it describes an extreme hypothetical of sacrifice and migration for Allah’s cause.

### Obedience Reveals Sincerity

Words are easy when obedience costs nothing. A difficult command reveals whether a person’s commitment is real or merely public performance. The few who would obey represent sincere believers whose trust in Allah is stronger than attachment to safety and possessions.

### “What They Were Advised to Do”

The believers were advised to obey the Messenger ﷺ and accept revealed judgement. Had they followed that guidance, it would have been better for them in this life and the Hereafter. Obedience protects people from the confusion, conflict, and consequences produced by hypocrisy.

### Better and More Reassuring

Allah describes obedience as better and more reassuring, *tathbīt* in meaning: it strengthens the heart and stabilizes the community. A person who knows that he has acted truthfully before Allah is less divided within himself. A community that shares a just standard is less vulnerable to manipulation.

### Legitimate Sacrifice

Islam honours sacrifice when it is lawful, purposeful, and directed toward Allah. It does not glorify reckless self-destruction or require people to abandon dependants without a valid reason. The command concerns obedience to revelation under prophetic leadership, not private individuals inventing extreme tests for themselves or others.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:67

> and We would have granted them a great reward by Our grace

**Commentary:**

### Reward beyond What Is Earned

This verse continues the promise attached to obedience. Allah would grant those who obey a great reward by His grace. The wording reminds believers that even when they fulfil their duties, the reward is not a debt Allah owes them. It is generosity from the Lord who enabled the obedience in the first place.

### Sacrifice Is Not Lost

The obedience discussed in the preceding verse may involve giving up comfort, property, security, or social approval. Allah assures the believer that sacrifice for truth is never wasted. The reward may include inner stability and communal good in this life, and forgiveness and Paradise in the Hereafter.

### Grace and Justice

Allah’s grace does not contradict justice. Justice means that no sincere deed is ignored; grace means that Allah multiplies reward beyond the strict measure of the deed. This gives the believer hope when his effort appears small or its worldly result is delayed.

### Obedience without Bargaining

A servant should obey Allah because He is worthy of worship, not only because a visible reward is expected immediately. Hope in reward strengthens obedience, but sincerity means remaining faithful even when the result is hidden.

### A Corrective to Hypocrisy

The surrounding passage contrasts the hypocrites’ excuses with the reward promised to sincere believers. Hypocrisy seeks benefits without responsibility; faith accepts responsibility while trusting Allah’s generosity.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:68

> and guided them to the Straight Path.

**Commentary:**

### Guidance as a Reward

Allah would guide the obedient to the Straight Path, *al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*. Guidance is both a gift and a result of responding sincerely to the truth. The more a person obeys Allah with humility, the more clearly he recognizes the next step.

### A Straight Path in Every Area

The Straight Path includes correct belief, worship, family responsibility, justice, economic honesty, courage, and mercy. It is not limited to private spirituality. The preceding verses show that accepting prophetic judgement and fulfilling responsibilities are part of walking this path.

### Avoiding Extremes

A straight path is balanced. It avoids hypocrisy and rebellion, but also avoids harshness, fanaticism, and self-righteousness. It joins obedience with mercy, courage with wisdom, and authority with accountability.

### Guidance and Community

The plural context reminds us that guidance shapes a community as well as individuals. A community guided by Allah builds trustworthy institutions, protects the weak, resolves disputes fairly, and supports people who are trying to return after failure.

### Asking for Guidance

Believers ask Allah for the Straight Path in every unit of prayer. This verse shows why that request must be continual: a person can know truth but drift through desire, fear, peer pressure, or pride. Guidance requires renewed obedience and reliance upon Allah.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:69

> And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be in the company of those blessed by Allah: the prophets, the people of truth, the martyrs, and the righteous—what honourable company!

**Commentary:**

### The Reward of Obedience

Obedience to Allah and His Messenger leads to the company of the people Allah has blessed. The promise is not limited to scholars, rulers, or people with extraordinary public achievements. A sincere servant can be raised through faithfulness, truthfulness, sacrifice, and righteousness.

### Four Honoured Groups

The prophets, *anbiyāʾ*, received revelation and conveyed Allah’s guidance. The people of truth, *ṣiddīqūn*, confirmed truth with exceptional sincerity and lived consistently by it. The martyrs, *shuhadāʾ*, are those honoured by Allah for truthful sacrifice in His cause. The righteous, *ṣāliḥūn*, are people whose belief and conduct are sound.

These ranks are distinct, but all are united by obedience.

### Company without Equality of Rank

Being in their company does not mean that every believer shares the rank of a prophet. It means companionship, honour, and nearness in Paradise according to Allah’s generosity. The promise encourages ordinary believers that faithful obedience connects them to the best people who ever lived.

### Love and Association

The Prophet ﷺ taught that a person will be with those he loves. Love should therefore be directed toward people of faith and righteousness and expressed by following their virtues. Merely admiring a righteous person while rejecting his obedience is not the complete meaning of companionship.

### Choosing Companions Now

The future company is shaped by present relationships. Good companions remind one another of Allah, encourage repentance, and restrain injustice. Corrupt companionship normalises sin and makes hypocrisy appear acceptable.

### What Honourable Company Means

The phrase is a celebration of belonging. The believer who may feel isolated in a hostile environment can hope to join prophets, truthful people, martyrs, and the righteous. The honour comes from Allah, not from worldly recognition.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:70

> This is Allah’s favour, and Allah fully knows ˹who deserves it˺.

**Commentary:**

### Favour from Allah

The blessed company described in the previous verse is Allah’s favour, *faḍl*. Believers earn reward through obedience, but they do not compel Allah to grant them honour. Faith, opportunity, ability, guidance, and the chance to do good are themselves gifts from Allah.

This understanding prevents both pride and despair.

### Allah Knows Who Deserves It

Allah fully knows who deserves the favour. He knows sincere obedience and hidden hypocrisy, the deed done under difficulty, the repentance after failure, and the opportunity a person ignored. Human beings judge outward appearances; Allah knows the whole reality.

### Reward and Humility

The verse does not discourage striving for high spiritual rank. It teaches that striving must be accompanied by humility. A believer asks Allah for acceptance rather than assuming that visible effort guarantees a particular status.

### The Value of Hidden Good

People whose goodness is private may be unknown to society but fully known to Allah. A caregiver, honest worker, patient parent, or person resisting temptation may receive an honour that no public ranking can measure.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:71

> O believers! Take your precautions and go forth either in groups or together.

**Commentary:**

### Precaution Is Part of Faith

Allah commands believers to take their precautions, *khudhū ḥidhrakum*. Trust in Allah does not mean ignoring danger, intelligence, planning, equipment, health, or security. The command recognizes that responsible preparation protects lives and helps a community fulfil its duties.

Neglecting obvious precautions and then calling the result reliance is not *tawakkul*.

### Groups or Together

Believers may go forth in groups or together according to the situation. The wording allows strategic flexibility: a community may act collectively or deploy smaller units when that is safer and more effective. The underlying principles are consultation, discipline, and obedience to legitimate leadership.

### A Wider Application

The verse applies beyond military circumstances. Travellers take safety precautions, organisations secure funds and records, families prepare for emergencies, and communities protect children and vulnerable people. Spiritual confidence should produce practical wisdom.

### Avoiding Fear and Recklessness

Precaution is not the same as cowardice. It prevents unnecessary loss while leaving believers ready to act when justice and protection require action. Nor is it paranoia: preparations must be based on reliable information rather than rumours or hatred.

### Collective Responsibility

The command is addressed to the community. Security and welfare should not be placed entirely on individuals who lack resources. Communities should coordinate, share information responsibly, protect dependants, and care for those who cannot participate directly.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:72

> There will be some among you who will lag behind so that if you face a disaster, they will say, “Allah has blessed us for not being there among them.”

**Commentary:**

### Hypocritical Withdrawal

The verse describes people who deliberately lag behind and then treat the suffering of believers as proof that their own avoidance was a blessing. Their words reveal not compassion but self-congratulation and distance from the community.

The passage does not condemn every person who stays behind. Valid illness, disability, family responsibility, or a lawful exemption can make non-participation righteous. The issue is avoiding responsibility through hypocrisy and then mocking those who serve.

### Misreading Safety

They call their absence a blessing because they see only physical survival. The Qur’an teaches that safety is not always the highest good, and danger is not always proof of failure. A person may preserve his body while losing courage, loyalty, or faith.

At the same time, Islam does not demand needless risk. The moral question concerns intention, responsibility, and lawful circumstances.

### The Harm of Gloating

Gloating over another person’s injury damages solidarity and adds pain to grief. A believer should support the wounded, learn from mistakes, and pray for those affected rather than use their suffering to justify his own cowardice.

### Community Trust

A crisis reveals who can be relied upon. Those who consistently avoid responsibility weaken collective safety, especially when they later discourage others. Communities should distinguish genuine inability from deliberate evasion without humiliating people who have legitimate reasons to remain behind.

### Blessing Is Measured by Allah

A blessing may be protection from harm, or it may be the opportunity to serve despite hardship. Allah knows which is which. The believer asks for wisdom, takes precautions, and accepts the outcome without claiming that every comfortable path is divinely approved.

- Qur’an 9:91 and 48:17 on valid exemptions and the absence of blame upon the genuinely unable.
- Qur’an 3:167–168 on hypocrites who stayed behind and blamed those who were killed.
- Qur’an 49:11–12 on mockery, backbiting, and harming people’s honour.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:73

> But if you return with Allah’s bounties, they will say—as if there had been no bond between you—“We wish we had been there with them to share the great gain!”

**Commentary:**

### Opportunistic Loyalty

The hypocrites who avoided danger would wish they had joined the believers if the expedition produced victory and material gain. Their changing speech shows that they are not committed to truth or community; they calculate which position benefits them.

They want the reward of sacrifice without accepting the risk and responsibility that accompany it.

### The Bond of Faith

The phrase “as if there had been no bond between you” exposes their lack of genuine brotherhood. True faith creates solidarity in hardship and prosperity. It does not disappear when a community suffers or reappear when there are spoils to share.

### Worldly Gain as a Test

The “great gain” may include victory, safety, reputation, or material spoils. The verse warns that worldly success can attract people whose motives were absent during the struggle. A community must distribute benefits justly and not allow opportunists to erase the contributions of those who served.

### Sincerity in Service

A sincere person serves Allah whether or not the effort is publicly rewarded. He does not measure every action by recognition or profit. If unable to participate, he supports the community in another lawful way rather than pretending to have shared a sacrifice he avoided.

### Justice without Contempt

The verse does not command believers to hate everyone who could not join. Genuine exemptions and different forms of service must be honoured. It condemns those who deliberately withdraw and then claim a share only after success becomes visible.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:74

> Let those who would sacrifice this life for the Hereafter fight in the cause of Allah. And whoever fights in Allah’s cause—whether they achieve martyrdom or victory—We will honour them with a great reward.

**Commentary:**

### Choosing the Lasting Life

The verse calls those who would sacrifice the temporary life for the Hereafter to fight in Allah’s cause. This does not mean despising life, family, lawful work, or worldly blessings. It means refusing to make worldly comfort the highest value when justice, protection, and faith require sacrifice.

### The Cause Must Be Allah’s

Fighting in Allah’s cause, *fī sabīl Allāh*, requires sincere intention and lawful conduct. It excludes revenge, tribal pride, conquest, terrorism, political domination, and personal fame. In the Qur’anic context, armed struggle is connected to defence, persecution, and protecting the oppressed under legitimate authority.

Individuals and groups cannot declare their own violence sacred simply by using religious language.

### Both Outcomes Are Rewarded

Allah promises a great reward whether the believers attain martyrdom or victory. This corrects the idea that success is measured only by surviving or winning. Sincere sacrifice is valuable even when the worldly outcome is hidden or painful.

At the same time, the promise does not make every death in a conflict martyrdom. Allah knows intention, cause, conduct, and truth.

### Courage without Recklessness

The verse can inspire courage, but it does not justify self-destruction. Believers must take precautions, protect noncombatants, obey lawful leadership, and seek peaceful resolution when possible. A person who unnecessarily endangers others may be acting from pride rather than devotion.

### Sacrifice in Wider Struggles

The spiritual principle also applies to non-military struggles: resisting sin, defending victims through lawful means, speaking truth, giving wealth, serving the needy, and accepting hardship for justice. Every struggle must be judged by its intention, means, and result.

- Qur’an 4:74–76 on sacrifice, fighting oppression, Allah’s cause, and Satan’s cause.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:75

> And what is it with you? You do not fight in the cause of Allah and for oppressed men, women, and children who cry out, “Our Lord! Deliver us from this land of oppressors! Appoint for us a saviour; appoint for us a helper—all by Your grace.”

**Commentary:**

### Defending the Oppressed

The verse challenges believers who remain passive while men, women, and children are oppressed and cry for deliverance. It presents protection of vulnerable people as part of the cause of Allah. Faith is not merely private devotion; it carries a duty to resist oppression and help those who cannot protect themselves.

### The Prayer of the Oppressed

The oppressed ask Allah to remove them from a land of oppressors and appoint a protector and helper. Their prayer shows both reliance upon Allah and the legitimacy of seeking human means of rescue. Believers should answer such cries through safe evacuation, shelter, advocacy, material support, and justice.

### Men, Women, and Children

The verse explicitly includes men, women, and children, showing that the vulnerable are not disposable in conflict. A military or political cause is not righteous if it harms the very people it claims to defend. Protection of civilians must remain central to any legitimate action.

### Fighting Is Not the Only Form of Help

The word “fight” is used in the context of armed oppression, but assistance can take many forms. Muslims may provide food, medicine, legal aid, refuge, truthful reporting, diplomacy, education, and financial support. The aim is liberation from oppression, not the glorification of violence.

### Lawful Authority and Limits

Armed defence requires legitimate authority, a just cause, necessity, proportionate means, and protection of noncombatants. The verse cannot be used by private groups to launch revenge attacks or to impose their own political agenda. Peaceful remedies should be pursued whenever they can remove harm.

### Allah’s Grace and Human Action

The oppressed ask for rescue by Allah’s grace, but the believers are called to become means through which that grace arrives. Tawakkul does not excuse indifference. It gives courage to act while leaving the final result to Allah.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:76

> Believers fight for the cause of Allah, whereas disbelievers fight for the cause of the Devil. So fight against Satan’s ˹evil˺ forces. Indeed, Satan’s schemes are ever weak.

**Commentary:**

### Different Aims in Conflict

The verse contrasts fighting for Allah’s cause with fighting for Satan’s cause. The difference is not merely which side claims a religious label; it is the aim, justice, method, and obedience involved. A struggle for protection, truth, and the oppressed can be worship when lawful, while aggression, revenge, domination, and persecution serve Satan even when wrapped in noble language.

### The Cause of Allah

Fighting for Allah’s cause, *fī sabīl Allāh*, requires sincerity and adherence to divine limits. It cannot include the murder of innocents, betrayal of covenants, torture, looting, or forced conversion. The Qur’an’s wider commands define the phrase and prevent it from becoming a slogan for private violence.

### Satan’s Forces

Satan’s forces include people and systems that promote disbelief, oppression, arrogance, and corruption. The verse also points to the inner struggle against Satan’s whispers: fear, hatred, greed, despair, and the desire for revenge. Believers must fight these impulses within themselves as they resist external injustice.

### Satan’s Schemes Are Weak

Satan’s schemes are weak because he has no power to compel a person who seeks Allah’s protection. His tools are deception, suggestion, false promises, and the exploitation of desire. A believer defeats him through knowledge, remembrance, obedience, and solidarity.

Weak does not mean harmless. A small temptation can cause great damage when a person welcomes it.

### No Blanket Condemnation

The verse’s battlefield context does not mean that every non-Muslim is a combatant or that Muslims may harm people because of belief alone. Peaceful neighbours and people who do not fight Muslims are to be treated with justice and kindness.

- Qur’an 35:6 and 36:60–62 on Satan as an enemy and the command not to worship or follow him.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:77

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who had been told, “Do not fight! Rather, establish prayer and pay alms-tax.”? Then once the order came to fight, a group of them feared those ˹hostile˺ people as Allah should be feared—or even more. They said, “Our Lord! Why have You ordered us to fight? If only You had delayed ˹the order for˺ us for a little while!” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “The enjoyment of this world is so little, whereas the Hereafter is far better for those mindful ˹of Allah˺. And none of you will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone.

**Commentary:**

### Patience before Permission

The believers were initially instructed not to fight and instead to establish prayer and pay zakāh. This reflects the early period when the Muslim community was commanded to endure persecution without launching armed conflict. Later, when fighting was permitted under specific circumstances, some people who had demanded action became afraid.

The sequence teaches that religious action follows revelation, not impatience or personal enthusiasm.

### Fear of People

A group feared hostile people as Allah should be feared, or even more. Natural fear of danger is not itself a sin. The blameworthy fear is allowing people’s threats to become greater than reverence for Allah, so that a person abandons justice, duty, or truth.

### The Wish for Delay

They asked for a delay because the command exposed the cost of their earlier words. It is easy to imagine courage before responsibility arrives. When sacrifice becomes real, a person discovers whether his desire was sincere or merely emotional.

### Worldly Enjoyment Is Brief

Allah reminds them that worldly enjoyment is little compared with the Hereafter. This does not make life worthless or excuse reckless fighting. It places temporary fear and comfort within the larger horizon of eternal accountability.

### No One Is Wronged

Allah will not wrong anyone, even by the thread of a date stone. No sacrifice, wound, fear, or patient restraint is lost. Nor is any injustice committed by a person overlooked. The believer can accept a difficult duty knowing that Allah’s final accounting is exact.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:78

> Wherever you may be, death will overcome you—even if you were in fortified towers.” When something good befalls them, they say, “This is from Allah,” but when something evil befalls them, they say, “This is from you.” Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “Both have been destined by Allah.” So what is the matter with these people? They can hardly comprehend anything!

**Commentary:**

### Death Cannot Be Escaped

Wherever people are, death will reach them, even in fortified towers. Strong buildings, wealth, technology, military power, and medical care can delay danger but cannot abolish the appointed term. This truth is meant to free believers from cowardice, not to encourage them to neglect safety.

Taking precautions remains part of responsible action; trusting precautions as absolute protection is an illusion.

### Blaming the Messenger

The people described attributed good events to Allah but blamed the Prophet ﷺ for evil. This reflects a common pattern: accepting success as deserved while assigning failure to a leader, messenger, or innocent victim. The Prophet’s duty was to convey guidance; he did not control people’s disobedience or the final outcomes of events.

### Everything within Allah’s Decree

The Prophet is instructed to say that both good and evil events are destined by Allah. Nothing occurs outside His knowledge and permission. This does not make Allah morally pleased with every evil act, nor does it remove human accountability. The next verse explains the distinction between Allah’s blessings and the evil a person brings upon himself.

### Understanding without Fatalism

Divine decree should produce humility, patience, and responsible effort—not passivity. A person must act to prevent harm, treat illness, improve planning, and correct injustice. After taking means, he accepts that the result and the appointed term belong to Allah.

### The Failure to Comprehend

The verse criticizes a shallow worldview that divides reality according to personal convenience: success is attributed to Allah while failure is blamed on a messenger. Sound understanding recognizes Allah’s sovereignty and examines human choices honestly.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:79

> Whatever good befalls you is from Allah and whatever evil befalls you is from yourself.[210] We have sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a messenger to ˹all˺ people. And Allah is sufficient as a Witness.

**Commentary:**

### Good as Divine Grace

Every good that reaches a person ultimately comes from Allah: life, guidance, health, provision, opportunity, protection, and the ability to perform good deeds. Human effort is real, but the ability and circumstances that make it possible are gifts from Allah.

Recognizing this produces gratitude rather than pride.

### Evil Connected to Human Choice

The statement that evil is from oneself points to human wrongdoing, negligence, disobedience, and misuse of blessings. People may contribute to harm through injustice, poor choices, arrogance, or failure to take precautions. The verse teaches self-examination instead of automatically blaming Allah, a messenger, or innocent people.

This does not mean that every illness, disaster, or suffering is the personal fault of the person affected. Allah may test people, and innocent people may suffer from the wrongdoing of others. Final judgement requires knowledge of the full situation.

### Reconciling the Two Verses

Verse 4:78 states that both good and evil occur within Allah’s decree, while this verse distinguishes their attribution. All events are created and permitted by Allah’s wisdom, but good is attributed to His grace and evil to the creature’s moral choice and action. This preserves both divine sovereignty and human responsibility.

### The Messenger to All People

The Prophet ﷺ was sent as a messenger to all people. His message is not restricted to one tribe, region, or historical class. He conveys guidance so that humanity can recognize Allah’s grace and correct the evil it brings upon itself.

### Allah as Witness

Allah is sufficient as a Witness to the truth of the Messenger’s mission and to every good and evil event. He sees the hidden cause, the intention, the harm, and the response. No one needs to manipulate the record before Him.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:80

> Whoever obeys the Messenger has truly obeyed Allah. But whoever turns away, then ˹know that˺ We have not sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a keeper over them.

**Commentary:**

### Obedience to the Messenger

Obeying the Messenger ﷺ is obedience to Allah because he conveys Allah’s revelation, explains it, and embodies its guidance. The verse establishes the authority of the authentic Sunnah alongside the Qur’an. Muslims cannot claim obedience to Allah while deliberately rejecting the Messenger’s clear instruction.

### The Messenger Is Not Worshipped

The verse does not turn the Prophet ﷺ into a partner with Allah. His authority is delegated and revelatory; Allah remains the only One worshipped. Respecting and obeying the Messenger is part of tawḥīd because Allah Himself commanded it.

### Turning Away

A person may turn away by rejecting the message, refusing a just Prophetic ruling, preferring desire over revelation, or treating the Sunnah as irrelevant. Honest questions and efforts to verify an authentic report are different from arrogant rejection.

### No Compulsion of the Heart

The Prophet ﷺ was not sent as a keeper or controller over people. He conveyed the truth, taught, warned, and invited, but he could not force faith into a heart. This protects the distinction between sincere guidance and coercive control.

Leaders and teachers should follow this model: convey clearly, advise wisely, and do not pretend to possess power over another person’s conscience.

### Responsibility after Conveyance

Once the message has been conveyed, each person is responsible for his or her response. The Prophet’s lack of control over rejection does not reduce the seriousness of turning away; it clarifies where responsibility lies.

### A Balanced Relationship with Authority

The verse supports principled obedience, not blind obedience to every person who claims to represent the Prophet. Authentic revelation, sound transmission, justice, and knowledge are necessary. Human authorities remain accountable and cannot claim prophetic status.
