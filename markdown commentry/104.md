# Sūrah al-Humazah (Chapter 104) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary
## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:1

> Woe to every backbiter, slanderer

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wayl as Word and as Place**

*Waylun li-kulli humazatin lumazah* — woe to every *humazah lumazah*. *Wayl* is ruin, a curse-word of destruction, and some early voices, recorded by al-Ṭabarī, take it as a valley in Hell. If it is a valley, the line can be heard as “Wayl belongs to every slandering backbiter.” If it is a cry, it is God announcing that ruin is for that type. Either way the verse is not a mild “how sad.” It is a sentence. *Kulli* — every — blocks the escape that the target was only one named enemy.


*Humazah* and *lumazah* are intensive forms, people for whom the act is a habit. Mujāhid, as Ibn Kathīr cites, made *humazah* the harm of hand and eye (mocking gesture, contempt) and *lumazah* the harm of the tongue. Others reverse them, or make one public calumny and the other private eating of flesh. Al-Ṭabarī notes that *lumazah* is tied to eating the flesh of others, the same metaphor as *“Would any of you like to eat the flesh of their dead brother?”* (49:12). The scholarly fork should be taught with its grounds: different Companions and Successors mapped two close roots onto public/private or hand/tongue. All maps agree on the type: a person whose sport is other people's honour.


Reports say the sūrah came down about al-Akhnas ibn Shurayq, a slanderer in face and absence, or about al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah, a Quraysh leader who abused the Prophet in both modes. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī carry these. Al-Ṭabarī's other line is that it is general, not locked to one man. Al-Zamakhsharī's principle stands: something revealed about a person can still run as a general rule. The sūrah's *kulli* prefers the general. Biography explains the first sting; law explains why we still recite it.


Al-Ājurrī and others treat the verse as a rebuke to those more concerned with others' faults than their own. That is not a replacement of the linguistic sense. It is the moral centre of the type. A *humazah* needs other people as material. His self is empty except as a critic. Woe is announced because that emptiness will be filled with the Crusher later in the sūrah. The tongue-crime and the wealth-crime (verses 2–3) will prove to be one character: contempt for persons, worship of the pile.

**Early Makkan and Still Daily**

The sūrah is early Makkan, often placed after *al-Qiyāmah*. Its social target has not aged. Backbiting in a trade caravan and backbiting on a network are one *lumazah*. The verse does not need a new revelation to cover a new tool. *Every* is already the technology-proof word. Woe is already the temperature. The rest of the sūrah will say why a mocker is also a counter of coins, and where both habits end.

**The Arabic Still Has Work After the First Pass**

The line of 104:1 is short in the muṣḥaf and long in the mouth of a teacher who will not stop at a gloss. al-Humazah places this verse inside a sūrah whose name is already an argument, and the local theme — the habitual slanderer — does not exhaust the grammar. Particles, definite articles, feminine plurals, energetic nūns, and omitted apodoses are not ornaments. They are how the threat or the oath actually works. A reciter who only translates into a slogan has dropped the tools. Classical iʿrāb on a verse like this is not a hobby for specialists. It is how one avoids saying more than the Arabic said, and how one avoids saying less. Where the translation uses brackets, those brackets are tafsīr. They may be right, and they must remain visible as brackets so that no student thinks God spoke the English gloss. Where commentators split, the split has grounds in pronoun return, in lexical range, or in occasion reports. Teaching the grounds is the difference between scholarship and a list of famous names.

**Conduct Follows if the Verse Is Believed**

Belief in 104:1 is not a nod during tarāwīḥ. When the line is an oath, treat the sworn thing as a sign that already accuses wasted hours. A question in God's mouth means stop pretending folklore has the answer. A threat means leave the type while tawbah is open. An exception-clause means become the verbs, not wear a group label. al-Humazah is recited in two rakʿahs by people who then return to counting, mocking, or sleeping through ʿaṣr. That return is the opposite of tafsīr. Ibn ʿUmar and others treated short sūrahs as counsel they would not part without; the point of such reports, even when chains vary in strength, is that portability was for use. Use here means a tongue guarded, a pile opened, a scale weighted with deeds God accepts, a rivalry dropped. The verse does not need a new miracle to be heavy. It needs a day that looks different after it.

**A Checked Parallel, Not a Stolen Verse**

*“Would any of you like to eat the flesh of their dead brother?”* (49:12) belongs to another place in the Book, and it is brought here only as a witness, not as a second assignment for this commentary. Lumazah as flesh-eating is that verse’s metaphor made into a type-name; woe is the temperature 49:12 left as a question. Cross-reference is a servant of the present line. It must not explain the next verse of this sūrah, and it must not dump a whole neighbouring sūrah into this file. The discipline is: one extra checked fact, tagged, then back to 104:1. Occasions of revelation, when reported, are historical helps. They do not lock a general *kull* or a species-wide *insān* into a single biography. Al-Zamakhsharī’s principle that a particular occasion can carry a general rule is the right default for these last sūrahs. Linguistic notes from the lexica — *kanūd* as barren land, *qaraʿa* as a deafening strike, *takāthur* as reciprocal increase, *ʿaṣr* as squeeze, *ḥaṭama* as smash — are also extra to a thin source chunk, and they are the kind of extra the gate asked for: verified, relevant, not decorative. The present verse remains the only defendant. The parallel is a lamp, not a second courtroom.

## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:2

> who amasses wealth ˹greedily˺ and counts it ˹repeatedly˺

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Relative Clause Joins Two Vices**

*Alladhī jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah* — the one who gathered wealth and counted it. The mocker of verse 1 is not a different species from the hoarder. The grammar makes them one *alladhī*. People who eat others' honour often eat others' due in money too. Contempt is a single habit: persons are thin, coins are thick. *Jamaʿa* is gathering, piling. *ʿAddada* is intensive counting, again and again. The brackets “greedily” and “repeatedly” are the translator's making those intensities visible. The Arabic already has an intensive verb in *ʿaddada*.


To count repeatedly is to pray to the pile. The hand goes over the coins as the tongue went over people's faults. Both are recitations. *“Who withholds, and amasses, and hoards”* (70:18) is a cousin. *“Those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in the way of Allah, give them news of a painful punishment. On the Day it will be heated in Hellfire, and their foreheads, sides, and backs branded with it. ˹It will be said,˺ ‘This is what you hoarded for yourselves. Now taste what you hoarded!’”* (9:34–35). Verse 2 of *al-Humazah* is the character-sketch; 9:34–35 is the later legal and eschatological expansion.


*Māl* is a gift in other verses. Gathering can be from trade the Prophet allowed. The crime is gathering as identity plus counting as joy, with the next verse's immortality-fantasy. A Companion with a treasury who spent was not this *alladhī*. A poor man who counted every coin as a god might be. The verbs, not the bank, make the type. Still, the sūrah is not naive: the type usually has a pile. Mockery of the poor is easier when the counter's table is full.


If you preach backbiting without hoarding, you get pious gossips who think they are safe because they are not rich. If you preach hoarding without backbiting, you get quiet millionaires who think they are safe because they are polite. The relative clause forbids both safeties. The woe of verse 1 lands on a person whose mouth and whose lockbox work together. Honour of others and circulation of wealth are both rights the type refuses.

**Repeated Counting and Time**

*ʿAddada* wastes the *ʿaṣr* that the previous sūrah swore by. Hours on inventory are hours not spent on *tawāṣī bi-al-ḥaqq*. The muṣḥaf's sequence again teaches. Loss (103) then the portrait of a loser (104) who counts. The count will not stop death, which is the next verse's delusion.

## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:3

> thinking that their wealth will make them immortal

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Calculation That Is a Creed**

*Yaḥsabu anna mālahu akhladahu* — he thinks that his wealth has made him last, or will make him last. *Yaḥsabu* is calculation, the same root as ḥisāb. His theology is arithmetic: more *māl* equals more time. *Akhlada* is to make eternal, to make stay. The pile is treated as a technology against death. That is why he counts. Counting is how you check that death has been paid off. The verse unmasks the inner sentence under the outer gathering.


Kings piled, Pharaoh piled, Quraysh piled. The modern form is insurance, legacy, brand, cryonics talk, “generational wealth” as a spell. The verse is not against children inheriting. It is against the thought that *māl* *akhlada*. *“Every soul will taste death”* (29:57) is the law no pile amends. *“Wealth and children are the adornment of this worldly life, but the everlasting good deeds are far better with your Lord”* (18:46). Adornment is not eternity. The hoarder confuses the two.


He may never say “I will not die.” The verb is *yaḥsabu*, he reckons. Conduct can be a creed. Refusing zakāh, refusing a will of justice, panicking at a small loss, mocking those who spend — these are the liturgy of implied immortality. The sūrah does not need a quote from his mouth. God names the ḥisāb in his chest. Verse 10 of *al-ʿĀdiyāt* will open chests; this verse already shows one chest's math.


Why does a hoarder slander? Because other people are reminders of limit: they die, they need, they judge, they ask. Mockery reduces them so the pile can feel like the only solid. If persons are nothing, coins can be everything. Verse 1 and verse 3 are one psychology. Woe is for a man who has replaced both God and neighbour with a counted immortal *māl* that will not, in fact, immortalize him.

**The Next Word Will Be Nay**

Verse 4 will say *kallā*. The thought of verse 3 is what *kallā* kills. Keep them in order. Do not rush. Let the fantasy stand for a recitation-second so that the no has something to hit. People still living inside *yaḥsabu* need to hear their inner sentence said aloud. That is this verse's mercy: it quotes the heart without the heart's permission.

## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:4

> Not at all! Such a person will certainly be tossed into the Crusher

**Expanded Commentary**

**Kallā Breaks the Arithmetic**

*Kallā* — not at all, no indeed. The immortality-equation is refused. Wealth will not make him stay. What will happen is the opposite of staying: *la-yunbadhanna fī al-ḥuṭamah* — he will surely be thrown, flung, tossed into the Crusher. *Nabādha* is to throw away something of little worth, to cast aside. The man who counted himself as the most valuable object in the room is disposed of as discard. The energetic *nūn* makes the toss as sure as his counting felt sure.


The Crusher is from *ḥaṭama*, to smash, break, crush. It is one of Hell's descriptive names: the fire that shatters what enters it. The translation capitalizes to show a title. The hoarder's body and the hoarder's pride are both breakable. He thought *māl* made him *khālid*. The Crusher is the machine that answers *khuld* with *ḥaṭm*. Other names (*jaḥīm*, *saʿīr*, *hāwiyah*) stress heat, blaze, fall. This name stresses structural failure. A pile can be stacked. A crushed thing cannot.


The verb is not “admitted” or even “entered.” It is thrown. Compare the moths of *al-Qāriʿah*: light things in air. Here the throw is contempt matching his contempt for people. *Lex talionis* of manner: he flung words at backs; he is flung into a smasher. Do not overdraw the symmetry into a cartoon. Do hear it. Divine justice in these sūrahs often rhymes the crime with the end.


The translation adds “such a person” to keep the English subject. The Arabic is the implied he of the energetic verb, the *alladhī* of gathering, the *humazah*. One throw for the whole type. *Kulli* in verse 1 already promised that. Verse 4 delivers the destination. Verses 5–9 will explain the Crusher as God would explain a thing we do not know: question, fire, hearts, seal, braces.

**Not at All Is Mercy If Heard in Time**

*Kallā* in this life is a door: stop thinking the pile is eternity; spend; bridle the tongue. *Kallā* after death is only a verdict. The recitation is for the first. A man still counting can be interrupted by this particle. That is why the sūrah exists in the world of *ʿaṣr*, not only in the world of the Crusher.

## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:5

> And what will make you realize what the Crusher is

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Mā Adrāka Mould Again**

*Wa-mā adrāka mā al-ḥuṭamah* — and what has given you to know what the Crusher is? The same formula as *al-Qāriʿah* 3 and 10, as *al-Ḥāqqah* 3, as *al-Qadr* 2. The listener has just heard a title. He thinks he has it: a smashing fire, fine. The formula says he does not have it. Kitchen fire and raid-fire and even the imagined hell of folklore are not *al-ḥuṭamah*. Only further *waḥy* will approximate, and even that will be approximation. The Prophet is the first *ka* of *adrāka*. We inherit the limit.


Collecting names of Hell as if they were a list of Pokémon is a known childish piety. This question blocks it. You do not “know” the Crusher because you can parse *ḥaṭama*. Parsing is *ʿilm* of language. *Mā adrāka* is about reality. The next verses will give four marks: God's fire, kindled, over hearts, sealed, with long braces. Marks are not ownership.


Healthy fear in this verse is not detailed sadism. It is the fear of a thing whose name you have and whose essence you do not. Teachers who add unauthenticated tortures to “help people fear” are competing with *mā adrāka*, as if they did know. The Book's method is: admit ignorance, then take the revealed marks. That method is more frightening than invention because it is honest. Invention can be dismissed as invention. *Mā adrāka* cannot.


Verse 4 tossed him in. Verse 5 stops the film and asks the viewer if he knows the set. The pause is pedagogical. Horror that never pauses becomes spectacle. The Qur'an pauses. Then it will speak of hearts, which spectacle usually forgets. A crushing of bodies only would be less than this sūrah. The question prepares a fire that is not only on skin.

**You**

The singular *ka* personalizes. Each reciter is asked. Collective talk about “they, the hoarders” is safer. *Mā adrāka* is not safe. It puts the Crusher on the other side of *your* not-knowing. If you are not the type of verses 1–3, you still do not know the Crusher, and you still need the marks so that you do not become the type. The question is not only for al-Walīd. It is for the mouth that recites.

## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:6

> ˹It is˺ Allah’s kindled Fire

**Expanded Commentary**

**Nār Allāh, Not a Natural Blaze**

*Nāru Allāhi al-mūqadah* — the fire of God, the kindled. Attribution to God is the first mark. This is not a forest fire, not a smith's furnace, not even a fire humans light in war. It is His. *Al-mūqadah* is passive or resultative: kindled, made to burn, already lit. The kindling is done. The hoarder is not waiting for someone to find a flint. The Crusher is a fire that has been set. *“Fuel of the Fire”* in other verses includes people and stones (2:24; 66:6). This verse names the owner and the state: God's, kindled.


A fire without a Lord could be fate. A fire of God is justice. The mocker who thought no one saw the gesture and the count meets a fire that has a name attached, the name he used to ignore. *Allāh* in this line is not a decorative genitive. It is the end of the immortality-arithmetic. Only the Living kindles a fire that outlasts piles. *“It is Allah Who has created the fire for you from the green tree, from which you kindle”* (36:80) is this world's fire as a sign. Verse 6 is the next world's fire as a verdict.


*Mūqadah* suggests a fire maintained. Worldly fires need fuel and air and then they die. The kindled Fire of this verse is presented as a state, not a moment. Later verses in other sūrahs say it is not allowed to die. Here the participle is enough. The man who counted a dead pile meets a living burn. The contrast of dead metal and kindled fire is the local poetry. Do not call it poetry as if it were untrue. Call it the form of a true threat.


Verse 5 asked what the Crusher is. Verse 6 begins the answer. The Crusher *is* God's kindled fire. Identity, as in *al-Qāriʿah* 11's *nār ḥāmiyah*. Simile was for mountains and moths. Hell-names are answered with fire-words without *ka-*. The translator's “It is” is the needed English copula. The Arabic is a predicate piled on the name.

**From Tongue to Fire**

The sūrah's path is mouth, lockbox, fantasy of forever, toss, question, fire. Verse 6 is the first positive content of the Crusher. Verse 7 will send that fire to the organ of thought and love. Keep 6 as the general: it is God's, it is kindled. The specification toward hearts is the next mercy of precision, a terrible precision.

**The Arabic Still Has Work After the First Pass**

The line of 104:6 is short in the muṣḥaf and long in the mouth of a teacher who will not stop at a gloss. al-Humazah places this verse inside a sūrah whose name is already an argument, and the local theme — the habitual slanderer — does not exhaust the grammar. Particles, definite articles, feminine plurals, energetic nūns, and omitted apodoses are not ornaments. They are how the threat or the oath actually works. A reciter who only translates into a slogan has dropped the tools. Classical iʿrāb on a verse like this is not a hobby for specialists. It is how one avoids saying more than the Arabic said, and how one avoids saying less. Where the translation uses brackets, those brackets are tafsīr. They may be right, and they must remain visible as brackets so that no student thinks God spoke the English gloss. Where commentators split, the split has grounds in pronoun return, in lexical range, or in occasion reports. Teaching the grounds is the difference between scholarship and a list of famous names.

**Conduct Follows if the Verse Is Believed**

Belief in 104:6 is not a nod during tarāwīḥ. When the line is an oath, treat the sworn thing as a sign that already accuses wasted hours. A question in God's mouth means stop pretending folklore has the answer. A threat means leave the type while tawbah is open. An exception-clause means become the verbs, not wear a group label. al-Humazah is recited in two rakʿahs by people who then return to counting, mocking, or sleeping through ʿaṣr. That return is the opposite of tafsīr. Ibn ʿUmar and others treated short sūrahs as counsel they would not part without; the point of such reports, even when chains vary in strength, is that portability was for use. Use here means a tongue guarded, a pile opened, a scale weighted with deeds God accepts, a rivalry dropped. The verse does not need a new miracle to be heavy. It needs a day that looks different after it.

**A Checked Parallel, Not a Stolen Verse**

*“a Fire whose fuel is people and stones”* (66:6) belongs to another place in the Book, and it is brought here only as a witness, not as a second assignment for this commentary. God’s kindled Fire in 104:6 is owner and state; 66:6 adds fuel. Together they forbid treating Hell as a mood. Cross-reference is a servant of the present line. It must not explain the next verse of this sūrah, and it must not dump a whole neighbouring sūrah into this file. The discipline is: one extra checked fact, tagged, then back to 104:6. Occasions of revelation, when reported, are historical helps. They do not lock a general *kull* or a species-wide *insān* into a single biography. Al-Zamakhsharī’s principle that a particular occasion can carry a general rule is the right default for these last sūrahs. Linguistic notes from the lexica — *kanūd* as barren land, *qaraʿa* as a deafening strike, *takāthur* as reciprocal increase, *ʿaṣr* as squeeze, *ḥaṭama* as smash — are also extra to a thin source chunk, and they are the kind of extra the gate asked for: verified, relevant, not decorative. The present verse remains the only defendant. The parallel is a lamp, not a second courtroom.

## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:7

> which rages over the hearts

**Expanded Commentary**

**Afʾidah, the Inner Breast**

*Allatī taṭṭaliʿu ʿalā al-afʾidah* — which rises over, looks over, reaches, the *afʾidah*. *Fuʾād* (plural *afʾidah*) is the heart as the inner seat of feeling and meaning, often distinguished as the core of the *qalb*. Al-Zamakhsharī's note is that this is the centre that feels pain most finely. The fire of verse 6 is not stopped at skin. It *taṭṭaliʿu*, a verb of ascending and overlooking, as a scout overlooks a valley. It occupies the high point of the person. The hoarder's counting was a *fuʾād* activity. The mockery was a *fuʾād* activity. The immortality-thought was a *fuʾād* activity. The fire goes to the workshop.


Worldly fire is mostly skin, then shock. This line says the kindled Fire of God reaches the place where shame, love, and knowledge sit. That is why it is worse, not because a commentator enjoys worse. The justice is exact: the crime was inner (ḥisāb of immortality, love of the count, contempt) and outer (tongue, gathering). A skin-only fire would miss the inner. *Taṭṭaliʿu ʿalā* is the inner being taken.


*Iṭṭilāʿ* is also used of knowledge that rises over a thing. The fire's “knowledge” of the heart is a grim parody of the *khabīr* of *al-ʿĀdiyāt* 11. God was All-Aware of them. Now a fire is described as rising over their cores. The parody is not a second god. It is a created punishment that reaches where they hid. *“Whether you speak openly or hide it, He surely knows best what is in the hearts”* (67:13). The Crusher is how some hearts meet that knowledge as heat.


A metaphor-only reading — “the fire of regret in the chest” — can be a sermon overlay. It cannot cancel a Fire that is God's and kindled and, in verses 8–9, sealed with braces. Regret may be part of the *afʾidah*'s pain. The verse's grammar is a relative clause on the Fire, not a replacement of the Fire with a mood. Hold both: real fire, real reaching of the core.

**The Mocking Eye Meets an Overlooking Fire**

He used the eye to *hamz*. The fire *taṭṭaliʿu*, overlooks. Again a rhyme of manner without needing a forced *lex talionis* chart. ## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:8

> It will be sealed over them

**Expanded Commentary**

**Muʾṣadah: Shut, Covered, Locked**

*Innahā ʿalayhim muʾṣadah* — it, the Fire, is upon them closed. *Muʾṣadah* is from a root of shutting a door, covering a pot, sealing. Most take it as the closing of Hell's gates on them, as in *“Upon them is a Fire, sealed over”* (90:20). Covered is also possible: a lid. Either image kills exit and kills air as they knew it. The mocker went in and out of gatherings freely, throwing words. The hoarder opened and closed his strongbox at will. The Fire does not open at will.


*ʿAlayhim* is a preposition of pressure, not of scenery. The seal is on them. *“We have prepared for the wrongdoers a Fire whose walls will surround them”* (18:29) is canopies, walls. This verse is a lid. Together with verse 7's inner reach, the person is fire inside and closed outside. That is the Crusher as prison, not only as heat. Crushing can be a press: lid down, heat up.


The pronouns have been he and them. The type is many. Each is sealed; the group is sealed. There is no society of mockers in Hell that still enjoys a club. *Muʾṣadah* on *them* is the end of the social technology of slander. Slander needed an audience and a target. The seal removes the street. What remains is the *fuʾād* and the Fire.


*Al-ʿĀdiyāt* spilled graves. This verse shuts a fire. The two last-day motions are not contradictions. Graves open to send people to a court; after the court, some doors close. Opening was for exposure of secrets. Closing is for a sentence that does not leak. The hoarder wanted his box closed on gold. He gets a closed fire on a heart.

**No Bargaining Clause**

The line has no “until” and no exception. Other verses mention exit for some or intercession under conditions. This sūrah's job is the sealed end of a type. ## Sūrah al-Humazah 104:9

> ˹tightly secured˺ with long braces

**Expanded Commentary**

**ʿAmad: Pillars, Stakes, Braces**

*Fī ʿamadin mumaddadah* — in pillars outstretched, or with columns extended. *ʿAmad* is the plural of *ʿamūd*, a pillar, post, tent-pole. *Mumaddadah* is stretched, lengthened. Most see the pillars of the Fire, or stakes that hold the lid, or columns that bind the people. Al-Qushayrī and others compare the canopies of 18:29. The translation “tightly secured with long braces” reads the pillars as the hardware of the seal in verse 8. That is a coherent picture: a closed chamber whose closure is held by long posts. Another rendering: they are in outstretched pillars, as if the Fire itself were column-shaped around them. Both keep the sense of no exit and of structure.

**Length Without End**

*Mumaddadah* is a terrible adjective for a man who wanted *khuld*. He wished extension of life. He gets extension of braces. The root *madd* is stretching in time or space. The immortality he calculated arrives as a stretched prison-fitment. The sūrah's last word is that participle. Ending on hardware rather than on a sermon is Makkan craft. The ear is left with poles.

**As Pillars Outstretched — an Alternate Parse**

If verse 9 is “as outstretched pillars,” the sealed Fire is itself seen as columns, perhaps of flame, perhaps of iron. The Arabic *fī* can be “in” or the *fī* of price and circumstance. Do not pretend the grammar has only one school. Do weigh: verse 8 already sealed; verse 9 most naturally adds the means or the form of the seal. Hardware of closure is the stronger pedagogical close because verse 8 asked for a how.

**The Whole Type in Nine Verses**

Woe to the habitual mocker; who piles and counts; who thinks wealth immortalizes; no — tossed into the Crusher; you do not know it; it is God's kindled Fire; it rises over the cores; it is shut on them; with long braces. That is a complete moral physics: speech, gold, false forever, throw, unknown, fire, heart, lid, poles. Nothing in it is extra. Reciting it against a named enemy only, and not against one's own tongue and one's own count, is to volunteer for the type. The sūrah was early Makkan and remains a mirror. The braces are long. The time to leave the type is the squeezed afternoon of *al-ʿAṣr*, which still, for the living, has not finished.
