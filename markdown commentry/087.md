# Sūrah al-Aʿlā (Chapter 87) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**The Most High and the Pathway of Ease**

Al-Aʿlā, "The Most High," is the eighty-seventh chapter of the Qur'an, comprising 19 verses of early Makkan revelation. It opens with the soaring, beloved liturgical command: *“Exalt the name of your Lord, the Most High (*Sabbiḥi isma Rabbika al-Aʿlā*)!”* (87:1). In the devotional life of the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ, Sūrah al-Aʿlā held a deeply cherished, sacred station: ʿĀ'ishah, ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās, and al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr reported in authentic collections (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 865 and Sunan Abī Dāwūd 1424) that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ loved this sūrah above all the shorter chapters, reciting it regularly in the first cycle of the Friday congregational prayer, during the two annual ʿĪd celebrations, and in the final *Witr* prayer of the night. In nineteen melodious, comforting verses, Sūrah al-Aʿlā reveals the fourfold stages of cosmic creation, promises the unlettered Prophet that he will never forget the divine revelation (*sa-nuqri'uka fa-lā tansā*), promises to ease his path toward ultimate ease (*wa-nuyassiruka li-l-yusrā*), and traces the golden thread of monotheism back to the ancient scrolls of Abraham and Moses.

**The Structural Symmetry and the Ancient Scrolls**

The architecture of Sūrah al-Aʿlā moves across three luminous movements:
1. The Fourfold Creativity of the Most High (1–5): Commands the glorification of the Lord: - Who created and proportioned (*khalaqa fa-sawwā*), - And Who determined measure and guided (*qaddara fa-hadā*), - And Who brought forth the green pasture (*akhraja al-marʿā*), - And then made it black stubble / dry chaff (*ghuthā'an aḥwā*).
2. The Prophetic Assurance and the Useful Reminder (6–13): Guarantees the preservation of the Qur'an in the Prophet's memory, pledges divine ease, and commands the distribution of guidance: *“So remind, if the reminder should benefit!”* It divides humanity into two psychological responses: - *The Soul with Awe*: *“He who fears [Allah] will be reminded.”* - *The Wretched*: *“But the most wretched one will avoid it—he who will enter the Great Fire, wherein he will neither die nor live!”*
3. The Secret of Purification and the Scrolls of Old (14–19): Delivers the immortal formula of spiritual felicity in verses 14–15: *“Certainly he has succeeded who purifies himself (*qad aflaḥa man tazakkā*), and remembers the name of his Lord and prays!”* It indicts human short-sightedness: *“Nay, you prefer the life of this world, while the Hereafter is better and more enduring (*wa-l-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā*)!”* The chapter concludes by connecting this revelation to the primordial spiritual history of mankind: *“Indeed, this is in the former scriptures—the scriptures of Abraham and Moses (*Ṣuḥufi Ibrāhīma wa-Mūsā*)!”*

## Sūrah al-Aʿlā 87:1

**The Manner of the Glorification**

The first direction is the direction of the separation: the glorification of the Name is the glorification that keeps the Name apart from every other thing, and the separation is what the command requires. **The Place in the Prayer**

The tradition records the place of the sūrah's verse in the prayer, and the place is the place of the prostration. The report states that when the verse of the sūrah of the Human — "So glorify the Name of your Lord, the Greatest" (56:74) — was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ told his Companions to enact it in the bowing, and when the verse of this sūrah was revealed, he told them to enact it in the prostration. The two verses are the two verses of the two positions, and the two formulas followed: the formula of the bowing is the glorification of the Greatest, and the formula of the prostration is the glorification of the Most High. The sūrah's verse is the verse of the prostration, and the prostration is the place where the worshipper is nearest to the Lord, and the formula of the place is the formula of the sūrah's command.

**The Name of the Sūrah**

The sūrah takes its name from the word of its first verse, and the name is the name of the attribute: al-Aʿlā, the Most High. The naming of the sūrah by the attribute is the Book's way of placing the sūrah under the attribute it states, and the sūrah states the attribute in its first word. The attribute is the attribute of the height above the creation, and the sūrah's statement of the attribute is the statement of the Lord above the acts it will name. The sūrah that opens on the attribute carries the attribute through its verses: the acts of the Lord are the acts of the Most High, and the encouragement of the Lord is the encouragement of the Most High, and the contrast of the sūrah is the contrast that the Most High states.
