# Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt (Chapter 49) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:1

> O believers! Do not proceed ˹in any matter˺ before ˹a decree from˺ Allah and His Messenger. And fear Allah. Surely Allah is All-Hearing, AllKnowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Address That Prohibits**

The sūrah opens with a prohibition, and the prohibition is the sūrah's way of setting its tone. The address is to the believers, and the command is not to proceed in any matter before Allah and His Messenger, and the command is the sūrah's master word for everything that follows. The word for the proceeding is the word for the stepping before, and the stepping is the sūrah's image for the whole range of the conduct it corrects, from the voice that is raised too soon to the report that is acted on too soon. The sūrah is from the late Medinan period, and it comes after the conquest of Makkah, when most of Arabia had come under the rule of the Muslim polity, and the community it addresses is the community of that later moment, larger and more diverse than the community of the earlier sūrahs. The newcomers to the polity are the ones the verse has in view, and the newcomers are the reason the sūrah begins where it begins. A community that has grown beyond the tribe needs the manners of the larger body, and the manners are what the first verse commands. The prohibition is the sūrah's door, and the door is the sūrah's way of entering the house of the community's conduct.

**The Manners Before the Leader**

In its immediate context, the verse enjoins the Muslims to avoid coarse manners in the presence of the Prophet, and the enjoining is the sūrah's way of reminding the recent joiners that the one they stand before is more than a tribal leader. The relation of the Bedouin to the chief of his own tribe is the relation of familiarity, and the familiarity is the ground of the coarseness. The sūrah's prohibition removes that ground: the one who stands before the Messenger of God is not to stand in the manner of the one who stands before his own chief, and the not-standing is what the verse commands. The form of the command is the command of timing, not to proceed before, and the timing is the sūrah's way of saying that the moment of speech and the moment of action belong to the order of the revelation, not to the impatience of the one who is present. The one who proceeds before has taken the moment for himself, and the taking is what the verse forbids.

**The Broader Reading, Opinion Against Command**

The exegesis carries the verse beyond the manners, and the broader reading is the sūrah's way of extending the prohibition from the door of the apartment to the heart of the faith. Some take the verse as the command not to contradict the Quran or the Sunnah of the Prophet, and the taking is the sūrah's way of making the prohibition a rule of the creed. Others take it as the command not to follow one's own opinion in place of the teaching of the Prophet, and the command is the sūrah's way of placing the opinion under the order. And the sūrah's context connects the command to the famous saying of the Prophet, that none of the community truly believes until he loves him more than his father and his children and all of mankind, and the saying is the sūrah's way of stating the love that the prohibition protects. The readings form a line from the manner to the creed, and the line is the sūrah's claim: the same prohibition that orders the voice orders the heart, and the heart is the ground of the voice.

**The Fear That Closes the Verse**

The verse closes with the standard close of the sūrah's addresses, the command to fear Allah, and the close is the sūrah's way of placing the prohibition on its ground. The prohibition is not stated as a rule between the community and its leader alone; it is stated as an act done before the one who hears and knows. The All-Hearing and the All-Knowing are the two names that close the verse, and the two names are the sūrah's way of saying that the proceeding before is not a private matter. The one who raises his voice in the presence of the Prophet is heard, and the one who proceeds before the decree is known, and the hearing and the knowing are the sūrah's ground for the fear. The fear is the sūrah's name for the state of the one who stands before the hearing, and the state is what the verse commands with the prohibition.

**The Sūrah's Door and Its House**

The position of the verse is the reason for its function. It is the first verse of the sūrah, and the sūrah is the sūrah of conduct, the sūrah that instructs the community in the manners of its encounter with the Prophet and with one another. The first verse states the master prohibition, and the remaining verses of the sūrah's first section unfold it in its forms: the raising of the voices in the second, the lowering of the voices in the third, the calling from behind the apartments in the fourth, the patience that would have been in the fifth, and the verification of the news in the sixth. Each of the verses is a form of the proceeding before, and the forms are the sūrah's way of making the first prohibition concrete. The door of the sūrah is the prohibition, and the house of the sūrah is the conduct, and the sūrah's claim, by its structure, is that the conduct is the unfolding of the first word.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:2

> O believers! Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet, nor speak loudly to him as you do to one another, or your deeds will become void while you are unaware.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Incident That Produced the Verse**

The tradition gives the verse its occasion, and the occasion is the argument between Abū Bakr and ʿUmar over the delegation of an outlying tribe. The two righteous ones each favored a different individual for the honor of receiving the delegation, and the favoring turned into a quarrel in the presence of the Prophet, and the quarrel was so loud that the voice of the Prophet was drowned out by the two. The account preserved in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, in the narration of Ibn Abī Mulaykah, states the weight of the moment in the community's own words: the two almost earned destruction when they raised their voices before the Prophet, and the almost is the sūrah's way of stating how close the moment came to the edge. One of the two recommended al-Aqrabʿ ibn Ḥabīs, the man of Banū Mujāshiʿ, and the other recommended another, and the recommendation was the matter of the argument. The sūrah's verse is the revelation that came at the moment, and the coming is the sūrah's way of stating that the incident was not passed over.

**The Voice Above the Voice**

The prohibition of the verse is stated in the measure of sound, and the measure is the sūrah's way of making the relation of the community to the Prophet visible. Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet, the verse commands, and the command is the sūrah's way of fixing the distance of the voice as the distance of the rank. The voice of the one who stands before the Messenger is not to be the louder voice, and the not-louder is what the verse states. The Quran states the same principle in its standing form in the sūrah of the light, where the summons of the Messenger is not to be treated as the summons of one another, "Do not treat the Messenger's summons to you ˹as lightly˺ as your summons to one another" (24:63). The two verses together fix the doctrine of the voice: the voice is the measure of the relation, and the relation is what the voice either preserves or breaks. The sūrah's verse takes the doctrine and states it in the form of the incident, and the form is the sūrah's way of making the doctrine the command.

**The Familiarity of the Newcomer**

The exegesis gives the verse its second object, and the second object is the new member of the polity. The verse speaks primarily to those who had recently joined the Muslim community and had taken an attitude of excessive familiarity with the Prophet, and the familiarity is the ground of the loudness. The command of the verse, in its practical form, is the command of the naming: the newcomer is not to call the Prophet by the name of intimacy, Muḥammad, but by the title of his office, the Messenger of God or the Prophet of God. The title preserves the office, and the office is what the community's relation to the Prophet is a relation to. The sūrah's point is that the relation is not a relation of kinship, and the not-kinship is what the title states. A community that addresses its leader by the name of intimacy has taken the leader for one of its own, and the taking is what the sūrah's prohibition removes.

**The Deeds That Become Void**

The verse states its penalty in the sūrah's gravest register, and the register is the voiding of the deeds. Or your deeds will become void while you are unaware, the verse says, and the saying is the sūrah's way of stating that the raising of the voice is not a small breach of the manner. The Quran maintains in its standing addresses that the deeds come to naught in the absence of the proper state of the one who does them, and the sūrah's verse takes the standing doctrine and applies it to the incident of the voice. The application is the sūrah's claim: the deed stands on the relation, and the relation is what the voice breaks, and the breaking of the relation is the voiding of the deed. The while you are unaware is the sūrah's way of stating that the voiding is not noticed at the moment it happens, and the not-noticing is what makes the voiding grave. A deed that is voided in the dark is a deed that is lost without the loss being felt.

**The First Form of the First Prohibition**

The position of the verse is the reason for its function. It is the second verse of the sūrah, and it follows immediately on the general prohibition of the first, and the following is the sūrah's way of unfolding the general in its first specific form. The first verse commanded the not-proceeding-before, and the second verse commands the not-raising-above, and the two commands are the two registers of the same prohibition, the register of the timing and the register of the volume. The manner is the subject of the sūrah, and the sūrah's first two verses state the subject in its two measures, the measure of the moment and the measure of the sound. The sūrah's claim, by the sequence, is that the conduct of the community toward the Prophet is a single matter with two faces, and that the two faces are stated in the two verses before the sūrah turns to the form of the conduct that the two verses approve.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:3

> Indeed, those who lower their voices in the presence of Allah's Messenger are the ones whose hearts Allah has refined for righteousness. They will have forgiveness and a great reward.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Approval of the Quiet**

The sūrah's second verse commanded the not-raising, and this verse states the raising's opposite, the lowering. The form of the sūrah is the form of the pair: the prohibition and the approval, the voice that is not to be raised and the voice that is to be lowered, and the pair is the sūrah's way of stating the subject of the section in its two directions. The verse does not merely forbid the loud; it names the quiet, and the naming is the sūrah's way of saying that the community is to be known by the quiet. Those who lower their voices in the presence of the Messenger, the verse says, and the saying is the sūrah's way of making the quiet the mark of the community. The mark is what the sūrah's remaining verses will show to be the sign by which the believers are recognized, and the recognition is the sūrah's way of completing the doctrine of the voice. The quiet, in the sūrah's account, is the outer form of the inner state, and the form is what the verse makes the state into.

**The Hearts That Are Tested**

The word the sūrah uses for the refinement of the hearts is the word for the assay, the testing of a metal for its purity, and the word is the sūrah's way of stating what has happened to the hearts of the quiet. The exegesis gives the work of the assay in its three readings: God has tested the purity of the hearts, and the testing is the assay itself; God has expanded the hearts for righteousness, and the expansion is what the assay makes possible; God has purified the intentions, and the purification is the result of the assay. The three readings are the three stages of the work, and the stages are the sūrah's way of stating that the quiet is not the cause of the refinement but its sign. The Quran states the assay in its plainest form in the verse on the losses of Uḥud, where the testing is the stated purpose of the trial, "Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts" (3:154). The sūrah's verse takes the assay and applies it to the manner, and the application is the sūrah's claim: the trial of the life and the trial of the voice are the same trial, and the quiet is the sign of the heart that has passed.

**The Forgiveness and the Reward**

The verse states the fruit of the refined heart in the sūrah's register of the reward, and the register is the sūrah's way of fixing the value of the quiet. They will have forgiveness and a great reward, the verse says, and the two fruits are the sūrah's way of stating that the quiet is not a cost but a gain. The forgiveness is for the state of the heart that the quiet signs, and the reward is for the work of the assay that the state carries. The sūrah's point is that the manner is the ground of the reward, and the ground is what the sūrah's verse states. A community that lowers its voices is a community that is rewarded, and the reward is the sūrah's way of saying that the quiet is the act that carries the fruit. The two fruits stand in the verse in the order of the forgiveness and the reward, and the order is the sūrah's way of saying that the cleansing comes before the giving, and that the giving is what the cleansing makes possible.

**The Voice as the Assay**

The doctrine of the voice, stated in the sūrah's two previous verses and in this one, is the doctrine of the assay. The voice is the instrument by which the state of the heart is made visible, and the instrument is the sūrah's way of giving the inner state its outer sign. The raising is the sign of the heart that has not passed the assay, and the lowering is the sign of the heart that has passed, and the two signs are the sūrah's way of dividing the community by the state that is not visible to the eye. The sūrah's three verses together state the doctrine in its full form: the general prohibition, the specific prohibition, and the specific approval, and the three together are the sūrah's way of making the voice the measure of the heart. The doctrine is the sūrah's claim about the community: it is a community that is known by the quiet, and the quiet is the sign of the heart that God has refined.

**The End of the First Section**

The position of the verse is the reason for its function. It is the third verse of the sūrah, and it closes the section of the voices, the section that began with the general prohibition and passed through the specific prohibition to the specific approval. The section is complete, and the completeness is the sūrah's way of stating the subject of the sūrah in its first range. The sūrah will now turn to the group that the section addresses, the group that calls from behind the apartments, and the turn is the sūrah's way of moving from the doctrine of the voice to the persons of the voice. The verse is the sūrah's way of ending the section on the approval, and the ending is the sūrah's claim: the community is to be the community of the quiet, and the quiet is the sign of the refined heart.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:4

> Indeed, most of those who call out to you ˹O Prophet˺ from outside ˹your˺ private quarters have no understanding ˹of manners˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Group Named at the Door**

The sūrah now names the group that its first section addresses, and the naming is the sūrah's way of moving from the doctrine of the voice to the persons of the voice. Those who call out to you from outside the private quarters, the verse says, and the saying is the sūrah's way of stating the subject of the section in its concrete form. The word for the private quarters is the word that gives the sūrah its name, and the word is the sūrah's way of fixing the subject. The group is the group of the callers, and the callers are the ones who stand outside the door and call to the one who is within, and the standing-outside is what the verse describes. The sūrah's claim, by the naming, is that the conduct of the first section is the conduct of this group, and that the group is to be described before it is corrected. The description precedes the correction, and the order is the sūrah's way of saying that the correction is the correction of a known state rather than of an imagined one.

**The Callers While He Slept**

The tradition gives the verse its incident, and the incident is the sūrah's way of making the verse concrete. A group of Bedouin came to the Prophet while he was sleeping, and they called for him to come out of his apartment. They did not know in which of the apartments of his wives he was, and the not-knowing is what sent them behind each apartment in turn, and the going-behind is what the verse describes in its words of calling from outside. The calling was in a crude manner, the tradition reports, and the crudeness is the ground of the verse. The sūrah's most of them is the sūrah's way of stating that the group is not one person but many, and that the many are the sūrah's subject. The incident is the sūrah's way of placing the verse in its moment, and the moment is the sūrah's way of making the correction the correction of an event rather than of an abstraction.

**The Words of the Caller**

The tradition preserves the words of one of the callers, and the words are the sūrah's way of stating the offense in its full form. One of the group, the man Aqrabʿ ibn Ḥabīs, is reported to have called out, O Muḥammad, verily my praise is sweet and my curse is bitter, and the calling is the sūrah's way of stating what the caller had put in his own hand. The reply of the Prophet was the one word of the sūrah's answer: only God is like that. The reply is the sūrah's way of stating the offense in its theological form, and the theological form is what the verse condemns. The caller had taken for himself the property of the one whose word carries both the sweetness and the bitterness, the one whose praise and whose curse are the two forces that move the affairs of the people. The taking is the offense, and the sūrah's verse is the correction of the taking. The verse was revealed, the tradition reports, on the occasion of the words, and the revelation is the sūrah's way of stating that the words were not passed over.

**The Understanding That Is Missing**

The final word of the verse is the state it assigns to the group, and the state is the sūrah's way of stating the ground of the reproof. Most of them have no understanding, the verse says, and the understanding is the understanding of the manner, the knowledge of the distance that the proximity requires. The sūrah's point is that the offense of the group is not malice but ignorance, and the ignorance is the ground of the reproof rather than the ground of the condemnation. The group did not call out of contempt; it called out of the not-knowing, and the not-knowing is what the verse names. The sūrah's most of them is the sūrah's way of leaving the exception, and the exception is the sūrah's way of saying that the group is not one state but several, and that the several are to be divided before they are judged. The verse's state is the sūrah's name for the condition that the correction of the following verses is made to remove.

**The Name of the Sūrah**

The word of the verse is the name of the sūrah, and the naming is the sūrah's way of fixing its subject. The private quarters, the apartments behind which the callers stand, are the image that the sūrah takes as its own, and the image is the sūrah's way of stating the relation of the community to the one who is within it. The sūrah's subject is the proximity, and the proximity is what the image states: the one who is within the apartment and the many who are without it, and the relation between the two. The name of the sūrah is therefore the sūrah's claim about its subject, and the claim is that the conduct of the community toward the Prophet is the conduct of the without-toward-the-within. The sūrah's remaining verses will extend the conduct beyond the proximity to the Prophet, to the conduct of the believers toward one another, and the extension is the sūrah's way of completing the subject that the first verse's word states. The apartment, in the sūrah's account, is the image of the whole house of the community, and the house is where the sūrah's remaining verses will be set.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:5

> Had they been patient until you could come out to them, it would have certainly been better for them. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Patience That Was Not Chosen**

The verse states the alternative to the calling, and the alternative is the patience. Had they been patient until you could come out to them, the verse says, and the saying is the sūrah's way of stating what the group could have done in place of what it did. The form of the verse is the counterfactual, the patience that was not, and the counterfactual is the sūrah's way of stating the ground of the better. It would have been better for them, the verse says, and the better is the sūrah's way of fixing the value of the patience. The sūrah's point is that the calling was not the only option, and that the option that was not chosen was the better one. The patience, in the sūrah's form, is the waiting at the door until the one within comes out in his own time, and the in-his-own-time is what the verse's counterfactual preserves. The verse does not state the better in its first form; it names it and leaves its content to the account that the tradition preserves, and the leaving is the sūrah's way of saying that the better is a fact and not an argument.

**The Reproof and Its Extension**

The verse is the sūrah's reproof of the group, and the reproof is the sūrah's way of stating the correction. The exegesis gives the reach of the reproof, and the reach is beyond the persons of the incident. The lesson of the incident, the exegesis says, is the lesson of the community's own protocol, and the lesson is the sūrah's way of making the incident a rule. The one who stands outside waits for the one who is within, and the waiting is the manner. The Prophetic saying the exegesis cites states the discipline in its general form: the one who knocks and is not admitted does not knock again, but withdraws, and the withdrawing is the discipline. The rule is the sūrah's way of completing the protocol, and the protocol is the sūrah's claim about the community: the manner of the door is the manner of the body, and the body is what the sūrah's verses are correcting from this point onward.

**The Request Behind the Calling**

The tradition gives the calling its purpose, and the purpose is the sūrah's way of stating what the group sought. The group had come with a request, and the request was the intercession for a number of captives taken in the fighting that their tribes had recently lost. The Prophet answered the request when it was presented to him in the proper form: half of the captives were set free, and the rest were left to the price of their release. The exegesis preserves a further line: on some accounts, the patience would have brought the freeing of the whole number, and the whole is the content of the better that the verse names. The sūrah's reproof is of the manner, not of the purpose, and the distinction is the sūrah's way of stating the ground of the reproof. The group sought a good thing, and the good thing is not what the verse corrects; the correction is of the manner in which the good thing was sought, and the manner is what the verse states.

**The Cost of the Manner**

The better that the verse names has its content in the account of the prisoners, and the content is the sūrah's way of stating the cost of the manner. The patience would have brought the freeing of all, and the calling brought the freeing of half, and the difference between the two is the cost. The sūrah's counterfactual is its way of stating that the manner is not without its price, and that the price is the difference between the better and the actual. The verse's claim, by the counterfactual, is that the group paid for its impatience, and that the payment was the half that was not freed. The sūrah does not state this in terms of the punishment; it states it in terms of the better that was lost, and the losing is the sūrah's way of making the cost concrete. The cost is not a penalty laid on from outside; it is the difference between two outcomes that the manner made possible or impossible. A community that learns the cost of the manner is a community that learns the value of the patience, and the value is what the verse's counterfactual states, stated in the currency of the good that was within reach and passed by.

**Closing Names of Mercy**

The verse closes with the two names, All-Forgiving and Most Merciful, and the closing is the sūrah's way of stating the ground on which the reproof stands. The reproof is real, and the better is lost, and the cost is paid, and the sūrah does not leave the group at the cost. The two names are the sūrah's way of saying that the reproof is not the end of the account, and that the end is the state of the one who is reproofed and forgiven. The sūrah's first section closes on the mercy, and the closing is the sūrah's claim about its own register: it corrects, and the correction stands on the ground of the one who forgives. The names are the sūrah's way of completing the section, and the completion is the sūrah's way of saying that the group is reproofed, and that the reproof is within the mercy.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:6

> O believers, if an evildoer brings you any news, verify ˹it˺ so you do not harm people unknowingly, becoming regretful for what you have done.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The News and Its Bearer**

The sūrah turns from the manners of the proximity to the manners of the report, and the turn is the sūrah's way of extending its subject. The verse states its injunction in its full form, and the form is the condition, the command, and the consequence, and the three together are the sūrah's way of stating the discipline in one sentence. The condition is the evildoer who brings the news, and the bearer is stated before the news, and the stating is the sūrah's way of saying that the report is not neutral. The news is carried, and the carrier is the ground of the report's weight. The command is the verification, and the verification is the sūrah's way of stating that the community is not to act on the report as it arrives. The consequence is the harm of the people and the regret of the one who harmed, and the two consequences are the sūrah's way of fixing the value of the verification. The verse is the sūrah's doctrine of the report, and the doctrine is the sūrah's way of stating the community's relation to what it hears.

**The Report That Was Checked**

The tradition gives the verse its occasion, and the occasion is the report that the sūrah's injunction was made to check. The standard account preserved in the tafsīr literature is the report of al-Walīd ibn ʿUqbā. The Prophet had sent him to the tribe of Banū al-Mustalīq to collect their alms, and the collecting was the mission. He returned with the claim that the tribe had turned against him, that they were raising a force to fall on the Muslims, and the claim was the news. The Prophet and the companions were about to set out against the tribe on the strength of the report, and the about-to-set-out is the moment the sūrah's verse was made to stop. The verse checked the community at the edge of the action, and the checking is the sūrah's way of stating that the report of the evildoer is the report that is to be verified. The incident is the sūrah's way of making the doctrine concrete, and the concreteness is the tradition's way of keeping the doctrine alive.

**The Harm and the Regret**

The verse states the two consequences of the unverified report in their order, and the order is the sūrah's way of stating the chain of the harm. The first is the harm of the people, the people who are harmed out of ignorance, and the ignorance is the state of the one who acts on the report before the verification. The second is the regret of the one who has harmed, and the regret is the state that follows the act. The sūrah's point is that the unverified news is the ground of the harm, and that the harm is the ground of the regret, and that the regret is what the community is to be spared. The sūrah's out of ignorance is its way of stating that the harm is done in the state of the not-knowing, and that the not-knowing is the ground that the verification removes. A community that verifies is a community that does not act in the state of the not-knowing, and the not-acting is what the sūrah's injunction commands.

**The Verification as the Discipline**

The doctrine of the verification, stated in the verse, is the doctrine of the community's care for its own ground. The community acts on what it knows, and the verification is the discipline by which the community makes sure that what it acts on is what it knows. The sūrah's injunction is not a command of doubt applied to every report from every bearer; it is a command of the test applied to the report of the one whose standing is the evildoer's. The exegesis gives the balance: the community treats the reports of the trustworthy as part of the normal course of its life, and the report of the evildoer is the exception that is to be checked. The balance is the sūrah's way of stating that the verification is a control and not a paralysis, and that the control is the community's way of keeping its ground solid. The sūrah's name for the control is the verification, and the control is the sūrah's claim about the community's relation to the news that moves it.

**A Turn Toward the Interior**

The position of the verse is the reason for its function. It is the sixth verse of the sūrah, and it closes the section of the proximity, the section that ran from the first address to the reproof of the callers. The sūrah has now stated the community's manners toward the one who is within the apartment, and it turns, with this verse, to the community's manners toward one another, and the turn is the sūrah's way of completing its subject. The verification is the bridge of the turn: the report that moves the community is the ground of its action against one of its own people, and the ground is to be tested before the action is taken. The sūrah's remaining verses will state the brotherhood of the believers and the forms of conduct that break it, and the verification is the sūrah's way of saying that the first of the breakings is the unverified report. The report is the ground of the harm that the sūrah's later verses will forbid, and the forbidding is the sūrah's completion of the discipline this verse begins.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:7

> And keep in mind that Allah’s Messenger is ˹still˺ in your midst. If he were to yield to you in many matters, you would surely suffer ˹the consequences˺. But Allah has endeared faith to you, making it appealing in your hearts. And He has made disbelief, rebelliousness, and disobedience detestable to you. Those are the ones rightly guided.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Presence That Is the Ground**

The verse states the presence of the Messenger as the ground of the order that the community is to keep, and the stating is the sūrah's way of placing the community's decisions in their setting. Know that the Messenger of God is among you, the verse says, and the knowing is the sūrah's way of saying that the presence is not to be taken for granted. The exegesis gives the presence in its two forms, and the two forms are the sūrah's way of stating its range. The first form is the presence at the moment of the revelation, the Messenger standing in the midst of the community, and the standing is the presence the verse states. The second form is the presence that continues after the standing, the presence of the one who is with the believers as long as the believers are the believers, and the continuation is what the Quran states in its plainest verse on the relation, when it gives the Prophet a stronger affinity to the believers than they have to themselves (33:6). The sūrah's claim, by the two forms, is that the community functions in the presence, and that the presence is the ground on which the order of the verse stands.

**The Inversion That Would Harm**

The middle clause of the verse states the inversion, and the inversion is the sūrah's way of stating what the order is not. Were the Messenger to obey the community in many matters, the community would suffer, the verse says, and the suffering is the sūrah's name for the state that the inversion produces. The exegesis gives the ground of the inversion in the standing character of the Messenger: he does not speak of his own whim, and the not-speaking-of-whim is what the Quran states in its plainest form, "Nor does he speak of his own whims" (53:3). His determination in the matters of the religion and the law is guided from above, and the guiding is what the obedience is the obedience to. If the one who is guided were to obey the ones who are not guided, the sūrah says, the order would be turned upside down, and the turning upside down is what would bring the suffering. The sūrah's form is the counterfactual, and the counterfactual is the sūrah's way of stating the order by its opposite: the community that obeys the Messenger is the community that is saved from the suffering, and the saving is what the verse's warning protects.

**The Love That Was Placed**

The verse states the second act of God in the community, and the act is the love. God has caused the community to love the faith, and the causing is the sūrah's way of stating that the love is not the community's own work. The exegesis gives the point of the causing: the faith and the guidance are a bounty from God, and the bounty is the ground of the love, and the ground is what the sūrah's next verse will name in its two words. The causing is also the sūrah's way of stating the humility that must always accompany the faith: the one who loves the faith loves what was placed in him, and the placed-in-him is what the love is the love of. A community that takes the love for its own achievement has lost the state of the receiver, and the losing is what the sūrah's word for the causing removes. The love that God made fair in the hearts is the love that stands in the state of the gift, and the state is what the verse's word carries.

**The Aversion That Is the Mark**

The verse states the third act of God in the community, and the act is the aversion. God has caused the community to despise the disbelief, the iniquity, and the disobedience, and the three objects of the aversion are the sūrah's way of stating the whole of what the faith turns from. The love and the aversion stand in the verse as the two works of God in the heart, and the two works are the sūrah's way of stating the heart in its full state: the heart that loves the faith and the heart that turns from the three, and the two are one state, the state of the heart that has been worked on by the one who places and the one who turns. The verse closes its first part with the naming of this state, and the naming is such are the rightly guided, and the naming is the sūrah's way of stating that the state has a name, and that the name is the mark by which the state is known. The mark is what the sūrah's account of the community in the sūrah that closes the revelation will describe in its own terms.

**The Order of the Community**

The doctrine of the verse, stated in its whole, is the doctrine of the order of the community. The order is the order of the revelation: the Messenger receives, and the community follows, and the following is what the presence makes possible. The Quran states the obedience in its standing form in the sūrah of the exodus, when it commands the taking and the leaving, "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it. And whatever he forbids you from, leave it" (59:7), and the taking-and-leaving is the order in its practical form. And it states the consequence of the inversion in the sūrah of the believers, where the truth following the desires of the people is the corruption of the heavens and the earth, "Had the truth followed their desires, the heavens, the earth, and all those in them would have certainly been corrupted" (23:71). The sūrah's verse holds the order and its two consequences together: the presence that makes the order possible, the inversion that would break it, and the works of God in the hearts that keep the community on the side of the order. The presence, in the sūrah's account, is not the presence of a man in a room; it is the presence of the ground on which the community stands.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:8

> ˹This is˺ a bounty and a blessing from Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, AllWise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Gift Named in Its Two Words**

The verse is the second half of a sentence that began in the previous verse, and the first half named the love and the aversion that God placed in the hearts of the community, while this one names the two works in their proper name, the bounty and the blessing. The sūrah's structure here is the sūrah's way of stating that the works have a name, and that the name is the gift. The bounty is the word for the good that benefits the one who receives it, and the blessing is the word for the good that exceeds the measure of what the receiver earned or expected, and the two words are the sūrah's way of stating the gift in its two directions: the direction of the benefit and the direction of the excess. The sūrah's claim, by the naming, is that the love and the aversion are not the achievements of the community, and that the not-being-achievements is what the two words state.

**The Humility of the Receiver**

The exegesis gives the point that the naming carries, and the point is the state of the receiver. The love and the guidance are a bounty from God, and the bounty is what points to the humility that must always accompany the faith for the faith to be the sincere faith. The receiver of the gift stands in the state of the receiver, and the state is the humility, and the humility is what the sūrah's naming preserves. A community that names the love for the faith as its own doing has moved from the state of the receiver to the state of the claimant, and the moving is what the sūrah's two words correct. The sūrah's claim, by the verse, is that the sincerity of the faith is the faith that knows its own ground, and that the ground is the gift, and that the knowing of the ground is what the humility is. The receiver who knows the gift is the receiver the sūrah describes, and the description is the sūrah's way of completing the statement of the works of the previous verse.

**The Two Names That Close the Verse**

The verse closes with the two names, the Knowing and the Wise, and the closing is the sūrah's way of stating the ground of the gift in its final form. The Knowing is the name of the one who placed the love and the aversion, and the placing was done with the knowledge of the hearts in which the works were made, and the knowing of the hearts is what the previous verse's word for the refining of the hearts had stated. The Wise is the name of the one who chose the works and chose their place, and the choosing is the sūrah's way of stating that the gift is not a general good scattered without aim but a good placed where the wisdom placed it. The two names stand at the close of the verse in the order of the knowledge and the wisdom, and the order is the sūrah's way of saying that the gift is known before it is wised, and that the wising is what the knowing makes possible. The sūrah's second section closes its statement of the presence with the two names, and the names are the sūrah's way of saying that the presence stands on the ground of the one who knows and the one who is wise.

**The Verse That Closes the Section**

The position of the verse is the reason for its function. It is the eighth verse of the sūrah, and it closes the section that ran from the discipline of the report to the order of the presence. The section has stated three things: the verification of the news that the evildoer brings, the order of the community in the presence of the Messenger, and the naming of the works of God in the hearts as the gift. The three together are the sūrah's way of stating the community in its relation to what it hears, in its relation to the one who leads it, and in its relation to the one who places in it what it loves and what it turns from. The section's form is the sūrah's way of stating the subject of the sūrah in its first range, and the range is the range of the community's interior. The sūrah will now turn to the community's exterior, to the relations of the believers with one another, and the turn is the sūrah's way of moving from the interior to the body.

**The Gift and the Body**

The movement of the sūrah from the gift to the body is the movement of the verse's claim. The works of God in the hearts are the gift, and the gift is what the community carries into its relations with one another, and the carrying is the sūrah's way of saying that the interior and the exterior are one state. The community that has the love placed in it and the aversion placed in it is the community whose exterior conduct is the conduct of the love and the aversion, and the conduct is what the sūrah's remaining verses will state. The verse is the sūrah's way of leaving the section of the interior with the gift named, and the naming is the sūrah's claim: the community is the gift in its walking, and the walking is what the sūrah's next section will describe in its forms of the dispute, the reconciliation, the brotherhood, the derision, and the backbiting.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:9

> And if two groups of believers fight each other, then make peace between them. But if one of them transgresses against the other, then fight against the transgressing group until they ˹are willing to˺ submit to the rule of Allah. If they do so, then make peace between both ˹groups˺ in all fairness and act justly. Surely Allah loves those who uphold justice.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Dispute of the Brothers**

The sūrah turns to the relations of the believers with one another, and the first relation it states is the dispute. The verse states the basic principle for dealing with a dispute between two parties of the believers, and the principle is the sūrah's way of stating that the dispute is a matter of the community's interior and not a matter of the community's exterior. The two parties are both parties of the believers, and the both-being-believers is what the verse's first word fixes, for the sūrah calls the opposing groups believers even while they are fighting, and the calling is the sūrah's way of stating that the sin of the fight does not remove the faith of the fighters. The command that follows is the command of the peace-making: make peace between them, and the peace-making is the duty of the community that stands outside the two parties, and the standing-outside is what the verse assigns to the believers who are not in the fight.

**The Help That Is the Prevention**

The hadith that the exegesis cites states the verse's doctrine in its plainest form, and the doctrine is the help of the brother in his two states. The Prophet enjoined the followers to help their brother whether he oppresses or is oppressed, and when he was asked what the help of the oppressor is, the answer was the one that the verse carries: the help of the oppressor is the prevention of his oppression. The hadith is the sūrah's way of stating the second clause of the verse, the fighting of the aggressing party, in its true form: the fight is a help, and the help is the prevention. The scholars who cite the hadith also rely on this verse for a doctrine of the creed, and the doctrine is that the committing of the sin, however grave, does not remove the one who commits it from the faith, a doctrine that stands against the doctrine of the sect that expels the sinner from the faith, and the standing-against is what the verse's calling of the fighters believers carries.

**The Fight as the Last Resort**

The verse states the escalation of the dispute in its three stages, and the three stages are the sūrah's way of stating the whole of the community's response. The first stage is the peace-making between the two, and the peace-making is the response of the community to the dispute as such. The second stage is the fight against the one that aggresses, and the fight is the response to the aggression that the peace-making has not stopped. The third stage is the return to the command of God, and the return is the goal of the fight, and the goal is what the verse states in its word for the returning, the turning back to the ordinance. The fight is directed at the aggressing party alone, and the direction is the sūrah's way of stating that the fight is not a civil war but a correction, and that the correction is what the sūrah's word for the fighting carries. When the aggressing party returns, the command returns to the first stage, and the returning is the sūrah's way of stating that the end of the dispute is the peace, and that the peace is made with justice.

**The Occasion in the Tradition's Words**

The tradition gives the verse its occasions, and the occasions are the sūrah's way of making the principle concrete. The account the exegesis preserves in one line is the visit of the Prophet to the leader of the hypocrites in Medina: the man told the Prophet to keep away, for the smell of his donkey offended him, and one of the Helpers answered the insult with a word of his own, and the word kindled the man's tribesman, and the two parties came to the exchange of blows, and the verse was revealed on the occasion. The exegesis preserves a second account of a scuffle between the two tribes of the Helpers, the scuffle in which the two groups flew to the palm branches and the sandals before they came to the blows, and the flying is the sūrah's way of stating the dispute in its first heat. A third account gives the verse its domestic form: a man of the Helpers who locked his wife in an upper room when she wished to visit her family, and the Prophet sent one to make the peace between the two, and the two agreed to refer the matter to the judgment of God. The three occasions are the sūrah's way of stating that the dispute runs through the community at every level, the level of the leader, the level of the tribe, and the level of the house.

**The Justice That God Loves**

The verse closes with the love of the just, and the closing is the sūrah's way of stating the ground of the peace-making. The peace between the two is to be made with justice, and the justice is the sūrah's way of stating that the peace-maker is not the party of either, and that the not-being-party is what the justice is. The Quran states the love of the fair in its standing form in the sūrah of the two-bannered, where the fair dealing with those who have not fought is the dealing that God loves, "Surely Allah loves those who are fair" (60:8), and it states the standing for justice in the sūrah of the women, where the witness for God is to stand firm even against oneself and one's kin, "O believers! Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives" (4:135). The hadith the exegesis cites states the boundary of the conduct in the dispute: the cursing of a Muslim is a sin, and the fighting of a Muslim is disbelief, and the boundary is what the verse's peace-making keeps within. One of the later commentators reads the verse in its inner form, and the reading is the sūrah's way of extending it: when the nature and the whim and the lust tyrannize over the heart, the intellect, and the spirit, the servant fights them with the sword of the vigilance, the arrow of the self-inspection, and the light of the conformity to the truth, and the fighting is the verse's fight in the interior, and the interior is where the verse's justice is first made.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:10

> The believers are but one brotherhood, so make peace between your brothers. And be mindful of Allah so you may be shown mercy.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Brotherhood That Is the Structure**

The verse states the brotherhood, and the statement is one of the most important statements in the Quran on the structure of the Muslim community. The believers are but brothers, the verse says, and the but-brothers is the sūrah's way of stating that the brotherhood is the whole of the relation and not one of its parts. The hadith the exegesis cites states the structure in its image: the believers are like a single structure, each part supporting the other, and the supporting is the sūrah's way of saying that the brotherhood is not a sentiment but a construction. A structure whose parts support one another is a structure that stands, and a structure whose parts do not support one another falls, and the standing is what the verse's brotherhood is for. The sūrah's claim, by the verse, is that the community is a construction, and that the construction is the brotherhood, and that the brotherhood is what the sūrah's remaining verses will show to be the ground of the conduct it commands.

**The Love That the Brotherhood Carries**

The exegesis gives the brotherhood its inner mark, and the mark is the love for the brother. The hadith the exegesis cites states the mark in its full form: the faith is not complete in a member of the community until he wishes for his brother the very good he wishes for himself, and the wishing-for is the sūrah's way of stating the brotherhood in its active form. The sūrah's own context gives the mark its ground: the sūrah emphasizes the bond of the faith that transcends the bonds of kinship, and the transcending is what the brotherhood is, and the brotherhood is what the community is called to make its ground in place of the alliances of the tribe. The hadith the exegesis cites gives the love its work: the Lord carries His servant forward so long as the servant carries his brother, and the carrying is the love in its act, and the act is what the sūrah's brotherhood states. The sūrah's claim, by the mark and the work, is that the brotherhood is the love that acts, and that the acting love is what the verse states as the state of the believer.

**The Two Who Love for the Sake of God**

The exegesis preserves the place of the brotherly love in the sūrah's account of the reward, and the place is the sūrah's way of stating the weight of the love. Among the types of people the Prophet said would be granted the Paradise, he listed the two souls bound by the love of the Lord, the two who come together for that love and go apart carrying it, and the coming-together-and-going-apart is the sūrah's way of stating the love in its full course. The hadith qudsī the exegesis cites states the reward of the love in its own words of God: at the judgment the Lord will call out for the ones whose love of one another was for His Glory, and in that day, the day when no shade exists except His, He will give them His shade, and the giving-of-shade is the sūrah's way of stating that the love for the sake of God is the love that carries the shade, and that the shade is the reward. The sūrah's claim, by the list and the qudsī, is that the brotherly love is not a private matter of the two, and that the two are known to the one who asks for them, and that the asking is what the reward is.

**The Reconciliation of the Brethren**

The second clause of the verse applies the brotherhood to the dispute that the previous verse stated, and the application is the sūrah's way of saying that the brotherhood is not a state that waits but a state that acts. So make peace between your brethren, the verse commands, and the command is the sūrah's way of stating that the reconciliation is the work of the brotherhood, and that the work is the brotherhood in its act. The address is to the community, and the community is the one who reconciles, and the one-who-reconciles is the sūrah's way of stating that the duty of the peace-making falls on the body of the believers and not on the two who are in the dispute. The previous verse gave the principle of the peace-making in its full form, the fight of the aggressing party and the return to the ordinance, and this verse gives the principle its name, and the name is the brotherhood, and the brotherhood is what the peace-making is the peace-making of.

**The Mercy That the Reverence Earns**

The verse closes with the reverence and the mercy, and the closing is the sūrah's way of stating the ground and the fruit of the reconciliation. Reverence God, the verse commands, and the reverence is the ground on which the peace-making stands, and the standing is the sūrah's way of saying that the reconciliation is not the work of the community's own power but the work it does in the state of the reverence. That haply you may receive mercy, the verse states, and the mercy is the fruit of the reverence, and the fruit is what the sūrah's way of saying that the reconciliation is done in the state that receives the mercy. The sūrah's claim, by the closing, is that the brotherhood is the brotherhood of the ones who fear, and that the fearing is what makes the reconciliation the reconciliation, and that the mercy is what the reconciliation is made for. The verse leaves the section of the brotherhood with the mercy named, and the naming is the sūrah's way of saying that the end of the brotherhood is the mercy, and that the mercy is the end of the sūrah's account of the community's interior.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

> O believers! Do not let some ˹men˺ ridicule others, they may be better than them, nor let ˹some˺ women ridicule other women, they may be better than them. Do not defame one another, nor call each other by offensive nicknames. How evil it is to act rebelliously after having faith! And whoever does not repent, it is they who are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Derision and Its Reversal**

The sūrah forbids the derision in its two forms, the derision of the people and the derision of the women, and the two forms are the sūrah's way of stating that the prohibition reaches the whole of the community. The verse states the prohibition with the reversal, and the reversal is the sūrah's way of stating the ground of the prohibition. Let not one people deride another, it may be that they are better than them, the verse says, and the may-be-better is the sūrah's way of stating that the one who derides assumes a superiority that he does not know, and that the not-knowing is the ground of the sin. The sūrah's claim, by the reversal, is that the measure of the person is not the measure that the eye takes, and that the eye's measure is the measure of the appearance, and that the appearance is not the measure of the one who knows. The two forms of the prohibition, the people and the women, are the sūrah's way of stating that the measure is one for the two, and that the one measure is the measure of the one who knows.

**The Boasting That the Verse Removes**

The context of the sūrah gives the verse its deeper object, and the object is the boasting on the ground of the lineage. The sūrah emphasizes the transition from the bonds of the tribe to the bonds of the faith, and the transition is what the verse's prohibition serves. The hadith the exegesis cites carries the warning in its full form: the people who keep boasting of the ancestors who are gone, ancestors who will be nothing but the burning coals of the fire, will stand more contemptible before God than the beetle that pushes its dung before it; the pride of the age of ignorance and the pride of the lineage have been taken from the community, and the person stands before God as the pious believer or as the wretched sinner, and all the children of Adam come from the dust. The sūrah's claim, by the hadith, is that the ground of the honor is removed from the lineage and placed in the piety, and that the placing is what the verse's prohibition states. The derision is the boasting in its small form, and the small form is what the verse forbids because it carries the large form in it.

**The Nickname and Its Occasion**

The second prohibition of the verse is the nickname, and the exegesis gives the nickname its occasion and its range. The prohibition of the insulting with the nicknames was said to have been made to abolish the practice of the age of ignorance, the practice of calling the people by the names that degraded them, and the abolishing is what the verse does to the practice. The report of one of the Helpers, preserved in the tafsīr literature, places the verse in the community's own experience: after the migration, most of the Helpers carried two or three names, and some of those names had grown current as a way of dragging the bearer down, and the verse removed that use from the names. One of the early readers gives the range of the prohibition: the one who has done the sin and turned back from it and set his way right is not to be pursued by the name of the deed, the name of the thief or the one who drank, and the not-pursuing is the sūrah's way of stating that the name fixes the person in his worst moment, and that the fixing is the sin. The Prophet is reported to have gone further and to have changed the names whose meanings were negative, the man who was named after the roughness being renamed after the softness, and the renaming is the sūrah's way of stating that the name is not without its effect on the one who bears it.

**The Wretched Name After the Belief**

The verse states the evil of the name in the state of the believer, and the stating is the sūrah's way of placing the prohibition in its context. How evil is the iniquitous name after having believed, the verse says, and the after-having-believed is the sūrah's way of stating that the sin of the name is greater in the one who has the faith, and that the greater is what the sūrah's word for the evil carries. The exegesis gives the sense in its two lines: the verse warns the ones who have become believers against calling the other believers by the degrading names, and the warning is the sūrah's way of stating that the name is the ground of the brotherhood's breaking; and the verse points to the manner in which the abuse of the others in word and deed can undo the benefit of the good deeds, and the undoing is the sūrah's way of stating that the name is not without its cost. The sūrah's claim, by the two lines, is that the iniquitous name after the belief is the regression, and that the regression is the state the verse calls the iniquity, and that the iniquity is what the belief is the belief against.

**The Repentance That Is the Exit**

The verse closes with the repentance, and the closing is the sūrah's way of stating the exit from the state it has described. Whosoever does not repent, they are the wrongdoers, the verse says, and the wrongdoers are the ones who stand in the state of the derision and the defamation and the nickname after the belief, and the standing is what the sūrah's final word names. The sūrah's claim, by the closing, is that the sins of the tongue, the derision, the defamation, and the nickname, are the sins that the tongue can repent from, and that the repentance of the tongue is the turning of the tongue from the name that degrades, and that the turning is the exit. The sūrah leaves the prohibition with the exit open, and the open exit is the sūrah's way of saying that the state of the derision is not the state of the believer, and that the state of the believer is the state of the one who has turned the tongue to the name that builds rather than the name that breaks.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:12

> O believers! Avoid many suspicions, ˹for˺ indeed, some suspicions are sinful. And do not spy, nor backbite one another. Would any of you like to eat the flesh of their dead brother? You would despise that! And fear Allah. Surely Allah is ˹the˺ Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Conjecture and Its Sin**

The sūrah opens this section with the conjecture, and the conjecture is the sin of the heart that the sūrah states before the sins of the tongue. Shun much conjecture, the verse commands, and the shunning is the sūrah's way of stating that the conjecture is not to be the habit of the community. The verse states the degree of the sin: some conjecture is a sin, and the some-is-a-sin is the sūrah's way of stating that the conjecture is not all one kind, and that the kind that is the sin is the kind that the heart takes for its ground. The hadith the exegesis cites states the warning in its two forms: beware of the conjecture, for it is the most misleading of the speech, and the most-misleading is the sūrah's way of stating the power of the conjecture over the one who holds it; and the leader who pursues the conjecture about his people corrupts his people, and the corrupting is the sūrah's way of stating the reach of the conjecture from the heart of the one to the body of the many. The sūrah's claim, by the verse and the hadith, is that the conjecture is the first of the sins that break the bond of the brotherhood, and that the first is what the sūrah forbids first.

**The Spying and Its Distinction**

The verse forbids the spying, and the exegesis gives the spying its distinction from the kindred act that is not the sin. The spying, in the sūrah's word, is the seeking of the fault of the brother, and the seeking is what the verse forbids, for the seeking usually indicates the suspicion of the sin of the other and the thinking ill of the other. The exegesis draws the line between the spying and the inquiring: the inquiring is done for the benefit of the one who is inquired about, and the for-the-benefit is what distinguishes the inquiring from the spying, and the distinguishing is the sūrah's way of stating that the act is not the act, and that the intention is what makes the act the sin. The saying of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, preserved in the sermon, states the discipline in its full form: do not be quick to expose the sin of the other, for the other may be forgiven for it, and do not feel secure from the small sin, for the small sin may be the one for which the exposure comes, and the one who comes to know the faults of the others is not to expose them in view of what he knows of his own faults, and is to remain busy in the gratitude that he has been saved from what the others are in. The hadith the exegesis cites states the punishment of the eavesdropper in the next life, and the punishment is the molten copper poured into the ears, and the pouring is the sūrah's way of stating that the ear that sought the fault receives in the ear.

**The Definition of the Backbiting**

The verse forbids the backbiting, and the exegesis gives the backbiting its definition in the word of the Prophet. The definition is the one preserved in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (2589): the Prophet asked the community whether they knew what the backbiting was, and the answer was that God and His Messenger know best, and the definition followed: the backbiting is the mentioning of the brother in the manner that he does not like, and when it was said that the mentioning is true, that the brother is as it is said of him, the answer was the one that fixes the sin: if what is said is in him, the one who said it has backbitten him, and if it is not in him, the one who said it has slandered him. The sūrah's claim, by the definition, is that the truth of the statement is not the ground of the permission, and that the ground of the prohibition is the manner that the brother does not like, and that the not-liking is what the backbiting is. The hadith the exegesis cites states the pursuit of the fault in its full form: do not harm the Muslims, do not shame them, and do not pursue their faults, for the one who pursues the faults of his brother, God pursues his faults, and the one whose faults God pursues, God disgraces him even in his own house; and in the other direction, the one who covers the fault of the Muslim in this world, God covers his faults in this world and the next, and the covering is the opposite of the pursuit, and the opposite is what the sūrah's prohibition of the backbiting preserves.

**The Image of the Dead Flesh**

The verse gives the backbiting its image, and the image is the sūrah's way of making the sin visible to the sense. Would any of the community desire to eat the dead flesh of his brother, the verse asks, and the desire is the question the sūrah puts to the sense, and the sense is what the sūrah appeals to. The answer the verse gives is the abhorrence: the community would abhor it, and the abhorring is the sūrah's way of stating that the sense already carries the judgment of the sin, and that the judgment is what the sense knows before the law names it. The flesh of the brother is the dignity of the brother, and the deadness of the flesh is the state of the one who is absent, and the absent is the one whom the backbiting takes, for the backbiting is the speaking of the brother in his absence, and the absence is what makes the speaking the eating. The sūrah's claim, by the image, is that the backbiting is the eating of the dignity of the absent brother, and that the eating is what the sense abhors, and that the abhorring is the ground on which the prohibition stands, a ground that is in the community before the verse is recited.

**The Turning of the Relenting**

The verse closes with the name of the Relenting, and the closing is the sūrah's way of stating the exit from the sins it has forbidden. The exegesis gives the correspondence that the name carries: the relenting of God and the repenting of the human are the two sides of the same verb, and the same-verb is the sūrah's way of stating that the human turning is the answer to the divine relenting, and that the answer is what the repentance is. The majority of the scholars state the repentance of the backbiting in its form: the ceasing of the backbiting and the intending not to return to it, and the ceasing-and-intending is the repentance in its minimum. The exegesis preserves the difference of opinion on the question of the apology to the one who was harmed: some hold that the seeking of the forgiveness of the victim is not necessary, for the knowledge of what was said would do the victim more harm than the not-knowing, and the better on this account is the praising of the wronged one among those who heard the negative words, and the defending of the injured one against the further backbiting, and the defending is the recompense of the words that were ill-advised. And when the backbiting opens into the slander and the false accusation, the punishment is the severe one that the sūrah of the light states, the eighty lashes and the non-accepting of the testimony of the one who accuses the chaste without the four witnesses (24:4). The sūrah leaves the section with the relenting named, and the naming is the sūrah's claim: the sins of the tongue are the sins that the tongue can turn from, and the turning is the state of the one to whom the relenting is the answer.

## Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt 49:13

> O humanity! Indeed, We created you from a male and a female, and made you into peoples and tribes so that you may ˹get to˺ know one another. Surely the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous among you. Allah is truly All-Knowing, All-Aware.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Address That Leaves the Community**

The sūrah has addressed the believers in every verse before this one, and this verse leaves the community and addresses the mankind. O mankind, the verse says, and the leaving is the sūrah's way of stating that what it has been teaching the community is the teaching of the human being as such. This is among the most famous verses of the Quran, and the fame is the sūrah's way of stating that the verse carries the sūrah's whole subject in its first word. The movement of the sūrah has carried the reader from the voice in the presence of the Prophet to the backbiting in the absence of the brother, and the verse now places the whole of that conduct on the ground of the constitution of the person, and the placing is the sūrah's way of saying that the manners of the community are the manners of the species. The address to the mankind is the sūrah's claim: the community is the species in its best state, and the species is what the community is made of.

**The Common Origin Stated Twice**

The verse states the origin of the person, and the statement carries two readings that hold the two times of the origin together. The exegesis gives the first reading: the creation from a male and a female is the creation of the first pair, the man and the woman from whom the species came, and the first-pair is the origin of the species. The exegesis gives the second reading: the creation from a male and a female is the creation of every person, each human being made from the substance of the man and the substance of the woman, and the each-person is the origin of the individual. The two readings are the sūrah's way of stating the origin in its two times, the time of the species and the time of the person, and the two times are the sūrah's way of saying that the common origin is the ground of the common dignity. A species that comes from one pair is a species whose members share the origin, and a person that comes from the two substances is a person whose being carries the mark of the two, and the mark is what the verse's address to the mankind states.

**The Peoples and Tribes for the Knowing**

The verse states the division of the species, and the division is given its purpose in the same breath. The purpose stated is the knowing of one another, and the knowing is the sūrah's way of stating that the diversity is the means by which the human being comes to the deeper appreciation of the human condition. The exegesis gives the range of the diversity: the tribe, the race, the ethnicity, the language, the nationality, the religion, and the range is the sūrah's way of saying that the diversity reaches every dimension of the social being. The Quran states the division in its standing form in the sūrah of the family, where the community is given its laws and its way in the plurality, "If Allah had willed, He would have made you one community, but His Will is to test you with what He has given ˹each of˺ you. So compete with one another in doing good" (5:48). The division, in the sūrah's account, is at once the knowing and the trial, and the two are the sūrah's way of saying that the diversity is the decree, and that the decree is to be met with the competition in the good rather than with the pride of the tribe.

**The Measure Moved From the Lineage to the Heart**

The verse moves the measure of the noble, and the moving is the sūrah's way of stating the new moral order. The noblest of you in the sight of God is the most reverent of you, the verse says, and the moving is from the lineage to the reverence, and the reverence is what the verse makes the measure. The exegesis gives the weight of the moving: in the state of Arabia before the revelation, the reverence, which in the old usage carried the sense of the fearing, and the nobility were held to be at the two poles, the noble was the one who did not fear, and the fearing was the mark of the low. The verse, from the late Medinan period, signals the revamping of the moral order, and the revamping is the sūrah's way of stating that the true worth is no longer the worth of the lineage and the grand display of the valor and the generosity, but the worth of the depth of the faith and the piety. The saying of the Prophet, preserved in the tradition, states the revamping in its full form: the lowly of the old age were raised by God through the religion, and the pride of the old age and its custom of the boasting of the kinsfolk and the noble descent were removed, and all the people, the white and the black, the Qurayshī and the Arab and the non-Arab, stand as the descendants of Adam, and the most beloved of the people to God on the day of the resurrection will be the most obedient and the most reverent of them. The sūrah's claim, by the verse and the saying, is that the measure has been moved from the exterior to the interior, and that the move is what the sūrah's whole account of the conduct of the community has been preparing.

**The Heart That Is the Reality**

The hadith the exegesis cites gives the moved measure its ground in the person, and the ground is the heart. The Lord does not look at the bodies of the people nor at their forms, the hadith says, but He looks at their hearts, and the looking-at-the-hearts is the sūrah's way of stating that the exterior is not the object of the divine regard. The hadith carries the teaching further: there is in the person a lump of the flesh, and if the lump is sound the whole body is sound, and if the lump is diseased the whole body is diseased, and the lump is the heart, and the lump-is-the-heart is the sūrah's way of stating that the heart is the reality that determines all the other dimensions of the being. The outward diversity that divides the human beings is, on this account, one of the greatest trials of the life of this world, and the trial is the sūrah's way of saying that the diversity tests the person's relation to the interior, and that the relation is what the verse's measure carries. The sūrah closes its teaching of the conduct with the teaching of the constitution, and the constitution is the ground on which the conduct stands: the voice is the voice of the heart, the backbiting is the backbiting of the heart, and the reverence that is the measure of the noble is the reverence of the heart.

The sūrah's claim, by the promise, is that the obedience is the condition of the not-diminishing, and that the not-diminishing is what the correction preserves: the tribe that says the lower word and keeps the obedience stands with the full weight of its deed, and the full weight is what the verse's promise carries.
