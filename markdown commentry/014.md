# Sūrah Ibrāhīm (Chapter 14) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:1

> Alif-Lãm-Ra. ˹This is˺ a Book which We have revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ so that you may lead people out of darkness and into light, by the Will of their Lord, to the Path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy—

**Expanded Commentary**

**Three Letters Spoken by Their Names**

The sūrah begins with *Alif-Lām-Rā*, three Arabic letters recited as names, not blended into a word. The same cluster opens Yūnus, Hūd, Yūsuf, and al-Ḥijr. Al-Raʿd, which sits among them, adds a *Mīm* and reads *Alif-Lām-Mīm-Rā*. Reciters treat each letter as its own unit, and that treatment is older than later taste. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: whoever recites a letter from the Book of Allah receives a good deed, and a good deed is ten like it; *Alif-Lām-Mīm* is not one letter, but *Alif* is a letter, *Lām* is a letter, and *Mīm* is a letter (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2910, graded ṣaḥīḥ by al-Tirmidhī). The same count applies here. The opening is already recitation, already reward, already a pause that the next clause does not swallow.

What the letters *mean* is a different question. Some early readers treated them as names of the sūrah, or as oaths, or as a signal that this Book is built from the same alphabet the Arabs already spoke. Others refused to assign a meaning and said the knowledge of them belongs to God. That last view is the most honest, because the verse does not decode them. It sets them in front of a Book and then states the Book's work. The silence itself teaches: not every first sound of revelation is a sentence you can unpack. Some of it is a sign that the speech is from above the speaker.

**A Book Sent Down for a Task**

*Kitāb* here is the Qur'an. The sentence does not stop at the fact of sending down. It names a purpose: so that you may lead people out of darkness and into light. The addressee is the Prophet ﷺ. The work is public. A Book that stays closed has not yet done what this verse says it was sent to do. The verb of revealing is God's; the verb of leading is given to the messenger. That pairing is the shape of prophecy in this sūrah: God sends, the Prophet carries, people are invited to move.

The purpose clause also limits what the Prophet is for. He is not sent to invent a path, or to force a heart, or to decorate a tribe. He is sent to move people from one state to another by means of this Book. Later verses will show how often that movement is refused. The opening already tells you what refusal costs. If the Book's work is to bring people into light, then turning away from it is not a private literary choice. It is a choice to remain in the dark.

Al-Baghawī, al-Māwardī, and al-Ṭabarī all take *a Book* as the Qur'an itself, not a generic scripture. That reading fits the address *to you*. A previous Book would not need to be revealed to this Prophet for this task. The sūrah that follows is the tool named in the first line.

**Darkness Here Is Plural**

Arabic says *ẓulumāt*, darknesses, not a single shade. Commentators fill the plural with disbelief, ignorance, and doubt. Light is then guidance, knowledge, and certainty. The image is not a mood. It is a map of the soul's condition. A person can be learned in trade and still dark in the matter this Book addresses. A person can be sure of a tribe's custom and still be in doubt about God. The verse treats those states as night, and treats the Book as the means of leaving them.

God is often the subject of the same image. *“Allah is the Guardian of the believers—He brings them out of darkness and into light”* (2:257). *“He is the One Who sends down clear revelations to His servant to bring you out of darkness and into light”* (57:9). *“He is the One Who showers His blessings upon you—and His angels pray for you—so that He may bring you out of darkness and into light”* (33:43). Here the Prophet is given a share in the same work, *by the Will of their Lord*. The two statements do not compete. God is the one who actually moves the heart; the messenger is the one who recites, warns, and leads. Take away either side and the verse collapses. A prophet without leave is only a voice. Leave without a prophet leaves the Book unrecited.

**Leave Belongs to the Lord, Not to the Reciter**

*Bi-idhni rabbihim* — by the leave, or will, of their Lord — sits in the middle of the purpose clause so that no one mistakes the Prophet's leading for independent power. Al-Ṭabarī reads the phrase as God's kindness in granting people success in being guided. The point is pastoral and doctrinal at once. Guidance is not a technique the messenger applies. It is a gift the Lord gives, using the messenger as the means.

That is why the verse can assign a huge task and still leave room for those who will not move. The Prophet's job is to lead; the Lord's will decides who follows. The next verses will name those who prefer this world and who try to bend the way. Their failure is not a failure of the Book's clarity. It is a failure of will on their side, and a withholding of success on God's side, and the verse has already prepared both readings by placing *leave* where it is.

The pronoun *their* Lord also matters. The people being led already have a Lord. The Book does not introduce a stranger. It calls them back to the One who already owns them, and it does so in the language of night and day that every listener already knows.

**The Path Is Named by Power and Praise**

The destination is *the Path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy* — *al-ʿAzīz, al-Ḥamīd*. Al-Rāzī takes the first name as the perfection of God's power, and the second as the fact that God deserves praise in all that He does. The pairing is not decoration. A path backed only by power could be a tyranny. A path backed only by praise could be a wish. Together they say that the way this Book opens is both irresistible in its right and beautiful in its end.

The same two names close a later verse about those who know: *“Those gifted with knowledge ˹clearly˺ see that what has been revealed to you from your Lord ˹O Prophet˺ is the truth, and that it guides to the Path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy”* (34:6). Knowledge, in that verse, is not a private spark. It is the ability to see that this revelation is true and that its path belongs to the One who is mighty and praised. 14:1 states the same path from the sending side. 34:6 confirms it from the receiving side.

Some read *the path* as the light already mentioned. Some read it as Islam itself. The two readings meet. Islam, in this sūrah's language, is the lit road, not a tribal badge. Al-Qummī's Shīʿī reading, which takes the path as the leadership of the Twelve Imams, is a later overlay. The verse names God by two of His names and points the traveler to Him. That is the load-bearing sense.

**This Opening Sets the Sūrah's Whole Work**

Ibrāhīm is a late Meccan sūrah of fifty-two verses, with a common report that verses 28–29 belong to Madinah after Badr. It sits fifth in the *Alif-Lām-Rā* group. Its name comes from Abraham's prayer later in the chapter, but its first sentence is not about Abraham. It is about a Book, a prophet, a movement from dark to light, and a path that belongs to God. Gratitude and ingratitude, patience and trust, the ruin of those who prefer this life — all of that hangs on this first claim. If the Book is not what this verse says it is, the warnings that follow have no ground.

The verse is also a comfort. The Prophet is told why the Book was given to him before he is told how hard the leading will be. The task is large and the leave is not his, which sounds like a burden until you hear the other half: he is not asked to create light. He is asked to carry a Book that already is light, toward a path that already has an Owner. The rest of the sūrah will test that assignment against Pharaoh's house, against vanished nations, against Satan's speech on the Day, and against Abraham's prayer in a barren valley. The first line has already said what success looks like: people walking out of night.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:2

> Allah, to Whom belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And woe to the disbelievers because of a severe torment

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Path's Owner Is Named in Full**

The previous verse ended on two names and a dash. This verse picks up the grammar and names the One those names belong to: Allah, to Whom belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. The path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy, is not an abstract road. It is the road of the God who already owns both worlds. Ownership here is not a legal title waiting to be enforced. It is a present fact. Sky and soil, the things people trade and the things they cannot reach, already belong to Him.

The same claim fills other places in the Book. *“To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth”* (2:255). *“To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth and whatever is in between and whatever is underground”* (20:6). *“All praise is for Allah, to Whom belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth”* (34:1). 14:2 uses the claim as a hinge between the path and the threat. If He owns both realms, then leaving His path is not an escape into some other landlord's land. There is no other landlord.

Al-Bayḍāwī reads the threat that follows as aimed at those who do not believe in the Book and so are not led by it out of darkness. That is the right link. Verse 1 gave the Book a task. Verse 2 names who is ignored when the task is refused, and what refusal costs.

**Woe Is Not a Soft Word**

*Wayl* is an Arabic cry of ruin. Translators render it “woe,” which can sound like a sigh. In the Qur'an's mouth it is closer to a sentence already written. It falls on *the disbelievers* — those who cover the truth of the Book just named — *because of a severe torment*. The cause is attached in the same breath. Woe is not a mood God happens to feel. It is the front edge of a punishment that the verse calls *shadīd*, severe, hard, not easily borne.

Some later sermons treat *wayl* as the name of a valley in Hell. Reports of that kind circulate in weak collections and in homiletic books. The wording of this verse does not require that geography. It requires a real threat attached to a real crime: disbelief after a Book has been sent to lead people into light. The safer reading is the one the Arabic already gives. Ruin is announced. The torment is severe. Details of landscape can wait for verses that actually describe them.

The threat also has a pastoral use. It is spoken in Mecca, to a Prophet whose people are still able to turn. A warning that names the Owner of heaven and earth is not gossip about a distant court. It is a last clear sign before the dark hardens.

**Disbelief After Light Has a Special Shape**

The people threatened here are not those who never heard. They are those who stand in front of a revealed Book and will not walk. The sūrah will soon describe them as people who prefer this life, who hinder others, who try to make the way look crooked. Verse 2 gives the verdict in advance: woe, and a severe torment. The later verses supply the habits that earn it.

That order matters. The Qur'an does not first catalogue sins and then wonder what God might do. It first names God as Owner, then names the cost of denying Him, then shows the lives that run toward that cost. Ownership makes the torment just. If the heavens and the earth were ownerless, disbelief would be a private opinion. Because they are His, disbelief is a trespass against the One in whose property the disbeliever already lives, eats, and argues.

The contrast with verse 1 is sharp on purpose. There the Prophet leads into light by the Lord's will. Here those who refuse that leading are not left as a neutral third group. They are addressed with woe. The Book does not have a middle shelf for people who heard and did not move.

**Belonging Cuts Off a Common Excuse**

People who reject messengers often talk as if the world were a shared commons in which God is one claimant among others. This verse removes that picture. Whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth belongs to Allah. The phrase *whatever is* is as wide as it looks. It includes the idols later mentioned in Abraham's prayer, the fruits he asks for his children, the ships and rivers and sun and moon that verses 32–33 will list as subjected for human use. None of those things is a rival owner. All of them are possessions of the One now named.

*“Allah ˹alone˺ is truly the Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”* (22:64) makes the same pairing of ownership and praise that 14:1–2 has just used. He does not own because He needs. He owns because He made and sustains. The disbeliever's torment is severe partly because the crime is ungrateful: living in a house and denying the householder.

The grammar of the verse is a single breath. Name, ownership, woe. You cannot take the first two and leave the third as rhetoric. A God who owns both worlds and then has nothing to say to those who deny Him would not be the God this sūrah is talking about.

**The Verse Is a Warning, Not a Description of the Saved**

Nothing here describes the believers' reward. That will come later, in gardens and in the greeting of peace. This line is reserved for the other side of the opening. The Book is light; its refusal is ruin. The restraint is a kind of mercy. The listener is not yet drowned in images of fire. He is told, while he can still choose, that disbelief has a bill, and that the One who will present it already owns the place where the disbeliever stands.

Meccan hearers knew *wayl* as a word used in laments and curses. Putting it in God's speech raises it from human heat to divine judgment. The Prophet is not told to invent a threat. He is told to recite one that already stands. That is why al-Bayḍāwī can call it a threat against those who do not believe in the Book: the sentence is itself part of the leading out of darkness. Some will hear woe and turn. Some will hear it and walk on. Both responses are already in view.

The severe torment is left unnamed in this verse on purpose. Later lines will speak of pus, of death that will not come, of garments of tar. Here the mind is meant to stop at the fact of severity, attached to the fact of ownership. That is enough for a first warning. The rest of the sūrah will not let the word grow vague.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:3

> ˹They are˺ the ones who favour the life of this world over the Hereafter and hinder ˹others˺ from the Way of Allah, striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked. It is they who have gone far astray

**Expanded Commentary**

**Preference Is Already a Verdict**

The disbelievers of the previous verse are now described by what they love. They favour the life of this world over the Hereafter. Al-Ṭūsī notes that they choose to remain here rather than to be there, even though this world is the house of ending and the next is the house of lasting. The Qur'an does not treat that choice as a small error of taste. Love directs pursuit. Ibn ʿAjībah put it in one line: whoever loves something prefers it and pursues it. The people named here are not confused about two equal rooms. They have ranked the rooms, and they have ranked the passing one first.

That ranking has a spiritual cost before it has a legal one. A heart fastened to this life has less room for what lies beyond the body. The Persian poet Fakhr al-Dīn ʿIrāqī wrote that every heart which prefers the caprice of this world grows in body and shrinks in spirit. The line is poetry, not revelation, but it names the same squeeze the verse assumes. You cannot put the Hereafter second and then claim you are still facing it. Preference is already a turning of the face.

The verse also refuses a fake piety that condemns “the world” as dirt while living for it. *Al-ḥayāt al-dunyā* is this near life, the one in front of the eyes. The crime is not farming, trade, or family. The crime is treating those things as the real home and the meeting with God as a rumour.

**Hindering Is the Public Face of That Love**

They do not only prefer. They hinder others from the Way of Allah. The Arabic *yaṣuddūna* can mean to turn away oneself and to turn others away. Both senses are live in the Book. *“Those who disbelieve and hinder ˹others˺ from the Way of Allah have certainly strayed far away”* (4:167). *“Those who disbelieve and hinder ˹others˺ from the Way of Allah, He will render their deeds void”* (47:1). In this sūrah the public reading is the stronger one, because the next clause is about making the way look crooked. You do not labour to bend a road you have merely left. You labour to bend a road you want other people to miss.

Al-Jalālayn and Ibn Ḥayyān take the hindering as turning people from Islam. Ibn ʿAjībah takes it as preventing faith. The difference is small. The Way is God's, not a party's. Blocking it can look like argument, mockery, social pressure, or the rewriting of what the Prophet has recited. All of those acts serve the first love. If this world is the prize, then a Way that asks you to spend, wait, and die in hope is a threat, and other people must be kept off it.

**Crookedness Is a Project, Not an Accident**

They strive to make the Way appear crooked. Al-Ṭabarī reads this as seeking to change God's way through lies, deviation, and falsehood. The verb is effortful. The way is straight; the labour is to make it look bent so that a listener will not trust it. That is a different crime from simple refusal. Refusal can be silent. This is propaganda. It treats revelation as a thing that can be edited until it fits the life already chosen.

The same pairing — hinder, make crooked — returns in other Meccan warnings. *“those who hindered ˹others˺ from Allah’s Way, strived to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, and disbelieved in the Hereafter”* (7:45). *“who hinder ˹others˺ from Allah’s Path, striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, and disbelieve in the Hereafter”* (11:19). The three clauses travel together because they are one life: love this world, block the road, then lie about the road's shape. Disbelief in the Hereafter is both the root and the cover. If there is no next life, then bending the Way is only politics.

The word *crooked* also tells you what the Way was before they touched it. You cannot make a thing appear crooked unless it was straight. Their project is a backhanded witness. They would not need to twist the message if the message already matched their preference.

**Far Astray Is a Distance, Not a Mood**

The close of the verse is a restriction: it is they who have gone far astray. Arabic *al-ḍalālu al-baʿīd* is error with mileage on it. Near error can still turn. Far error has walked a long way from the road and has spent that walk recruiting others. The Book uses the same measure for people who combine disbelief with blocking: they have strayed far away (4:167). Distance here is moral, not geographic. A man can live beside the Kaʿbah and still be far.

The restriction *it is they* answers a temptation. Suffering, poverty, or confusion can look like being lost. This verse will not let those states steal the word. The ones far astray are the ones who preferred the near life, blocked the Way, and tried to bend it. The ranking of loves is the first step of the distance. The rest is walking.

That is why a later line of this sūrah can speak of ashes blown on a stormy day. Deeds built on this preference have no weight. They were aimed at a world that does not keep what it is given. Far astray, in that light, is not only a description of the people. It is a description of their labour's destination.

**The Three Habits Are One Person**

It would be a mistake to split the verse into three types: the worldly, the hinderer, the distorter. The grammar makes them one group. Preference funds the hindering. Hindering needs the distortion. Distortion protects the preference. A preaching that attacks only “love of the world” while ignoring the public blocking will miss the people this verse is actually about. So will a preaching that attacks only “extremists who block” while leaving the love of this life unexamined. The sūrah wants the whole figure in view.

Abraham, later in the chapter, will ask to be kept from idols and will ask for prayer and fruit in a barren valley. That prayer is the opposite life: the next world first, the Way kept straight, children protected from the thing that leads many astray. Verse 3 is the negative of that portrait. Read them against each other and the sūrah's moral map is plain. One life prefers what passes and bends the road. The other asks to be kept from the bend and to be made to pray.

The verse is also a warning to anyone who carries the Book. If leading people into light is the Prophet's task, then the people described here are the active resistance to that task. They are not background noise. They are why the opening had to mention leave, woe, and a severe torment so quickly. Light has enemies who prefer the dark and who work to keep the dark looking like wisdom.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:4

> We have not sent a messenger except in the language of his people to clarify ˹the message˺ for them. Then Allah leaves whoever He wills to stray and guides whoever He wills. And He is the Almighty, All-Wise

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Messenger Speaks the Tongue He Was Sent In**

No messenger is sent except in the language of his people. The rule is stated as a negation so that no exception hides in the corner. Language here is *lisān*: tongue, idiom, the way a people actually talk. Al-Kalbi stresses that messengers know the idiom of those they address. Al-Baghawī says the point is that they will be understood. Clarity is the reason named in the verse itself: *to clarify the message for them*. God does not send a riddle in a foreign grammar and then blame the village for not decoding it.

This Prophet spoke to Arabs in their own speech. The Book says the Qur'an was sent down *“in a clear Arabic tongue”* (26:195). Arabic here is a mercy of clarity, not a racial fence. The same revelation that uses this tongue will later say, *“O humanity! I am Allah’s Messenger to you all”* (7:158). Language of sending and width of mission are two different facts. Moses spoke to Israel in the tongue they used. The last Prophet spoke first in the tongue of Quraysh, and the message was then carried, by that same Arabic, to every later people who would hear it in translation. The verse explains the first half. It does not cancel the second.

**Clarity Removes One Excuse and Leaves Another**

*Li-yubayyina lahum* — so that he might make it clear for them — is the purpose of the shared tongue. Once the message is clear, a hearer cannot say, “I never understood the words.” He can still refuse the meaning. The verse is built for that second moment. After the clarifying comes the split: Allah leaves whoever He wills to stray and guides whoever He wills. Explanation is given to all who share the tongue. Guidance is not. The first is a humanly audible event. The second is God's act in the heart.

That order protects two truths that people like to pull apart. If you drop the first, God looks unfair: He would punish people for a speech they could not parse. If you drop the second, God looks idle: a clear sermon would then save everyone who heard it, and the Book's many reports of hearing without believing would make no sense. The verse holds both. Messengers clarify. God guides or leaves to stray.

*“Had Allah willed, He could have easily made you one community ˹of believers˺, but He leaves to stray whoever He wills and guides whoever He wills”* (16:93). *“Whoever Allah guides is truly guided. And whoever He leaves to stray, you will find no guardians for them besides Him”* (17:97). 14:4 is the same doctrine placed after a statement about language, so that no one uses “I did not get the words” as a cover for “I will not take the words.”

**Straying After Clarity Is Not a Trap**

*Yuḍillu* and *yahdī* are God's verbs. Some readers hear them as arbitrary. Al-Rāzī and others, reading the wider Book, tie them to what people do with the clarification they have been given. A person who fights the clear message is left in the stray he chose. A person who accepts it is guided. The will in *whoever He wills* is not a coin toss. It is the will of the Almighty, All-Wise, the two names that close the verse. Power without wisdom would be caprice. Wisdom without power would be advice. Together they say that the split after the sermon is both unstoppable and just.

Ibn Kathīr, commenting on this line, notes that every prophet before Muhammad ﷺ was sent to his own people in their language, while Muhammad ﷺ was sent to all. Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh reported that the Prophet ﷺ said he was given five things given to no one before him, among them: a prophet used to be sent to his people in particular, whereas he has been sent to all people (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 335; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 521). That hadith does not break this verse. It completes it. The last messenger still spoke the language of the people he first stood among. The difference is the scope of the call, not the method of speech.

**Arabic Does Not Imprison the Call**

A shallow reading of this verse has been used to claim that the Prophet was sent only to Arabs, because he spoke Arabic. The Book blocks that claim in more than one place. *“And so We have revealed it as an authority in Arabic”* (13:37) explains the form. *“O humanity! I am Allah’s Messenger to you all”* (7:158) explains the addressee. Form is local so that the first hearers have no fog. Address is global so that later hearers have no fence. Translation of the meaning is how the clarifying continues after the first tongue. The legal and recitational Arabic of the Qur'an remains the revealed wording. The duty to make the message clear does not end at the edge of the peninsula.

The verse also has a quiet lesson for anyone who teaches this Book. If God sent messengers in the tongue of their people so that the message would be clear, then teaching that hides behind unused jargon, or that refuses to explain hard words in the sentence that uses them, has stepped off the method of prophecy. Clarity is not a lowering of the message. It is the reason the message was put in a human mouth.

**Mighty and Wise Close the Split**

The last sentence is not a tag. *He is the Almighty, All-Wise* tells you how to hear *leaves to stray* and *guides*. Mighty means no one forces guidance out of Him or blocks His leaving. Wise means He does not do either without knowledge of the person and of the message that person heard. The names from verse 1 return in a new pair: there *Almighty, Praiseworthy* named the path; here *Almighty, All-Wise* name the sorting of those who hear about the path.

Al-Jalālayn compresses the line: God then sends astray whom He will and guides whom He will; He is Mighty in His kingdom, Wise in His actions. That is the whole theology of the verse in one breath. Language is the kindness. Clarifying is the messenger's work. The split is God's. The names stop the split from being read as either weakness or cruelty. A hearer who wants a different arrangement — a God who explains and then must save — is asking for a different Book. This one has already said how sending works.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:5

> Indeed, We sent Moses with Our signs, ˹ordering him,˺ “Lead your people out of darkness and into light, and remind them of Allah’s days ˹of favour˺.” Surely in this are signs for whoever is steadfast, grateful

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Same Task, an Earlier Messenger**

The opening of the sūrah gave this Prophet a Book so that he might lead people out of darkness into light. This verse gives Moses the same order, with signs instead of a named Book, and with *your people* instead of *people* at large. The echo is deliberate. Prophecy is one work repeated. Moses is not a decoration from Israelite memory. He is the proof that the task in verse 1 is older than Mecca.

*With Our signs* covers the staff, the hand, the plagues, and the speech at the mountain — the public proofs that he did not invent his mission. The command that follows is not “impress them” or “defeat Pharaoh first.” It is *lead your people out of darkness and into light*. The political exit from Egypt, told at length in 7:103–155, 20:9–97, 26:10–66, and 28:3–46, is the outer face of a deeper exit: from the dark of bondage and idol-fear into the light of serving God. A people can leave a country and still stay in night. Moses is told to lead both journeys.

The addressee of the command is Moses; the people are Israel. The reciter in Mecca is meant to hear his own assignment under theirs. If God sent Moses with signs for this work, then sending Muhammad ﷺ with a Book for this work is not a new religion invented against the past. It is the past arriving again.

**Allah’s Days Are More Than a Calendar**

*Ayyām Allāh* — the days of God — is an Arabic idiom with more than one load. Al-Qurṭubī takes it as blessings, the favours God poured on Israel: rescue from Egypt, the sea, manna, shade. Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabarī read the same way in the first instance, because the next verse will name the rescue from Pharaoh. Other readers, including Abū al-Futūḥ, hear punishments as well: the days when God seized earlier deniers. Both senses live in the word *days* when a people speak of “the days of so-and-so” — the days that made history, whether as gift or as blow.

A third, thinner reading takes *days* as long stretches of God's work, as in *“a day with your Lord is indeed like a thousand years by your counting”* (22:47). That cosmic sense is real in other verses. Here the next line about Pharaoh's house pulls the meaning down to events a people can remember. Moses is told to remind, not to speculate. Reminding needs a story the listeners already half know.

The two pastoral methods sit together in the one order. Lead them into light. Remind them of the days. Fear of past ruin and thanks for past rescue are both ways of moving a people. A reminder that only threatens makes hard hearts. A reminder that only flatters makes forgetful ones. God's days include both kinds of morning.

**Patience and Gratitude Are the Eyes That See**

*Surely in this are signs for whoever is steadfast, grateful.* *This* points back to the days, or to the whole sending of Moses. Signs are there either way. Not everyone sees them. The verse names the two traits that can: *ṣabbār*, intensely patient, and *shakūr*, grateful. The forms are intensive. Ordinary patience and a passing thanks are not the measure. The person who holds steady under the weight of obedience, and who thanks God for the weight being a gift, is the person for whom the days become signs rather than old news.

The pairing is not unique to this sūrah. *“Surely in this are signs for whoever is steadfast, grateful”* (31:31). The same close follows the scattering of a proud people (34:19) and the stilling of ships (42:33). Four times the Book says that patience and gratitude are the conditions of reading history as sign. Ibn al-Qayyim later built a whole argument on that pairing: faith, in practice, is doing what God wants and holding still under what He sends, which is gratitude and patience sharing one life. A report often repeated in later books says faith is two halves, patience and gratitude. A numbered, graded Prophetic source for that exact wording was not confirmed here, so it is left as a later formula, not as a cited ḥadīth. The Qur'an's own four verses are enough. They already join the two traits as the eyes that can see.

**Moses Stands as a Pattern, Not as a Rival**

Some hearers in Mecca treated the Israelite stories as foreign stock. This verse will not allow that. Moses is sent *with Our signs* — God's, not Israel's. The darkness and light are the same image as verse 1. The people he leads are a people, as Quraysh are a people. The days he reminds them of are God's days, not a tribal archive. If the Meccan listener cannot see himself in Moses' assignment, he has not yet read the verse.

The difference of scope remains. Moses is told *your people*. Muhammad ﷺ is told, in verse 1, *people*. The method is the same; the width grows. That is why this sūrah can move from a general Book to Moses without changing subject. It is still talking about how God gets a people out of night.

Signs for the patient and grateful also imply a warning for the restless and ungrateful. A person who will not wait, and who will not thank, can stand in front of the same days and see only weather. Pharaoh saw signs and called them magic. Israel saw rescue and later asked for idols. The verse does not pretend that signs work like a key in every lock. It names the lock that opens.

**Reminder Is Part of Leading, Not an Extra**

The command has two verbs: lead, and remind. Leading without reminding becomes politics — a movement with no memory of why it moves. Reminding without leading becomes folklore — a memory with no road out of the dark. Moses is given both. The Prophet reciting this in Mecca is given both. The Book in verse 1 is the means of leading; the stories that follow, including this one, are the reminder of God's days.

That is why the sūrah will not rush past Pharaoh, or past Noah, ʿĀd, and Thamūd. Those are days. The patient and grateful read them and take warning and thanks. The people of verse 3, who prefer this life and bend the Way, read them as someone else's problem. The split in verse 4 — God leaves to stray and guides — is already visible in how two listeners hear the same reminder.

The verse ends without telling the end of Moses' story. It does not need to. The point is the sending, the order, and the kind of heart that can see signs in it. The next lines will put the reminder into Moses' own mouth, which is the method made audible: a prophet standing in front of his people and telling them to remember what God already did.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:6

> ˹Consider˺ when Moses said to his people, “Remember Allah’s favour upon you when He rescued you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment—slaughtering your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moses Does What He Was Told to Do**

The previous verse ordered Moses to remind his people of God's days. This verse is that reminder spoken. *When Moses said to his people* places us inside the command, not beside it. The first word he uses is *remember*. The object is *Allah’s favour upon you*. Favour here is *niʿmah*, a concrete blessing, not a vague warmth. He then names it: God rescued you from the people of Pharaoh. Reminding, in prophetic speech, is not a mood. It is pointing at a fact the listeners would rather file away.

The almost identical wording in *“˹Remember˺ how We delivered you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment, slaughtering your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord”* (2:49) shows that this is a set piece of Israel's memory in the Qur'an. God says it in al-Baqarah in His own voice. Moses says it here in his. The two voices agree. The people are not allowed to remember the rescue as a national luck story. It is God's favour, and it came through a torment that He also names.

**Rescue Is Named Only After the Torment Is Named**

Moses does not skip to freedom. He makes them hear the house they were taken from: the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment, slaughtering your sons and keeping your women. The torment is *sūʾ al-ʿadhāb*, an evil, ugly punishment. The method is specific. Sons were killed. Women were kept. Exodus memory and Qur'anic memory meet on that cruelty. A people that forgets the killing of its sons will also forget the God who stopped it.

*Keeping your women* has been read as leaving daughters alive for service and humiliation, and as a policy meant to cut a people's future while using its women. Either way the design is demographic and moral at once. Pharaoh does not only hurt bodies. He tries to decide who will exist. The favour of rescue is therefore not only a change of address. It is the return of a future.

Moses puts all of this in a *when* clause under *remember*. The people are not being given new information. They are being told that the information they have is a debt. Favour remembered becomes gratitude. Favour forgotten becomes the ingratitude the next verses will name.

**The Severe Test Has Two Faces**

*That was a severe test from your Lord.* *Balāʾ ʿaẓīm* can mean a great trial by hardship or a great trial by gift. Al-Bayḍāwī notes that *balāʾ* can, depending on context, name a blessing, and that the great trial here may allude to the deliverance itself. Al-Ṭabarī and others keep the harder sense: the slaughter and the keeping were the test. Both readings are grammatically possible. The stronger one, in this sentence, includes both. Being crushed by Pharaoh was a test of patience. Being rescued from him was a test of thanks. Verse 5 already said that signs belong to the patient and the grateful. Verse 6 shows the two materials those traits are tested on.

Calling the test *from your Lord* is the hard part of the theology. Pharaoh is the criminal. The test is still from God. The Qur'an will not give Pharaoh the dignity of being an independent source of history. He is a tyrant whose tyranny God used as a proving ground, then broke. Israel is not asked to thank Pharaoh for the lesson. Israel is asked to remember God's favour in the breaking, and to know that the years under the torment were not outside the Lord's hand.

That reading is what later unbelief always resists. People will grant that God gives gifts. They will not grant that God tests through a slaughter they did not choose. Moses, speaking to survivors, does not soften the sentence. He names the killing, names the keeping, and then names the Lord as the one whose test it was. Honesty about pain is part of reminding.

**Favour Is Plural in Fact, Singular in the Sentence**

The word is *niʿmah*, singular, but the content is a cluster: rescue, the end of slaughter, the end of the policy against women, the existence of the people Moses is now addressing. A singular noun for a bundle of gifts is how Arabic often speaks of grace. The point is that it all comes from one source. Split the gifts and you get folklore: a clever escape, a weak army, a lucky wind. Keep them as one favour and you get worship.

Moses says *upon you*. The people standing in front of him are the ones who carry the debt, including those born after the sea, because a people inherits both the rescue and the duty to remember it. That is why the same speech can be recited in Mecca to a different people. Quraysh have their own favours: the Sacred House, safety, trade. The method of the verse is transferable. Remember the favour. Name the danger you were taken from. Call it a test from your Lord. The content changes with the people. The grammar of thanks does not.

**A Prophet's Reminder Is Not Flattery**

Nothing in Moses' sentence praises Israel's virtue. He does not say, “You endured well.” He says God rescued you, Pharaoh tortured you, your Lord tested you. The subject of the good verb is God. The subject of the evil verb is Pharaoh's people. The people of Moses are the object of both. That grammar keeps memory from becoming pride. A people that tells the story as “we won” has already lost the reminder. A people that tells it as “He saved us from that, and it was a test” is ready for the next sentence, which will be about thanks and increase.

The verse also refuses a piety that talks only about inner states. Sons slaughtered and women kept are public facts. Favour that does not include bodies, children, and political rescue is not the favour Moses names. Islam's later readers who treat *niʿmah* as only a spiritual mood have stepped away from this speech. God is thanked here for a historical act that left a people alive.

The severe test from the Lord remains the last word of the quotation in this verse. It leaves the listeners in a sober place: alive because of mercy, alive inside a trial. How they answer that will be the subject of the proclamation that follows. Moses has done the first half of reminding. He has made them look at the day they were saved, and at the price of the years before it.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:7

> And ˹remember˺ when your Lord proclaimed, ‘If you are grateful, I will certainly give you more. But if you are ungrateful, surely My punishment is severe.’”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Proclamation, Not a Hint**

Moses is still speaking, or the sūrah is still inside his reminder. *When your Lord proclaimed* raises the next sentence above ordinary advice. *Taʾadhdhana* is a public announcement, the kind that leaves no one able to say he was not told. The speaker of the quoted words is the Lord. The content is a law of dealing: thanks brings increase; ingratitude meets severe punishment. Al-Ṭūsī takes thanks here as gratitude for blessings joined to acknowledging them. The proclamation is not about a feeling that stays in the chest. It is about a stance toward gifts already in the hand.

Increase (*la-azīdannakum*) is left unspecified on purpose. Al-Kalbi lists increase of good in this world, of blessing in the next, or of both. The verse does not freeze the currency. A people who thank for rescue may be given more safety. A person who thanks for knowledge may be given more light. A person who thanks for a little food may be given enough. The promise is God's to measure. The condition is not. Thanks is the human side of the bargain, and it is named first.

**Gratitude Is Confession Before It Is Mood**

Arabic *shukr* is the opposite of *kufr* in this pairing, and *kufr* is both ingratitude and covering. To thank is to uncover the gift and name its Giver. To be ungrateful is to cover the gift, or to use it against the One who gave it. Ibn Kathīr reads the ungrateful side as covering the blessing, hiding it, or striving against it. That is why the punishment is not a random storm. It matches the crime. A gift covered is a gift that will not be increased. A gift used as a weapon against the Giver meets severity.

Abū Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: whoever does not thank people has not thanked Allah (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 1954). The report does not replace this verse. It extends the same logic downward. Thanks that never reaches the human means by which a gift arrived is already a covering. The proclamation in 14:7 is vertical — you and your Lord — but a life that thanks God while trampling people has not understood what uncovering a gift means.

Luqmān is taught the same grammar from the other end: *“Be grateful to Allah, for whoever is grateful, it is only for their own good. And whoever is ungrateful, then surely Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”* (31:12). Thanks benefits the thankful. Ingratitude does not injure God. 14:7 adds what 31:12 leaves implicit: the thankful will be given more, and the ungrateful will meet a punishment that is severe. Need and threat sit on the human side. The Lord remains the one who proclaims.

**Increase Is a Test That Looks Like a Gift**

The promise *I will certainly give you more* can be misread as a vending machine. Put thanks in, take goods out. The rest of the sūrah will not allow that. Delay, as other verses teach, can be a drawing-on toward ruin. *“Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin”* (3:178). So the increase of 14:7 is increase given to thanks, not increase given to appetite. The same extra years, extra wealth, extra safety can be either *shukr*'s fruit or *istidrāj*'s bait. The difference is the stance of the receiver, which this verse has already named.

That is why the proclamation is placed after the reminder of Pharaoh. Israel has just been told that rescue was a favour and a severe test. Now they are told how to live after rescue: thank, and more will come; cover, and the punishment is severe. A people fresh from the sea is in the most dangerous position in the sūrah's moral map — loaded with gift, able to treat the gift as a right. The announcement arrives before that habit sets.

**The Ungrateful Side Is as Certain as the Grateful Side**

The two halves are both sworn. *If you are grateful, I will certainly give you more. But if you are ungrateful, surely My punishment is severe.* Arabic uses energetic nūns and *inna* so that neither clause sounds like a maybe. Readers who like only the first half have to cut the verse. The Lord who promises increase is the same Lord who promises severity. Soft preaching that quotes only *I will give you more* has turned a proclamation into a slogan.

Severe punishment here is not yet described as pus or tar. It does not need to be. The people hearing Moses already know severity: slaughter of sons, keeping of women. The next ungratefulness will not meet a lighter hand. *“If you disbelieve, then ˹know that˺ Allah is truly not in need of you, nor does He approve of disbelief from His servants. But if you become grateful ˹through faith˺, He will appreciate that from you”* (39:7). Need is never on God's side. Approval and increase are. The proclamation of 14:7 is that economy stated as law.

**The Line Is Still Spoken to the Living**

Moses reports the proclamation to a people who can still choose. The verse is not a tomb inscription over a finished nation. It is a rule for anyone who has just been told to remember a favour. Meccan hearers, reciting it on the Prophet's tongue, stand in the same place: given a House, given safety, given a Book that leads out of darkness. Thanks would mean walking the path of verse 1. Ingratitude would mean the preference and the blocking of verse 3. Increase and severity are already in motion around them.

A report from Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq, often cited in Shīʿī tafsīr, says that when God gives a blessing and the servant acknowledges it in the heart and praises with the tongue, God commands increase before the blessing has run out. The chain of that wording is not established here as a Prophetic ḥadīth, so it is left as a later gloss. The gloss does, however, match the verse's own order: inner confession, spoken praise, then more. The proclamation does not ask for a performance. It asks for a truthfulness about gifts that God can then enlarge.

The last word in the ungrateful clause is *severe*. The last word in the grateful clause is *more*. Both are open in size and closed in direction. No one who hears the proclamation can claim that the terms of living after rescue were unclear.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:8

> Moses added, “If you along with everyone on earth were to be ungrateful, then ˹know that˺ Allah is indeed Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moses Closes the Door on a False Fear**

After the Lord's proclamation, Moses adds a sentence of his own. The addition is not a rival law. It is the theology under the law. If you, and everyone on earth with you, were ungrateful, God would still be Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy. Thanks does not feed Him. Ingratitude does not starve Him. The increase promised in the previous verse is therefore not a payment God needs in order to remain God. It is a mercy He chooses to attach to thanks.

The hypothetical is as wide as it can be: you, plus everyone on earth. Arabic *jamīʿan* gathers the species. Even a planet of covering would not put a dent in Him. *“O humanity! It is you who stand in need of Allah, but Allah ˹alone˺ is the Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”* (35:15). Moses is reciting that fact to a rescued people who might imagine that their rituals keep the heavens running. They do not. The heavens were running when they were still under Pharaoh.

**Self-Sufficient Names a Freedom, Not a Distance**

*Ghanī* is the One who has no need. In human talk, self-sufficiency can sound like coldness. In this verse it is the ground of clean worship. A God who needed thanks would be bargaining. A God who does not need thanks can give increase as gift and punish ingratitude as justice, without either act being a repair of Himself. Al-Jalālayn, on the ungrateful half of verse 7, already said the recompense for ingratitude is punishment. Verse 8 adds that the punishment is not God collecting a debt He is owed in order to be complete. It is God answering a crime He did not require in order to be praised.

*Ḥamīd*, Praiseworthy, sits beside *Ghanī* so that needlessness is not mistaken for a blank. He is worthy of praise whether or not a single throat offers it. Praise is true speech about Him, not a supply He runs on. The pairing returns often. *“And know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”* (2:267), in a passage about giving. *“Whoever is ungrateful, then surely Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”* (31:12). *“Allah was not in need ˹of their faith˺. For Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”* (64:6). Moses is not inventing a title. He is applying a name the Book keeps placing next to human refusal.

**A Whole Earth of Refusal Would Not Be a Crisis in Heaven**

The sentence is meant to humble, not to despair. If the entire earth uncovered no gift, God would not be the loser. The loser would be the earth. That is the only honest scale. People who treat religion as a service they render to a needy deity will either swell with pride when they worship or collapse when they fail. Moses cuts both. Worship is for your own good, as Luqmān was told. Refusal is your own ruin, as verse 7 said. Heaven is not waiting to see which way the vote goes.

This also protects the Prophet who is reciting the sūrah. Quraysh's refusal cannot be read as a failure that injures God, or as a failure that injures the worth of the message. *“If you disbelieve, then ˹know that˺ Allah is truly not in need of you”* (39:7). Moses said it first to Israel. Muhammad ﷺ recites it over a town that thinks its idols and its trade are the centre of the story. They are not. The centre does not move when a town covers its gifts.

**Praiseworthy Stops a Second Error**

Needlessness alone could be heard as: God does not care. *Praiseworthy* blocks that. He cares enough to proclaim increase and severity. He cares enough to send Moses and to send this Book. What He does not do is depend. The names teach a relation that is full of command and empty of need. That is why the ungrateful are in danger rather than in a position of leverage. You cannot withhold from the One who does not require your holding.

Moses adds this after a promise of more. The order is kind. First: thank and you will be increased. Then: even if you do not, He remains who He is. The second sentence keeps the first from becoming a story about a God who is anxious to be thanked. Increase is certain for the grateful because He is generous, not because He is hungry for acknowledgement.

**The Speaker Is Still the Rescued People's Prophet**

Moses has standing to say this. He has just named the slaughter, the keeping of women, the rescue, the test, and the proclamation. Now he tells them that their thanks, even if the whole earth joined their refusal, would not be the thing that upholds God. A prophet who had not walked them out of a house of torment could not say this without sounding like a scold. Coming from Moses, it is the last piece of reminding: you were saved for your own sake, and your Lord was already Self-Sufficient while you were still slaves.

The verse is short. Its work is large. It detaches God's being from the scoreboard of human religion. It leaves thanks as wisdom and ingratitude as folly, not as a supply crisis. It prepares the later stories of nations who covered their gifts and vanished. Those nations did not starve God. They starved themselves. Moses says that before the stories arrive, so that no listener can hear a vanished people as a near-miss in heaven. Heaven was never the one at risk. The rescued are the ones at risk, and the risk is their own covering of a gift that did not make God richer when it was given and will not make Him poorer when it is denied.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:9

> Have you not ˹already˺ received the stories of those who were before you: the people of Noah, ’Âd, Thamûd, and those after them? Only Allah knows how many they were. Their messengers came to them with clear proofs, but they put their hands over their mouths and said, “We totally reject what you have been sent with, and we are certainly in alarming doubt about what you are inviting us to.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question Assumes the Stories Have Already Arrived**

*Have you not already received the stories* is not a request for information. It is a rebuke dressed as a question. The people of Noah, ʿĀd, and Thamūd are named because their accounts are already in this Book and already in Arabian memory. Noah's people meet the flood in 7:59–64, 11:25–48, and Sūrah 71. ʿĀd, the people of Hūd, are in 7:65–72 and 11:50–60. Thamūd, the people of Ṣāliḥ, are in 7:73–79 and 11:61–68. A Meccan listener who says “we never heard” is not telling the truth this verse will accept.

*Those after them* opens the list beyond the three famous names. Then the verse cuts human counting: only Allah knows how many they were. Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabarī take this as the exact number of later communities. Al-Kalbi hears it as a way of saying they were vast. Both readings serve the same end. The archive is larger than the stories people retell. *“We have told you the stories of some of them, while others We have not”* (40:78). *“Also ˹We destroyed˺ ’Ȃd, Thamûd, and the people of the Water-pit, as well as many peoples in between”* (25:38). History, in this sūrah, is not a short list of cautionary tales. It is a long field whose full census belongs to God.

**Clear Proofs Came Before the Gesture**

Their messengers came to them with *bayyināt*, clear proofs. Al-Nīsābūrī takes these as the miracles that affirmed prophethood. The order in the sentence matters. Proofs arrive. Then hands go over mouths. Then speech of rejection and doubt. The verse will not let anyone tell the story as “we never saw evidence, so we doubted.” The evidence is named first. The refusal is named second. That is the moral sequence of every ruined people this sūrah will use.

Clear proofs do not mean that every hearer is compelled. Verse 4 has already said that messengers clarify and God then guides or leaves to stray. What the proofs do is remove the excuse of fog. Noah's people heard a warning. ʿĀd heard Hūd. Thamūd saw the she-camel. The later unnamed nations saw whatever God gave their messengers. The pattern does not require us to know each proof. It requires us to know that proof came, and that hands then rose.

**Hands Over Mouths Is a Gesture with More Than One Reading**

*Fa-raddū aydiyahum fī afwāhihim* — they put their hands into their mouths — is a picture, not a legal term. Al-Rāzī records the main options. They bit their fingers in rage at the messengers. They covered their mouths to hide laughter. Or the hands are the disbelievers' and the mouths are the messengers', a metaphor for trying to stop the preaching, to cram the words back. A fourth, simpler reading is the gesture of refusal itself: we will not take this in.

The rage-reading fits *we totally reject what you have been sent with*. The mockery-reading fits a people who find prophecy funny. The silencing-reading fits a people who want the street quiet again. None of these needs to be declared the only sense. The gesture is hostility made visible. It is the body doing what verse 3 called hindering and making the way crooked. Before the mouth speaks its formula of doubt, the hands have already said no.

What they then say has two halves that do not sit easily together. *We totally reject* is certainty. *We are certainly in alarming doubt* is uncertainty. The combination is a known human trick: absolute refusal of the message, plus a fog of doubt about the invitation, so that the speaker can sound both brave and cautious. The verse quotes both so that neither mask works. Rejection is named. Doubt is named. The hands have already shown which one is in charge.

**Doubt Here Is Not Honest Enquiry**

*Shakk mubīb* is doubt that alarms, a suspicion that has become a stance. Honest enquiry asks and waits. This doubt arrives after clear proofs and after a gesture of stuffing words back. It is doubt as a creed. The invitation they mention — *what you are inviting us to* — is the call of verse 1, out of darkness into light, onto the path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy. They do not describe that invitation. They describe their feeling about it. The feeling is then used as an argument.

Later verses will have the messengers answer the feeling with a question about God Himself. This verse stops at the people's line so that the line can be heard in its thinness. Total reject, alarming doubt, no account of the proofs they were just said to have received. A sermon that treats all doubt as noble would not be able to read this sentence. Some doubt is a hand over a mouth.

**Only God Knows How Many, and That Is Part of the Warning**

The unnamed majority of destroyed nations is not a flourish. It means that the famous three are samples, not the full set. A town that says “we are not Noah's people, we are not ʿĀd, we are not Thamūd” has not yet answered the verse. Those after them are the category that town might already be in. God knows the number. The reciter does not. The warning is therefore wider than any list a preacher can finish.

The stories have been received. That is the opening. Receiving stories and joining the people in the stories are different acts. This verse is the test of whether reception has become memory of the kind Moses demanded: a memory that changes the present. Quraysh have heard. If they put their hands over their mouths, they are not in a new plot. They are in a very old one, whose headcount only God has.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:10

> Their messengers asked ˹them˺, “Is there any doubt about Allah, the Originator of the heavens and the earth? He is inviting you in order to forgive your sins, and delay your end until your appointed term.” They argued, “You are no more than humans like us! You ˹only˺ wish to turn us away from what our forefathers worshipped. So bring us some compelling proof.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The First Question Hits the Doubt at Its Root**

The people have just said they are in alarming doubt about the invitation. The messengers do not begin with a defence of themselves. They ask whether there is any doubt about Allah, the Originator of the heavens and the earth. Al-Kalbi hears the question as: is there doubt about His Being, or about His right to be worshipped? The next words — Originator of the heavens and the earth — are the evidence that should end that doubt. *Fāṭir* is the One who splits a thing into being, who originates what was not there. Sky and soil are not inherited furniture. They had a beginning, and the One who began them is the One now inviting.

The move is exact. The people wanted to talk about an invitation they found doubtful. The messengers talk about the Inviter. If He is not in doubt, the invitation cannot be treated as a rumour from nowhere. A later line of the Book asks the same thing in another key: they have not shown God His proper reverence when they said He revealed nothing to any human (6:91). Doubt about revelation often pretends to be careful. This verse treats it as a failure to look at the Originator already in front of the eyes.

**The Invitation Is Forgiveness and a Fixed Term, Not a Grab for Power**

*He is inviting you in order to forgive your sins, and delay your end until your appointed term.* The purpose of the call is named, and it is good for the called. Forgiveness of sins. Delay until a term already set — al-Qurṭubī takes the term as death. The messengers are not inviting people into a smaller life. They are inviting them into a cleaned life that is allowed to run to its appointed end instead of being cut by a destruction like Noah's flood or ʿĀd's wind.

Al-Ṭūsī notes that the verse says *some of your sins* in the Arabic *min dhunūbikum*, and he links that to sins God does not forgive in the same way, above all *shirk*. *“Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating others with Him ˹in worship˺, but forgives anything else of whoever He wills”* (4:48). Al-Ālūsī adds the rights of other people, which wait on the wronged. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others treat *some* as an Arabic way of saying the sins that are yours, with *min* as explanation rather than reduction. The disagreement has grounds. 4:48 is a real limit on what is forgiven without tawḥīd. The style of classical Arabic also uses *some* where the intent is the sins in question, not a stingy portion. The stronger pastoral reading for this sentence is the generous one: the Inviter is offering to forgive, and to let you live out your term. The legal reading of 4:48 still stands beside it. Associating partners is not a small stain the invitation leaves in place.

**“You Are Only Human” Is the Oldest Dodge**

The people's reply ignores the Originator and the offer. They say: you are no more than humans like us. You wish to turn us from what our forefathers worshipped. So bring a compelling proof. Three clauses, three evasions. The first denies that a human can carry God's message. The second treats the call as a social coup against ancestral worship. The third demands a proof after the verse has already said that clear proofs came.

The first clause is a refrain in the Book. *“Has Allah sent a human as a messenger?”* (17:94). *“Is this ˹one˺ not human like yourselves?”* (21:3). *“This is only a human like you, who wants to be superior to you”* (23:24). *“How can humans be our guides?”* (64:6). The demand under the question is for an angel, or for a king, or for anything that would not look like a neighbour. The messengers will answer that in the next verse. Here the sūrah lets the dodge stand so it can be seen. Humanity of the messenger is treated as a disproof, when it is in fact the method of verse 4: a messenger in the language of his people, so that the message is clear.

**Forefathers Are Used as a Shield, Not as Evidence**

*You only wish to turn us away from what our forefathers worshipped* changes the subject from God to genealogy. The worship is defined by who did it, not by whether it matches the Originator of the heavens and the earth. That is a different religion from the one the messengers preach. In the first, the past of the tribe is the proof. In the second, the origin of the world is the proof. The two proofs do not argue in the same court.

The charge *you wish* also paints the messengers as ambitious. 23:24 makes the same accusation: he wants to be superior to you. A people who love this life, as verse 3 said, will always hear a call to God as a grab for rank. They cannot imagine a motive that is not the motive they would have. The invitation to forgiveness is therefore rewritten as a wish to turn people from ancestral gods. The rewrite is useful. It lets the hearer fight for family honour instead of answering a question about the Originator.

**The Demand for Proof After Proof Is a Stall**

*So bring us some compelling proof* — *sulṭān mubīn*, a manifest authority — sounds reasonable until you remember the previous verse. Messengers came with clear proofs. Hands went over mouths. Then this demand. A proof that has already been refused can be demanded forever. The demand becomes a way of never deciding. Verse 11 will say that bringing a proof is not in the messengers' power without God's leave. The people are asking the messengers to produce, on command, what only God sends.

The whole reply is a refusal to stay on the messengers' ground. Asked about God, they talk about the messengers' bodies. Offered forgiveness and a full term, they talk about fathers. Shown proofs, they ask for proofs. That pattern is the “alarming doubt” of verse 9 made into conversation. It is not enquiry. It is a method for remaining where they are, among the things their forefathers worshipped, until the term they were offered runs out in a different way than they planned.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:11

> Their messengers said to them, “We are ˹indeed˺ only humans like you, but Allah favours whoever He chooses of His servants. It is not for us to bring you any proof without Allah’s permission. And in Allah let the believers put their trust

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Humanity Is Granted, Then Placed Under Favour**

The messengers do not deny the first charge. *We are indeed only humans like you.* The same confession is ordered on this Prophet's tongue: *“I am only a man like you, ˹but˺ it has been revealed to me that your God is only One God”* (18:110; see also 41:6). Humanity is not a hole in the mission. It is the form of the mission. Verse 4 already required a shared tongue. A shared body is the other half of that kindness. An angel at the market stall would not clarify. He would stun.

The *but* that follows is the real answer. Allah favours whoever He chooses of His servants. Al-Ṭūsī and al-Zamakhsharī take the favour as prophethood, and al-Ṭūsī adds the power to work miracles. The people had used *like us* to mean *no different from us in rank*. The messengers use *like you* to mean *the same species*, then point to a difference that is God's to give. Favour (*mann*) is not a medal the messenger pinned on himself. It is a selection from above. The argument “you eat food, therefore you cannot be sent” fails as soon as sending is recognised as God's choice among servants, not as a promotion a man votes for himself.

**Proofs Are Not a Service Messengers Run**

*It is not for us to bring you any proof without Allah’s permission.* The people had demanded a compelling proof on the spot. The answer moves the demand to the only One who can meet it. *“It was not for any messenger to bring a sign without Allah’s permission”* (40:78). Signs belong to the Sender. Messengers carry what they are given, when they are given it. A proof produced to satisfy a taunt would not be a sign. It would be a performance.

This sentence also protects the messengers from a role they were never assigned. They clarify. They do not operate the machinery of miracles as a shop. Permission (*idhn*) here matches the leave in verse 1 and the leave in verse 4. The whole sūrah keeps putting God's leave between human work and human result. Leading into light is by the Lord's will. Guiding and leaving to stray are His. Signs are by His permission. Trust, which this verse is about to command, is the only sane stance inside that arrangement.

The refusal to produce a sign on demand is not weakness. It is accuracy. People who will not accept a human messenger will also not accept a sign they asked for, once it comes. Pharaoh's magicians' stories, and the she-camel of Thamūd, are already in the Book as proof of that. The messengers will not pretend otherwise.

**Trust Is Named as the Believers' Place**

*And in Allah let the believers put their trust.* *Tawakkul* is to place the outcome with God after the work is done. The messengers have conceded their humanity, ascribed their rank to God's favour, and denied that proofs are theirs to dispatch. What remains is trust. The command is not only for the messengers. It is for *the believers*, the group that includes anyone who has taken the invitation. *“So in Allah let the believers put their trust”* (3:160). *“Say, ‘Nothing will ever befall us except what Allah has destined for us. He is our Protector.’ So in Allah let the believers put their trust”* (9:51).

Al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī, reading this verse with the next, treat this first close as the introduction of the virtue, and the next close as the need to keep it under harm. That is a sound division. Here trust is the answer to a demand the messengers cannot meet. They will not fake a sign. They will trust the One whose permission signs require. A believer who panics when the proof does not arrive on the enemy's timetable has not yet heard this line.

**Favour Cuts Both Pride and Envy**

God's choosing of some servants for prophethood is a scandal to a people who think rank must be tribal or earned in the usual way. The verse does not apologise for the scandal. It names it as favour. That naming also cuts the messenger's pride. He is a human. The extra is given, not seized. *Mann* in Arabic can mean a reminder of a gift that humiliates if it is thrown in someone's face. Here the messengers do not throw it in the people's face as a boast. They use it as a theological fact: the difference you are angry about is God's, not ours.

A community that accepts this sentence can honour a prophet without turning him into another deity, and can refuse a prophet's enemies without needing him to stop being human. Both errors — making the messenger a god, and making the messenger impossible because he eats — are blocked by the same pair of clauses.

**The Verse Is Already a Proof of a Kind**

What the people asked for was *sulṭān mubīn*, a manifest authority. What they receive is a speech that will not lie about what messengers are. That speech is itself a kind of authority: moral, not theatrical. A man who says “I cannot bring you a sign unless God permits” is not running a cult of his own power. The honesty is part of the clarifying verse 4 required. Whether the hearers will take it as proof is the split verse 4 also named. Guidance and straying remain God's. Trust remains the believers' work. The messengers have now said all three in one answer.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:12

> Why should we not put our trust in Allah, when He has truly guided us to ˹the very best of˺ ways? Indeed, we will patiently endure whatever harm you may cause us. And in Allah let the faithful put their trust.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Trust Has a Reason, and the Reason Is Guidance Already Given**

The messengers turn the virtue they just named into a question that answers itself. Why should we not put our trust in Allah, when He has truly guided us to the very best of ways? Al-Ṭūsī paraphrases: what do we have, if we do not trust Him? Or: what excuse remains? The ground of *tawakkul* here is not a guess about the future. It is a fact about the past and present: He has guided us. A people who have been put on the roads of truth have no coherent reason to snatch the outcome back from the One who put them there.

*Subulanā* — our ways, our roads — is plural. Commentators take it as the paths of the religion, the best of ways in the translation's gloss. Guidance is not a single private spark. It is a set of roads the messengers now walk, and the walking is itself evidence that trust is rational. You do not leave the Guide because the town is loud. You trust Him because He has already shown you where to put your feet.

The question form is not uncertainty. It is a refusal to treat trust as an extra burden. After humanity has been granted, favour has been ascribed to God, and signs have been returned to His permission, trust is the only sentence left that fits the facts.

**Patience Is Named as the Shape Trust Takes Under Harm**

*Indeed, we will patiently endure whatever harm you may cause us.* The people have not yet, in this stretch of the sūrah, named expulsion and forced return to the old creed. That comes in the next verse. The messengers already know what harm looks like, because every people in verse 9 used hands and words against the call. *Adhaytumūnā* is hurt, injury, the torment a town can inflict on a preacher and his circle: mockery, blows, boycott, prison, the threat of death. Trust that cannot survive that list is not the trust this verse is talking about.

Patience here is the intensive endurance verse 5 joined to gratitude. The messengers will wait under the harm without handing back the mission. That waiting is not passivity. It is the decision not to meet a taunt with a fake sign, and not to meet a threat with apostasy. Al-Rāzī's distinction is useful: verse 11 introduces trust; verse 12 shows it being kept. Keeping it is work. The second close of this verse will say so.

The harm is attributed to *you* — the addressees. The messengers do not blame a fog or a fate in the abstract. They look at the people in front of them and say: what you do to us, we will bear. That frankness is part of clarifying. A prophet who pretends the town is kind when it is not has already started to lie.

**The Second Command Is for People Who Already Trust**

*And in Allah let the faithful put their trust.* The wording shifts from *believers* in verse 11 to *the faithful* / those who trust, *al-mutawakkilūn*, in this close. Al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī read the shift as a call to persist. Those who have already placed their affairs with God must place them with Him again, under the new harm. *“In Him I put my trust. And in Him let the faithful put their trust”* (12:67) uses the same turn in Jacob's speech: a personal declaration, then a command to the group that claims the virtue.

Repetition of the command across two verses is not padding. The first time, trust is the answer to a demand for signs. The second time, trust is the answer to coming pain. A person can trust God with miracles and still snatch the matter back when the pain starts. The sūrah will not leave that gap unnamed.

**Guidance Makes Trust a Debt, Not a Leap**

People sometimes speak of trust as a jump into the dark. This verse speaks of it as a walk in a light already given. He has guided us to the ways. Therefore we trust. The logic is the reverse of a wager. A wager trusts in order to find out. These messengers trust because they have already been shown. That is why they can promise patience before the harm is fully described. They are not waiting to see whether God is worth it. They have been put on His roads, and the roads are the evidence.

The phrase *the very best of ways* in the translation is a gloss on a dense Arabic plural. It keeps the reader from hearing “our ways” as private methods. The ways are God's, given to them, walked by them. Best is not a taste. It is a claim that no ancestral worship, of the kind the people defended in verse 10, is a rival road of equal worth. Trust in God includes a refusal to treat the fathers' gods as an alternative guidance.

**The Sentence Is Public, and That Is the Point**

All of this is said *to them*, the hostile town, not only in a huddle of believers. The messengers announce their trust and their patience as part of the argument. The town is meant to hear that threats will not buy a sign and will not buy a return. *Tawakkul* here is not a silent inner state. It is a public stance that changes what the enemy can hope for. If the preachers will bear harm and will not produce miracles on demand, the town's usual tools — pain and taunt — have less purchase.

That public stance is what the next verse will try to break with a clearer threat: leave the land or return to our faith. Verse 12 has already said the answer. Harm will be borne. Trust stays with God. The faithful are commanded to keep it there. A reader who treats the next threat as a surprise has not believed this verse's promise of patience.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:13

> The disbelievers then threatened their messengers, “We will certainly expel you from our land, unless you return to our faith.” So their Lord revealed to them, “We will surely destroy the wrongdoers

**Expanded Commentary**

**When Arguments Fail, the Town Reaches for the Gate**

The disbelievers' next sentence is not another question about humanity or proof. It is a threat. We will certainly expel you from our land, unless you return to our faith. The tools have been used in order: hands over mouths, ancestral honour, demand for a sign. None of them moved the messengers, who promised patience under harm. So the harm is now specified as exile, and the price of staying is apostasy. Al-Bayḍāwī notes that although messengers are named, their followers may be included. A town that wants the street quiet will not stop at the preacher.

The same bargain was offered to Shuʿayb: *“O Shu’aib! We will certainly expel you and your fellow believers from our land, unless you return to our faith.”* He asked, *“Even if we hate it?”* (7:88). Lot's people used expulsion without even offering a return: *“Expel Lot’s followers from our land!”* (27:56). The pattern is older than this sūrah. When a message cannot be answered, the land is treated as a creed, and the creed is treated as a condition of residence. Belief becomes a visa.

**“Return” Does Not Mean the Messengers Once Shared the Creed**

*Taʿūdunna* looks like “you shall return.” Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Kalbi, and al-Zamakhsharī warn against hearing that as proof that the messengers had earlier been idolaters in the full sense the town means. In Arabic usage, *ʿāda ilā* can mean to take up a state, to come to a creed, not only to go back to one previously held. Shuʿayb's answer in 7:89 makes the other case explicit: we would be fabricating a lie if we returned to your faith after God saved us from it. Some messengers were raised inside a pagan town and then saved. The verb still does not grant the town's story that prophecy is a phase and the old gods are home.

What the town wants is clear either way: stop the new speech, join the public worship we know. The land is *our* land in their mouth. Ownership of soil is used as ownership of worship. Verse 2 has already said that whatever is in the heavens and on the earth belongs to Allah. The threat is therefore a claim against a prior Owner. They speak as landlords of a street that is not theirs.

**The Lord Answers Before the Messengers Bargain**

*So their Lord revealed to them, “We will surely destroy the wrongdoers”* (14:13). The messengers do not have to design a reply to exile. Revelation arrives. The speaker of the destruction is *We*, God, and the object is *the wrongdoers* — *al-ẓālimīn* — those who have put the message, the land, and the threat in the wrong places. Al-Qushayrī reads the line as a promise of help against enemies. *“Our Word has already gone forth to Our servants, the messengers, that they would surely be helped, and that Our forces will certainly prevail”* (37:171–173). *“Allah has decreed, ‘I and My messengers will certainly prevail’”* (58:21). 14:13 is that decree arriving inside a local crisis.

The speed of the turn is part of the teaching. Threat, then revelation. The sūrah does not linger on the messengers' fear. It does not need to. Verse 12 already promised patience. This verse shows what patience is waiting on: a word from their Lord that the wrongdoers will not keep the land they are using as a weapon.

**Wrongdoing Here Is the Whole Stance, Not Only the Sentence**

*Ẓulm* is putting a thing in the wrong place. Expelling a messenger from a land God owns, in order to protect gods God did not send, is *ẓulm* in that exact sense. The people of verse 3 who prefer this life, hinder the Way, and make it look crooked are the same figure. The threat of exile is the hindering made municipal. Destruction is then not a random disaster. It is the Owner taking the land back from tenants who used it to silence His speech.

The verse ends on a comma in the translation, because the next verse will add the other half of the promise: you will dwell in the land after them. This verse's own load is the destruction. It should be heard before the inheritance is heard. God does not first gift a house and then happen to remove the old occupants. He names the occupants as wrongdoers and says He will destroy them. The moral sequence stays clean.

**Exile Is the Town's Last Theological Argument**

There is a reason this threat is so common in the prophet stories. A message that speaks about the Originator of the heavens and the earth cannot be confined as a private hobby. It changes the town's public life. The town, loving this world, will always try to make the map smaller: leave, or become us again. The Lord's reply makes the map larger: the land you call yours is the stage of a destruction I will perform. The argument over residence becomes an argument over who actually rules the soil.

Mecca heard this while the Prophet ﷺ and his companions were still in a town that would later expel them. The verse does not need a footnote to do its work. A people plotting exile are already inside a story whose next sentence, in God's mouth, is destruction of the wrongdoers. Whether that destruction is a battle, a famine, a later opening of the town, or the Last Day is God's to time. The verdict is not in doubt. The landlords have been named. The Owner has spoken.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:14

> and make you reside in the land after them. This is for whoever is in awe of standing before Me and fears My warning.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inheritance Is the Second Half of the Same Sentence**

The destruction of the wrongdoers in verse 13 is not an empty lot left to the wind. God continues: and make you reside in the land after them. Al-Baghawī takes *after them* as after the wrongdoers have perished. Al-Nīsābūrī and al-Qushayrī take the residents as the messengers and those who followed them, inheriting land and homes. The same transfer is stated of Israel: *“And ˹so˺ We made the oppressed people successors of the eastern and western lands, which We had showered with blessings”* (7:137). Exile was the town's threat. Residence after the tyrants is God's counter-threat, which is also a promise.

The land in dispute does not change hands by the messengers' military genius. *Nuskinannakum* is God's verb: We will make you dwell. The people who said *our land* lose the pronoun. The people who were told to leave keep the soil by a different title — not ancestry, not force, but God's placing. That placing is as certain in the Arabic as the destruction. Both clauses carry the energetic nūn. Both are *We* sentences. The town's bargain — leave or revert — is replaced by a bargain the town was not offering: they fall, you dwell.

**The Promise Has a Named Audience**

*This is for whoever is in awe of standing before Me and fears My warning.* The inheritance is not a tribal jackpot. It is for a kind of person. *Maqāmī* — My Station, My standing — is read in more than one way, and the ways stack rather than cancel. Ibn ʿAjībah and al-Rāzī take it as the standing of a human before God on the Day of Judgment, as in *“And whoever is in awe of standing before their Lord will have two Gardens”* (55:46) and *“those who were in awe of standing before their Lord and restrained themselves from ˹evil˺ desires”* (79:40). Ibn ʿAjībah also allows God's awareness of what people do, secret and open, and the greatness of God's own majesty. Al-Baghawī takes *My warning* as the punishment of the next life.

Awe of the standing and fear of the warning are the inner conditions of people who can be trusted with a land. A people who wanted the land as a tool to silence prophecy cannot be the heirs. A people who live as if they will stand before God can. The verse therefore turns a political outcome — who lives where — into a spiritual criterion. Soil follows fear of God, not the other way around.

**Fear Here Is Not Panic**

Arabic *khāfa* in this line is the fear that restrains, the awe that keeps a person from treating God's warning as theatre. It is the opposite of the town's fear, which was fear of a new creed in the street. One fear expels messengers. The other fear inherits the street after the expellers are gone. The sūrah has been training that second fear since verse 2's woe and verse 7's severe punishment. This verse says what that fear is for, in this world: it qualifies a people to remain when a wrongdoing people is removed.

The pairing with patience and trust in verses 11–12 is tight. Those who will bear harm, who will not fake a sign, who will not revert, are the same people who stand in awe of God's station. Their patience is not a tactic for seizing houses. It is a stance before God that God then rewards with houses. Mix the order and you get a piety that uses the next life as a slogan for land-hunger. Keep the order and land is a by-product of awe.

**After Them Means History, Not a Metaphor Only**

Some readers, uncomfortable with God promising real soil, rush to make the dwelling purely spiritual. The verse will not help them. *The land* in a sentence about expulsion from *our land* is the same kind of land. Noah's story, ʿĀd's story, Thamūd's story, Israel's story after Pharaoh — all of them include empty towns and people who walk into them. 14:14 belongs to that series. The Day of Judgment remains the deeper standing. It does not cancel the nearer one: a people destroyed, a people made to reside.

That historical sense is also a warning to the heirs. *After them* means the previous people were residents too, until they were not. Awe of the standing is not a one-time ticket. It is the condition that must last, or the new residents become the next *them*. The sūrah will later show ungrateful people leading their own into ruin. Inheritance without the fear named here is just a delay before another destruction.

**The Verse Completes the Public Argument**

Messengers were told they were only human. They were told to bring a sign. They were told to leave or revert. God has now said, in their hearing and in the town's future, that the wrongdoers will be destroyed and the fearing will dwell. The public argument is over, even if the town does not know it yet. *Tawakkul* has something to rest on that is not a feeling: a revealed promise about land and about the kind of person land is for.

Mecca, again, is inside the address. The Prophet ﷺ is not told the date. He is told the pattern. Standing before God, fearing the warning, remaining under harm — those are the terms. Residence after the wrongdoers is God's to schedule. The next verse will say that both sides sought a judgment and that the stubborn tyrant failed. This verse has already named the winner's qualification. It is not stubbornness. It is awe.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:15

> And both sides called for judgment, so every stubborn tyrant was doomed

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Call for Judgment Is Mutual, the Outcome Is Not**

*Istiftāḥ* is to ask for a victory, a decision, an opening that will show who is right. The translation says both sides called for judgment. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Kalbi, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ṭabarī take the seekers, in the first instance, as the messengers asking God for help against their people, as Shuʿayb does: *“Our Lord! Decide between us and our people in truth, and You are the best of those who decide”* — the sense of 7:89. The disbelievers, in their own way, had also asked for a showdown: bring a proof, or we expel you. When both sides ask for a decision, God gives one. The decision is not a draw.

*So every stubborn tyrant was doomed.* *Jabbār ʿanīd* is the tyrant who compels, the one who stubbornly pushes back against the truth. Failure here is not a mood. It is the end of his plot: the destruction promised in verse 13, the loss of the land in verse 14, the Hell that the next verses will open. Al-Qushayrī says the messengers' request was answered and their people were destroyed. Al-Bayḍāwī says God aided the messengers and withheld success from those who acted haughtily. The verse compresses a long history — Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd, and the unnamed rest — into one grammar: they asked, the tyrant failed.

**Stubbornness Is a Kind of Strength That Does Not Survive the Decision**

A *jabbār* looks strong in the town. He owns the gate, the threat of exile, the ancestral gods, the ability to hurt. *ʿAnīd* adds the refusal to turn when the proofs have come. That combination is impressive until judgment is actually given. Then it is the definition of the one who fails. The sūrah has been building this figure since verse 3: preference for this life, hindering, bending the Way. Verse 15 gives him a name and a result.

The word *doomed* in the translation, and *failed* in older ones, both point to *khāba*: to lose, to be disappointed, to miss what was hoped for. The tyrant hoped to keep the land and the creed by force. He misses both. The messengers hoped for a decision from their Lord. They receive it. Same request for opening; two different contents of hope. The verse is cruel only to the hope that was built on wrongdoing.

**Judgment in This World Does Not Cancel the Next**

Some of the openings this verse points to are historical: a flood, a wind, a cry, a drowning, a later inheritance of houses. Those are real. The next two verses will not let anyone think that the tyrant's failure is only political. Hell is still beyond him. Pus is still his drink. Death will come from every side and will not finish him. Worldly doom is the first page. It is not the book.

That two-stage failure matters for readers who treat every delayed tyrant as a counter-example. A *jabbār* who still holds a gate is not a successful man in this verse's terms. He has not yet met the judgment both sides asked for, or he has met only the first instalment. *“Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them”* (3:178). Delay is not the opposite of *khāba*. Delay can be the long road toward it.

**Both Sides Called, So No One Can Say the Decision Was Unasked**

The mutuality of the request is a moral lock. The disbelievers wanted a compelling proof, a contest, a clearing of the street. They got a clearing. The messengers wanted God to decide. He decided. A people who dare God to act, then complain when He acts, have no standing. Badr, which some reports attach to verses 28–29 of this sūrah, is one later instance of a people who asked for a decision and received one they did not like. The pattern is already here, before any Madinan field.

Shuʿayb's prayer in 7:89 remains the cleanest model of the messengers' side: decide between us in truth; You are the best of deciders. They do not ask for a decision that merely favours their party. They ask for truth. The tyrant's failure is then not a tribal win. It is what truth does to stubborn compulsion when it is finally allowed to speak.

**The Verse Is a Hinge, Not a Summary to Rest On**

Everything from verse 9 to verse 14 has been conversation and threat and promise. Verse 15 is the hinge that turns talk into result. After it, the sūrah will not return to the tyrant's arguments. It will describe his drink, his death that will not die, his deeds as ash. The hinge should be read slowly. Both sides called. Every stubborn tyrant failed. If that sentence is true, then the work of the previous verses — trust, patience, refusal to fake a sign, refusal to revert — is not a noble way to lose. It is the way to be on the side that does not fail when the opening comes.

A preacher who uses this verse only to gloat over dead nations has missed the living addressee. The *jabbār ʿanīd* is a type still available. The call for judgment is still a prayer the oppressed can make. The failure of the tyrant is still God's to time. What the verse forbids is despair on the messengers' side and confidence on the side that owns the gate. The gate is not the decision. The decision has another Owner. Until that Owner speaks, patience looks like losing. After He speaks, the same patience looks like the only side that asked for truth rather than for a quieter street. The verse is written so that no one who has heard it can treat a locked gate as a theology.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:16

> Awaiting them is Hell, and they will be left to drink oozing pus

**Expanded Commentary**

**Hell Is Placed in Front of the Tyrant, Not Behind a Fog**

The stubborn tyrant of verse 15 is the subject al-Ṭabarī assigns to these next lines. *Min warāʾihi jahannam* can be read as “beyond him lies Hell” — after death — or as “before him lies Hell,” as if he already stands on its edge. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī allow both. The translation's *awaiting them* keeps the future without losing the nearness. Hell is not a rumour for this man. It is the next room. Worldly failure was the first doom. This is the one that does not end when the land changes hands.

The shift from history to the Fire is abrupt on purpose. A reader who wanted the story to stop at inherited houses is not given that courtesy. The sūrah's warning has always had two ranges: days of God in this world, and a torment called severe in verse 2. Verse 16 begins to show the second range in the tyrant's own body.

**The Drink Is Named so That Luxury Can Be Heard as a Lie**

*He will be given to drink of oozing pus.* *Māʾ ṣadīd* is not water. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabarī take it as a mix of blood and pus. Al-Baghawī and al-Zamakhsharī say it oozes from the bodies of the people of Hell. The verb is passive: he will be left to drink, he will be given. Choice is over. The man who used a town's water, wine, and milk as proofs that he was favoured is now on a different ration.

The Book repeats this drink so that no one treats it as a one-off metaphor. *“Let them then taste this: boiling water and ˹oozing˺ pus”* (38:57). *“except boiling water and ˹oozing˺ pus”* (78:25). The pairing of boiling water and pus is the hospitality of Hell: heat that does not clean, liquid that does not quench. 14:16 gives the pus its own line, then the next verse will slow down the swallowing. The sūrah wants the drink to be seen, not scanned.

**A Tyrant's Mouth Is the Right Place to Begin the Punishment**

He threatened with his mouth. He demanded proofs with his mouth. He named the land *ours* with his mouth. The first detailed torment is therefore a drink he cannot refuse and cannot enjoy. The justice is not cartoon symmetry, but it is not random either. A life of taking what he wanted from the earth ends in a liquid the earth would not have offered him. *Ṣadīd* is what a body leaks when it is ruined. He is made to take that in as food.

Al-Qurṭubī, on the next verse, will note the bitterness and heat that make him gulp. This verse only serves the cup. Serving is enough. The stubborn man who would not take the messengers' invitation to forgiveness is made to take this. The invitation of verse 10 — forgive your sins, delay you to a term — is the opposite hospitality. He refused the first table. He does not get to refuse the second.

**Awaiting Is a Present Tense for a Future that Has Already Been Decided**

*Awaiting them* in English can sound like a queue that might be cancelled. In the Arabic picture, Hell is already there, in front or beyond, as a fact of the man's path. The decision of verse 15 has sorted him. What remains is arrival. That is why the sūrah can speak of Hell as lying *warāʾahu* while he is still, in the story, a tyrant in a town. The end is so sure it can be described as geography around him.

This also explains why delay in the world is not mercy for him in the sense he thinks. Every extra day of gate-owning is a day he walks toward a drink already poured. *Istidrāj* — being drawn on by gifts — is the hidden twin of this verse. The pus is not a change of God's mind. It is the destination of a mind that would not change.

**The Line Is Short Because the Next Line Will Not Let You Look Away**

Sixteen is almost only a placing and a cup. The grammar is spare: Hell ahead; pus to drink. Spare is not kind. It is the style of a sentence that will be unpacked by the choking, the death from every side, and the refusal of death in verse 17. A writer who padded this verse with extra images would be competing with the next one. The right work here is to let Hell be in front, and to let the drink be named, and to know whose cup it is: every stubborn tyrant who asked for a judgment and then failed it.

People who prefer this life, as verse 3 said, live as if the mouth's pleasures were the real harvest. This verse answers that harvest with a liquid that is harvest too — the harvest of bodies in Fire. The far astray of verse 3 and the failure of verse 15 meet in a cup. There is nothing left to argue. There is only what he will be given to drink.

The cup also answers the ancestral worship of verse 10. Those fathers were praised as givers of meaning and of feast. Hell's feast is the truth of a table set without God. No idol ever poured this, and no idol can take it away. The drink is from the Lord the tyrant would not take as Originator. That is why the verse can be so short in its naming and so long in its effect. Once the liquid is identified, every other luxury in the story is shown as a loan that has come due.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:17

> which they will sip with difficulty, and can hardly swallow. Death will overwhelm them from every side, yet they will not ˹be able to˺ die. Awaiting them still is harsher torment

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sip Is Work, and the Work Does Not Refresh**

The pus of the previous verse is now swallowed in slow motion. They sip it with difficulty and can hardly swallow. Al-Qurṭubī hears gulping forced by bitterness and heat. Al-Bayḍāwī hears choking. The mouth that would not take a clear message is now a mouth at war with a liquid. Sipping is usually a sign of control, of tasting. Here it is the opposite: tiny intakes because a full swallow will not go down, and because the stuff will not be left undrunk.

The difficulty is part of the punishment, not a delay of it. A drink that went down easily would be a lesser cruelty. This one occupies the throat. Time in Hell is often pictured as fire on the skin. This sūrah insists on the inside of the neck. The tyrant is not only around the Fire. He is trying to get a mouthful of it past his own body, and his own body will not cooperate.

**Death Comes From Every Side and Is Then Refused**

*Death will overwhelm them from every side, yet they will not be able to die.* Al-Rāzī takes this as every part of the body being hit with the pains that normally end a life, without the ending. Al-Bayḍāwī notes that death would have been a rest. The rest is withheld. *“I wish death was the end!”* (69:27) is the cry of someone given his record in the left hand. 14:17 is that wish refused in advance. Overwhelmed by death, not granted death: the man is kept at the border where dying would be a mercy, and the border does not open.

*From every side* blocks the hope of a painless corner. A body in this world can turn away from a blow. The verse says the blows complete the circle. Skin, gut, throat, heart — the next life of the tyrant is a full surround. *“Whenever their skin is burnt completely, We will replace it so they will ˹constantly˺ taste the punishment”* (4:56). Replacement of skin and refusal of death are two ways of saying the same thing: the punishment is not allowed to finish the person, because finishing would end the tasting.

**Harsher Torment After This Is a Door, Not a Ceiling**

*Awaiting them still is harsher torment.* After Hell in front, after pus, after choking, after death from every side without dying, the verse says there is worse. The mind wants a maximum. The Book will not give one that the tyrant can budget for. *Ghalīẓ* is thick, harsh, dense. The next instalment is not a repeat. It is heavier. Some commentators take this as a move from one level of the Fire to a deeper one. Some take it as the endlessness itself, which is harsher than any single scene. The verse does not draw the diagram. It says the line you have just heard is not the last line.

That refusal of a last line is itself part of the justice. The stubborn man lived as if there were no last line to his own harm of others. He expelled, killed, covered gifts, bent the Way. His punishment is built as a series that does not announce its end. *Awaiting* returns from verse 16. Hell was awaiting. Harsher torment is still awaiting. Arrival is never arrival in this sequence. It is always a step into more.

**The Body Is the Tyrant's Last Kingdom, and It Revolts**

He was a *jabbār*, a compeller of other bodies. Now his own throat will not obey, his own death will not complete, his own sides are all enemy. The political reading is not a stretch. A man who treated people as things to be moved now cannot move the smallest swallow. The sūrah does not need to spell out the irony. It only needs to stay with the body long enough for the irony to be felt.

This is also why the earlier offers — forgiveness, delay to a term, a land for those who fear the standing — were mercy in a stricter sense than comfort-language admits. They were exits from this sequence. A term appointed in verse 10 would have been an ordinary death, a door. The death in verse 17 is a crowd of deaths with no door. Choosing the invitation was choosing a death that ends. Refusing it is choosing a death that cannot.

**Nothing Here Is Theatre for the Saved to Enjoy**

The detail is for warning, not for spectacle. A believer who reads the sipping and the surround of death as entertainment has joined the wrong crowd. The sūrah placed these lines after a long argument that could still have been heard in time: Originator, forgiveness, term, trust, patience, awe of the standing. The Fire-writing is what the argument costs if it is refused. It is also what the Owner of verse 2 meant by a severe torment. Severity now has a throat, a circle of death, and a still harsher wait.

The patient and the grateful of verse 5 were said to see signs in God's days. This day, the Day this tyrant has entered, is a sign too, but it is a sign with no benefit left for him. The benefit is for the living reader who can still swallow water, still die an ordinary death, still take the invitation that this man did not take. The verse should send that reader back to verse 10, not forward into curiosity about the next grade of heat.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:18

> The parable of the deeds of those who disbelieve in their Lord is that of ashes fiercely blown away by wind on a stormy day. They will gain nothing from what they have earned. That is ˹truly˺ the farthest one can stray

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Parable After the Fire, Not Instead of It**

The tyrant's drink has been named. Now the sūrah turns to what his life's work was worth. The deeds of those who disbelieve in their Lord are like ashes blown hard on a stormy day. Al-Rāzī says the picture is total: wind on a storm day leaves no trace of the ash. So disbelief nullifies the labour. The parable is not a softer ending after Hell. It is the account-book of the same people. Fire was what they meet. Ash is what they bring.

*Deeds* here can be heard in three ways, and al-Rāzī lists them. Their good acts — charity, feeding, courage — become ash because they were not built on faith in the Lord. Their evil acts — idol-worship, blocking the Way — were ash from the start. Or both together, a whole life's pile, grey and light, ready for the first real wind. The third reading is the most honest. A disbelieving life is not empty of effort. It is empty of weight. *“It is they who reject the signs of their Lord and their meeting with Him, rendering their deeds void, so We will not give their deeds any weight on Judgment Day”* (18:105). No weight. Ash.

**Storm Wind Is the Day, Not a Random Weather**

The verse specifies a stormy day, a fierce blowing. This is not a kitchen draught. It is the Day when secrets are tried, or the collapse of a life when its false ground is removed. Ash on a calm morning might still be swept into a pan and kept. Ash in a gale is gone. The people of verse 3 preferred this world. This world is the place of winds. Nothing they stacked for it survives the weather God sends against it.

*They will gain nothing from what they have earned.* Al-Bayḍāwī takes this as no trace of heavenly reward. Al-Ṭūsī says they will not benefit even from the good they did. Earning (*kasb*) is the Qur'an's word for what a soul truly owns of its acts. Here the earning is real as effort and worthless as outcome. That is a harder sentence than “they did nothing.” They did. They will not have it. *“The deeds of those who deny Our signs and the meeting ˹with Allah˺ in the Hereafter will be in vain. Will they be rewarded except for what they have done?”* (7:147). What they have done, once disbelief has voided it, is the vain thing 7:147 and 14:18 both name.

**Farthest Stray Returns as a Measured Distance**

*That is truly the farthest one can stray.* Verse 3 already said they have gone far astray. This verse says what far means: labour that cannot be collected, a path whose end is a gale over ash. Al-Bayḍāwī reads extreme error as the farthest distance from the path of truth. Al-Rāzī adds the grief of watching the labour go. Distance and regret are two faces of the same farness. You can walk a long way in this world, fill a life with projects, and still be, in the only map this sūrah trusts, at maximum error.

The parable also answers a boast. Disbelievers in Mecca had deeds they were proud of: feeding pilgrims, keeping oaths inside the tribe, courage in raids. The verse does not say those acts never happened. It says they have the weight of ash once the Lord is denied. Pride in ownerless virtue is the storm's fuel. The wind does not argue with the ash. It removes it.

**Ashes Were Something Before They Were Ash**

The image assumes a prior burning. Deeds become ash because they were spent in a fire that did not purify — the fire of showing off, of idol-service, of this-world preference. Or they become ash at the Judgment, when the Lord's wind tests them. Either way, ash is a remainder of a fire, not a thing that grew as ash. The people had a life. The parable is cruel because it grants the life and then grants it no harvest. *“Those who disbelieve and hinder ˹others˺ from the Way of Allah, He will render their deeds void”* (47:1). Voiding is a verb that needs a deed to void.

That is why repentance in time still matters, and why this verse is spoken while hearers can still attach their labour to the Lord. Ash is the picture of a finished case. A living person who believes and does good is not in this parable. He is in verse 23's gardens, which this stretch has not yet reached. The warning is: do not let your earning be the kind of earning a storm owns.

**Nothing From What They Earned Is the Economic Close of Preference**

Verse 3 began with preferring this life over the next. Verse 18 closes the account of that preference. You favoured the near life; you earned for it; you will gain nothing from the earning. The far stray is not only a spiritual mood. It is a total loss on the only day that audits. Gardens, later, will be the opposite economics: deeds that remain, rivers that do not blow away. The sūrah is not against earning. It is against earning that has no Lord.

A patient, grateful reader, the kind verse 5 named, can look at this parable and check his own pile. What in it would survive a stormy day? What was done for a face that dies, a town that expels prophets, a father-worship that cannot face the Originator? Those questions are the lawful use of other people's ash. The unlawful use is to assume the gale is only for them. The wind in the verse is God's. It knows every pile.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:19

> Have you not seen that Allah created the heavens and the earth for a reason? If He wills, He can eliminate you and produce a new creation

**Expanded Commentary**

**Creation Is Not a Hobby, and the Proof Is in Front of the Eyes**

*Have you not seen* is the Qur'an's way of pointing at a fact already available. Allah created the heavens and the earth *bi-l-ḥaqq* — in truth, for a reason, not as a game. Al-Māwardī says He did not make them falsely or in vain, but for an exalted purpose. *“He created the heavens and the earth for a purpose”* (64:3). *“He is the One Who created the heavens and the earth in truth”* (6:73). *“Did you then think that We had created you without purpose, and that you would never be returned to Us?”* (23:115). 14:19 stands in that line. Sky and soil are evidence that the story has a point, and that the point includes a return.

The stubborn tyrant lived as if the world were a stage for his gate. Ash in the previous verse was his labour. This verse is the set he stood on. The set has an Author, and the Author had a reason. A world made *in truth* cannot be the final home of a life made of preference and blocking. It is a moral world. It will not keep a tyrant as its meaning.

**Eliminate You Is a Power Already Proven by Making You**

*If He wills, He can eliminate you and produce a new creation.* The people who treated land as theirs are told they are removable. Al-Bayḍāwī reads it as making the present creation nonexistent and bringing another in its place, because He is the Creator of the heavens and the earth. The same warning is almost a refrain. *“If it is His Will, He can remove you altogether, O humanity, and replace you with others”* (4:133). *“If He wills, He can do away with you and replace you with whoever He wills, just as He produced you from the offspring of other people”* (6:133). *“If He willed, He could eliminate you and produce a new creation”* (35:16). 14:19 is not a new threat. It is the Creator's freedom restated after a parable of worthless deeds.

*You* in this verse can be the disbelievers just described, or the human species as a whole. Both readings work. A people can be removed, as ʿĀd was removed. A kind can be replaced, as this kind was produced from earlier wombs. The inheritance of verse 14 is the small version of the same act: one group out, another made to dwell. The new creation is the large version. Neither is hard, which the next verse will say. This verse only needs the seeing: the One who made the sky can unmake the people under it.

**Reason in Creation Blocks Two Lies at Once**

If the world were pointless, ash would be no tragedy and Hell would be a melodrama. If the world were a closed machine with no Author, elimination and replacement would be empty talk. *For a reason* defeats both. There is an Author. He had a purpose. That purpose can include removing a people who used the world against Him. The verse is therefore comfort for the messengers and terror for the *jabbār*. Comfort: the stage is not owned by the man who currently shouts on it. Terror: the reason of creation is not your survival.

Seeing here is not a vision in sleep. It is the ordinary look at sky and earth, joined to the news this Book has been reciting. Anyone who can see a heaven can be asked this question. The failure to see is the same failure as putting hands over mouths in verse 9. Evidence is public. The will to treat it as decoration is the stray.

**A New Creation Is Easy to Say and Heavy to Hear**

Listeners like continuity. They want their children to inherit their gate, their name, their gods. *A new creation* is the end of that wish. God is not bound to this cast of characters. The patience of the messengers is partly patience for that freedom of God to act. They do not need the present town to be eternal. They need the present town to be judged in truth, which is how the heavens and the earth were made.

The phrase also looks toward the resurrection, which is another new creation of the same people, not a different species. Both senses — replacement by others, and reconstitution of the dead — live in the wording. The first is the historical threat. The second is the standing of verse 14's *maqām*. A person who fears that standing does not need to be told that God can make a new creation. He already lives as if he will be made again and questioned. A person who does not fear it needs the historical threat first: you can be removed before that Day, and still not escape it.

**The Question Is Addressed to a Prophet Who Can See, and Through Him to Those Who Will Not**

*Have you not seen* is singular in the Arabic, often aimed at the Prophet ﷺ. He has seen. The recitation makes the seeing public. Those who preferred this world are being told that the world they preferred is a created thing with a reason, and that they are not a necessary part of its reason. If that sentence is believed, exile threats shrink. If it is not believed, the next verse's ease of replacement will sound like rhetoric. It is not rhetoric. It is the same power that started the heavens, applied to a people who thought they were the heavens' point.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:20

> And that is not difficult for Allah ˹at all˺

**Expanded Commentary**

**Difficulty Is a Human Word, and It Does Not Apply**

The previous verse named a huge act: eliminate you, produce a new creation. This verse removes the last cushion a hearer might hide in — the thought that such an act would strain the Doer. *That is not difficult for Allah at all.* Al-Bayḍāwī: it is not hard or impossible. It is easy, because He is All-Powerful by nature. Arabic *ʿazīz* in this construction is the thing that overpowers you, the thing you cannot manage. God's acts are not in that class. Making a people vanish and raising another is, for Him, in the same category as the rain He already sends.

The shortness of the verse is the point. A long defence of God's power would suggest that the power needed defending. One clause is enough. The heavens and the earth in verse 19 were the evidence. This line is the conclusion. If He made those, He does not pant when He replaces you.

**Easy for Him Is Not Small for You**

People hear *not difficult* and think the threatened act must therefore be casual, almost trivial, not worth fear. The verse means the opposite. Because it is not difficult, there is no technical reason it should not happen. A hard task might be delayed by the worker's limits. An easy task is delayed only by the worker's will. God's will, in this sūrah, has already been named as the thing that guides and leaves to stray, that grants signs or withholds them, that destroys wrongdoers and seats others in the land. None of that was difficult. Neither is a new creation.

So the comfort some take from delay — “if He could replace us He would have” — is a misreading of ease. Ease plus delay is a test, the same *balāʾ* Moses named, the same extra time 3:178 warned against. Difficulty is not what is holding the door shut. Forbearance is. Forbearance is not inability.

**The Line Ties Power to the Names Already Used**

Verse 1 called Him the Almighty, the Praiseworthy. Verse 4 called Him the Almighty, All-Wise. Verse 8 called Him Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy. A God with those names cannot find a new creation *ʿazīz* against Himself. Might that struggled would not be the might of verse 1. Wisdom that could not replace a people would not be the wisdom that split guidance from straying. Self-sufficiency that needed this particular cast of humans would not be the self-sufficiency Moses taught. Verse 20 is those names in a single practical sentence.

Al-Māwardī's remark that He is All-Powerful by nature belongs here. Power as a nature, not as a mood, means there is no day on which replacement would be harder than it is today. The tyrant's health, the town's walls, the number of sons — none of these register as resistance in the only scale that matters.

**A New Creation Includes the Day No One Votes On**

The resurrection is the new creation every denier has to face, whether or not his people are replaced in history. *Not difficult* therefore also answers the old mockery: can rotten bones be raised? If eliminating a living nation and producing another is not hard, returning the same nation from dust is not hard. The verse does not pause to argue biology. It places the whole file — death, replacement, raising — inside God's ease.

That is why the next scene, when it comes, can open with everyone appearing before Allah. Appearance is not a logistical problem. It is a decided fact waiting on a time. The stubborn man's *how* questions are already out of court. The only live question is the one verse 14 named: are you in awe of the standing, or are you the people who will be removed to make room for those who are?

**The Verse Should End the Argument About Capability**

After this line, a debate about whether God *can* is finished. Debates about whether He *will*, and when, and for whom, remain as matters of fear and hope. The sūrah will go on to the appearance of all, the appeal of followers to leaders, Satan's speech, gardens, good trees and bad trees. Those are about moral outcome. They are not about a God who might find the outcome technically awkward. 14:20 is the small lock on that door.

For the Prophet ﷺ reciting it in Mecca, the sentence is also a quiet. The town is loud about its permanence. The Book answers with seven English words and a pair of brackets. Permanence is not in the town's gift. It is not even in the town's power to make permanence difficult for God. Nothing they have is difficult for Him to end, and nothing He has promised is difficult for Him to begin. The ash of verse 18, the removal of verse 19, the Hell of verses 16–17 — all of them sit inside this ease. That is the fear. It is also the ground of the messengers' trust.

Ease of this kind also explains why signs were never the messengers' to dispatch. If replacement of a people is not hard, then a proof in the sky is not hard either, and withholding it is not weakness. Permission in verse 11 and ease in verse 20 are one doctrine. God does what He wills, without strain, and He does not do what He does not will, without that refusal being a lack. The town that asked for a compelling proof was asking as if God were a craftsman on a deadline. This verse says the craftsman is not tired. The deadline is not theirs to set. When He replaces, it will not be because He finally gathered enough strength. It will be because the reason for which He made the heavens has reached the next act.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:21

> They will all appear before Allah, and the lowly ˹followers˺ will appeal to the arrogant ˹leaders˺, “We were your ˹dedicated˺ followers, so will you ˹then˺ protect us from Allah’s torment in any way?” They will reply, “Had Allah guided us, we would have guided you. ˹Now˺ it is all the same for us whether we suffer patiently or impatiently, there is no escape for us.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Appearance Together Ends the Old Hierarchy**

They will all appear before Allah. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Ṭūsī take the appearing as the coming forth from graves on the Day. *All together* is the first shock. Leaders and led arrive in one crowd. The land that was *ours*, the gate, the threat of exile — none of that sorts the line. Verse 19 said God can eliminate a people and produce a new creation. Verse 21 is the other gathering: the same people, remade, standing. Appearance is not a debate about bones. It is a public fact the sūrah has already called not difficult.

The lowly then speak to the arrogant. The words are social words from the old world — we were your dedicated followers — used now as a claim for protection. Followership was the currency of the town. It is offered to the Fire as if the Fire took that currency. The question *will you then protect us from Allah’s torment in any way* already knows the answer in its *any way*. Even a portion would do. They are bargaining for a shield the leaders never owned.

**The Leaders’ First Sentence Is a Theology They Did Not Preach**

*Had Allah guided us, we would have guided you.* On earth they spoke as if guidance were their product: our faith, our fathers, our land. Here they tell the truth they would not tell. Guidance is God's. Without it they had nothing to give. Al-Qurṭubī reads the line as: had we been guided to faith, we would have guided you. The past tense is a confession that the whole career of leadership was unguided. It is also a refusal of blame that still sounds like blame of God. They are not wrong that guidance is God's to give. They are wrong if they mean that their own blocking, preference, and threats were not theirs.

The sūrah has already held both sides of that. Verse 4: God leaves to stray and guides whom He wills. Verse 3: they favoured this life, hindered, made the Way crooked. The leaders now quote the first and bury the second. A true sentence about God's will is being used as a last piece of management. The followers asked for protection. They are given a lecture.

**Patience and Panic Are Equal Once the Door Is Shut**

*Now it is all the same for us whether we suffer patiently or impatiently, there is no escape for us.* Patience was a virtue in verses 5 and 12. Here it is named only to be emptied. After the judgment, patience does not earn, and impatience does not hasten an exit. Al-Qurṭubī takes *no refuge* as no way to put distance between themselves and the Fire. The leaders cannot save the followers because they cannot save themselves. The skill that made them leaders — talking, threatening, arranging — has no object left.

This is the wider scene of *“when they will dispute in the Fire, and the lowly ˹followers˺ will appeal to the arrogant ˹leaders˺, ‘We were your ˹dedicated˺ followers, will you then shield us from a portion of the Fire?’ The arrogant will say, ‘We are all in it! ˹For˺ Allah has already passed judgment over ˹His˺ servants’”* (40:47–48). 14:21 is the same court with a slightly earlier script: not yet “we are all in it,” but already “no escape.” The judgment has made the old chain of command a shared ruin.

**Followership Will Be Audited as a Deed**

The lowly are not innocent by rank. They were dedicated. Dedication is an act. *“Every soul will be detained for what it has done”* (74:38). Choosing a leader who blocked the Way is one of the things done. The appeal *we were your followers* tries to move the file from the self to the chief. The chief cannot take it. Satan, in the next verse, will refuse it too. The sūrah is closing every transfer window. No leader, no devil, no father will carry what a soul earned.

That is why the verse spends so many words on a conversation that changes nothing. The point of the conversation is that it changes nothing. People who lived by following need to hear, while they can still change whom they follow, that the following will not be a shield. The time to leave a bad chief is before the appearing.

**No Escape Is the End of the Town’s Map**

The town thought in maps: our land, your expulsion, the road out. *No escape* is the end of maps. The Fire is not a country you can leave by another gate. The leaders say *for us*, including themselves in the trap they used to administer. That pronoun is the only honest thing they have said as a group. They are not *we the chiefs* any longer. They are *we the trapped*.

A hearer still in the world should not take that *we* as a reason to despair of God's guidance. The leaders' *had Allah guided us* is true as theology and false as excuse. Guidance is still being offered in the Book that recites this scene. The appearing has not yet happened. The lowly can still stop being dedicated to the arrogant. The arrogant can still stop waxing. Verse 21 is a future conversation. Its use is to prevent it.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:22

> And Satan will say ˹to his followers˺ after the judgment has been passed, “Indeed, Allah has made you a true promise. I too made you a promise, but I failed you. I did not have any authority over you. I only called you, and you responded to me. So do not blame me; blame yourselves. I cannot save you, nor can you save me. Indeed, I denounce your previous association of me with Allah ˹in loyalty˺. Surely the wrongdoers will suffer a painful punishment.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**He Speaks After the File Is Closed**

Satan's speech is timed. Al-Baghawī and al-Kalbi take *after the judgment has been passed* as the moment when people are already placed in the Garden or the Fire. He does not speak while a deal might still be struck. He speaks when speech is only exposure. The leaders have just failed the followers. Now the older leader — the one behind the leaders — fails them all.

He begins with a true sentence: Allah has made you a true promise. The promise is resurrection, account, reward and punishment, the whole of what this sūrah has been saying. Then he names his own promise and admits it failed. Al-Ṭabrisī takes *failed you* as *I lied*. Al-Bayḍāwī takes Satan's promise as the claim that there would be no rising, or that idols would intercede if there were. *“Satan only makes them ˹false˺ promises and deludes them with ˹empty˺ hopes. Truly Satan promises them nothing but delusion”* (4:120). Verse 22 is 4:120 from the liar's own mouth, too late to help anyone who believed him.

**Authority Was Never His, and That Is the Trap He Named in Advance**

*I did not have any authority over you. I only called you, and you responded to me.* Al-Ṭabrisī: I had no power to force you to disbelieve; I had whispering and calling. Islamic teaching holds that the self has inner and outer tempters, and that none of them compel. *Waswasah* is suggestion. Response is the human act. *“from the evil of the lurking whisperer”* (114:4) is a prayer against a whisperer, not against a possessor who moves the limbs without the will. Satan here tells the truth he hid in the world: the chain was call, then response. The response was theirs.

That is why *do not blame me; blame yourselves* is legally correct and morally vicious. Correct, because *“Every soul will be detained for what it has done”* (74:38), and because the messengers had already brought clear proofs (Ibn Kathīr). Vicious, because the caller now abandons the called in the same breath. He used them. He will not carry them. *“I have absolutely nothing to do with you. I truly fear Allah—the Lord of all worlds”* (59:16) is the same desertion in another picture. At Badr he promised to be at their side, then cowered: *“I have absolutely nothing to do with you”* (8:48). Verse 22 is that cowering made final.

**Neither Can Save the Other**

*I cannot save you, nor can you save me.* The old partnership is dissolved into two helpless sides. Followers of Satan often treat him as a power who will at least share the cost. He refuses the share. They cannot help him either, which strips the last fantasy — that a crowd of wrongdoers is a kind of army in the Fire. There is no army. There is a caller who cannot answer a cry, and a crowd who cannot answer his.

Ibn Kathīr reads the cries as the mutual call for rescue that will not be met. Abū al-Futūḥ notes that each is taken to task for his own wrong. The sūrah has now closed three doors: leaders cannot shield followers (v. 21), Satan cannot shield his clients, clients cannot shield Satan. The only remaining shelter was the one offered in verse 10: God's invitation to forgive and to delay to a term. That door is behind them.

**He Denounces the Partnership They Ascribed**

*Indeed, I denounce your previous association of me with Allah in loyalty.* Al-Ālūsī takes *aforetime* as this world: Satan already rejected being treated as a partner. Al-Ṭabrisī allows a reading that points back to his refusal to bow to Adam, a disbelief older than their worship of him. Al-Rāzī hears him saying he had already rejected God. The pastoral sense does not need the dispute settled. What the crowd did — giving Satan a loyalty that belongs to God — is something Satan himself now spits out. Their religion was not even shared by the one it served.

That denunciation is the last unkindness. People who associated him wanted a patron. The patron's final public act is to call their association disbelief and to announce a painful punishment for the wrongdoers. He agrees with the verdict against them. He does not repent. He testifies.

**The Painful Punishment Is Named by the Enemy**

*Surely the wrongdoers will suffer a painful punishment.* Coming from Satan, the sentence has a strange authority. He is not warning. He is agreeing with the court. The *ẓālimūn* of verses 13–14, the tyrant of verse 15, the drinkers of pus, the people of ash — he files them under one name and one end. A hearer who would not take this from a prophet is being made to take it from the devil. If even he says the promise of God was true and his own was a failure, the argument about whose word stands is over.

The verse is long because the speech is a full unmasking: true promise, false promise, no authority, only a call, blame yourselves, no mutual rescue, denunciation, punishment. Nothing in it is a new law. Everything in it is the law the sūrah already taught, now spoken by the one who spent a life denying it. That is the humiliation of the wrongdoers, and the warning of the living. The call is still only a call. The response is still yours.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:23

> Those who believe and do good will be admitted into Gardens, under which rivers flow—to stay there forever by the Will of their Lord—where they will be greeted with “Peace!”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Other Crowd Is Named at Last**

After tyrants, pus, ash, failed leaders, and Satan's speech, the sūrah names the other group in one breath: those who believe and do good. Faith and work are not two optional tracks. They are the pair that enters. *Yudkhalūna* is passive: they will be admitted. Entry is given, not stormed. Gardens with rivers under them are the Qur'an's standing picture of a life that does not blow away. Ash was the disbelievers' earning. Rivers are this earning's place. The contrast is not subtle, and it is not meant to be.

*To stay there forever* answers the death that would not die in verse 17. That was endless harm. This is endless stay. Duration itself is not the gift; duration of the right thing is. A Fire that does not end and a Garden that does not end are both forever. They are not the same forever. The verse will not let anyone hear *khālidīn* as a mere clock. It is a home.

**Leave Returns, Now as the Door of the Garden**

*By the Will of their Lord* — *bi-idhni rabbihim* — is the same leave that stood in verse 1 between the Prophet's leading and the people's light. Entry into gardens is not a wage God is forced to pay by the workers' hours. It is admission by permission of the Lord they believed. The grammar protects tawḥīd at the moment of reward. Good deeds matter. They do not coerce. The people of verse 8 were told that a whole earth of ingratitude would not touch God's self-sufficiency. A whole earth of faith would not make Him a debtor either. He admits whom He wills, and He has willed to admit those who believe and do good.

That leave also ties this verse back to the messengers' *it is not for us to bring a proof without Allah’s permission*. The same God whose permission governs signs governs gardens. The believers who trusted in verses 11–12 are now seen at the end of that trust. They did not trust a mood. They trusted a Lord whose leave actually opens places.

**Peace Is the Greeting, and It Is a World**

They will be greeted with “Peace!” *Salām* is the word, the state, and the name of the home. *“in which their prayer will be, ‘Glory be to You, O Allah!’ and their greeting will be, ‘Peace!’”* (10:10). *“Their greeting on the Day they meet Him will be, ‘Peace!’”* (33:44). *“And ‘Peace!’ will be ˹their˺ greeting from the Merciful Lord”* (36:58). *“When they arrive at its ˹already˺ open gates, its keepers will say, ‘Peace be upon you! You have done well, so come in, to stay forever’”* (39:73). *“There they will never hear any idle talk—only ˹greetings of˺ peace”* (19:62). 14:23 is a short member of that family. After Satan's speech, which was all blame and desertion, the first word of the Garden is a word that does not accuse.

Peace here is not a truce between enemies. The enemies are not in this place. It is the end of the harm verse 12 promised to endure. Messengers said they would bear whatever hurt the town caused. The greeting of peace is that hurt's opposite, spoken as welcome. *“Allah invites ˹all˺ to the Home of Peace”* (10:25) was the invitation. This verse is arrival.

**Believe and Do Good Is the Opposite of the Whole Previous Portrait**

The disbelievers preferred this life, hindered, bent the Way, put hands over mouths, demanded signs, threatened exile, drank pus in the picture, found their deeds ash. Believe-and-do-good is not a vague niceness set against that list. It is faith in the Lord of verse 2, and work that will not become ash because it is done for Him. The parable of the good tree is about to make that work visible as fruit. This verse states the destination before the parable, so that the tree is not an aesthetic. It is a tree whose people end in gardens.

Rivers under gardens also answer the drink of verses 16–17. Liquid in this sūrah has been a test of hospitality. Pus was the tyrant's cup. Rivers are the believers'. The Book does not need to say “unlike the pus.” The sequence says it.

**Forever by Leave, Greeted by Peace — the Two Clauses Need Each Other**

Stay without peace would be a locked room. Peace without stay would be a visit. The verse gives both, and puts God's will between them as the cause. A piety that talks only of peace in this world, without the forever of the next, has half the sentence. A piety that talks only of endless gardens as a private villa, without the greeting that makes them a community under God, has the other half. The people admitted are greeted. They are not dumped in a landscape. Someone says *Peace* to them — angels, the Lord, one another, all of these in the wider Book. 14:23 lets the word stand alone so it can fill the place.

The living reader is still on the side of the sentence where admission has not yet happened. Believe, do good, trust the leave of the Lord, endure the harm. The greeting is ahead. Satan's speech is ahead too, for the other crowd. The verse exists to make the choice of crowd look as stark as it is: ash and pus and no escape, or gardens, rivers, forever, peace. No one is admitted by accident, and no one who is admitted is left unwelcomed.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:24

> Do you not see how Allah compares a good word to a good tree? Its root is firm and its branches reach the sky

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Parable That Asks Whether You Can See**

*Do you not see how Allah compares* is the same pointing as verse 19's *have you not seen*, now aimed at a likeness rather than at the sky. A good word is as a good tree: root firm, branches in the sky. The seeing required is not botany. It is the ability to recognise a true word by the way it is planted and the way it rises. Al-Qurṭubī takes the good word as the shahādah, *there is no god but God*. That reading has the strength of the next verses, which will speak of fruit by the Lord's leave and of God making believers firm with the firm word. A word that is only noise does not get a root. This one does.

The tree is not named in the verse. A well-known report fills the name without trapping the parable in one species. Ibn ʿUmar said the Prophet ﷺ asked about a tree whose leaves do not fall, which is like a Muslim; Ibn ʿUmar thought of the date palm and was too shy, as the youngest, to speak; the Prophet ﷺ then said it is the date palm (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 61; also 5448). Date palms hold the soil of Arabia with a deep tap, keep their green, and feed people year after year. The report is a key, not a lock. A good word could be pictured by any tree that does what this verse says. The date palm is the tree the first hearers would have walked past on the way home.

**Firm Root Is the Unseen Half of Speech**

A word is usually judged by sound. This parable judges it by root. *Aṣluhā thābit* — its root is firm, fixed, not rocking. Ibn ʿAjībah says the meaning of the shahādah is rooted in the hearts of the people of tawḥīd as the palm is rooted in earth. Al-Rāzī speaks of a tree of knowledge planted in the land of the heart, growing stronger, sending fruit out of branches that reach the Divine realm. Those are later unfoldings of a simple claim: a good word has a below. What you cannot see of it is what keeps it from being weather.

The people of verse 3 had words too — crooked ones, ancestral ones, threats. Those words will get their tree in verse 26, and it will have no stability. The difference is not volume. The difference is whether the word is fastened in a Lord who does not move. Firm root is faith that has gone into the unseen and stayed. A slogan has no root. It has a season.

**Branches in the Sky Are the Public Half**

The same tree that hides its root shows its branches. They reach the sky. A good word does not stay a private murmur. It rises: prayer, teaching, a life that can be seen, a knowledge that aims above the town. Al-Rāzī's “branches in the sky” as forms of knowledge that reach the Divine realm is one reading of that rise. Another is simply the height of a palm against an Arabian horizon — obvious, shading, unashamed. The parable needs both halves. Root without branch is a buried seed that never confessed. Branch without root is the bad tree's first cousin, tall for a while.

Verse 1's path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy, is this height in another image. The word leads up. The people who preferred this world grew out, not up. Their labour was ash in a horizontal wind. This tree's labour, the next verse will say, is fruit in every season. Height is for bearing, not for show.

**Allah Is the One Who Sets the Likeness**

The verse does not say that people invented a clever comparison. *Allah compares.* Parables in this Book are His teaching method, which verse 25 will state. That matters for anyone who treats likenesses as decoration. If God sets a word beside a tree, then words have the kind of reality trees have: they can be planted or ripped, they can feed or poison, they can last a century or a day. Speech is not a free zone above creation. It is inside creation's laws, under the same Lord.

*Do you not see* also rebukes a blindness that can recite the shahādah and still live as if words were cheap. Seeing the parable would mean inspecting one's own word for root and height. The inspection is the point. The date palm in the ḥadīth is like a Muslim, not like a sentence on a wall. The good word is a person standing.

**Sky-reaching Does Not Mean the Root Has Left the Ground**

A mistake of later piety is to love the branches — states, claims, lofty talk — and neglect the firmness below. The verse names root first. Another mistake is to love the root as a secret and refuse the sky, as if tawḥīd were safer unspoken. The verse names branches second, and they reach. The good tree is both planted and public. Moses' reminder, the messengers' trust, Abraham's prayer still ahead — those are branched words, visible, costly. They grew from a root the town could not see and could not uproot.

The next verse will add fruit and leave and the purpose of parables. This verse's own work is the stance of the tree: fastened, rising. A reader who has that picture has what 14:24 came to give. The word that will not blow away in the storm of verse 18 has already been shown. It looks like a tree that weather does not own.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:25

> ˹always˺ yielding its fruit in every season by the Will of its Lord. This is how Allah sets forth parables for the people, so perhaps they will be mindful

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fruit Is What the Firm Tree Is For**

The good tree of the previous verse now bears. It yields fruit in every season by the Will of its Lord. A root and a sky-line would still be a monument. Fruit makes it a gift. Al-Rāzī reads the fruit as the ongoing produce of a soul rooted in knowledge: good words, right acts, humility, weeping, lowliness — not a single harvest. *In every season* refuses the religion of moods. A tree that fruits only in one weather is not this tree. The believer whose word is good is not useful only in ease, or only in Ramadan, or only when the town is kind. The seasons turn. The yielding does not stop.

*By the Will of its Lord* — again *bi-idhni rabbihā* — keeps the fruit from becoming a human boast. Palms fruit because God permits the crop. Hearts fruit because God permits the state. The leave that admitted people to gardens in verse 23 is the leave that puts dates on a tree in verse 25. Same Lord, same permission, two pictures. Take the permission out and you have a technique of self-improvement. Leave it in and you have worship that produces.

**Every Season Is a Claim Against the Storm**

Verse 18's ash blew on a stormy day. This tree fruits through seasons, including storm seasons. The two pictures are meant to quarrel. Labour without faith is a pile for the wind. A good word with a firm root is a plant the wind feeds by the Lord's leave. *Every season* also means the fruit is not stored from a past golden age. Ancestral worship, the people's shield in verse 10, was a claim that the old crop still counted. This verse says the tree now standing is the one that feeds. A dead parent-tree with no present fruit is not the likeness God set.

Ibn ʿUmar's date palm, already cited from Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 61, is a tree that in Arabia can be counted on when other plants fail. That reliability is part of why the Prophet ﷺ likened a Muslim to it. The likeness is moral and economic at once. People live off palms. People should be able to live off the good word in a believer — shade, food, a standing thing in a flat land. If they cannot, the root may not be as firm as the tongue said.

**Parables Are Set so That People Might Remember**

*This is how Allah sets forth parables for the people, so perhaps they will be mindful.* The *how* points back at the tree. God teaches by likeness because likenesses can be walked around in the mind. A legal sentence about the shahādah can be nodded at. A tree with fruit in every season can be tested against a life. *Laʿallahum yatazakkarūn* — perhaps they will remember, take heed — is hope with no guarantee. Verse 4 already said that clarifying does not force guidance. Parables are a form of clarifying. Some will see. Some will not.

*For the people* keeps the teaching public. The parable is not an esoteric riddle for a club. Date palms were not esoteric. Anyone could ask what a good word is like, look at a tree, and be put to work. Later, dense readings — tree of knowledge, branches in the Divine realm — are allowed as fruit of the parable, not as a lock on it. The verse's own purpose clause is modest: that they may remember. Remember the good word. Remember the Lord of the leave. Remember that fruit is the test.

**Mindfulness Here Is Memory That Changes a Season**

*Tadhakkur* is not a footnote in the heart. It is the act of bringing a truth back until it alters the next thing you say. A person who hears the parable and then returns to crooked speech has not remembered. A person who hears it and inspects his year's crop — what words, what acts, what seasons of silence — has. The sūrah's earlier command to Israel was *remember Allah’s favour*. This command is *remember* by means of a tree. Both memories are meant to produce thanks and patience, the two eyes of verse 5.

Perhaps they will be mindful also explains why the sūrah did not stop at Hell's drink. Terror alone can freeze. A tree that fruits can draw. The Book uses both. Pus was for the tyrant already doomed in the picture. Fruit is for the living who can still plant a word.

**The Lord's Leave Makes Every Crop a Prayer**

If fruit comes only by permission, then asking for fruit is part of tending the tree. Abraham will soon ask for safety, for freedom from idols, for hearts that incline, for fruit in a barren valley. That prayer is this verse in a human mouth. He wants a good word planted in a dry place, and he knows the crop is God's to give. Readers who treat 14:25 as a pretty image and then skip Abraham's asking have separated what the sūrah joins. The parable teaches the law of the crop. The prayer applies it.

Yielding in every season, then, is not a promise that the believer will feel plenty. It is a description of a word God has allowed to keep producing, including in the season of harm the messengers promised to bear. Dates in a drought are still dates. Good speech in a hostile town is still fruit. The leave of the Lord is the only irrigation the verse names. That is enough for a parable whose aim is remembrance, not a farming manual.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:26

> And the parable of an evil word is that of an evil tree, uprooted from the earth, having no stability

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Second Tree Is Not a Lesser Version of the First**

An evil word gets its own likeness. It is not a good tree with less fruit. It is an evil tree, uprooted from the earth, with no stability. Al-Kalbi takes the evil word as the statement of disbelief, the opposite of the shahādah. The pairing is exact. One word is planted. The other is ripped. One has a firm root. The other has none that holds. The sūrah will not let anyone treat all speech as the same stuff in different volumes. Words belong to two species.

Ibn ʿAjībah names the evil tree as the *ḥanẓalah*, the colocynth or bitter apple, a desert plant whose roots sit shallow and whose fruit is harsh. Al-Kalbi contrasts it with the date palm's deep fix. Whether or not the verse requires that species, the botany of the sentence is clear: this tree's relation to the earth is already a failure. *Uprooted* in the Arabic is a past picture — *ijtuththat* — torn out, as if the ripping has already happened. Instability is not a risk. It is the state.

**No Stability Is the Truth of a Word That Has No Lord**

*Mā lahā min qarār* — it has no stability, no resting place, no decision to stay. The good word rested in a Lord who does not move. The evil word rests in a town, a father, a mood, a threat. Towns change. Fathers die. Moods pass. Threats meet verse 15. So the word that seemed loud in the gate has no *qarār*. Ash in a storm was the same news in another image. Here the image is a plant that never had a right to the soil it occupied.

That is why ancestral worship, for all its claim of age, is unstable in this parable's terms. Age of a custom is not depth of a root. A custom can be old and still sit on the surface, ready for the first real pull. The messengers' word looked new and had the deeper fix, because its root was the Originator of the heavens and the earth, not the memory of a tribe. Newness and oldness are the town's categories. Planted and uprooted are God's.

**Uprooted From the Earth Also Means It Does Not Feed the Earth**

A palm gives shade and food to the ground it occupies. A bitter shallow plant takes space and gives a fruit no one can live on. The evil word works like that in a people. It occupies mouths. It does not nourish. It can even poison the water of a town's meaning, as Pharaoh's word did, as the Quraysh's mockery did. When it is torn out — by a destruction in history, or by the Judgment, or by a person who finally recants — the earth is not poorer. Verse 14's new residents inherit a land whose bad tree has been pulled.

The passive *uprooted* matters. The evil word does not politely leave. It is extracted. God is the unseen gardener of both parables. He plants by leave. He pulls by will. *“Allah leaves whoever He wills to stray and guides whoever He wills”* (14:4) is that gardening in another sentence. The bad tree's lack of stability is not an accident of soil. It is the end of a word that would not be rooted in Him.

**The Parable Judges Propaganda as Well as Creed**

Verse 3's people strove to make the Way look crooked. That striving is an evil word at work: lies about the path, jokes about the messenger, threats dressed as civic duty. Such speech can look vigorous, even woody, for a season. This verse says to wait for the pull. Shallow roots can hold a plant in still air. The sūrah has already promised wind. A preacher who envies the reach of bad speech has forgotten which tree has *qarār*. Reach without root is the definition of the thing that will not stay.

The colocynth also fruits, but the fruit is a warning. Not every yield is the yield of verse 25. A bad word produces too: followers, wealth in a crooked market, a quiet street after the prophet is expelled. Then the crop is shown to be bitter, and the plant is shown to be loose. *Having no stability* is the last audit of that crop.

**Two Trees, Two Ends, One Seeing**

Verse 24 asked if you see. Verse 26 is the other half of the seeing. A person who only contemplates the good palm has half the parable and a sweet taste. A person who only stares at the ripped plant has fear without a place to plant. The sūrah gives both, in order, so that a life can be compared to each. Which word do you repeat? Which one has gone into the ground of the heart? Which one would survive a pull?

God making believers firm with the firm word is the good tree's root described as His act, and His leaving of wrongdoers to stray is the bad tree's looseness described as His act. This verse already leans that way. Instability is not only a botanical accident. It is what becomes of a word God does not make firm. The living still have a word they can take, and a word they can drop. The uprooted tree is the picture of the second choice completed. A town can chant an evil word until the chant feels like earth; the parable says the earth never accepted it. The pull, when it comes, will not be a surprise to the tree. It was always loose.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:27

> Allah makes the believers steadfast with the firm Word ˹of faith˺ in this worldly life and the Hereafter. And Allah leaves the wrongdoers to stray. For Allah does what He wills

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Firm Word Is God's Act in the Believer, Not a Slogan the Believer Holds Up**

The good tree had a firm root. This verse names who makes the root firm. Allah makes the believers steadfast with the firm Word in this worldly life and the Hereafter. Al-Nīsābūrī takes the firm word as the full shahādah: there is no god but God, and Muhammad is the Messenger of God. Al-Bayḍāwī says it protects faith from the trials of this world, and in the next life it makes them firm when the questioning of the grave begins. The subject of the verb is God. Steadfastness is not a personality type. It is a making.

Al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: when a Muslim is questioned in his grave he will testify that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah and that Muhammad is Allah's Messenger, and that is what is meant by this statement (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4699). The Prophet ﷺ himself tied the Hereafter half of the verse to the grave. The worldly half is every trial before that: threat of exile, crooked speech around you, the preference of this life pulling at the heart. Same word, two times of need. God uses it in both.

**This Life and the Next Are One Exam With Two Rooms**

People split religion into a public life and a secret afterlife. This verse will not split the word that holds a person. The shahādah that keeps you from reverting under a town's threat is the shahādah you will need when two questioners sit you up. If it is only a password for the grave, it will not have been a life. If it is only a social badge in the street, it will fail when the street is gone. *In this worldly life and the Hereafter* is one prepositional run for that reason.

*“So whoever renounces false gods and believes in Allah has certainly grasped the firmest, unfailing hand-hold”* (2:256). The hand-hold and the firm word are kin. Both are tawḥīd as something that can be gripped, not as a mist. 14:27 adds that the grip is God's making. 2:256 adds that the grip requires a renouncing. Together they describe a person who has dropped the evil word of verse 26 and been made to hold the good one of verse 24.

**Wrongdoers Are Left to Stray by the Same Will**

*And Allah leaves the wrongdoers to stray.* The pairing is the pairing of verse 4, now applied after the two trees. Believers are made firm. Wrongdoers are left. Al-Bayḍāwī's comments on leading astray in the wider Book belong here: it is not a coin toss. It is the wise will that meets a person who has chosen the shallow plant. *“Whoever Allah guides is truly guided. And whoever He leaves to stray, you will find no guardians for them besides Him”* (17:97). The leaders of verse 21 said *had Allah guided us*. This verse agrees that guidance is His, and it names the other side of the same sentence without letting it become an excuse. Wrongdoers are *ẓālimūn* — people who put things in the wrong place — before they are people who are left.

*For Allah does what He wills* closes the file on anyone who wanted a God who must firm every tongue that recites, or who must leave no one. He does what He wills. The names of verses 1 and 4 — Mighty, Praiseworthy, Wise — are how that will is to be heard. Not as caprice. As the freedom of the Owner of verse 2.

**Grave Questioning Makes the Verse Practical**

If the Prophet ﷺ said this line is the Muslim answering in the grave, then preparing for this verse is preparing a word that is true enough to be said when no audience remains. Munkar and Nakīr, named in other reports as the two questioners, ask about Lord, religion, and man sent. The answers are not information. They are the fruit of a tree that fruited in every season. A person who only borrowed the town's answers will find, as the lowly found with their leaders, that the loan is called in and the lender is gone.

This-world firmness is the rehearsal. Prayer, spending, patience under harm, refusal of the evil word — those are how a word becomes *thābit* before the grave asks for it. God makes it firm; the believer lives inside that making. The verse does not describe a trance. It describes a life that can stand two weathers.

**The Will at the End Is Comfort and Warning Together**

*Allah does what He wills* is comfort to the messengers who could not force a sign and could not force a heart. Their Lord is not blocked by a town. It is warning to the town that treated its own will as the last fact. A people who exchange blessing for ingratitude, as the next verses will say, are not operating a machine God cannot stop. He does what He wills. He has already said He makes some firm and leaves some to stray.

The good tree, the bad tree, the firm word, the leaving — these four images are one doctrine in four pictures. Speech is real. God governs its standing. The living still speak. Which word they plant is the only work this verse leaves in their hands. The making and the leaving stay in His. A mouth can recite tonight; the making is asked for in every trial and in the grave that will not take a borrowed answer.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:28

> Have you not seen those ˹disbelievers˺ who meet Allah’s favours with ingratitude and lead their own people to their doom

**Expanded Commentary**

**Seeing Here Is Seeing a Swap**

*Have you not seen those who meet Allah’s favours with ingratitude* — more literally, who exchange the blessing of God for *kufr*. Al-Jalālayn: they swapped thanks for covering. The verb is a trade. Blessing went out of their hands as if it were coin, and ingratitude came in as if it were goods. Verse 7 had promised increase for thanks and severity for covering. This verse points at people who took the second deal and then, worse, took their people with them.

Al-Baghawī and al-Jalālayn take the first referents as the disbelievers of Quraysh. On that reading the blessing is the Prophet ﷺ himself, sent among them, a Book to lead out of darkness. They met that favour by calling him names, by blocking the Way, by the threat of exile that verses 13–14 already treated as a type. Ibn Kathīr links the doom they caused their people to Badr, where Quraysh led their own into a field and a grave. Al-Jalālayn allows a wider destruction they brought on their own. The verse can carry the particular and the general. A tribe that is given a messenger and then walks him to the edge of the town is exchanging blessing for *kufr* in the most exact sense.

**Leading Your Own to Doom Is a Special Crime**

They do not only ruin themselves. They *lead their own people to their doom*. The arrogant of verse 21 are already visible. Followers will later ask for a shield. Here the leading is still in process, in the world, toward an *abode of perdition* that verse 29 will name as Hell, and that history can also name as a battlefield. A chief who takes a people into unbelief is not a private sinner with a large family. He is a man using kinship as a rope toward the Fire.

The sūrah's whole argument about fathers is under this line. Forefathers were the shield in verse 10. Now living leaders use the same bond — *their people* — to walk a tribe into ruin. Blood is not a protection. It is a responsibility. Moses was told to lead his people out of darkness. These men lead theirs into an evil dwelling. The two leadings are the two trees in social form.

**Ingratitude After Favour Is the Sūrah's Central Sin**

From verse 5's reminder of God's days, through verse 7's proclamation, through Moses' statement that God is Self-Sufficient, the sūrah has been about thanks. 14:28 is the portrait of the ungrateful as a political animal, not only as a person who forgot to say *al-ḥamd*. He trades the blessing and then administers the trade for others. Quraysh had the House, safety in a hungry land, trade routes, and then a messenger from among themselves. Meeting all of that with mockery and with a call to stick with the fathers is the exchange this verse wants seen.

*Have you not seen* makes the seeing obligatory for the Prophet ﷺ and, through the recitation, for anyone who knows a people given much and turning it into a banner against God. The question is not curiosity. It is *look at them*. The doom is not only future. A people already walking toward Badr, or toward any field where unbelief will be paid in bodies, is already being led.

**Doom Has an Address, and the Next Verse Will Write It**

This verse ends on the leading, not yet on the fire. The pause is useful. Before Hell is named, the human act is named: exchange, then lead. No one arrives in the evil dwelling as a tourist. Someone traded, someone followed, someone led. The lowly of verse 21 will try to make that leading a defence. 14:28 already calls it a crime of the leaders *and* a path the people were led on. Both will be asked.

Reports that verses 28–29 are Madinan, after Badr, fit the particular reading without locking the verse. A Meccan recitation already had the type: ʿĀd's chiefs, Pharaoh, the arrogant of every town in verses 9–15. Badr would then be one more day of God, the kind Moses was told to remind of — a day when a people who exchanged blessing met the severity verse 7 named. Whether the two verses dropped after that field or before it, the moral grammar is the same.

**The Blessing They Exchanged Was Not Small Enough to Excuse the Trade**

If the blessing is the Prophet ﷺ, the trade is the largest available. If the blessing is the cluster of gifts verses 32–34 will list — rain, fruit, ships, rivers, sun, moon, day, night, everything asked for — the trade is still insane. You do not swap a sky full of provision for a word that has no root. You do not swap a messenger for a quieter street. The verse's *have you not seen* assumes that the insanity is visible. What hides it is the habit of calling the trade “our way” or “what our fathers did.” This line strips those names and leaves the transaction: favour out, ingratitude in, people marched toward ruin.

A reader who leads anyone — a house, a circle, a platform — is inside the warning. Leading is not neutral. It either takes people out of darkness, which was the work of verse 1 and verse 5, or it takes them to an abode the next verse will call evil. There is no third destination for a leader in this sūrah. The exchange of blessing is already a leading; the only question is toward which house.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:29

> In Hell they will burn. What an evil place for settlement

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Abode Has a Name Now**

Verse 28 ended on doom. This verse writes the address: in Hell they will burn. Al-Nīsābūrī takes the abode of perdition as this Hell. Ibn Kathīr's Badr reading does not cancel that. A field can be the door. The house is still *jahannam*. *Yaṣlawnahā* is they will roast in it, they will heat themselves at it as if it were a fire they approached and then could not leave. The leaders who led their people, and the people who were led, meet in one oven. Verse 21 already said there is no escape. Verse 29 says what the inescapable place is like to live in.

*What an evil place for settlement.* *Bi'sa al-qarār* — what an evil stability, what a foul resting. The bad tree had no *qarār*. Hell has a *qarār*, and it is a cursed one. The word that meant “stability” for the good tree's opposite now means “dwelling” for the ungrateful. They wanted a settlement — a land, a creed, a street without prophets. They get a settlement. It is the wrong one.

**Burning Is the End of the Exchange**

They exchanged blessing for ingratitude. The return on that trade is fire. Verse 7 said the punishment for covering a gift is severe. Severity here is not a simile. It is combustion of the people who led and were led. The hospitality of Hell has already been shown as pus and a death that will not complete. Burning is the more common word, placed now against *settlement* so that no one hears Hell as a passing storm. You settle in a place you do not leave. *Khalidīn* was used for gardens. This *qarār* is the other forever, spoken as a dwelling so the mind will not treat it as weather.

Quraysh loved settlements: Mecca as a safe hollow, summer and winter journeys, a House that drew tribes. *Evil place for settlement* is a direct insult to that love. You wanted a home that lasts. This is the home that lasts if you trade the blessing. Badr, on the historical reading, sent some of them there quickly. The verse does not need the date to work. Any people who lead their own into covering God's favour are already choosing this real estate.

**An Evil Dwelling Answers the Threat of Exile**

The disbelievers told messengers: we will expel you from our land. God told messengers: we will make you reside after them. Now the ungrateful are given their residence. The map of verses 13–14 is completed for the losing side. They keep a place. It is Hell. The sarcasm is God's, not a commentator's. Settlement was their idol. Settlement is their sentence.

*Bi'sa* is a word of dispraise used in the Book for the worst outcomes. It does not argue. It judges. A listener still able to change settlements is the only person for whom the dispraise is useful. The people already burning are not being persuaded. The recitation is for those who still have a House, a messenger, rain, fruit, and a choice about thanks.

**Leaders and Led Share the Oven, Not the Excuse**

The grammar of 28–29 does not split the burning. *They* who exchanged and *they* who were led to doom are the *they* who burn. Later, in the Fire, they will split into lowly and arrogant for a conversation that changes nothing. This verse keeps them together. Shared settlement is the truth under the later quarrel. You can argue about who led. You cannot argue about where the leading arrived.

That togetherness is a warning to followers who think Hell is a chiefs' problem. It is a warning to chiefs who think numbers will cool a fire. *We are all in it*, the arrogant will say in 40:48. 14:29 says it without their mouth.

**The Verse Is Short Because the House Needs No Tour**

A long description here would compete with verses 16–17, which already gave drink and unending death, and with verses 49–50, which will give chains and tar. This line only needs the name, the burning, and the dispraise of the dwelling. Settlement is the key word. Everything in the sūrah about land, exile, inheritance, trees rooted or ripped, comes to rest on two *qarārs*: the Garden's peace and this evil one. Choosing a word, a leader, a thanks or a covering, is choosing which settlement you are walking toward.

Moses told a rescued people that a whole earth of ingratitude would not touch God. This verse tells the ungrateful what ingratitude touches. It touches them. It seats them. It burns. The blessing they traded is not in the house with them. That is the poverty of the settlement, worse even than the heat: a home with no favour left in it, only the fire that favour becomes when it is covered.

Settlement also mocks the patience of verse 17. There, death came from every side and would not finish. Here, the place itself is the unfinished thing: you arrive, you stay, the staying is the punishment. People in this world work for a *qarār* — a house, a name, a grave in a known plot. The verse says the work can succeed in the worst way. You can get a permanent place. Check which one you are buying with the trade of verse 28. The evil of the dwelling is not only heat. It is that the dwelling fits the life that chose it: a life that wanted a world without a prophet, and is given a world without peace.

The dispraise *what an evil place* is therefore not extra emotion on top of Hell. It is the moral name of a real estate the ungrateful already signed for when they led their people and covered their gifts. Signing was easy. Living there is the long cost. A hearer who still sits in a town with rain and a Book is not yet a resident of this *qarār*. He is a person being shown the contract. Burning is what the contract contains. He can still refuse the pen.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:30

> They set up equals to Allah to mislead ˹others˺ from His Way. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Enjoy yourselves! Surely your destination is the Fire.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Equals Are the Machinery of the Leading**

How did they lead their people to doom? They set up equals to Allah. *Andād* are rivals, likes, peers placed beside the One who has none. Idols of stone are one form. Chiefs, fathers, appetites, a town's way — anything given the loyalty verse 22 said belongs to God — are others. The purpose clause, *to mislead others from His Way*, is read by al-Bayḍāwī as result treated as purpose. They may not have sat down to design misguidance. Misguidance is what setting up equals does, so the Book can speak of it as the end they were aiming at. *“When one is touched with hardship, they cry out to their Lord… and set up equals to Allah to mislead ˹others˺ from His Way”* (39:8) uses the same structure. The equal is never a private ornament. It pulls a street off the path of verse 1.

The Way they bend is the Way they tried to make crooked in verse 3. Equals are the crookedness given a body. A people told to walk toward the Almighty, the Praiseworthy, are handed a second object and told it can share the walk. That second object is how leaders manufacture a creed that feels ancestral and still fights the Originator.

**The Prophet Is Told to Speak a Bitter Permission**

*Say, “Enjoy yourselves! Surely your destination is the Fire.”* Al-Bayḍāwī calls the first half ironic. It sounds like leave to persist. It is the opposite of a blessing. *“So enjoy yourselves, for you will soon know”* (16:55). *“So enjoy yourselves, for soon you will know”* (30:34). The enjoyment is the last of the *istidrāj* — gifts that feel like approval while they draw a person on. Verse 7's increase was for thanks. This enjoyment is the increase that is actually rope.

The destination is named in the same breath so that no hearer can take *enjoy* as good news. *The Fire* is the settlement of verse 29, the drink of 16, the surround of death in 17. The Prophet ﷺ does not have to invent a curse. He is ordered to recite a permission that is already a sentence. That order also protects him from a false kindness: watering down the end so that the town will like the preacher. The town's end is the Fire. Saying so is part of leading out of darkness.

**Equals Mislead Because They Split the Fear and the Thanks**

A heart that fears God and thanks God, the two traits of verse 5 and verse 14, cannot keep an equal. The equal takes some of the fear (this god might hurt us if we leave him) and some of the thanks (this god, or this chief, or this trade, is what feeds us). The Way of Allah needs both fear and thanks undivided. Setting up *andād* is therefore not an extra sin beside ingratitude. It is ingratitude's method. You cover the Giver by naming other givers.

Abraham will soon ask to be kept from idols because they have led many astray. This verse is that fact in the present tense, in Mecca. The equals are already up. The misleading is already the result. The only new thing is the command to the Prophet to tell them to enjoy the arrangement on the road to the Fire. Abraham's prayer will be the opposite speech: keep me and my children away. Both speeches assume that idols do real social work. They are not jokes. They are roads.

**Destination Is a Journey Word, and the Journey Has Already Started**

*Maṣīrakum* — your becoming, your arriving, your journey's end — is the Fire. They are not waiting to see whether they will start traveling. Preference of this life, hindering, equals, enjoyment: that is the traveling. The Fire is where that road is built to go. A preacher who treats Hell as a sudden hole at the end of an otherwise neutral life has not read *destination*. Destinations explain roads.

Enjoy yourselves, then, is also: keep walking. The irony is fierce because the walking feels like staying — staying with fathers, with the House as they have redefined it, with the quiet street. In the sūrah's map they are in motion toward a settlement already called evil. The command to say this out loud is a last mercy. Some who hear *Fire* as the end of their enjoyment might drop the equals. Some will hear only *enjoy* and take it as licence. Verse 4's split is still in force.

**The Line Returns the Argument to the Prophet's Mouth**

For several verses the sūrah has described them. Now it puts a sentence on the Prophet's tongue. *Say.* He is back in the work of verse 1: leading by speech. The speech is not a new Book. It is a verdict on a people who set up equals. After Satan's long unmasking and the two trees and the firm word, this is the pastoral application: tell them their joy is on a road that ends in fire. Do not bargain. Do not decorate the equals as culture. Name the destination.

A listener who has no carved idol is not therefore outside the verse. Equals can be a following, a pile of wealth, a self that will not be told. Whatever takes a share of God's Way is in the net. Enjoyment of that share, with the Fire as end, is the sentence. The next verse will tell the Prophet what to say to the other crowd: pray, spend, before a day with no bargaining and no friends. Two sayings, two crowds, one messenger. 14:30 is the saying that burns.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:31

> Tell My believing servants to establish prayer and donate from what We have provided for them—openly and secretly—before the arrival of a Day in which there will be no ransom or friendly connections

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Other Speech the Prophet Must Give**

After *enjoy yourselves, your destination is the Fire*, the Lord says *tell My believing servants*. Two messages, two audiences, one mouth. The servants are named as His, and as believing, before they are told what to do. Belonging comes first. Then work: establish prayer, donate from what We have provided, openly and secretly. The work is timed: before a Day with no ransom and no friendly connections. The Fire-crowd was told their end. This crowd is told their remaining time.

*Establish prayer* is not “have feelings of worship.” *Aqīmū* is set it up, make it stand, the same verb used for a thing that should not collapse. Prayer is the public and private root of the good word, the daily fruit of verse 25. Spending from provision is the fruit made economic. *What We have provided* keeps the money from becoming an equal. You give from a gift, not from a rival store of your own. Verse 34 will say you could not count the gifts. This verse says: move some of them now.

**Open and Secret Cuts Two Diseases**

Give openly and secretly. Open giving teaches a town and kills the hoarding that verse 3's love of this life produces. Secret giving kills the showing-off that would turn the gift into a statue of the giver. A religion that only gives in public becomes a market of reputations. A religion that only gives in secret can hide stinginess behind humility. The verse wants both weathers of the same tree. Seasons differ; fruit continues.

The provision is God's in both cases. That fact, once believed, makes open giving less proud and secret giving less fearful. You are not the source. You are the pipe. Moses has already said that a whole earth's covering would not impoverish God. Spending, then, is not a rescue of God. It is a rescue of the spender from the Day this verse is about to name.

**The Day Without Bargain and Without Friends**

*Before the arrival of a Day in which there will be no ransom or friendly connections.* Al-Qurṭubī's comment on the twin sentence in *“Spend from that which We have provided you before a day comes wherein there shall be neither bargaining, nor friendship, nor intercession”* (2:254) belongs here. Negotiation will not work. Friends will not work. The lowly asked the arrogant for a shield and were refused. Satan told his clients to blame themselves. This verse tells believers to act while shields and friends still look real, because they will not be real then.

*Ransom* is the rich man's hope: I will buy my way. *Friendly connections* is the well-placed man's hope: I will be known. Both hopes are the town's hopes. The Day dissolves the town as a market and as a clan. What remains is what was prayed and what was spent from provision. The good tree's fruit is the only currency that still spends. Ash is the rest.

**Before Is the Kindest Word in the Verse**

The command is not “pray and spend on the Day.” It is *before*. Time is still a mercy. The delay that is *istidrāj* for the ungrateful is, for these servants, a chance to plant. Establishing prayer takes years of days. Spending secretly takes a lifetime of unseen choices. The verse assumes a stretch of ordinary time and then puts a wall at the end of it. Live toward the wall. Do not live as if the wall were a rumour.

40:18, 43:67, 44:41, 70:10 all say in different ways that a friend will not be a friend on that Day, or that no one will be questioned about his friend. 14:31 puts that news under a practical order rather than under a scare alone. If friends will not help, be the kind of person whose file does not need that help. Pray. Give. Now.

**My Believing Servants Is a Title That Obliges**

He could have said “tell those who believe.” He said *My believing servants*. Servanthood is the opposite of setting up equals. A servant does not give God a peer. A servant prays, because that is what a servant does in front of a Lord, and spends, because the Lord's property is in his hand on trust. The title is honour and burden together. Honour: they are His. Burden: they are told to look like it before the Day strips every other look.

The Prophet ﷺ, in telling them, is still doing verse 1's work — leading out of darkness into light — now in the form of a timetable. Darkness here would be living as if ransom and friends will still work. Light is prayer and spending while they still can. The next verses will list the provision in detail, so that no one can say they did not know what “what We have provided” meant. Ships, rivers, sun, moon, day, night, everything asked for. 14:31 is the order to move some of that list toward God before the list can no longer be moved.

Openly and secretly also means the Day will audit both kinds. A public gift done for a face will not ransom. A hidden gift done for God will not need a friend to mention it. The verse is training a people to live without the two currencies that verse 21 and verse 22 already showed failing. Practice now, in prayer that needs no audience and in spending that can bear an audience or refuse one. When bargaining dies and friendships die, that practice is what a soul has left. The Lord who calls them *My servants* is the Lord who will still know the secret gift when every connection has gone quiet.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:32

> It is Allah Who created the heavens and the earth and sends down rain from the sky, causing fruits to grow as a provision for you. He has subjected the ships for your service, sailing through the sea by His command, and has subjected the rivers for you

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Provider Is Named Before the List Is Read**

*It is Allah Who* — the restriction matters. Not the fathers, not the equals, not the town's luck. He created the heavens and the earth, the same claim as verse 19, now turned toward food and travel. Rain from the sky, fruit as provision for you, ships subjected, rivers subjected. The people told to spend in verse 31 are being shown the warehouse. You cannot donate from what We provided if you think you provided it.

Fruit here is the earth's answer to the tree of verses 24–25. That tree fruited by the Lord's leave in a parable. These fruits grow by rain He sends, as provision, not as a parable. The two fruitings teach one Lord. A person who prays under a good word and eats dates from a palm is in the same economy. Both crops are *for you* and both are *from Him*. Ingratitude is eating while naming another giver.

**Rain Is the First Middle Term Between Sky and Table**

He sends down water from the sky, then brings forth fruits thereby. The chain is short and visible: cloud, rain, plant, mouth. Arabian hearers knew years when the chain broke. The verse does not argue a science. It assigns the chain. *For your provision* makes the assignment personal. The rain is not a neutral weather event that happens to help. It is provision, *rizq*, the same word as verse 31's “what We have provided.” Spending from rain's children is spending from a sky God opened.

Creation of heavens and earth in verse 19 was “for a reason.” This verse names one of the reasons as it touches human need: you eat. A tyrant who treats fruit as tribute to his gate has stolen the reason. A believer who eats and does not spend has stopped the reason halfway. The list is not for wonder as a dead end. It is for wonder that becomes prayer and giving.

**Ships Sail by His Command, Not by the Wood's Cleverness**

He has subjected the ships for your service, sailing through the sea by His command. Subjection (*sakhkhara*) is a strong verb: the thing is made to work for you. Ships are human-built, and the verse still will not give the builder the last credit. They run on a sea He holds, by a command He gives. *“Do you not see that the ships sail ˹smoothly˺ through the sea by the grace of Allah so that He may show you some of His signs? Surely in this are signs for whoever is steadfast, grateful”* (31:31). Patience and thanks, the eyes of 14:5, return as the way to read a hull on water.

Mecca lived off caravans more than hulls, but the sea was not foreign, and the point is not local industry. Travel, trade, the movement of provision from a place that has it to a place that needs it — all of that is subjected. A people who say “our journeys, our winter and summer” without *by His command* are already exchanging blessing. Quraysh's pride in safe routes sits under this line whether or not they boarded a ship.

**Rivers Are the Land's Version of the Same Mercy**

And He has subjected the rivers for you. Fresh water that stays, that can be led to a field, that carries a boat of another kind — all of it *for you*, all of it subjected. Abraham will soon settle his offspring in a valley without cultivation and ask for fruit. He knows this verse from the other end: a place with no river still eats if God inclines hearts and sends provision. The subjected river is the ordinary form of that miracle. The barren valley is the extraordinary form. Both refuse the idea that water is an idol's gift.

Subjection of river and ship also answers the stubborn tyrant's claim to land. If even the water through the land is subjected for people by God, then *our land* in verse 13 was already a false title. You do not own what is made to serve you by someone else's command. You are a user on leave. Verse 14's awe of the standing is the right stance for a user. Verse 30's enjoyment on the way to Fire is the wrong one.

**The List Has Only Started, and Already It Obliges**

Sun, moon, night, day, everything asked for, the uncountable blessing — those wait in the next two verses. This verse's own work is the big frame (heavens and earth) and the first practical mercies (rain, fruit, ships, rivers). It is enough to make the spending of verse 31 look small beside the stock. It is enough to make the equals of verse 30 look silly beside a sky that rains. It is enough to make Moses' reminder of one rescue look like a sample of a much longer bill.

*For you* is repeated because the sūrah will not let the gifts be abstract. They land on a people who can still thank or cover. The next verses will tighten the personal claim until counting fails. Here the claim is already a life: you eat fruit God grew with rain God sent, you move on water God subjected, and you were just told to pray and to give before a Day when none of this can be bargained. The warehouse is open. The clock is not.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:33

> He has ˹also˺ subjected for you the sun and the moon, both constantly orbiting, and has subjected the day and night for you

**Expanded Commentary**

**The List Rises From Water to Sky**

Ships and rivers were yesterday's scale. Now the sun and the moon, both constantly orbiting, and day and night. All of it *for you*, all of it subjected. *Taskhīr* is the same verb: made to serve. A person who can believe that a river is subjected and then refuse to believe it of the sun has split the world into a tame half and a wild half. This verse will not allow the split. The lights that run the calendar of farms, prayers, and caravans are in the same grammar as the hull on the sea.

*“And He has subjected for your benefit the day and the night, the sun and the moon. And the stars have been subjected by His command. Surely in this are signs for those who understand”* (16:12). 14:33 is that teaching without the stars and without the close about understanding. The close of this sūrah's list is not *for those who understand*. It is, in the next verse, mankind as wrongful and ungrateful. The signs are the same. The diagnosis of the viewer is harsher here.

**Constantly Orbiting Is a Mercy That Does Not Take a Day Off**

*Dāʾibayn* — both at work, constantly, without quitting. Sun and moon do not strike. Harvests depend on that refusal to strike. So do the prayer times, the month of the fast, the pilgrim's moon. A God who subjects two bodies to an unfailing circuit has already answered the fear that provision is a mood. The circuit is older than Quraysh and will outlast the tyrant of verse 15. *For you* does not mean “your property.” It means “your benefit,” the way a tool benefits a worker who does not own the forge.

Constancy also shames human worship. Idols do not orbit. Chiefs sleep. Equals of verse 30 have to be propped up with festivals. The sun does not. Abraham's later fear of idols is partly this contrast: things that cannot even move themselves are given a share beside the One who keeps two lamps on a path that never fails.

**Day and Night Are Subjected, so Time Is Not a God**

He has subjected the day and night for you. Time feels like the last untamed thing — it takes the young, it ends the term of verse 10. This verse will not make time a second deity. Day and night are servants in the same sentence as moon and sun. They serve work and rest, hunting and hiding, thanks and patience. *“He is the One Who created everything in the earth for you”* (2:29) is the wide claim. 14:33 is the claim as clock.

A people who prefer this life, as verse 3 did, treat day and night as the whole story. Subjected day and night are not the whole story. They are tools. Tools point past themselves to the One who subjected them. When night is only for the singer and day only for the market, the subjection has been received as loot and not as sign. Verse 5 wanted the patient and grateful to read days of God. These are also days of God, daily, not only the days of drowning and rescue.

**Custodian, Not Tyrant**

The source-teaching that humans are vicegerents, *“He is the One Who has placed you as successors on earth… so He may test you with what He has given you”* (6:165), belongs under *taskhīr*. What is subjected to you is a test, not a licence to burn the garden. The stubborn tyrant used land as a weapon against messengers. That is *taskhīr* inverted: instead of serving with what was made to serve you, you use it to fight the Maker. Hell's settlement is the end of that inversion. Gardens with rivers are the end of the right use.

Custodianship also blocks a later piety that treats nature as filthy. The sun is not filthy. It is subjected. Gratitude for it is part of the thanks verse 7 demanded. A believer who never looks at the circuit of moon and sun as a gift has a hole in his *shukr* the size of the sky.

**For You Does Not Mean the Sky Needs You**

Moses already said that a whole earth of covering would not touch God. Sun and moon would keep their circuit if every human vanished. *For you* is generosity, not need. That is why ungratefulness in the next verse is *ẓulm* — putting the gift in the wrong place — and not a wound in the sun. The lamps will still go to work tomorrow. The question is whether you will.

Abraham is about to ask for a secure city and for fruit in a barren valley. He asks because he has this verse in his bones: the big lights are already subjected; the small hollow of Mecca can be given a share of that subjection if God wills. Prayer for security and fruit is not a doubt about sun and moon. It is an application of *for you* to a specific family in a specific valley. The list of 32–33 is the general mercy. The prayer of 35–37 is the particular ask. Both assume that nothing in the sky or the clock is an equal. All of it is a servant, and the servants have one Lord.

Constancy of sun and moon is also a quiet teacher of verse 5's patience. A farmer who watches the circuit learns that provision has a rhythm not set by panic. A worshipper who times prayer by the same circuit learns that the day is already divided for him. Ungratefulness, in that light, is not only covering a fruit. It is treating an unfailing clock as scenery. The sūrah will not let scenery be the last word. Scenery is subjected. The next sentence will say that even what you asked for has been granted, and that counting will fail. The failure of counting starts here, with two lamps no one can inventory in benefits.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:34

> And He has granted you all that you asked Him for. If you tried to count Allah’s blessings, you would never be able to number them. Indeed humankind is truly unfair, ˹totally˺ ungrateful

**Expanded Commentary**

**Asked For, Given, and Still Not the Whole Store**

He has granted you all that you asked Him for. Al-Bayḍāwī reads the asking as tongues, or as the asking of need without words: a body that needs water “asks” by being thirsty. Al-Qurṭubī, because of the particle *mā*, allows “He gives you of what you do not ask,” sun and moon being gifts no infant requested. The translation's *all that you asked* should not be heard as “every whim.” Jābir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: there is none who utters a supplication except that Allah gives him what he asked or prevents an evil equal to it, so long as he does not ask for sin or the cutting of kinship (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 3381). A longer form in Musnad Aḥmad (11133) adds a third: or He stores it for him in the next life. 14:34 sits with that teaching. Asking is real. Giving is real. The form of the gift is God's.

The list of rain, fruit, ships, rivers, sun, moon, day, night is already more than a mouth usually names. *All that you asked* then looks less like a blank cheque and more like: you live inside answers you have forgotten were answers.

**Counting Fails, and That Failure Is the Point**

If you tried to count Allah's blessings, you would never be able to number them. *“If you tried to count Allah’s blessings, you would never be able to number them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”* (16:18). The two verses share the count and split the close. 16:18 ends on forgiveness and mercy. 14:34 ends on humankind as unfair and ungrateful. Same uncountable store. Two different punches. This sūrah, which has been about thanks since Moses, wants the failure of arithmetic to indict the counter, not only to praise the Giver.

Al-Baghawī notes that people cannot thank for all of them either. Inability to count is therefore inability to complete *shukr*. The proclamation of verse 7 — if you are grateful I will give more — is not a demand for a finished inventory. It is a demand for a stance: uncover what you can see, name the Giver, spend from it. The rest of the pile, the part no list will finish, is held in mercy. Using the unfinished list as an excuse not to thank is the *ẓulm* the verse is about to name.

**Unfair and Ungrateful Are One Human Habit**

*Indeed humankind is truly unfair, totally ungrateful.* *Ẓalūm* is intensively wrongdoing, putting things in the wrong place. *Kaffār* is intensively covering gifts. The pair is the human default this sūrah has been fighting. Wrongdoing: treat the subjected sun as a right, treat the messenger as a nuisance, treat the land as *ours*. Ungrateful: cover the rain, cover the rescue from Pharaoh, cover the Book that leads out of darkness. *“Surely Allah is ever Bountiful to humanity, but most people are ungrateful”* (2:243). *“Humankind is ever ungrateful”* (17:67). 14:34 is the harshest of that family because it comes after a list that should have made covering impossible.

*“They are truly wrongful ˹to themselves˺ and ignorant”* (33:72), at the end of the verse of the Trust, is a cousin diagnosis. Humans took on a trust the mountains refused. This verse says how they handle the smaller trusts of fruit and clock: they misplace them and they cover them. Abraham is about to stand up as the exception, a man who asks, thanks, and begs to be kept from idols. The general rule is 14:34. The exception has to pray not to fall back into the rule.

**The Verse Is Not Hatred of the Species**

A line that says humankind is *ẓalūm kaffār* can be misread as a curse with no door. The door is the whole sūrah: thanks brings increase, the good word fruits, believers are made firm, gardens greet with peace. The diagnosis is of the species as it acts when it follows its drift. Individuals are invited out of the drift. Calling the drift by its name is part of the invitation. Soft talk that never says *ungrateful* leaves people inside verse 28's exchange without a word for it.

The intensive forms also mean this is not a rare vice. It is a strong, repeated one. A day of thanks does not cancel a life of covering. The patient and grateful of verse 5 are intensive too — *ṣabbār shakūr* — because the disease they fight is intensive. Ordinary manners will not outwork *kaffār*. A rooted word, a firm making by God, a habit of spending before the Day — those are the scale of the cure.

**After the Count Fails, Prayer Begins**

The next verse will not offer a better inventory. It will offer Abraham's mouth. That is the right sequel. When numbering has failed, asking and fearing idols is what a truthful human does. He does not pretend to have finished the list. He asks for safety, for distance from idols, for fruit in a barren place, for prayer to be established, for forgiveness on the Day. Those asks are how an ungrateful species produces a grateful man.

Readers who stop at *you could not number them* as a pretty line about abundance have heard the first half. The second half is an accusation. Abundance plus covering is the plot of this sūrah's villains. Abundance plus thanks is the plot of its prophets. 14:34 names the first plot as the human average. The prayer that follows is how the average is broken, not by completing the count, but by turning to the One whose gifts broke the count.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:35

> ˹Remember˺ when Abraham prayed, “My Lord! Make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure, and keep me and my children away from the worship of idols

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sūrah's Name Stands Up and Speaks**

After a list of gifts and a verdict on the species as unfair and ungrateful, Abraham prays. The sūrah that bears his name has waited until now to give him a mouth. *When Abraham prayed* is a command to remember, the same style as Moses' reminder. What he asks is not a private comfort. He asks for a city and for a family: make this city secure, and keep me and my children away from the worship of idols. Security without tawḥīd would be Quraysh's later boast. Tawḥīd without a safe hollow would be a family dying in a valley. He wants both.

Al-Qurṭubī takes *this land* as Mecca, the place to which Abraham brought Hagar and Ishmael. Ibn ʿAbbās's long report in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (3364) describes that leaving: a dry valley, no friend, no crop, a mother running between two hills for water. The prayer in this verse is the speech that fits that scene. A man who has just set his child down in a place with no cultivation asks first that the place not become a slaughter-yard, and that the child not become an idol-worshipper.

**Security Is a Gift Quraysh Would Later Cover**

*Make this city secure.* Ibn Kathīr hears the answer in *“Have they not seen how We have made ˹Mecca˺ a safe haven, whereas people ˹all˺ around them are snatched away?”* (29:67). *“Who has fed them against hunger and made them secure against fear”* (106:4). The hollow became a sanctuary. People were snatched on the roads around it. Quraysh ate that security as a right and set up equals inside it. Verse 28's exchange is then an exchange of Abraham's answered prayer. He asked for a safe city. They were given it. They met it with ingratitude and with idols, the second thing he had asked to be kept from.

*“And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, ‘My Lord, make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure and provide fruits to its people—those among them who believe in Allah and the Last Day’”* (2:126). That parallel adds fruit and a limit: the provision-prayer is for believers, and God answers that even the disbeliever will be given a little enjoyment. 14:35 splits the ask: safety here, idols in the next clause, fruit two verses later. The three needs of a settlement — peace, pure worship, food — are the opposite of verses 28–30: doom, equals, enjoyment on the way to Fire.

**Keep Me, Not Only Them**

*Keep me and my children away from the worship of idols.* Al-Ṭabarī: make us totally distant from that worship. Abraham includes himself. A prophet does not treat idolatry as a children's disease. The human average of verse 34 — unfair, ungrateful — is a current that can take a father too if he is not kept. *Ijnubnī* is “make me avoid, put me aside from.” Distance is God's act, like the making-firm of verse 27. Abraham does not boast that his knowledge will save him. He asks to be placed far from the thing that has, as the next verse will say, led many astray.

Children in the prayer are a people, not only two boys. Ishmael in Mecca, Isaac in Syria, and whoever grows from them. The sūrah has already shown how leaders lead their own to doom. Abraham wants the opposite leading: a line kept from idols. He cannot guarantee the line. He can ask. The asking is how a grateful man fights the species-verdict without denying it.

**Idols Are Named Because They Are the Live Threat in This City**

The prayer is not against a generic error. It is against *aṣnām*, carved things, the furniture of the House as Quraysh would fill it. Abraham rebuilt a House for God. His children would later ring it with peers. The prayer sees that future and begs against it. When the Prophet ﷺ recites this in a Mecca full of those peers, the city's founding father is being heard against the city's present. That is why the sūrah is named Ibrāhīm. The argument with Quraysh is an argument with their claim to Abraham.

Security asked from God is also a refusal to ask it from the idols. People put gods around a shrine in order to feel safe in a hungry land. Abraham asks the Lord of the shrine for safety, and asks to be kept from the extra gods. The two clauses are one tawḥīd. If God does not secure the city, extra wood will not. If God does secure it, extra wood is an insult.

**The Prayer Is the Good Word Planted in a Valley**

Verse 24's tree had a firm root and sky-branches. This prayer is that tree as speech: rooted in a Lord, rising as a public ask for a city and a lineage. Fruit will be asked in verse 37. Leave of the Lord will be assumed throughout. A barren valley is the last place a shallow plant would choose. Abraham plants anyway, because the gardener is the One who subjects sun and moon. Quraysh, living in the answered version of this prayer, are the people of verse 34 unless they take the second clause as seriously as they take the first. They loved the secure city. They did not love being kept from idols. Abraham would not have split the two. Neither does this verse. Security asked from God also trains the tongue for the Day in verse 31. A city can be safe in this world and still have to meet a Reckoning. Abraham knows that; his last ask will be forgiveness when the account stands. This first ask is not a request to skip that Day. It is a request that the road toward it not be a road of slaughter and carved peers. A secure hollow full of extra gods is not what he meant. The Prophet ﷺ standing in that hollow, reciting this, is the founder's second clause coming home.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:36

> My Lord! They have caused many people to go astray. So whoever follows me is with me, and whoever disobeys me—then surely You are ˹still˺ All-Forgiving, Most Merciful

**Expanded Commentary**

**Idols Are Not Neutral Wood**

*They have caused many people to go astray.* The pronouns point at the idols of the previous verse. Al-Qurṭubī calls it a figure: lifeless things misguide by the way people serve them. *“They have already misled many”* is also said of other objects of worship in Nūḥ's story (71:24). The point is not that stone has a will. The point is that a cult around stone does real work. Verse 30 said equals are set up to mislead from the Way. Abraham, standing at the start of this city's story, already knows the statistic. Many. Not a few eccentrics. The House he wants kept pure is a House that will be famous enough to attract a crowd, and a crowd can be led wrong.

That is why he asked to be kept, he and his children. Fame of a sanctuary is not safety of a heart. The more people come, the more an idol can eat. Abraham's fear is pastoral, not aesthetic. He is not offended by bad art. He is afraid of a machine that eats *nās*, human beings, in large numbers.

**Whoever Follows Me Is of Me**

*So whoever follows me is with me.* Al-Qurṭubī: whoever follows Abraham in God's oneness belongs to his creed. *Of me* is kinship by tawḥīd, not by blood alone. The sūrah has been hard on blood used as a shield for false worship. Here blood is rewritten. A follower from another people is Abraham's. A child who takes idols is, in the next clause, handed to God's forgiveness rather than claimed as automatically saved. *Millat Ibrāhīm*, the way of Abraham, is this sentence made into a name. Quraysh claimed the name and dropped the following. The verse will not let them keep both.

Following here is the opposite of the dedicated followership of verse 21. There, following arrogant chiefs ended in a shared Fire. Here, following Abraham is being counted with a prophet who asked to be kept from idols. Choose the object of following with care. The lowly will not be able to switch objects on the Day.

**Disobedience Is Handed to Forgiveness, Not to Abraham's Revenge**

*And whoever disobeys me—then surely You are still All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.* Abraham does not appoint himself the warden of the disobedient. He names God. Al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī read this against 4:48: God does not forgive that partners be ascribed to Him, but forgives what is less than that for whom He wills. On that reading, Abraham is not offering a pardon for *shirk*. He is leaving non-followers to a God whose forgiveness has a known law. Another reading, which they also record, is that this was before Abraham was told that *shirk* is the unforgivable, or that it includes a hope even there. The first reading is stronger once 4:48 is on the table. The second reading has a pastoral softness the Arabic can bear if *disobeys me* means less than idolatry.

What both readings share is more important than the dispute: Abraham does not curse the disobedient in this clause. He does not ask for their destruction. He asks, in effect, that God be God toward them. *Ghafūr raḥīm* are names, not a loophole Abraham invented. A prophet who has just said that idols eat many still will not play God toward the eaten. Compare the tyrant's mouth, which only knew expulsion and forced return. Compare Satan's mouth, which only knew blame yourselves. Abraham's mouth, faced with disobedience, says You are Forgiving, Merciful.

**Mercy Here Does Not Cancel the Warning**

The sūrah has been severe: pus, ash, evil settlement, Fire as destination. This verse does not take those back. It places a prophet's hope next to them. People who disobey Abraham may still meet the Fire if their disobedience is the covering of verse 28. They may meet forgiveness if their disobedience is a sin God has willed to cover. Abraham does not sort the file. He states the names. That is a different job from verse 30's *say, enjoy yourselves, your destination is the Fire*. Both jobs are in the Book. The Prophet ﷺ recites both. A religion that only has Abraham's second clause becomes indulgence. A religion that only has verse 30 becomes a torch without a door. The sūrah keeps the door and the torch.

**With Me Is a Narrow, Generous Line**

Narrow: you have to follow. Generous: following, not pedigree, is the test. Ishmael's later children who kept idols were not *of me* in this sense, even if they kept the House. People of other tongues who took Abraham's tawḥīd are of him. Verse 4's messenger-in-the-language-of-his-people is how that following spreads. The good word of verse 24 is the content of the following. Abraham's *with me* is therefore the same tree, spoken as kinship.

The idols have many. Abraham claims those who follow him and releases those who do not into God's names. That is how a founder prays without founding a tribe-idol. Quraysh made Abraham a tribe-idol. This verse is the evidence against that theft.

*Many* is also a warning to anyone who measures truth by crowd. Idols win numbers. Abraham does not ask to outnumber them in this line. He asks to define a people by following, and to leave the rest to names that can still forgive what they will. A later messenger in this valley will be told that most people do not believe. That does not make Abraham's *with me* small. It makes it true. The good tree does not need a forest of bad trees in order to be a tree. It needs a root. Following is the root Abraham will accept as family.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:37

> Our Lord! I have settled some of my offspring in a barren valley, near Your Sacred House, our Lord, so that they may establish prayer. So make the hearts of ˹believing˺ people incline towards them and provide them with fruits, so perhaps they will be thankful

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Settlement With No Crop, Next to a House**

*I have settled some of my offspring in a barren valley, near Your Sacred House.* Al-Kalbi takes the offspring as Ishmael; al-Qurṭubī includes Hagar. The valley is Mecca, without cultivation. The House is the Kaʿbah. Ibn ʿAbbās's report in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (3364) fills the scene: Abraham left them with a water-skin and a bag of dates, turned away, and Hagar called after him until she asked, “Did Allah command you this?” He said yes, and she said Allah will not neglect us. This verse is Abraham talking to God about that act. He does not describe himself as abandoning. He describes himself as settling, *askantu*, next to a House that is Yours, for a purpose he now names.

*So that they may establish prayer.* Al-Qurṭubī, noting prayer's rank, allows “so that they may uphold the religion.” The narrower sense is enough and better. Prayer is the first work of verse 31, the standing of a servant in a place with no farm. Abraham does not say “so that they may found a market.” He says prayer. The market will come as an answer to the later asks. The purpose of the placing is worship beside the House. If the descendants forget that, they have forgotten why they were put down.

**Incline Hearts, Because a Barren Valley Cannot Feed a Rumour**

*So make the hearts of believing people incline towards them.* The translation inserts *believing*, which matches the limit in 2:126 and the need of a pure settlement. Arabic *afʾidatan min al-nās* is “hearts of some people.” Abraham does not ask for a flood of any customer. He asks for affection, *tahwī*, a leaning of hearts, so that a family in a dead valley is not alone. Love of the House, love of the people of the House, the later pull of ḥajj and ʿumrah — those are this clause as history. God answered it. People still lean.

The ask is honest about sociology. Prayer in a void with no one arriving is possible for a prophet and hard for a line. Abraham wants a community to form around the prayer, not a prayer to be added onto a community that formed for trade. The order in the verse is the order he wants in the city: House, prayer, then hearts, then fruit. Quraysh reversed it: fruit and hearts first, House as a venue, prayer as a custom among others. The prayer of 14:37 is the blueprint they sat on and did not read.

**Fruit in a Place With No Trees**

*And provide them with fruits, so perhaps they will be thankful.* Ibn Kathīr: Mecca had no palms, and fruit came in from outside. Al-Jalālayn points at Ṭāʾif's later gardens as part of the answer. Either way the miracle is ordinary-looking: a dry bowl that eats from other people's rain. Verse 32 said God grows fruit by rain as provision. Verse 37 asks for that provision in a place the rain does not make a field. The asking is an application of *taskhīr*. If ships and rivers can be subjected, a valley can be fed.

*So perhaps they will be thankful* is the sūrah's keyword returning as hope, not as guarantee. *Laʿallahum yashkurūn* matches verse 25's *so perhaps they will be mindful* and verse 7's law of thanks. Abraham knows verse 34's average. He still plants a perhaps. Fruit is for thanks, not for the building of equals. When later Mecca had fruit and no thanks, it was this *perhaps* going unanswered by the people, not by God. God provided. They covered.

**Our Lord, Twice, Because the Place Is His Before It Is Theirs**

He says *Our Lord* at the start of the settling and again before the House. The House is *Your* Sacred House. Offspring, valley, House, prayer, hearts, fruit, thanks: the chain is held at both ends by the Lord. Abraham is in the middle as the one who settled and the one who asks. He does not claim to have chosen a clever real-estate. He claims to have placed a child where God has a House, and then he asks God to make that placement livable.

That is the opposite of verse 13's *our land*. Land here is a valley without crop, owned as sacred by God, occupied by a family on leave. Security was asked in verse 35. Livability is asked here. Together they are a complete civic prayer: safe, pure, fed, visited, praying, grateful. The sūrah has now given the city its charter. Everything Quraysh later did can be measured against it. Everything the Prophet ﷺ later did in that city can be measured against it too. He established prayer. He taught thanks. He broke the idols Abraham feared. The prayer was answered in a son from this line, reciting this sūrah in the same valley.

**Fruit Is Asked as a Means, Thanks as the End**

The last clause is the sūrah in miniature. Provide fruit so that they may thank. Provision without thanks is verse 34. Thanks without provision is a test God sometimes gives; Abraham, in a dead valley, is allowed to ask not to be left in that test forever. He has already accepted barrenness as a starting condition. He has not accepted barrenness as a final theology. God can feed a void. The *perhaps* of thanks is the human side, never forced, always the point of the feeding. A city that becomes fat and forgets this clause has not outgrown Abraham. It has left him.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:38

> Our Lord! You certainly know what we conceal and what we reveal. Nothing on earth or in heaven is hidden from Allah

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prayer Turns From Asking to Being Known**

Abraham has asked for safety, distance from idols, a following, forgiveness for the disobedient, a settlement, hearts, fruit, thanks. Now he says: You know what we conceal and what we reveal. Al-Bayḍāwī: God knows better than they do what will benefit them, and is more merciful to them than they are to themselves. The line is not information for God. It is confession from the servant. Asking continues, in this sūrah's logic, even when the Asked already knows, because asking is how servanthood shows and how a response is hastened. Al-Bayḍāwī says that too. Need displayed is part of the worship.

*What we conceal and what we reveal* covers the inner fear of idols and the public settling of a child, the unspoken dread of a dry valley and the spoken *Our Lord*. A religion of only public rites would skip the conceal. A religion of only secrets would skip the reveal. Abraham puts both under one knowledge. Verse 31's open and secret spending is the same pair in another file. God is the auditor of both rooms.

**Nothing Hidden, on Earth or in Heaven**

The second sentence widens from *we* to the cosmos. Nothing on earth or in heaven is hidden from Allah. The shift from *You* to *Allah* is a shift from address to declaration, as if the prayer, for a moment, turns into a teaching for whoever is listening. Earth is the barren valley. Heaven is the sky that will have to send the fruit. Both are transparent to Him. Verse 2's Owner of what is in the heavens and the earth is also the Knower of what is in them. Ownership without knowledge would be a title. Knowledge without ownership would be a spy. Together they are the Lord Abraham is talking to.

*“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘Whether you conceal what is in your hearts or reveal it, it is known to Allah’”* is the sense of 3:29. 14:38 is that doctrine inside a father's prayer, which makes it tender and severe at once. Tender: the fear you cannot phrase is already heard. Severe: the idol you hide in a private room is already seen. Quraysh's inner attachment to equals is not saved by being inner.

**Knowing Does Not Make Asking Redundant**

People who discover that God knows everything sometimes stop praying, as if knowledge were the same as a finished transaction. Abraham knows that God knows, and he is in the middle of a long ask. The sūrah wants both facts in one mouth. Knowledge is why the ask can be honest: no need to perform a feeling God cannot check. Knowledge is also why the ask is not a briefing. You are not bringing news. You are bringing need.

That is the cure for two false prayers: the prayer that tries to impress, and the prayer that tries to inform. Abraham's prayer does neither. It places a family in a valley and then says You already see the placing, the fear, the hope of thanks. Please act. The acting asked for is still real. Knowing is not the acting. Knowing is the ground of trust that the acting, if it comes, will be wise.

**Earth and Heaven Close the Places Where a Person Might Hide a God**

Idols are earthly. Some people also hide a god in the sky as a luck, a star, a fate. *Nothing hidden on earth or in heaven* sweeps both floors. The equals of verse 30 have no back room. Satan's call in verse 22 had no private frequency God missed. The lowly's dedication to leaders was noted. The tyrant's inner love of this life was noted. This sentence, in Abraham's mouth, is the same seeing applied to a household trying to do right. Even a prophet's family is inside the seeing. That is why he asked to be kept, not only his children.

The barren valley is a good place to learn this. There is nowhere to hide a store of water. There is nowhere to hide a second helper. Hagar's running between the hills in the Bukhārī report is a body in an open bowl, seen. The well that answered her was seen too. 14:38 is that openness made into theology.

**The Verse Holds the Rest of the Prayer in Place**

Without this line, the asks could sound like a man trying to manage God. With it, the asks are a man standing in the open. Forgive, feed, incline hearts, keep from idols — all of that now sits under You know, and nothing is hidden. The next verse can praise for Ishmael and Isaac in old age because the Hearer of this verse is also the Knower. Hearing and knowing are two names for the same lack of distance.

A believer reciting this does not have to be in Mecca. Conceal and reveal are portable. The Day of verse 31, with no ransom and no friends, is the public version of this knowledge. Abraham accepts the knowledge early, in a valley, while there is still time to ask. That timing is the mercy. Known, and still invited to speak. Seen, and still told to pray. The species of verse 34 covers gifts. This man uncovers even his hiding, and calls the uncovering *Our Lord*.

The sentence also steadies a parent who has just asked for hearts to incline and fruit to arrive. Those asks can become anxiety: what if no one comes, what if the child starves, what if I chose the wrong hollow. You know what we hide — including that anxiety — and what we reveal. Nothing in the sky that must send rain, nothing in the earth that must stay barren or break, is off His map. The prayer can continue without the pray-er pretending to be the manager of weather. Knowledge, in this use, is not a cold eye. It is why trust in verses 11–12 can live in a valley with no crop.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:39

> All praise is for Allah who has blessed me with Ishmael and Isaac in my old age. My Lord is indeed the Hearer of ˹all˺ prayers

**Expanded Commentary**

**Praise Interrupts the Ask Because a Gift Has Already Arrived**

Abraham has been requesting. He now praises. All praise is for Allah who has blessed me with Ishmael and Isaac in my old age. The children are already there when he speaks this line. Ishmael through Hagar, Isaac through Sarah, both against the clock of a body that had no right, by ordinary counting, to expect them. *“And his wife was standing by, and she laughed. Then We gave her good news of Isaac, and after him of Jacob… She wondered, ‘How can I have a child when I am so old, and this husband of mine is an old man?’”* — the sense of 11:71–72. 51:28–29 tells the same wonder. 14:39 is the father's *ḥamd* after the wonder has become two names.

Praise in the middle of a prayer is not a change of subject. It is the thanks verse 7 required, spoken over the most personal item on the uncountable list of verse 34. He could not number all gifts. He can name these two. Naming them as a blessing *in old age* keeps the miracle in the sentence. A man who treats sons as a due has already started covering. Abraham treats them as a granted thing, late, from the One he is still asking.

**Two Sons, Two Houses of the Story**

Ishmael is the child of the barren valley. Isaac is the child of the glad tidings in the tent. The sūrah is named for the father of both, and this verse refuses to drop either name. Later quarrels that try to keep one son and misplace the other are not this mouth. He praises for both in one breath, in old age, as one blessing with two bodies. The House by which he settled some offspring is Ishmael's scene. The line of Jacob is Isaac's. The Book that recites this in Arabic is being recited by a descendant of the first, confirming the second. Praise for both is tawḥīd applied to family history.

Old age in the sentence is also a quiet answer to verse 10's *delay your end until your appointed term*. Abraham's term included a wait that looked like a closed door. Then two doors. People who mock a delayed gift, or who take a late gift as proof they were owed, cannot pray this verse. He waited. He was given. He praised. He is still asking, in the next verse, for prayer to be made in him and in the progeny. The gift of sons is not the end of need. It is a new field of need.

**The Hearer of Prayers Is Named Because These Sons Were Asked For**

*My Lord is indeed the Hearer of all prayers.* *Samīʿ al-duʿāʾ* is a title proved by the biography he has just named. He asked. He was heard. Ishmael and Isaac are the evidence, not a theory of hearing. The barren valley's other asks — security, hearts, fruit — sit under the same title. If He heard the impossible ask for a child in old age, He can hear a valley into a sanctuary. The title is faith with a memory, not faith with a wish.

Hearing here is not mere registration. Verse 38 already said God knows the hidden and the shown. Hearing in 39 is knowing that answers. The two verses work as a pair: You know; You hear. Knowledge without hearing would be a silent archive. Hearing without knowledge would be a guess. Abraham's Lord is both, and the sons are the exhibit.

**Praise Belongs to Allah, Not to the Father's Body**

*All praise is for Allah who has blessed me.* The grammar gives God the blessing and Abraham the receiving. Old age makes that grammar visible. A young father can fool himself that the child is his project. An old father holding Ishmael, then Isaac, has fewer places to hide the Giver. *Wahaba* — bestowed, granted — is the verb of a gift, the same family of words as the *niʿmah* Moses told Israel to remember. Children are in that family. Treating them as idols is the disaster Abraham asked to be kept from. Praising God for them is the protection.

This is also why the ungrateful average of verse 34 is so ugly when it lands on a birth. A child arrives. Covering says “I, we, our line.” Uncovering says what this verse says. The sūrah has put the right sentence in the founder of the city that would later cover even a messenger from that line.

**The Hearer Still Has More to Hear**

He does not close the prayer with the praise. The next two verses will ask for prayer in the progeny, for acceptance, for forgiveness on the Day. Praise is a station in the prayer, not the exit. That is mature *shukr*: name what came, name the Hearer, keep asking. A thanks that stops asking has started to treat the gift as a finished story. Abraham's story is not finished. Mecca is not yet a city of prayer. The Day of Reckoning is not yet. He has been heard in the matter of sons. He will ask to be heard in the matter of their worship and of a whole believing people's forgiveness.

The Prophet ﷺ reciting this is himself an answer to a nearby prayer in 2:129: a messenger from among them. Hearing, in this family, is a long act. 14:39 is one proof along that line, placed in the father's mouth so that every later gift in the valley can be read as the same Hearer, not as the valley's luck.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:40

> My Lord! Make me and those ˹believers˺ of my descendants keep up prayer. Our Lord! Accept my prayers

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sons Were Not the Last Ask; Prayer Is**

He has been given Ishmael and Isaac. He now asks to be made a performer of prayer, he and his progeny. Al-Qurṭubī: a performer of prayer can mean one firm in the religion. Al-Bayḍāwī: one persistent in the act itself. The translation's *keep up prayer* holds both: establish it, continue it. Verse 37 settled offspring so that they might establish prayer. Verse 40 asks that the settler and the line actually be made into people who do that. Placing a child by a House does not automatically produce a worshipper. God has to make the worshipper. Abraham knows that, which is why the verb is *make me*.

The translation inserts *believers* among the descendants, which matches his earlier *whoever follows me is with me*. He is not asking that every biological twig become a prayer-leader regardless of tawḥīd. He is asking for a praying line inside the line. Quraysh could claim descent and skip the prayer that was the purpose of the settling. This verse is the claim they cannot honestly make unless they keep the prayer up.

**Make Me — the Prophet Includes Himself Again**

As in *keep me from idols*, he does not stand outside the need. Old men with answered prayers can retire into being symbols. Abraham asks to be made a *muqīm al-ṣalāh* still. Persistence is a making, like the steadfastness of verse 27. A person can have two miraculous sons and still need to be made to pray. The sūrah will not let family success become a religion.

Prayer here is also the daily form of the good word. Root in the heart, branches in the sky, fruit in every season: the five prayers are that tree's most obvious crop. Asking to be made to keep them up is asking for verse 25 to be true of a household. The barren valley has no other public work worth naming yet. Trade will come. Prayer has to come first or the trade will become an equal.

**Accept My Prayer Is a Second Humility**

*Our Lord! Accept my prayers.* Al-Bayḍāwī: this may be a request that God accept his worship in general, or this supplication in particular. Either way Abraham does not treat his own asking as automatically received. He has just called God the Hearer. Hearing is not the same as accepting as worship. People can be heard as need and still have a deed returned as ash, if the deed is for show or for an equal. *Taqabbal* is the word of 2:127, when Abraham and Ishmael raise the foundations: accept this from us. The House and the prayer share a fear: that the work be done and not taken.

That fear is healthy. It keeps the good tree from becoming a monument to the planter. God makes the prayer; God accepts the prayer. Abraham occupies the middle as the one who asks to be made and asks to be accepted. Servanthood is that middle. The arrogant of verse 21 never stood there. They issued. They did not ask to be accepted.

**Progeny Are a Longer Risk Than a Lifetime**

A man can perhaps guard his own prayer for a remaining decade. A progeny is centuries. Abraham puts the centuries in God's making. The later messenger who recites this in the same valley is part of the answer: he establishes prayer, he teaches it, he names this grandfather. So are the people who still face the House Abraham asked for. So, painfully, are the generations who had the House and lost the prayer's meaning among idols. The ask does not force the centuries. It opens them to a making. Verse 4 still stands: God guides whom He wills. Abraham's *make my descendants keep up prayer* is a request that He will include them.

The insertion of *believers* in the translation is therefore not a softening. It is a limit Abraham already taught. Disobedient progeny are in God's forgiveness and mercy (v. 36), not in a guaranteed miḥrāb. Prayer is for those who follow. The father asks for a following that prays, not for a census that recites his name.

**The Verse Ties the City's Purpose to a Daily Act**

If this ask is answered, Mecca is a city of established prayer. If it is not, Mecca is a city of a House used for other things. The whole sūrah's argument with ingratitude lands on this practical point. Thanks is not a feeling about rain. It is a body that stands, bows, and spends, as verse 31 said. Abraham, having praised for sons, asks for the bodies of the family to be put into that shape, and for the asking itself to be taken. A reader who loves the story of Ishmael and Isaac and does not keep up prayer has taken the gift-clause of verse 39 and dropped the purpose-clause of verses 37 and 40. The prayer you are reciting is the founder telling you what the gift was for. Sons without prayer would be verse 34's unfairness in a household form: a gift covered by being turned into a clan-name. He will not have that. Make me pray. Make the believing ones among them pray. Accept the asking. The three clauses are one fear of wasted blessing, spoken by a man who has already seen blessing arrive late and does not want the lateness to become a story about him instead of about the Hearer. A House without established prayer is already an equal in the making; he asks to be spared that outcome while the valley is still empty enough to hear the ask.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:41

> Our Lord! Forgive me, my parents, and the believers on the Day when the judgment will come to pass.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Last Ask in the Valley Is Not More Fruit**

Abraham does not close with a request for a larger harvest. He asks for forgiveness — for himself, for his parents, and for the believers — on the Day when the judgment will come to pass. The city he has been building in speech still has to meet a Reckoning. Security, inclined hearts, dates in a bowl without trees: none of that will stand up and testify. *Yawm yaqūmu al-ḥisāb* is the morning the account gets to its feet. Verse 31 already warned that ransom and friendly connections will not work that morning. This line applies for the only thing that will: forgiveness from the Lord of the account.

He puts his own name first. Two sons in old age, a House, a prayer that will be recited until the end of time — and still *forgive me*. The intensive ungratefulness of verse 34 is not a verdict he applies only to strangers. A prophet does not retire from need. The right last word of a long ask is not “I have done enough.” It is “cover me when the numbers are read.”

**Family Is Loved Without Being Made into a Shield**

Parents come next. The Book is not shy about Abraham's father elsewhere. A promise to seek forgiveness for him stands in 19:47. A later limit stands in 9:114: the plea was because of a promise, and when the man was clear as an enemy of God, Abraham turned away from that alliance. 6:74 names Āzar in the argument against idols. Those passages must govern how 14:41 is heard. Love of parents is commanded. Love of parents is not a device for smuggling *shirk* into the next life. The verse can be the earlier tenderness, or a request for believing parents, or a hope under the condition of faith. It cannot, beside 9:114, be a charter for unlimited intercession of blood.

What remains, after the limit is honoured, is still a lesson. This religion does not teach a son to spit out his origin. It teaches him to ask, and then to let God be God when the asking meets a declared enmity. The arrogant chiefs used family as a rope toward the Fire. Abraham uses family as a name in a prayer, then leaves the verdict with the One who knows what is hidden and what is shown.

**Believers, Not a Tribe, Fill the Third Slot**

The crowd in the ask is *the believers*, not “my people” in the Quraysh sense. Whoever has taken the good word, given thanks, established prayer, spent before the Day — those are the people he wants covered when the account stands. A local prayer about a valley ends as a global request. The Day of verse 21 gathers everyone. Abraham wants his speech attached to the side that will hear *Peace*, not to a genealogy that might hear Satan's *blame yourselves*.

That width is the opposite of dedicated followership. Followers of chiefs asked the chiefs to shield them. Here a prophet asks God to forgive a people who will not be able to shield one another. No friendly connections, verse 31 said. This connection — a prophet's *Our Lord* over a believing crowd — is not a friendship of the town's kind. It is an early application to the only court that will be open.

**The Account Standing Is the Heading of Everything Still to Come**

The sūrah's last movement will tell the Prophet not to think God unaware of the wrongdoers, will describe staring eyes and vacant hearts, will quote a plea for a little delay, will mention ruins already walked through, plots that cannot shift mountains, a changed earth, chains, tar, a swift reckoning, and a message sufficient for anyone who will take heed. Abraham has already named that movement's day as the day of *ḥisāb*. He asked for mercy on it. God will now describe terror on it. Both descriptions are true for different crowds. The prayer and the warning are one sky.

Cities do not speak when the account stands. Pedigree does not speak. The House does not speak unless the lives around it were prayer. Abraham spent seven verses founding a settlement in words. He ends by placing the settlement under a Day that can unfound any settlement that forgot why it was placed. That is order, not despair. The hollow is for prayer. Prayer is for a Lord. The Lord will reckon. Forgiveness is how a reckoned people survive the reading.

**Forgive Is the Word Satan Will Not Say**

Satan's speech offered blame and desertion. Leaders offered “had we been guided.” Abraham offers *ighfir*. He does not deny that selves have a bill. He asks the only One who can close a bill to close it for a named list. The tyrant's sequence — oozing drink, death that will not finish, a harsher wait — is an unclosed bill. This verse is the request that another sequence be given to people who believed. The living who still recite it are being handed the founder's last habit: before you stop speaking to God about a city, speak to Him about the morning when cities will not matter, and ask to be among those He covers. The account will stand whether the ask is made or not. Abraham makes it while a valley can still hear him, so that the people of the House later cannot say their founder taught them only how to be fed. He taught them how to beg for cover when feeding will be over.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:42

> Do not think ˹O Prophet˺ that Allah is unaware of what the wrongdoers do. He only delays them until a Day when ˹their˺ eyes will stare in horror—

**Expanded Commentary**

**Unaware Is the Thought the Verse Forbids**

The Prophet ﷺ is told not to think that Allah is unaware of what the wrongdoers do. The thought is a temptation, not a doctrine he holds. Delay looks like absence. A town still loud, still expelling, still setting up equals, can feel like a God who has not noticed. This line forbids that reading. Al-Rāzī's argument is the right shape even when restated in other words: a Lord who never called injustice to account would be a Lord who did not know it, or could not stop it, or liked it. None of those is the God of this sūrah. Therefore the delay is not forgetfulness. It is a granting of time.

*“If Allah were to punish people ˹immediately˺ for their wrongdoing, He would not have left a single living being on earth. But He delays them for an appointed term”* (16:61). *“If Allah were to punish people ˹immediately˺ for what they have committed, He would not have left a single living being on earth. But He delays them for an appointed term”* (35:45). 14:42 is that doctrine aimed at a messenger's chest. The wrongdoers' file is full. The clock is still running. The running is mercy that can be used as verse 7's thanks, or as verse 30's enjoyment on the way to Fire.

**Delay Is the Same Clock Moses Named as a Test**

*He only delays them until a Day.* *Yuʾakhkhiruhum* is the verb of respite, the same family as Iblīs being delayed, the same family as the term appointed in verse 10. For the wrongdoer, extra time is extra sin unless he turns. *“They are only given more time to increase in sin”* (3:178). For the believer watching, extra time is a trial of trust. Verses 11–12 already trained that trust. This verse applies it to the sight of unpunished *ẓulm*. The land is still “theirs.” The messenger is still threatened. God is not unaware. He is waiting for a morning the next clause will begin to show.

Abraham has just asked for forgiveness on the Day the account stands. The wrongdoers are being delayed to that same Day. Two crowds, one date. The date is not unknown to God. It is unknown to the people who treat delay as proof that the date is a myth.

**Eyes Will Stare in Horror Is the End of Coolness**

The Day is described first by the organ that did not see: eyes will stare, transfixed, in horror. Al-Nīsābūrī pictures eyeballs pushed by terror. The glance that used to slide over a messenger, over a ruin, over a parable of two trees, will lock and not come back. Verse 19 asked *have you not seen*. Verse 24 asked *do you not see*. This Day is seeing without the option of looking away. The next verse will finish the body: necks, heads, glance that does not return, vacant hearts. 14:42 only needs the eyes, because unawareness was the accusation, and eyes are how awareness arrives too late.

Horror here is not entertainment. It is the moral of delay. What was not feared when it was news will be feared when it is sight. The Prophet is not to envy their current un-staring calm. The calm is borrowed. The stare is the payment.

**The Address Is Singular so That the Messenger Is Held**

*Do not think* is to him. A messenger can carry other people's despair without naming it as his own. The verse names it anyway, as a thought not to host. Comfort in this form is stern. It does not say “they will be punished tomorrow at noon.” It says God knows, God delays, the delay ends in a Day of locked eyes. That is enough to keep leading out of darkness without needing the town to collapse this week.

It is also enough to keep the messenger from inventing a softer God who is too polite to look at *ẓulm*. Unaware would be that polite God. This Book will not have him. The Owner of heavens and earth in verse 2, the Knower of conceal and reveal in verse 38, is the same watcher here. Wrongdoers do not labour in a blind spot. They labour on a stage whose Judge is letting the scene run to a written minute.

**Until a Day Ties This Verse to Abraham's Last Word**

Abraham said *the Day when the judgment will come to pass*. This verse says *a Day when eyes will stare*. Same morning, two cameras. One is the believer's application for cover. One is the wrongdoer's face. The sūrah wants the Prophet — and anyone who recites after him — to hold both. Delay without the Day would be a licence. The Day without delay would have emptied the earth already, as 16:61 said. Together they are the government of a Lord who is neither unaware nor hasty in the way panic is hasty.

The dash at the end of the translation is a held breath. The body of the wrongdoer is about to be described in motion. This verse's work is the theology under the motion: not unaware, only delaying, until eyes can no longer pretend. A reader who is still able to move his glance is not yet on that Day. He is in the delay. The only sane use of the delay is the use verse 31 named: pray, spend, before the morning when staring is all that is left of sight. Wrongdoers count the quiet as proof. This verse counts the quiet as rope. Rope can pull a person to thanks or to a deeper covering. The Prophet is told which reading to refuse, so that he will not stop leading while the rope is still in their hands.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:43

> rushing forth, heads raised, never blinking, hearts void

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Body Arrives Before the Mouth Has a Line**

Verse 42 ended on staring eyes. This verse puts the rest of the person in motion: rushing forth, heads raised, never blinking, hearts void. No speech yet. Speech will come in verse 44 as a plea for delay. First the sūrah wants the reader to see a human being whose organs have already lost their ordinary jobs. Running is not toward a goal he chose. Heads up is not pride. Unblinking is not courage. An empty heart is not peace. Every sign that used to mean power in the town now means the town is gone.

Ibn ʿAjībah reads the rush as humiliation, like slaves being driven. Heads raised, he says, from fear or from chains at the neck, as in *“We have put shackles around their necks up to their chins, so their heads are forced up”* (36:8). The glance that does not return to them is terror that will not look at the self, or a chain that will not let the chin drop. Al-Bayḍāwī takes vacant hearts as understanding burned out by stupor. Four short English phrases, four failures of the old self. The *jabbār* of verse 15 is still a body. The body is no longer his.

**Rushing Forth Is the Opposite of the Delay They Enjoyed**

They were delayed. Now they run. The extra years of verse 42 were slow. This morning is fast. *Muhṭiʿīn* is stretching forward, hurrying, necks out. People who would not walk toward a prophet's word are made to hurry toward a standing they denied. *“The Trumpet will be blown ˹a second time˺, then—behold!— they will rush from the graves to their Lord”* (36:51). 14:43 is that rush as posture. The delay they treated as a home turns out to have been a waiting room. When the name is called, the body knows before the mouth does.

The rush also answers verse 3's preference for this life. This life was the place of unhurried enjoyment. The next life, for the wrongdoer, is haste without arrival that helps. They ran after the near life. They are now run into the far one.

**Never Blinking Is Sight Without Relief**

A blink is a mercy. The eye gets a black instant, a rest, a chance not to see. *Never blinking* — the glance not returning to them — is sight as punishment. Verse 19 and 24 asked if they saw. They did not, in the way that changes a life. Now they see in the way that cannot be stopped. The ruins of verse 45, the parables of the two trees, the drink of pus — all the images they scanned as talk — become the air. An unblinking eye is an eye with no inner room left.

That is why preaching that only shocks the retina is not this verse's use. This verse is for people who can still blink, still look away, still look back and repent. The unblinking is what looking away turns into when the looking away has been a life.

**Hearts Void Is the Inner Match of the Outer Stare**

*Afʾidatuhum hawāʾ* — their hearts are air, a vacancy. The heart in this Book is the place of the firm word, the root of the good tree, the seat God makes steadfast in verse 27. Vacant means that seat is empty of the understanding that could pray, remember, or even plot. Al-Bayḍāwī's stupor is not sleep. It is a mind that has nothing left to work with. The leaders who still had speeches in verse 21 will speak from this vacancy. The speech will not be wisdom. It will be a reflex of the old hierarchy, then a confession of no escape.

A vacant heart is also the end of the evil word. That word had no stability in verse 26. Here the organ that held it is shown as hollow. What filled it — fathers, land, equals, enjoyment — cannot survive the staring. Hollow is what those fillings were always going to leave.

**The Verse Is a Still Picture on Purpose**

Four phrases, no argument, no name of God. The theology was in verse 42. This is the photograph. A writer who padded it with extra doctrine would be talking over the image. The image is the doctrine made flesh: delay ends, the body tells the truth the mouth would not. Rush, raised head, locked glance, empty chest. Anyone who has seen panic in this world has a small version. This is the large version, on a Day when panic is accurate.

Abraham asked to be forgiven when the account stands. These people are shown as already unable to ask well. Their ask, when it comes, will be for a little more of the delay they wasted. The photograph is meant to make that later sentence sound as thin as it is. A vacant heart does not suddenly become a remembering heart because the mouth finds a formula. The time to fill the heart was the delay. The time to fill it is now, for anyone whose glance can still return to him.

The four phrases also undo the costume of the gate. A chief in this world is still, to his street, a man who walks at his own speed, looks where he likes, and carries a full chest of plans. Strip the costume on the Day and the same man is a driven body, a forced chin, an eye that cannot rest, a chest of air. The sūrah does not need to say “this is that man.” The grammar of 42–43 has already named the wrongdoers as the ones delayed to this posture. Anyone who loves the costume is being shown the body underneath it, in time to love something else: a heart God makes firm, a glance that still knows how to look at a ruin and change course.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:44

> And warn the people of the Day when the punishment will overtake ˹the wicked among˺ them, and the wrongdoers will cry, “Our Lord! Delay us for a little while. We will respond to Your call and follow the messengers!” ˹It will be said,˺ “Did you not swear before that you would never be removed ˹to the next life˺?”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Warn, Because the Photograph Is Still Future**

The Prophet is told to warn the people of the Day when the punishment overtakes them. Al-Baghawī, al-Kalbi, and al-Qurṭubī take the Day as the Judgment. Ibn ʿAbbās, as al-Qurṭubī notes, heard *the people* as the Makkan idolaters in the first instance. The warning is public either way. Verse 43 was a still picture. This verse puts a sentence in the pictured mouths, and then a reply. The work of the living messenger is to recite both before either is lived.

The wrongdoers will cry: Our Lord! Delay us for a little while. We will respond to Your call and follow the messengers. The request is the thing they would not give the messengers: time, a hearing, a following. They want a short respite — *ajalin qarīb* — a near term, a little more of what verse 42 said they had already been given. The delay they spent as enjoyment they now want as a second chance. *“Oh! If only we could be sent back, we would never deny the signs of our Lord and we would ˹surely˺ be of the believers”* (6:27). 14:44 is that cry as a request for a short extension rather than a full return. The heart of it is the same.

**The Offer to Follow Comes After Following Would Cost Nothing**

We will respond to Your call and follow the messengers. The call was verse 10: forgiveness, a term appointed. The messengers were human, they said, and they wanted a sign, and then they wanted them out of the land. Following is cheap in a photograph where the Fire is already the air. *“But no! ˹They only say this˺ because the truth they used to hide will become all too clear to them. Even if they were to be sent back, they would certainly revert to what they were forbidden. Indeed they are liars!”* (6:28). The Book does not take the deathbed-of-the-cosmos conversion as a reliable contract. It names it as a reaction to being shown. 14:44 lets them say the good sentence, then answers with their own earlier oath, which is a more efficient unmasking than a lecture.

**The Oath They Swore Was That There Would Be No Removal**

Did you not swear before that you would never be removed to the next life? Al-Qurṭubī takes the swore-against end as the move from this world to the next and the punishment there. Many tie the oath to *“They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that Allah will never raise the dead to life”* (16:38). The reply is not “God has now decided otherwise.” The reply is “you already testified the opposite of what you now want.” A person who swore there was no moving cannot ask, without shame, to be moved back for a little while in order to follow. The oath is quoted against the plea so that the plea cannot pose as new information. They knew the claim. They denied the destination. They now want a layover.

*Removed* in the translation is the right discomfort. They treated this life as a residence with no eviction. Verse 20 said replacement is not difficult. Verse 14 promised residence after wrongdoers for people who fear the standing. This verse shows the evicted still arguing the lease.

**A Little While Would Not Be Used as They Promise**

Even if the little while were granted, 6:28 says they would return to what they were forbidden. The sūrah does not need to repeat that verse in full to have its force. The whole of 14 has shown how delay is used by the ungrateful: more covering, more equals, more leading of their people. A short delay asked for in horror is still delay. Their history with delay is the evidence. The warning to the living is: do not wait to be the person who asks for a little while. Be the person who uses the long while already in the hand.

Responding to the call and following messengers are exactly verse 1 and verse 4's work, offered too late. The living can still do both. That is why the verse begins with *warn*, not with *describe for sport*. Warning is a form of the leading out of darkness. Some will hear their future mouth and shut it now by opening it in obedience. Some will swear again that they will not be moved. Both hearings are already in the split of verse 4.

**The Cry Names God, and That Is Not Yet Faith**

*Our Lord!* in their mouths is accurate. He is their Lord. Verse 2 said so of everything in the heavens and the earth. Saying the name in a request for delay is not the shahādah of verse 24. It is a reflex of need, the kind 39:8 described: hardship, then a cry, then, if relief came, equals again. The reply about the oath treats the cry as evidence, not as a conversion. A vacant heart, verse 43, can still form a sentence. The sentence does not fill the heart.

The Prophet who warns with this verse is not told to negotiate the little while. He is told to make the future conversation public while the oath is still being sworn in Mecca. That is kindness with a hard edge. The edge is the only kindness left for a people who use delay as proof that eviction is a myth.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:45

> You passed by the ruins of those ˹destroyed peoples˺ who had wronged themselves. It was made clear to you how We dealt with them, and We gave you ˹many˺ examples

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ruins Were a Curriculum, Not Scenery**

You passed by the ruins of those who had wronged themselves. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Qurṭubī hear the addressees as the people of Mecca, whose caravans ran past the traces of ʿĀd and Thamūd and other peoples who had rejected messengers. Winter and summer journeys of Sūrah 106 were not only trade. They were field trips through God's days, the days Moses was told to remind of. *It was made clear to you how We dealt with them.* The dealing was not a rumour from a distant book only. It was stone, empty houses, stories the road still told.

*Wronged themselves* is the Qur'an's usual verdict on destroyed nations. The wrong was not first a crime against a neighbour, though it included that. It was a crime against the selves who covered gifts and fought messengers. Hell as an evil settlement is the far house. These ruins are the near house, already visible in this life. A people who walk through someone else's *qarār* gone cold and then swear they will never be removed have used the curriculum as a picnic site.

**Made Clear Is the Same Verb as the Messenger's Job**

Verse 4 said messengers are sent in a people's tongue to clarify. This verse says the ruins themselves were made clear. God teaches by wreckage as well as by speech. The two clarifications are meant to agree. If a messenger says “nations before you put hands over mouths and then failed,” and the road to Syria still shows the failure, the hearer who denies is not short of evidence. He is the person of verse 9 with a travel diary.

Al-Bayḍāwī: the Makkans could still witness the vestiges and hear the inherited stories. Clarity, then, is mixed media — eye, ear, memory, this recitation. *We gave you many examples* is the Qur'an's own parables added to the road's parables. Two trees, ash in a storm, a good word, an evil word: those are examples. ʿĀd's wind and Thamūd's cry are examples that occupy geography. The verse stacks them so that “we never saw a people like that” will not stand.

**Passing By Is Not the Same as Taking Heed**

The verb is *sakantum* in the source-rendering “you settled in the dwellings,” and the translation here is *you passed by the ruins*. Both senses live in the Arabic file of this passage: residence in a land that had known earlier wrongdoers, and traffic through their remains. Either way the crime is unupdated living. You occupy, or you travel, as if the previous tenants' file were closed without remainder. Verse 9 asked if the stories had not come. This verse says the houses came too. A story you can walk through and still not fear is a story you have decided not to be in.

That decision is the far stray of verses 3 and 18. Examples do not work like keys in every lock. They work for the patient and grateful, verse 5. For others they are tourism. Quraysh were expert tourists of other people's disasters and amateurs of their own.

**Examples in the Book Make the Ruins Speak Arabic**

A silent ruin can be given a pagan caption: bad luck, a stronger tribe, a change of wells. The Qur'an captions it: We dealt with them. The *We* is the point. Weather did not randomly eat ʿĀd. The Owner of verse 2 collected a debt. Once that caption is on the ruin, passing by becomes a kind of recitation. Refusing the caption is another covering of a gift — the gift of someone else's end, shown in time to change yours.

Parables and ruins together also protect the hearer from two thin religions: a religion of only stories with no stones, and a religion of only stones with no moral. The Book will not let the past be myth, and will not let archaeology be godless. You passed by. It was made clear. We gave examples. Three gifts. Ingratitude here is walking on.

**The Verse Is Spoken While the Addressee Can Still Turn the Caravan**

Warning in verse 44 was about a future Day. This verse is about a past already underfoot. The two times bracket the living. Ahead, staring eyes. Behind, empty dwellings. Between them, a people with a House Abraham asked to be secure, with fruit he asked to be given, with a messenger he asked to be sent. Using the behind as a proof that “it happens to others” is the last misuse of a gift. The examples were given so that the others would be enough. If they are not enough, the Day of verse 44 will not be a surprise. It will be the same dealing, without a road left to pass by on.

A believer who travels now still passes ruins, some of them new. The verse does not expire with Thamūd. Any people who wronged themselves and left a trace are part of the curriculum. The test is whether the passer-by updates his own settlement — prayer, thanks, no equals — or only takes a picture. Abraham asked for a city that would thank. This verse is what a city sees when it will not.

Passing by also has a mercy in it that the verse will not let the passer waste. God could have left the dwellings erased without a trace, so that no later caravan would have to think. He left enough stone to think with. Clarity is a gift in the same family as rain and fruit. Covering it is the same *kufr* as covering those. A people who take photographs of Thamūd and then swear they will never be moved have not lacked a teacher. They have lacked the patience and thanks verse 5 said were the eyes that can see a day of God when the day is someone else's. The examples were many so that one would be enough. Many, refused, become an extra weight on the file, not an extra excuse.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:46

> They devised every plot, which was fully known to Allah, but their plotting was not enough to ˹even˺ overpower mountains ˹let alone Allah˺

**Expanded Commentary**

**Plotting Is the Town's Last Craft**

They devised every plot. The people who put hands over mouths, demanded signs, threatened exile, set up equals, swore they would not be moved — they also planned. Planning is not a sin in itself. Messengers planned journeys. Abraham planned a settlement. The plots here are the plots of verse 13 raised to a method: how to silence a message, how to keep a street, how to make the Way look crooked. Al-Rāzī takes *their plot lies with God* as God knowing the plot. Al-Kalbi allows knowing its repayment. Both are in the translation's *fully known to Allah*. There is no back room. Verse 38's nothing-hidden is now applied to conspiracy.

*“Those ˹disbelievers˺ before them ˹secretly˺ planned, but Allah has the ultimate plan. He knows what every soul commits”* (13:42). *“They are certainly devising ˹evil˺ plans, but I too am planning”* (86:15–16). *“And ˹so˺ they made a plan, but We too made a plan, while they were unaware”* (27:50). 14:46 is that family of verses in the key of this sūrah: known, and not enough.

**Not Enough to Overpower Mountains Is a Scale, Not a Joke**

Their plotting was not enough to overpower mountains, let alone Allah. Al-Zamakhsharī: no plot, however large, even mountain-sized, overcomes God's will. Al-Kalbi and al-Zamakhsharī also hear mountains as a figure for God's signs, the Law, prophecy — things as fixed as peaks, with roots like the good tree. On that reading the plot is an attempt to uproot what verse 24 said cannot be uprooted by weather. Either reading leaves the plot small. Mountains do not move because a town met at night. The Lord of mountains does not move because a town met at night.

The translation's *let alone Allah* is a gloss that names the true comparison. If the plot cannot shift rock, it cannot shift the One who subjected sun and moon. Verse 20 said replacement of a people is not difficult. Plotting against a messenger is a people trying to make their own replacement impossible. The scale of 14:46 says the attempt does not register as weight.

**Known Is Already a Defeat**

A plot's first need is darkness. Fully known to Allah means the first need was never met. They planned as if verse 42's “do not think He is unaware” were not already on the page. Awareness is not a camera that will be reviewed later only. It is present tense. The ruin they passed by in verse 45 was a previous plot's outcome, already filed. Their new plot is another file in the same cabinet. Knowing, in this use, is not passive. It is the first half of dealing.

That is comfort for a messenger who hears of meetings against him. He does not need to map every scheme. The schemes have an Owner who already has the map. Trust in verses 11–12 is practical here. Patience under harm includes patience under plotting. The harm is known. The plot is known. The mountains are still standing.

**Mountains Also Recall the Firm Word**

The good tree's root was firm. Mountains in the Qur'an are pegs, weights, things that keep the earth from shaking. A plot that cannot overpower mountains cannot overpower a word God has made firm in verse 27. The two firmnesses are cousins. One is geology. One is tawḥīd. Quraysh's plots against a recitation were attempts to do geology to a word. The verse says the word is in the mountain class, not in the ash class.

Ash, in verse 18, was what *their* deeds became in a wind. Mountains are what *His* signs are in a plot. The weather that eats ash does not eat peaks. Choosing which order you belong to — ash or mountain — is choosing which word you plant.

**The Verse Closes the Human Side of the Argument**

After ruins, oaths, staring eyes, vacant hearts, there is not much left for a town to try except a better scheme. This line says the better scheme is already inside God's knowledge and outside the class of things that move the world. The next verse will tell the Prophet not to think God will fail His promise to messengers. Plotting was the human attempt to make that promise fail. 14:46 is the attempt weighed and found light.

A reader who plots in smaller rooms — a lie about a path, a pressure on a relative, a dressing of this life as the only residence — is in the same scale. The plot does not have to be a tribal council. It has to be a device against a message. Devices against messages do not move mountains. They move the plotter toward the Day when eyes stare. The living still have a different craft: prayer, spending, a good word with a root. That craft does not need darkness. It needs the leave of the Lord who already knows the other kind.

Mountain-scale is also a comfort about fear. People who are plotted against often inflate the plot until it fills the sky. This verse puts the plot next to a peak and next to God, and the plot loses both comparisons. What remains for the plotted-against is the work already assigned: clarify, trust, bear harm, not produce a fake sign. The plotters remain busy with a craft that cannot shift the thing they hate. That busyness is part of their earning. Swift reckoning will not miss it, and will not need it to have succeeded in order to count it as a deed. Failure of a plot is not innocence of a plot. Known, and light, and still a file: that is the whole of 14:46.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:47

> So do not think ˹O Prophet˺ that Allah will fail to keep His promise to His messengers. Allah is indeed Almighty, capable of punishment

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Second “Do Not Think,” Aimed at the Promise**

Verse 42 said do not think He is unaware. This verse says do not think He will fail His promise to His messengers. The two thoughts are twins. Delay looks like unawareness. Delay also looks like a broken appointment. *“Surely Allah does not break His promise”* (3:9). *“Allah’s promise is ˹always˺ true. And whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?”* (4:122). 14:47 is the same truth in the negative, with an active participle that the commentators note is unique in this use: do not take Him to be a breaker of promise. Not-breaking is treated as belonging to what He is, not as a policy He might revise.

The promise in view is the one spoken in verses 13–14: We will destroy the wrongdoers and make you reside after them; and the wider word *“that they would surely be helped, and that Our forces will certainly prevail”* (37:172–173). Messengers were told to trust. This verse tells the last of them that the trust is not a mood. It is a reading of God's nature.

**Messengers Are the Named Beneficiaries, Because They Are the Named Targets**

The promise is *to His messengers*. Plots were against them. Exile was against them. “Bring a sign” was against them. If the promise failed, the failure would land on the people who had already given up fake signs and fake returns to the old creed. The verse therefore comforts a specific chest. It also warns a specific town: the One you are plotting against has appointments with the people you are plotting against, and He keeps them.

Abraham's prayer is inside this promise too. A messenger from the valley, asked for in 2:129, is walking around Mecca reciting. God's not-failing includes that long line. Quraysh's plot is a plot against a promise older than their fathers' fathers.

**Almighty, Capable of Punishment — the Names Under the Promise**

*Allah is indeed Almighty, capable of punishment.* *ʿAzīz* is the name from verse 1's path and verse 4's close. *Dhū intiqām* is Possessor of vengeance, capable of exacting a just repayment. A promise to messengers without power would be a wish. Power without just repayment would be a storm. Together they say the appointment will be kept in a way that matches the crime. The staring eyes, the vacant hearts, the evil settlement, the Fire as destination — those are not extra heat. They are what *intiqām* looks like when a people has been delayed, warned, shown ruins, and has gone on plotting.

Vengeance in English can sound like heat of temper. In this name it is the Owner collecting what *ẓulm* owes. Verse 2's woe and severe torment sit inside it. So does verse 7's severe punishment for ingratitude. The names at the end of 14:47 are a summary of the sūrah's God: not unaware, not a promise-breaker, not weak, not indifferent to what was done to His messengers and to the people those messengers tried to lead.

**Do Not Think Is Also Do Not Preach a Softer Ending**

The Prophet might be tempted, under harm, to offer a God who overlooks rather than a God who keeps appointments. This verse forbids that softening as a thought, which means it forbids it as a message. Leading out of darkness includes saying that the path's Owner pays. Gardens with peace are one payment. *Dhū intiqām* is the other. Dropping the second in order to make the town comfortable is a plot of another kind, against the promise from the inside.

The living who recite it in easier rooms are not outside the address. Any delay in seeing wrongdoers stopped can host the same thought. The verse does not give a date. It gives a nature. Dates belong to God. Natures, in this line, are already stated. He does not fail messengers. He is able to repay. That is enough to keep a people praying and spending before the Day, without needing the repayment to land this afternoon.

**The Promise Is the Mountain the Plot Could Not Move**

Verse 46 said plots do not overpower mountains. Verse 47 names the mountain in moral terms: a promise God does not fail. Trying to make a messenger look abandoned is trying to move that. The names *Almighty, capable of punishment* are why the mountain does not move. Might keeps the promise from being a bluff. Capacity to punish keeps the promise from being only a shelter with no gate for justice.

The next verse will change the earth and the heavens. That change is one way a promise to messengers is kept: the stage itself is replaced, and all appear before the One, the Supreme. 14:47 is the theology; 14:48 is the scenery of the keeping. The Prophet is told, between the two, not to host the thought that would make the scenery unnecessary. It is necessary. It is coming. It is not a failure of the One who promised. It is how He looks when He does not fail.

*Capable of punishment* also answers a piety that wants a God of promise without a God of repayment. Messengers are not the only ones with an appointment. Wrongdoers have one too. Keeping the first appointment includes keeping the second. A Lord who saved messengers and left *ẓulm* unweighed would have failed a different promise — the promise of verse 2, the woe, the severe torment, the evil settlement. The names at the close hold both appointments in one might. Recite them when delay is loud. They are louder, in the only scale this sūrah trusts, than a town that still has its gate.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:48

> ˹Watch for˺ the Day ˹when˺ the earth will be changed into a different earth and the heavens as well, and all will appear before Allah—the One, the Supreme

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Stage Is Not Permanent Either**

Watch for the Day when the earth will be changed into a different earth and the heavens as well. The land they called *ours*, the sky that sent rain, the sun and moon subjected for them — even these are not the last furniture. Al-Rāzī records two readings: the attributes change while the substance remains, or the change is so total that earth and heaven become something else in substance. The verse does not pick a laboratory. It picks a shock. *“When the sun is put out, and when the stars fall down, and when the mountains are blown away”* (81:1–3). *“When the sky splits open, and when the stars fall away”* (82:1–2). *“The ˹whole˺ earth will be in His Grip, and the heavens will be rolled up in His Right Hand”* (39:67). 14:48 is that family in one sentence: other earth, other heavens.

Verse 19 said He can eliminate you and produce a new creation. This is the large version, not only of a people but of the room the people stood in. Plots that cannot move mountains are now shown a Day when mountains are not the point. The room moves.

**All Will Appear Before the One, the Supreme**

And all will appear before Allah — the One, the Supreme. Al-Baghawī and al-Jalālayn: driven from graves, face to face with their Lord. *“The Day all will appear ˹before Allah˺. Nothing about them will be hidden from Him. ˹He will ask,˺ ‘Who does all authority belong to this Day? To Allah—the One, the Supreme!’”* (40:16). 14:21 already had them appearing. 14:48 puts the appearing after the cosmic change, so that no one thinks the court is held on the old floor. The old floor was part of the test. The court is held when the test's room has been folded.

*Al-Wāḥid al-Qahhār* — the One, the Paramount, the Supreme who overpowers. Equals of verse 30 have no desk at this appearing. Idols Abraham feared have no desk. Leaders have no desk except as defendants. Satan has a speech, not a throne. Oneness here is not a slogan. It is the staffing of the Day. Overpowering is not a mood. It is why the earth can be changed without a committee.

**Change of Earth Answers the Love of This World**

Verse 3's people preferred this life. This verse takes this life away as a surface. If you favoured the near earth, hear that it will be other than the earth. The preference is then revealed as a preference for a costume. Gardens in verse 23 are not this earth's parks upgraded. They belong to the order that begins when this order is changed. Hell's settlement is not a basement of the current crust. It is a dwelling in the new arrangement. Loving the present furniture as if it were the last furniture is the far stray.

Abraham asked for a secure city on this earth. That ask is not cancelled. It is placed. A secure city is for prayer until the earth is changed. After the change, security is the greeting of peace in gardens, or it is gone. Founding a valley is still holy. Mistaking the valley for the Last House is the Quraysh error.

**Appearance Is Public, so Hiding Is Over**

Verse 38 said nothing is hidden on earth or in heaven. When earth and heaven are changed, hiding has no furniture left. All appear. The lowly and the arrogant, the plotters, the people who passed ruins, the people who planted a good word. The One sees them as He always did. They see that they are seen. That is the difference. Unawareness, which verse 42 forbade as a thought about God, is now forbidden as a thought about the self. You are not offstage.

*The Supreme* also names why appearance cannot be declined. A court you can skip is not this court. *Qahhār* is the name that ends no-shows. The rush of verse 43 is the body agreeing.

**The Verse Is the Scenery of the Promise Just Made**

Do not think He will fail messengers, verse 47 said. This is what keeping looks like at the largest scale. Not only a local defeat of a town, though that happens. A replacement of the theatre in which towns existed. Messengers who were called liars will appear, and the people who called them liars will appear, and the room will not be the room in which the calling happened. That is more than revenge. It is truth made the climate.

A reader still on the present earth is being told the present earth is a term, like the term of verse 10. Use it as Abraham used a barren valley: pray, ask, thank, spend. Do not use it as a plotters' room. The different earth is not a rumour to decorate a sermon. It is the next floor. All will appear. The One, the Supreme, will not have to raise His voice. The change of the world will have been the raising.

Substance or attribute — al-Rāzī's two camps — can remain a scholarly fork. What a believer needs is the fork's trunk: this order is not the last order. Prayer toward a House on this earth is still commanded. Loving this earth as if folding it were impossible is still the far stray. Abraham's city is a trust until the trust's term ends. The Supreme who overpowers does not ask the city's permission to fold the map. Appearance before Him is the meaning of the fold. People of reason, who will be named in the last verse, can hold a House in the hand and a changed earth in the mind without dropping either. That double hold is mindfulness, not confusion.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:49

> On that Day you will see the wicked bound together in chains

**Expanded Commentary**

**Seeing Replaces Guessing**

On that Day you will see. The addressee is the Prophet ﷺ, and through him anyone who recites. The wicked — *al-mujrimīn*, the guilty, whom al-Jalālayn takes as the disbelievers — will be bound together in chains. Verse 43 was their running. This is their binding. Motion without freedom, then fastening without honour. The *jabbār* who compelled others is compelled. The dedicated followers who wanted a shield are chained to the people they chose. *Together* is the cruel mercy of the image: the hierarchy they loved is kept as hardware, not as help.

*“and others bound together in chains”* (38:38). *“And when they are tossed into a narrow place inside ˹Hell˺, chained together, then and there they will cry out for ˹instant˺ destruction”* (25:13). 14:49 is the first half of that family: the fastening, before pitch and faces in the next verse. The eye is told to look now, in recitation, so that the real looking will not be the first education.

**Together Is the End of the Private File**

They wanted to be a party. They are a party. The lowly asked to be protected as followers. The chain grants the togetherness and removes the protection. Satan said he could not save them nor they him. The chain is that truth made metal. A crowd that plotted in verse 46 is a crowd that can be looped in one pull. Individual account still stands — verse 51 will say every soul is repaid for what it earned. The chain does not cancel the soul. It shows that earned togetherness in *ẓulm* becomes a togetherness in restraint.

People who fear loneliness in the next life more than they fear God will find this image hard in a new way. The wicked are not left alone. They are left with each other, unable to leave each other. Company without peace is one of Hell's designs. Verse 23's greeting of *Peace* is company of the other kind. Choose which togetherness you are practising.

**Chains Answer the Threat of Exile**

They told messengers: we will expel you. Expulsion is a use of space: you will not be with us. Chains are a use of space: you will not be without us, and none of you will walk. The people who managed gates are managed. The land they called theirs is gone with the changed earth of verse 48. What they have left of “place” is a fastening to other guilty bodies. *Qarār* as evil dwelling in verse 29 now has furniture: fetters.

The seeing is also a promise to messengers who were bound, beaten, or boycotted in this life. The hardware moves sides. Not as a cartoon of revenge only. As a display of which power was real when both sides called for judgment in verse 15. The stubborn tyrant failed. The failure, on this Day, is visible as iron.

**The Wicked Are Named by Crime, Not by Tribe**

*Mujrimīn* is a moral name. Pedigree does not chain you. Crime does. Abraham's *whoever follows me is with me* is the opposite fastening, a fastening of creed that becomes peace. This fastening is creed too: the evil word, the equals, the covering of gifts. A person from Ishmael's valley can be in these chains. A person from a people with no House can be out of them. The sūrah has been working toward that sorting since verse 4.

You will see — the verb forbids a purely inner religion that never looks at outcome. The Book wants the outcome looked at while looking can still change a life. Chains in a verse are not there to train cruelty in the reader. They are there to train fear of becoming the kind of people who can be honestly grouped by guilt.

**Bound Is the Opposite of the Subjected World They Abused**

Sun, moon, ships, rivers were subjected *for you* as a trust. Chains are subjection *of you* as a sentence. The same Lord does both. The difference is the use of the first. Custodians who became tyrants meet a tighter *taskhīr*. The body that would not bow in prayer is made unable to move as it wills. Verse 40's ask to be made a performer of prayer looks, from here, like an ask to be trained in a binding that is honour — a binding to times and to a Lord — rather than this binding to other criminals.

The next verse will dress them. This verse only needs the grouping and the iron. A reader whose hands are still free is being asked what he is binding himself to in the delay: a good word that God makes firm, or a party that God will one day loop in a single chain. The seeing is future. The choosing is not.

Iron also answers the mouth that asked for a little delay in verse 44. A body in chains is a body whose little while is over. Following messengers, which they offered too late, is a following of people who were often bound in this life by rope and boycott. The hardware changes owners. The moral does not need to be preached as gloating. It needs to be preached as a reason to join the crowd that will not need iron: the crowd Abraham asked to have forgiven, the crowd that kept up prayer, the crowd whose togetherness is *Peace*. Chains are a togetherness too. The delay is for picking which. After the Day, the picking is a completed fact you will see, not a meeting you will still call. Choose the crowd while choosing is still a deed.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:50

> with garments of tar, and their faces covered with flames

**Expanded Commentary**

**Clothes Were Identity in the Town; They Are Identity Here Too**

Bound together, they now receive garments of tar, and their faces covered with flames. Al-Jalālayn takes the garments as shirts. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Jalālayn, and al-Ṭabrisī take pitch as a sign of how badly they will be scorched. Tar sticks. Tar burns. A shirt of it is a clothing that will not come off and will not stop being fuel. In this world they dressed for rank, for festival, for the gate. Here the cloth is the crime made wearable.

*“As for the disbelievers, garments of Fire will be cut out for them and boiling water will be poured over their heads, melting whatever is in their bellies, along with their skin”* (22:19–20). 14:50 is a shorter member of that clothing-line: pitch, then faces in flame. The sūrah has already given a drink of pus. It now gives a shirt and a face. The person is dressed for the settlement of verse 29. Nothing in the outfit is accidental.

**Faces Covered With Fire Is the End of Face as Honour**

Arabic *wajh* is face and honour both. Covering the face with flame is the end of the honour-system that swore by fathers and expelled messengers to keep a reputation. They would not face the message. They are faced with fire. *“And all faces will be humbled before the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining. And those burdened with wrongdoing will be in loss”* (20:111). Humbled faces there; flaming faces here. Both are the opposite of the raised heads of verse 43. Raised then, as a forced posture of fear. Covered now, as a burning that does not leave the part of the body that used to do pride.

Never blinking, in verse 43, meets flames on the face in verse 50. Sight was already punishment. Now the organ of sight is in a climate of fire. The images stack without needing a diagram. A preacher who turns the stack into relish has missed the sūrah's use. The stack is warning, the last visual before verse 51's justice-clause. Look while looking is still a choice.

**Tar Is Sticky Justice**

Pitch does not wash. The evil word had no stability, but it stained. This garment is the stain given weight and heat. Deeds that were ash in the storm of verse 18 are, from another angle, a shirt that will not blow off. Both pictures can be true: worthless as reward, clinging as punishment. You gain nothing from what you earned, verse 18 said. You still wear what you earned, this verse says. Nothing as credit. Everything as clothing.

The people who set up equals to enjoy themselves on the way to the Fire (v. 30) arrive, and the enjoyment's end is a fabric that enjoys nothing. The irony does not need a commentator. The sequence does the work.

**Covered Is Also Hidden, and Hidden Is Not Saved**

A face covered with flames is a face no one will honour and a face that cannot hide in a different sense: the covering is the exposure. There is no veil left that is not itself the penalty. Verse 38's God who knows conceal and reveal does not need to pull a cloth. The cloth is fire. Appearance before the One, the Supreme, in verse 48, is now appearance as a burning surface. The private inner covering of gifts has become a public outer covering of flame.

Together with chains, the picture is complete enough. Binding for the group. Shirt and face for the person in the group. Verse 51 can now say why without adding more furniture.

**The Living Still Choose a Garment**

Prayer in verse 31, the greeting of peace in verse 23, the fruit of the good tree — those are another clothing, spoken of elsewhere as garments of piety. This verse does not name that opposite, because its job is the wicked on that Day. The opposite is already in the sūrah. A reader who only collects Hell-images and does not establish prayer is wearing, in practice, toward tar. A reader who lets the image push him into verse 40's ask — make me keep up prayer — has used the image as it was sent.

Abraham asked to be kept from idols because they led many astray. Many, on this Day, are a chained crowd in pitch. The founder's fear had a face. This is the face. The delay in which a person can still change shirts is the delay of verse 42. It has not ended until the eyes stare. Until then the wardrobe is still, in some measure, a choice. After that the garment is given, and tar does not come off.

Pitch and flame also complete the drink of verses 16–17. Liquid inside, cloth outside, fire on the face: the person is a climate, not a tourist in one. Death from every side without dying is easier to hear once the shirt is tar. There is no dry corner of the self left to wait in. That is why verse 51 must follow as law. Without a matching of soul to earning, the climate would be a story. With it, the climate is what covering a blessing feels like when the covering is taken off and put back on as cloth. The living who still wear ordinary shirts are not being asked to enjoy the picture. They are being asked to spend, pray, and drop equals while cloth is still cloth and a face can still turn toward a House without burning. That turning is still open in the delay God has already named. It will not stay open once the eyes lock.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:51

> As such, Allah will reward every soul for what it has committed. Surely Allah is swift in reckoning

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Pictures Were a Recompense, Not a Spectacle**

As such — *li-yajziya* — Allah will reward every soul for what it has committed. The chains, the tar, the faces, the gardens earlier, the peace, the ash, the pus: they are all under this *so that*. God is not decorating a stage. He is paying souls. *“Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom’s weight. And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over and will give a great reward out of His grace”* (4:40). *“Today every soul will be rewarded for what it has done. No injustice Today! Surely Allah is swift in reckoning”* (40:17). 14:51 is that law placed under the last images so that no one can call them excess.

*Every soul* blocks the last transfer. Leaders, Satan, fathers, party, chain-mates — none of them take the file. 74:38 said every soul is detained for what it has done. This verse says the detention is a payment matching the doing. The chain grouped them. The payment still names them one by one.

**Reward Includes the Harsh Side of the Word**

English *reward* in the translation has to carry *jazāʾ*, which is repayment either way. Gardens are *jazāʾ*. Tar is *jazāʾ*. The soul that believed and did good is paid in rivers and peace. The soul that covered gifts and set up equals is paid in a settlement that burns. Using “reward” only for the first is a habit this verse will not allow. Both are what the soul committed, given back. Injustice would be a Garden for covering, or a Fire for a good word God made firm. The sūrah has been explaining why neither of those will happen.

What it has committed — *mā kasabat* — is the earning of verse 18, now with a different outcome for a different earner. Ash was the earning of disbelief, worthless as credit. This verse's earning is the whole moral harvest, good or bad, made into a match. Swift reckoning means the match will not be delayed once the Day has begun. The delay was before the Day, in verse 42. On the Day, the account that Abraham named as standing is a fast account.

**Swift Does Not Mean Careless**

*Surely Allah is swift in reckoning.* The name returns from *“Surely Allah is swift in reckoning”* (2:202) and its sisters (3:19, 3:199, 5:4, 24:39, 40:17). Swift because He already knows, verse 38, and does not need a long audit to discover a life. Swift because no soul will be left in a queue that is itself a second injustice. Swift because the One, the Supreme, does not share the bench. Human courts are slow because they are ignorant, many, and weak. This court is none of those.

Careless is the fear some people project onto speed. Atom's weight in 4:40 is the answer. Speed and precision are together in a Knower. Abraham could ask for forgiveness for a crowd of believers because the crowd will still be read as souls. The lowly will not be lost in the arrogant. The secret gift of verse 31 will not be lost in the public one.

**Every Soul Pulls the Sūrah Back to Responsibility**

Satan said blame yourselves. This verse is why he was legally right and morally vile. Right: the soul's earning is the soul's payment. Vile: he said it after calling. The calling was his earning. The response was theirs. Each file opens. Swift reckoning will not confuse the two, and will not let either hide in the other.

Messengers clarified. God guided or left to stray. People preferred, hindered, planted a word, thanked, covered. All of that is *what it has committed*. The theology of verse 4 does not erase the earning. It frames it. A soul that refused a clear tongue is not paid as if the tongue had been foreign. A soul that took the good word is not paid as if it had invented the word. Leave of the Lord and earning of the soul are the two rails the payment runs on.

**The Line Is the Right Quiet After the Fire-Images**

Without this verse the tar would hang in the air as heat. With it the heat has a why, and the why is the soul. A reader who wants more images is not given them. He is given a law and a name: swift in reckoning. The next verse will step all the way back to what the whole sūrah was: a message, a warning, a knowledge that God is One, a reminder for people who have minds. 14:51 is the hinge. Payment, then purpose. What you did, then why this Book was sent. The soul that hears both can still change what it will have committed by the time the reckoning, which is swift, begins.

Swiftness is also a mercy for the believer who feared that a secret gift would be lost in a crowd, or that a chief's volume would drown a quiet file. Atom's weight does not get lost in speed. It gets found because of knowledge that does not need to hunt. Abraham's bulk request for the believers is not a request that God skip the reading. It is a request that the reading, which will be exact and fast, end in cover for people whose earning was faith and good work. The wrongdoer's fear of speed is a different fear: there will be no long argument left, no little while, no friend at the bench. Both fears are reasonable. Only one of them still has a use in the delay. Use it: commit what you want the swift reading to find.

## Sūrah Ibrāhīm 14:52

> This ˹Quran˺ is a ˹sufficient˺ message for humanity so that they may take it as a warning and know that there is only One God, and so that people of reason may be mindful

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sūrah Names What It Has Been**

This is a sufficient message for humanity. Al-Qurṭubī takes the proclamation as the Qur'an, an admonition meant to put fear of punishment where it belongs. The translation's *sufficient* is a gloss on *balāgh*: a reaching, a delivery that has arrived. Verse 1 began with a Book sent to lead people out of darkness into light. Verse 52 ends by saying the delivery is enough. Not enough to force hearts — verse 4 already denied that. Enough as a message. No one who has heard this sūrah can claim the warning was not delivered, or that Oneness was not stated, or that a mind was given nothing to work with.

*For humanity* keeps the width of verse 1's *people*. Abraham's valley, Moses' people, the unnamed nations God alone counts — the message is not a clan memo. A messenger spoke it in one tongue so that it would be clear, verse 4. The clarity is now offered as a completed *balāgh*.

**Three Purposes, One Delivery**

So that they may take it as a warning, and know that there is only One God, and so that people of reason may be mindful. Warning is the first purpose. The pus, the chains, the tar, the staring eyes, the ruins, the failed plots — those are the warning working. Knowing that He is One is the second. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Baghawī: reflect until the fact of Oneness is understood, then worship Him and leave false gods, whether those gods are the world, the self's caprice, or Satan, as Ibn Ḥayyān lists. Mindfulness among people of intellect is the third. *Ulū al-albāb* are the ones whose cores still work — the opposite of vacant hearts in verse 43.

The three purposes are ordered like a path. Warning wakes. Oneness orients. Mindfulness continues. A person who only collects fear has stopped at one. A person who recites Oneness as a slogan without being warned has stopped at two. A person of reason who is mindful without bowing to the One has claimed the third as a club. The verse wants the three together, which is how the sūrah actually ran: terror and trees and Abraham's mouth and a Day of changed earth.

**People of Reason Are Not a Separate Religion**

*Albāb* are inner cores, minds that can hold a parable. *“Surely in this are signs for those who understand”* (16:12) was the close of a sister-list of subjected lights. This close names the understanders as the intended remainder of the warning. Al-Qushayrī: whoever takes heed is saved. 2:269, 3:7, 3:190, 13:19, 38:29, 39:9, 39:18, 39:21, 40:54 all honour this group. They are not philosophers on holiday from prayer. They are people who can hear a good tree and a bad tree and not need a fourth. The sūrah has been easy for them in that sense: two words, two trees, two settlements, two crowds. Reason here is the ability to live inside a binary the Book has made moral, not the ability to dissolve it.

Vacant hearts on the Day are what unused *albāb* become. Using them now is verse 25's *so perhaps they will remember*, restated as a class of person.

**Oneness Is the Knowledge the Whole Chapter Served**

Know that there is only One God. The path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy; the Owner of heavens and earth; the Originator the messengers asked about; the Lord who subjects sun and moon; the Hearer of Abraham; the One, the Supreme, on a changed earth — all of that is this knowledge in stages. Equals were the rival lesson. The message is sufficient to retire the rivals: idols, chiefs, Satan, this life as last house, plots as a second power. Knowing Oneness, in Ibn Ḥayyān's practical sense, is turning away from those. The sūrah has not left the turning abstract. Prayer, spending, thanks, a firm word, fear of the standing: those are how the knowledge looks when it is not only a sentence.

**The Book That Began With Letters Ends With Enough**

Alif-Lām-Rā opened a recitation whose meaning God did not decode. This verse closes a recitation whose purpose God does decode. Between them: a prophet leading into light, a people who prefer the near life, Moses reminding of days, nations with hands on mouths, trust under harm, a tyrant's drink, ash, a new creation that is not difficult, leaders who cannot shield, Satan who will not save, gardens of peace, two trees, a firm word, an exchanged blessing, a list of gifts no one can count, Abraham in a barren valley, a Day of staring and chains. The delivery of all that is called sufficient.

Sufficient does not mean the hearer is finished. It means the Sender has not under-sent. Warning, Oneness, mindfulness — the three tasks now sit with the people of reason and with anyone who still has a delay to use. The light of verse 1 is still the destination. The message of verse 52 is still the means. A soul that takes the warning, knows the One, and remembers, has done what this chapter was sent to make possible. The rest — gardens or tar — is the payment verse 51 already named, swift, matching what the soul committed after a Book this complete had reached it. A message called sufficient leaves the last insufficiency where it belongs: not in the Sender, in the hearer who will not take the warning, will not know the One, and will not remember. That hearer has not been under-taught. He has been, in this chapter's own word, far astray.
