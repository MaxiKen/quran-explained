# Sūrah al-Mujādilah (Chapter 58) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:1

> Indeed, Allah has heard the argument of the woman who pleaded with you ˹O Prophet˺ concerning her husband, and appealed to Allah. Allah has heard your exchange. Surely Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Private Grievance Receives a Public Hearing**

A woman's complaint about her marriage stands at the opening of this revelation. She has a husband, a problem, and words with which to seek relief. None of these is dismissed as too ordinary for divine attention. The verse begins with the assurance that Allah has heard her. Before any account of a remedy, it establishes that her experience has entered the sphere of judgment. Domestic suffering is not outside the concerns of faith merely because it occurs within a home.

The woman is described through her action rather than introduced by her family or rank. She argues her case and turns to Allah. This gives the verse a clear focus: the person seeking a hearing matters, not a social credential that might make her worth hearing. Her unnamed presence in the wording also prevents the lesson from depending on admiration for one famous person. The complaint is particular, but the principle does not require another distressed wife to possess the same name or standing. A relationship ordinarily treated as private can contain an injustice that deserves serious attention. Privacy is not a reason to treat the injured person's speech as an intrusion.

**Pleading With the Prophet Is Not Abandoning Trust in Allah**

Her appeal has two directions. She speaks to the Prophet about her husband and complains to Allah. The verse places these actions together without setting them against each other. Seeking a decision from a human authority does not cancel reliance on God; prayer does not require the person praying to stop explaining the facts. Her words belong both to a practical effort to resolve a dispute and to an act of dependence on the One who can grant relief.

Nor is the word argument necessarily a charge of irreverence. Within this verse, it names her sustained presentation of a difficulty. She does not disappear after saying that she is distressed. She presses the matter in an exchange that Allah hears. The wording gives no warrant for treating all insistence from a person seeking justice as insolence. At the same time, it does not establish that every argument must be correct simply because it is earnest. The decisive assurance concerns divine hearing, not an unlimited endorsement of every possible demand. The proper distinction is between refusing guidance and asking for a grievance to be understood. Here the woman addresses the Messenger while appealing to the Lord who sent him. Her need for an answer is expressed inside that relationship of trust.

**The Exchange Is Heard Beyond Human Limits**

The verse first identifies the woman's plea and then includes the conversation between her and the Prophet. Attention moves from one speaker's distress to the whole exchange. Allah does not receive an isolated quotation stripped of its reply, tone, or circumstances. His hearing encompasses the encounter. A judgment grounded in such knowledge is not vulnerable to the gaps that affect a witness who catches only part of a conversation.

A report from ʿĀʾishah supplies a closely relevant contrast. She recalls Khawlah coming to complain about her husband and explains that some of the woman's speech escaped her hearing, although Allah heard it. This is ʿĀʾishah's account, not a saying placed on the Prophet's lips: Sunan al-Nasāʾī 3460, graded ṣaḥīḥ by Darussalam on the checked edition ([Sunan al-Nasāʾī 3460](https://sunnah.com/nasai:3460)). The report identifies the complainant while showing the limit of a nearby human listener. It does not require us to invent a louder voice, a public assembly, or a dramatic interruption. Its force lies in the opposite direction. What a person nearby could miss was fully known to Allah. Divine hearing is not measured by the reach of human ears, and a quiet grievance is not a lesser grievance because few people hear it.

**Being Heard Does Not Depend on Performing Distress**

The woman does not have to make herself visible to a large audience before her need becomes real. Allah's hearing precedes any later fame attached to the incident. This matters because public recognition and actual suffering do not always coincide. People can speak without being believed, remain unseen while bearing a burden, or lack the skill to make their circumstances persuasive. The verse locates final knowledge beyond those unequal conditions. It does not promise that every listener will become fair, but it denies that human inattention is the last word about a person's case.

That assurance also creates a responsibility for those who receive complaints. They cannot possess divine knowledge, and the verse does not tell them to pretend otherwise. They can, however, avoid turning their own limited hearing into a verdict that nothing serious has happened. Listening, asking for clarification, and allowing the complainant to finish are appropriate responses to that limitation. These are implications of the scene rather than additional details about what occurred. The divine declaration honors a conversation that some people might regard as a distraction from more important business. It establishes that a person asking for help within marriage is not stepping outside religion's concerns. Her speech is among the words for which a just response must be sought.

**Hearing and Seeing Join the Words to Their Circumstances**

The closing names, All-Hearing and All-Seeing, explain why the opening assurance can be trusted. Allah knows the spoken plea, but His knowledge is not confined to what can be put into speech. Seeing accompanies hearing. The verse thus closes without separating the complaint from the life in which it arises. A person can conceal conduct behind an account of it, and a sufferer may struggle to describe what has happened. Neither concealment nor difficulty of expression creates a blind spot in divine knowledge.

These names are not merely an ending that could be detached from the incident. Hearing corresponds directly to the exchange; seeing completes the assurance that its reality is known. Together they bring comfort and accountability into the same sentence. The complainant is not forgotten, and the relationship about which she complains is not beyond scrutiny. The verse does not supply all the facts needed for an independent reconstruction of the dispute, so it should not be made to carry imagined scenes or speeches. Its own point is complete without them: a woman sought a hearing concerning her husband, the Prophet received her plea, and Allah declared that He had heard. The authority behind the response belongs to the One whose knowledge exceeds every human account of the encounter.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:2

> Those of you who ˹sinfully˺ divorce their wives by comparing them to their mothers ˹should know that˺ their wives are in no way their mothers. None can be their mothers except those who gave birth to them. What they say is certainly detestable and false. Yet Allah is truly Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Abusive Formula Cannot Create a Mother**

The verse breaks the authority of a marital declaration by exposing its false claim. A husband compares his wife to his mother in order to make their marital relationship forbidden. Allah answers that the comparison does not turn the wife into a mother. The point is more than a correction of vocabulary. The man has tried to make a personal utterance carry the force of a real relationship, with serious consequences for another person. Revelation refuses to let his claim define reality.

The practice is called ẓihār. Ibn Kathīr explains that this term comes from ẓahr, meaning the back, because the customary formula compared the wife to the speaker's mother's back. His commentary also identifies its use as a form of repudiation before Islam ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:2](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/2/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). That checked linguistic detail explains why an apparently strange bodily comparison could have such serious force. It was not a casual observation about resemblance. It used a recognized expression to deny marital intimacy. The verse does not accept the convention merely because people understand it. A custom can give words social power while leaving the assertion they contain both false and unjust. Here divine judgment separates those two facts.

**Birth Defeats the Husband's Invented Relationship**

The appeal to the woman who gave birth to him supplies a simple test that the husband's declaration cannot meet. His wife did not become his mother when he spoke. Nothing in her history changed, and nothing in his origin changed. An act of speech cannot manufacture the event of birth. The argument is deliberately plain because the convention depends on treating a familiar expression as though its use could override a fact everyone knows.

This is not a general rule that words never have legal effects. The verse itself judges a particular use of words and treats it as a serious wrong. A denial that speech can create biological motherhood does not amount to a denial that promises, testimony, and other declarations matter. Rather, the claim made here lacks the authority its speaker assigns to it. Nor should this sentence be detached from its dispute and used as a complete account of every kind of protected kinship. Its immediate concern is the supposed change from wife to mother produced by ẓihār. Reading it within that concern preserves the argument's precision. The wife remains someone whose marital position requires a truthful judgment; she is not a different relative because her husband has pronounced a comparison. The real relationship is not at his disposal to rename without limit.

**False Speech Can Also Be Morally Detestable**

The verse condemns the declaration on two grounds. It is false, and it is detestable. These judgments overlap without being identical. Falsehood concerns the relationship between the assertion and reality. Moral ugliness concerns what the assertion does within the marriage and the kind of conduct it expresses. A husband might insist that no one literally believes his wife gave birth to him. That defense would not remove the wrong. The convention still borrows the prohibition attached to motherhood and uses it to injure the marital bond.

Conversely, describing the declaration as merely rude would understate the verse. It is not only an unkind way of saying something otherwise legitimate. Allah rejects the claim on which the formula depends. This combination prevents a familiar escape: treating socially accepted language as innocent because everyone knows what it means. Shared understanding can explain how a harmful practice works; it cannot establish that the practice is right. The verse judges the inherited convention instead of allowing the convention to judge the wife. Its language also places responsibility on the speakers. The wrong is not attributed to the women against whom the words are used. Nothing here suggests that a wife's age, temperament, or ability to bear children could make a false declaration become true or morally sound.

**The Community Must Correct Its Own Accepted Conduct**

The address concerns people within the audience of revelation. The practice is not described only as something objectionable that outsiders do. Those receiving divine guidance must examine familiar conduct inside their own homes. Belonging to the community does not place inherited habits beyond criticism. Indeed, the direct address makes correction an internal obligation rather than an opportunity to congratulate oneself on the failings of others.

The translation uses the language of sinful divorce to communicate the social effect of the practice. That wording should not be made to settle every technical question about divorce law. Ibn Kathīr distinguishes the old use of ẓihār as divorce from the Islamic treatment of it, under which the utterance does not simply function as an ordinary final divorce. This distinction helps keep the verse's immediate argument clear. It invalidates the invented motherhood and condemns the speech; it does not leave the husband's formula as the ultimate rule governing the marriage. Nor does rejection of the formula mean nothing happened. The wife has been subjected to a wrongful declaration, and the speaker remains answerable for it. Between accepting an abusive custom and dismissing it as harmless, the verse establishes a third position: deny its false claim while taking its misuse of speech seriously.

**Pardon Opens a Future Without Renaming the Wrong**

The ending joins a severe moral judgment to the names Ever-Pardoning and All-Forgiving. The transition does not retract the condemnation. Falsehood has not become truth, and the detestable has not become acceptable. Instead, the speaker is not confined forever to the wrong he has done. Divine mercy makes correction meaningful because guilt need not be the final description of a person's relation to Allah.

This ordering matters. If pardon were mentioned without identifying the wrong, the injured wife could hear only an excuse for her husband's conduct. If condemnation stood alone, the speaker might imagine that acknowledgment can accomplish nothing. The verse gives neither message. It names the act accurately and then names the Lord whose forgiveness exceeds the act. Mercy belongs to Allah, not to the husband's power to declare his own behavior harmless. The verse therefore cannot be used to pressure the wife to pretend that no injury occurred. Its promise concerns the character of divine judgment, where pardon is possible without confusion about the offense. The argument remains focused on this declaration: a wife is not transformed into a mother by comparison, the claim is morally and factually wrong, and the path back to Allah is not closed by the fact that the wrong has been exposed.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:3

> Those who divorce their wives in this manner, then ˹wish to˺ retract what they said, must free a slave before they touch each other. This ˹penalty˺ is meant to deter you. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do

**Expanded Commentary**

**Retraction Requires More Than a Change of Mood**

A husband who wants to undo his wrongful declaration cannot simply resume married life as though the words had never been spoken. The verse attaches an action to retraction. This prevents the husband's shifting wishes from becoming the only rule: first he repudiates the marital relation through a false comparison, then he expects its privileges to return whenever he desires them. The wife is not required to live under a sequence of declarations whose force is determined solely by his convenience.

The rule concerns ẓihār, the repudiating comparison of a wife to a mother. It should not be generalized into a claim that every form of divorce can be withdrawn by freeing an enslaved person. The demonstrative expression in the translation ties this ruling to the specific manner of repudiation under discussion. Its scope is therefore narrow enough to be applied meaningfully. At the same time, the ethical structure is clear: a damaging use of speech creates responsibility that cannot be erased by a second utterance alone. Wanting a different outcome is the beginning of a return, not proof that repair is complete. The required action places a real obligation between the abusive statement and the restoration the speaker now seeks.

**The Meaning of Return Has Legal Consequences**

The Arabic expression concerning return to what was said generated different legal readings. Ibn Kathīr records al-Shāfiʿī's view that retaining the wife after the declaration, with an opportunity to divorce her but without doing so, constitutes the relevant return. He also records Aḥmad's focus on resuming intercourse or intending it, and reports more than one formulation from Mālik ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:3](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/2/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). These are positions about what activates the obligation, not disagreements over whether the abusive declaration was commendable.

The textual issue can be stated without pretending that the verse contains a complete account of each school's reasoning. Keeping the marriage in place conflicts with a declaration that rejects its marital character; this makes continued retention relevant. Wanting intercourse more directly reverses the prohibition expressed through the comparison; this makes intention and conduct relevant. The translation adopts the sense of wishing to retract. For explaining that wording, intended restoration is the most direct starting point. It would nevertheless be too strong to turn that explanatory preference into a verdict that all other legal formulations lack grounds. The verse firmly establishes the required sequence even where jurists differ over the exact point at which return is legally present. A specific marital case needs that distinction, not a compressed list of school names masquerading as a ruling.

**Release Gives the Penalty a Beneficiary Beyond the Couple**

The required act is the freeing of an enslaved person. The penalty therefore does not consist only of an inward feeling or a statement addressed to the injured wife. It changes another person's condition. A man who has used speech to impose a wrongful restriction within his marriage must undertake an act of release beyond it. This is an ethical relation between offense and remedy visible in the verse, though it should not be presented as an exhaustive explanation of Allah's reasons.

The act has a concrete beneficiary. Someone's freedom is the substance of the obligation, not a symbol that can be satisfied by declaring oneself more generous. The verse thus connects repair to a change in the world outside the wrongdoer's emotions. It also treats emancipation as a required good rather than leaving the matter entirely to optional kindness. This specific command does not by itself provide a full history of slavery or a comprehensive account of every legal condition attached to manumission. Those are larger questions requiring their own evidence. Its own contribution is exact: in this case, restoration of marital intimacy is preceded by a mandated release from enslavement. Reducing the penalty to the husband's inconvenience would overlook the person whose life the required action directly changes.

**The Order of Actions Protects the Rule From Evasion**

The requirement must be fulfilled before the spouses touch one another. Ibn Kathīr reports an explanation from Ibn ʿAbbās that the touching here means sexual intercourse, and also records stricter restrictions on preliminary physical contact in the discussion. The shared center is not obscure: the husband may not claim the restored sexual relationship first and leave the commanded repair for an indefinite later time. The obligation stands before access, not as an optional payment after it.

That order prevents a familiar form of evasion. A person can acknowledge a duty verbally while arranging circumstances so that there is never a convenient time to fulfill it. Here the desired resumption is itself held back until the required action has been performed. The verse does not rely only on the speaker's promise to behave better. It gives the obligation a place in the sequence of conduct. Nor is the burden transferred to the wife. The husband made the declaration, and the command addresses those who made it. Mutual language about touching describes the relation to be resumed; it does not make the wife responsible for his wrongful words. Distinguishing the shared act from the individual obligation keeps the verse from becoming another means of pressuring the person against whom ẓihār was pronounced.

**Deterrence Reaches Conduct That Others Cannot Inspect**

The verse explains the penalty as a deterrent and closes with Allah's awareness of action. These clauses connect the visible requirement with conduct that may occur in private. The rule is not designed merely to produce an acceptable public account of what the husband has done. It addresses the actual order of his behavior, whether or not other people can inspect the household or verify his intentions.

Deterrence also looks beyond the present wish to reconcile. A serious consequence makes the original declaration harder to treat as a casual weapon during anger. The speaker is given reason to restrain his tongue before creating a problem that requires substantial repair. That purpose does not turn the penalty into retaliation for its own sake. The act required benefits another person and permits an ordered return rather than leaving the marriage under an invented permanent prohibition. Allah's knowledge holds the whole process together: the declaration, the desire to retract, the required release, and the timing of resumed intimacy. No clever description can substitute for doing what is commanded. The verse therefore makes responsibility both practical and inwardly serious. A return must become an act under a known rule, not merely a private conviction that the offense should now be forgotten.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:4

> But if the husband cannot afford this, let him then fast two consecutive months before the couple touch each other. But if he is unable ˹to fast˺, then let him feed sixty poor people. This is to re-affirm your faith in Allah and His Messenger. These are the limits set by Allah. And the disbelievers will suffer a painful punishment

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inability Changes the Duty Rather Than Erasing It**

The verse makes room for two distinct limits: lack of means and inability to fast. A husband who cannot afford the required emancipation is directed to fasting. If he cannot undertake that fast, he must feed the poor. These are ordered alternatives, not three interchangeable ways of selecting whatever seems easiest. Each transition is tied to a stated inability. The form of the obligation changes because the person's capacity matters; the responsibility to respond does not disappear merely because its first form is unavailable.

This structure keeps justice and mercy together. A command with no allowance for genuine incapacity could demand what a person cannot do. A permission to choose freely among alternatives could turn a serious corrective into a matter of convenience. The verse takes neither course. It recognizes a difference between lacking resources, lacking physical ability, and simply preferring another option. That distinction is essential to its application. It also prevents poverty from being treated as proof of a lesser moral standing. The person without wealth is still addressed as someone capable of an accountable response, though that response may take a different form. Divine law here does not confuse an inability to perform one act with a refusal to submit to any act at all.

**Consecutive Fasting Makes Return a Sustained Commitment**

The fast extends through two consecutive months. Consecutive means the prescribed period is connected, not a collection of occasional days fitted around preference. The verse does not merely require that a total amount of fasting be accumulated at some convenient future time. Its wording makes continuity part of the commanded action. That gives the correction a sustained shape: the person must live under an ordered restraint rather than dispose of the matter in one quick expression of regret.

The restriction on sexual relations remains in place during this required preparation. Fasting is not presented as permission to resume intimacy while gradually completing the penalty. The command places completion before the couple's return to that aspect of married life. This timing protects the obligation from indefinite postponement. It also means that the desire for reconciliation cannot be used to pressure the wife into overlooking the unfinished requirement. The verse itself does not list every event that might interrupt a consecutive fast or determine which interruptions require restarting. Those practical cases belong to detailed legal treatment. The plain rule should be taught without inventing exceptions, but also without pretending that a short commentary has settled every illness, journey, or involuntary interruption. What is certain here is the ordered and continuous character of the prescribed fast.

**Feeding the Poor Turns Incapacity Toward Another Good**

When fasting cannot be undertaken, the command is to feed sixty poor people. The alternative has recipients, not merely a monetary figure attached to it. Their need is part of the act's purpose as a form of expiation. The husband who cannot carry out the bodily discipline is not told simply to announce that he is excused. He is directed toward an action that supplies others with food. The response to one household's wrongdoing thus reaches people outside that household.

The specified number must not be dissolved into the vague instruction to be charitable. Sixty gives the command a definite scale. At the same time, this verse does not state a modern weight, cash price, or menu. Turning it into a universal payment figure without further legal evidence would add a rule the wording does not supply. Questions about food quantities and whether repeated provision to one recipient satisfies a school's interpretation require careful verification. They should not be settled by casually equating a historical measure with two meals or with a present-day currency amount. The immediate point is strong enough without such shortcuts: inability is accommodated through a defined act of provision, and the poor are actual beneficiaries of the required repair rather than illustrations of the husband's renewed good intentions.

**The Founding Case Shows That Help Can Make Repair Possible**

A checked report gives the rule a human setting. Khuwaylah describes her husband's lack of means and inability to fast, after which the Prophet directs that sixty poor people be fed. She offers to contribute a second basket of dates to help fulfill the obligation. The account is in Sunan Abī Dāwūd 2214. Al-Albānī grades it ḥasan with an exception concerning the basket-measure clause; that qualification is displayed with the report and must not be dropped ([Sunan Abī Dāwūd 2214](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2214)). No precise basket capacity is used here as an authenticated measure.

The report adds more than a memorable detail. It shows that a required act can be made possible through assistance rather than through denial of the husband's poverty. Help does not abolish the command; it enables performance. Khuwaylah's contribution also appears as her offered participation, not a rule making every injured wife pay for her husband's declaration. Her generosity cannot be converted into a burden imposed on other women. The distinction preserves both her agency and the location of responsibility. The legal demand remains serious, while the people involved need not be left alone with a duty they cannot presently finance. Mercy can operate through practical support without falsifying the account of who committed the wrong.

**Faith Accepts Limits Instead of Preserving a Harmful Custom**

The verse explicitly connects the ruling to faith in Allah and His Messenger. Belief is not treated as an inward claim unrelated to how a marriage is governed. Accepting the revealed correction means allowing it to displace the husband's inherited power to use a wrongful formula. The required acts are therefore not isolated penalties attached to an otherwise unchanged attitude. They train conduct under a different authority.

The phrase limits set by Allah names a boundary that desire does not redraw. A husband cannot insist that his private wish for intimacy authorizes him to ignore the prescribed sequence. Nor can the community claim loyalty to revelation while treating the old practice as untouchable. The warning to disbelievers makes refusal of divine authority grave, but it should not be flattened into an automatic declaration that every lapse in carrying out the rule expels a person from faith. The verse distinguishes the order to reaffirm belief from the warning about disbelief; it does not provide a complete procedure for judging particular individuals. Its practical demand is submission to a known boundary, with allowance for genuine incapacity and no allowance for making convenience the law.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:5

> Indeed, those who defy Allah and His Messenger will be debased, just like those before them. We have certainly sent down clear revelations. And the disbelievers will suffer a humiliating punishment

**Expanded Commentary**

**Resistance to Guidance Is Not a Rival Source of Authority**

The verse addresses those who place themselves in defiance of Allah and His Messenger. Their stance is active opposition, not simply the presence of a difficulty they have not yet understood. The warning belongs to the relation between revealed authority and a will that refuses it. A person may possess wealth, influence, or an accepted custom on his side, but none of these turns resistance into a competing right to define what Allah has commanded.

The pairing of Allah and His Messenger gives the offense its full direction. The Messenger is not an independent deity whose claims stand alongside Allah's claims. He communicates the guidance of the One who sent him. Opposition to that guidance cannot be made harmless by saying that one accepts God in some general sense while rejecting the authority of His messenger. At the same time, this verse is not a permission for every religious speaker to identify disagreement with himself as defiance of Allah. An interpreter can misunderstand, an official can act unjustly, and a question can seek truth rather than resist it. The text's severity depends on the authority actually being opposed. Keeping that distinction clear protects the warning from misuse while leaving its demand for obedience fully intact.

**Debasement Reverses the Claim to Stand Above the Command**

The promised outcome is debasement. This concerns more than the frustration of a plan. It overturns the assumed standing of people who act as though divine direction can be dismissed without consequence. Their posture claims a kind of superiority; the judgment exposes that claim as empty. What they regarded as freedom from obedience does not become freedom from accountability.

Ibn Kathīr explains the verse's final humiliation as a fitting response to proud refusal to follow and submit. That checked explanation adds a moral relation between the stance and its consequence: the abasement answers arrogance, rather than functioning as an unexplained display of force ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:5](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/5/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This is the commentator's interpretation of the verse, not a separate Prophetic report. It helps distinguish divine judgment from the human wish to humiliate an opponent simply because he is disliked. The passage identifies a refusal of revealed authority and declares its end. It does not command listeners to invent degrading treatment of people they accuse. The right response is to abandon the conceit that resistance establishes independence. Allah's command is not weakened because a person regards obedience as beneath him, and a person's social standing does not alter that relation.

**Earlier Resistance Does Not Establish a Successful Precedent**

The comparison with people before them places the present stance within a longer pattern. Defiance can feel new to those practicing it, especially when its language or social setting differs from earlier examples. The verse denies that novelty of circumstance changes the basic relation. Earlier opponents were also brought low. The current group has no reason to imagine that its resources or confidence create a new law of immunity.

No earlier nation is named in this sentence. That restraint should be respected. A commentary need not supply a procession of historical examples in order to make the comparison effective, especially when each example would require its own careful account. The argument rests on similarity of stance and outcome, not on a claim that every event in history happened in the same way. Nor does the comparison produce a timetable for when every opponent must visibly fall. It asserts a pattern under divine judgment without giving the reader a calendar. The lesson is directed against exceptionalism: the belief that what applied to others cannot apply to oneself. Remembering earlier judgment is useful only if it challenges that belief. Treating the past as a store of other people's failures while preserving one's own imagined exemption would leave the verse's warning unanswered.

**Clear Revelations Make Refusal Different From Being Left Unguided**

The central statement about clear revelations gives the warning its setting of communicated guidance. Allah has not left people to infer every obligation from uncertain appearances and then condemned them for failing to guess correctly. Revelation has been sent down. The clarity claimed here belongs to the evidence and guidance Allah provides, not to the supposed infallibility of every human explanation offered in His name.

That distinction guards against a serious abuse. A teacher cannot settle a disputed interpretation merely by pointing to the general clarity of revelation and declaring every question rebellious. Clear divine communication and a person's complete understanding of every application are not the same thing. The verse concerns defiance in the presence of guidance; it does not abolish the work of learning, asking, and distinguishing sound interpretation from error. At the same time, complexity in some questions must not become a universal excuse for disregarding what is clear. One cannot turn the existence of difficult cases into a claim that no command has binding force. The sentence therefore presses toward an honest response to available guidance. Evidence is not sent merely to decorate a community's speech. Its arrival establishes a claim upon conduct, and deliberate refusal of that claim is not equivalent to innocent lack of information.

**Humiliating Punishment Denies That Unbelief Confers Dignity Before Allah**

The final sentence names a punishment marked by humiliation. Pain and humiliation are not identical descriptions. Pain concerns suffering; humiliation concerns the collapse of pretended standing. In this verse, the latter answers a stance that refuses to bow to revealed authority. The closing therefore returns to the promise of debasement with a focus on the character of the consequence, not merely on the fact that there will be one.

This does not mean that believers are invited to strip others of ordinary human dignity. A divine description of punishment is not a command to insult, abuse, or deny fair treatment to anyone labeled an unbeliever. Nor should the sentence be used as a shortcut for judging the faith of particular people whenever they commit a wrong. It gives a grave warning concerning disbelief and defiance; it does not supply all the knowledge needed to decide every person's inward condition. Its force falls first on the claim that rejecting guidance can secure an honorable independence from Allah. Such independence does not exist. The verse joins clear communication, historical warning, and promised abasement so that resistance cannot present itself as a safe or superior position. Guidance asks for a response, and social confidence cannot make that response unnecessary.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:6

> On the Day Allah resurrects them all together, He will then inform them of what they have done. Allah has kept account of it all, while they have forgotten it. For Allah is a Witness over all things

**Expanded Commentary**

**Resurrection Brings the Whole Group Before Its Judge**

The people described are raised together. No one can remain outside the reckoning by outliving a witness, moving elsewhere, or belonging to a generation that came later. The gathering closes the distance that earthly life places between actors and the consequences of their actions. Death has not dissolved the identity of those who acted, and the passage of time has not dissolved their responsibility. Allah raises the people whose deeds will be disclosed.

Together does not mean that individual responsibility is absorbed into a single group identity. The object of the coming disclosure is what they have done. Each person's conduct remains part of the account even when everyone is present at once. A shared setting therefore does not provide anonymity. Nor does the verse require the reader to imagine the size or layout of the gathering in order to understand its force. Its first claim is sufficient: those who acted within the scattered conditions of earthly life will be assembled under one judgment. Separation had allowed them different opportunities to conceal, forget, or escape notice. Resurrection removes those practical advantages without erasing the particularity of what each person did. The gathering is comprehensive, and the account remains concerned with actual conduct.

**Disclosure Makes Their Own Actions the Subject of Judgment**

Allah will inform them of their deeds. Judgment is not presented as an unexplained sentence delivered to people who can never know what it concerns. Their own actions become the subject of divine disclosure. What they did may have been renamed, justified, concealed, or displaced from attention during life. The disclosure returns the matter to its truth, beyond the descriptions by which they made it acceptable to themselves or others.

The verse's order is important: resurrection is followed by being told. The people concerned are present for the unveiling of their account. This is not merely information stored somewhere beyond their awareness. They must confront it. At the same time, the sentence does not make Allah's knowledge depend on their agreement with the account. He is the One who informs, not a judge awaiting facts that only the defendants can supply. Their knowledge changes; His does not. Ibn Kathīr explains the disclosure as including the details of deeds, whether good or evil ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:6](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/5/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). That explanation fits the comprehensive language without narrowing the account to only the actions a person considers memorable. The moment of disclosure is therefore also a reversal of control over the story one has told about one's life.

**Forgotten Does Not Mean Removed From the Account**

The sharpest contrast is between Allah's preserved account and their forgetting. Human memory loses details even when no one is trying to hide them. It can also become selective, keeping an injury suffered while neglecting an injury inflicted. The verse does not specify a single psychological cause for the forgetting. Its point holds across those possibilities: what has left their attention has not left Allah's knowledge.

This does not make ordinary forgetfulness a separate offense in every case. The warning concerns the mistaken inference that an action no longer matters because its actor no longer remembers it. Forgetting what one did is different from being forgiven for it. Time can weaken recollection without supplying moral repair. The distinction makes accountability more exact than a person's present ability to narrate the past. A remembered deed and a forgotten deed are not judged by different standards merely because one is easier to recall. It also means that a wrongdoer's faded memory does not erase the reality of another person's suffering. An account governed only by the offender's recollection would be unstable and often unjust. The verse places the record beyond that instability. No lapse in the creature's memory creates a corresponding gap in the knowledge of the Creator.

**Divine Witness Makes the Record More Than a Human Archive**

Allah is a Witness over all things. This closing description grounds the preserved account in direct and complete knowledge. Human archives depend on what someone noticed, what was written, what survived, and how later readers understood it. Even a carefully maintained record can omit an event that no one saw or mistake the meaning of a fragment. The verse's account is not subject to those limits because its ultimate witness is Allah.

The claim also joins outward action to its full reality. A deed can look generous while serving a concealed purpose, or look insignificant while involving an important act of faithfulness. This verse does not offer an exhaustive list of the inward matters included in judgment, but its comprehensive ending leaves no basis for imagining a protected zone outside divine knowledge. Witness is not an ornamental title added after the problem of forgetting. It explains why the account remains complete despite that forgetting. Nor is the image one of Allah consulting a record because He has lost the facts. Preservation and witnessing are presented together. The record is trustworthy because nothing was absent from His knowledge when it happened and nothing becomes absent afterward. That is the foundation on which the disclosure rests, not the survival of a human chain of recollection.

**Brief Speech Belongs to the Same Serious Account**

A checked parallel in 50:18 describes even an uttered word as attended by an observer ready to record it ([Qurʾān 50:18](https://quran.com/qaf/18)). This adds a concrete case to the general statement here without turning the commentary into an account of another passage. Words can vanish from memory almost as soon as they are spoken. Their short duration does not mean they fall outside the deeds Allah preserves. A remark that took seconds to utter can outlast the speaker's recollection in its effect on another person.

The parallel helps prevent an overly narrow picture of accountability as a list of only major public events. The present verse says Allah has kept account of all that concerns the deeds being disclosed. It does not assign people the impossible task of reproducing that complete record from memory before they can turn toward Him. The response available now is more practical: take responsibility for known wrongs, cease treating concealment as safety, and recognize that divine knowledge is more accurate than self-description. The assurance of complete witness also gives meaning to unnoticed fidelity. Although the warning concerns those facing judgment in this passage, the reason given is universal in scope: Allah witnesses everything. The forgotten act is not the lost act, and being unseen by people is not being outside the account.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:7

> Do you not see that Allah knows whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth? If three converse privately, He is their fourth. If five, He is their sixth. Whether fewer or more, He is with them wherever they may be. Then, on the Day of Judgment, He will inform them of what they have done. Surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Closed Conversation Remains Within an Open Knowledge**

The opening question asks for recognition of something already implied by belief in Allah: His knowledge encompasses heaven and earth. A private meeting cannot be an exception to that scope. Its participants occupy a place within creation, and their words occur within the world He knows. Closing a door changes who among human beings can listen; it does not change the conversation's relation to its Creator. The argument moves from the largest field of knowledge to a small and carefully enclosed human setting.

Najwā means private consultation or confidential speech. Its privacy describes the relation between the speakers and other people, not a successful withdrawal from divine knowledge. This distinction is the verse's foundation. People can legitimately know something their neighbors do not know. They cannot turn that limited human secrecy into absolute secrecy. The question therefore confronts a practical contradiction: affirming Allah's universal knowledge while behaving as though an unobserved meeting lies outside it. The answer does not depend on discovering the location of a particular gathering. Wherever it occurs, it is already within the scope stated at the beginning. The heavens and earth leave no remainder in which a creature can establish independence from being known.

**The Numbers Remove Every Imagined Exception**

Three speakers are not alone in the sense they imagine, and neither are five. The fourth and sixth express the inescapability of divine knowledge in terms drawn from the speakers' own count. They can count the people admitted to their discussion, but that count does not identify everyone who knows it. Human membership and complete knowledge are different matters. Allah need not become an invited participant for their words to be fully known to Him.

The sentence then includes numbers smaller and larger than those named. This closes off a superstitious reading in which three and five have a unique power or a peculiar danger. The examples are not a code for selecting a safer group size. Their purpose is comprehensive, and the explicit inclusion of other numbers supplies the proof. A pair cannot claim exemption because it is smaller, nor can a crowd gain concealment through its size. The movement from particular numbers to every number also gives the warning a concrete force. Universal statements can remain distant in thought; a counted conversation is easy to picture. The verse brings the universal claim into that familiar scene and then makes clear that changing the scene's details cannot defeat the claim.

**Being With Them Is Not Becoming One of Their Bodies**

The opening and closing concern knowledge. That frame governs the statement that Allah is with the speakers. Ibn Kathīr explains the accompanying presence here through Allah's complete knowledge, hearing, and sight. He also preserves Aḥmad's observation that the verse begins and ends by naming divine knowledge ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:7](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/7/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked observation supplies a reason for the interpretation rather than merely an appeal to a scholar's name. The context itself identifies what the statement is asserting.

A bodily reading would mistake the conversational illustration for a description of Allah occupying another seat. Nothing in the verse requires that picture. Its concern is not the addition of a created person but the impossibility of escaping divine awareness. Knowledge is not a weak substitute for presence here: it includes every word and circumstance that the speakers might try to conceal. Nor does being with them mean approving their purposes. The verse makes the statement about all such gatherings, including those whose deeds will require exposure. Accompaniment in knowledge must therefore be distinguished from special help or favor. Allah can know a meeting completely while condemning what its participants choose to do within it.

**Location Cannot Turn Secrecy Into Safety**

The words concerning wherever they may be remove spatial escape after numerical escape has been removed. A different room, another settlement, or an isolated place does not place a conversation beyond Allah. The point is not that some places are easier for Him to observe than others. All location belongs to the creation His knowledge encompasses. Moving a meeting can prevent a human observer from hearing it, but it cannot move the speakers outside that relation.

This produces accountability without authorizing indiscriminate human intrusion. Allah's complete knowledge is not a command that every person must expose every private matter to everyone else. Human beings remain limited, and the verse does not appoint them universal investigators of one another. Its direct demand falls on the speakers: act with recognition that concealment from people does not eliminate responsibility. Privacy can serve trust, care, or necessary deliberation; it can also serve misconduct. What matters for judgment is what the participants actually do, not the mere existence of a closed conversation. The theological assurance makes secrecy morally serious without making privacy itself a sin. A person who preserves another's confidence and a person who hides a harmful scheme are both known, but their purposes are not thereby made equivalent.

**A Secret Will Become Part of an Account Its Speakers Hear**

The verse ends with future disclosure. Allah will tell the participants what they did on the Day of Judgment. Knowledge is therefore not presented as an abstract divine attribute with no consequence for the meeting. What is known now will become an account heard by those who acted. A conversation designed to control who can know something will not control the final disclosure of its own reality.

The object of that disclosure is their conduct, not simply a transcript of sounds. Private deliberation can form intentions, coordinate actions, and assign responsibilities. The participants may remember different parts or later describe their involvement differently. Allah's perfect knowledge does not depend on their agreement about what happened. The closing assurance makes His account reliable precisely where human accounts may be partial or self-serving. It also places the proper response in the present. The speakers need not wait for their discussion to become public before asking whether it is truthful and right. They already know that public discovery is not the ultimate test of accountability. The verse thus joins universal knowledge, intimate conversation, unrestricted place, and final disclosure into one argument: what can be hidden from other people cannot be removed from Allah's knowledge or from the judgment grounded in it.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:8

> Have you not seen those who were forbidden from secret talks, yet they ˹always˺ return to what they were forbidden from, conspiring in sin, aggression, and disobedience to the Messenger? And when they come to you ˹O Prophet˺, they greet you not as Allah greets you, and say to one another, “Why does Allah not punish us for what we say?” Hell is enough for them—they will burn in it. And what an evil destination

**Expanded Commentary**

**Returning After a Prohibition Makes the Conduct Deliberate**

The people described have already received a prohibition and have resumed the same conduct. Their fault cannot be explained as simple ignorance of the boundary. The movement back to what was forbidden reveals a decision to preserve the practice despite correction. Private speech becomes a means of sustaining defiance rather than an innocent exercise in confidentiality.

Three objects of their consultation are named: sin, aggression, and disobedience to the Messenger. These overlap without being empty repetition. Sin marks the conduct as wrong before Allah. Aggression identifies its movement against others. Disobedience names the rejection of the authority that has already told them to stop. Ibn Kathīr makes this distinction between misconduct concerning the speakers themselves and wrongdoing that reaches other people ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:8](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/8/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). Their meeting is thus judged by its purpose and persistence, not merely by its low voices. The verse does not make every confidential conversation suspect. It identifies a specific kind of counsel whose participants know the rule, coordinate harmful purposes, and return to the practice. Secrecy provides them a setting for mutual encouragement in something they have already been required to abandon.

**An Apparent Greeting Can Carry an Intended Injury**

The scene changes from private consultation to a face-to-face greeting. The speakers approach the Prophet with words that resemble courtesy but do not convey the greeting Allah gives him. Their outward participation in a familiar social form conceals an opposite intention. This joins the two scenes: private counsel hides its purpose from observers, while the greeting hides its hostility inside an apparently acceptable form.

A verified report from ʿĀʾishah records a group using al-sām, death, rather than the greeting of peace. She understood the hostile wording and replied sharply. The Prophet restrained her response and explained that he had already answered them by returning the words upon their speakers. This is Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6256 ([Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6256](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6256)). The report establishes the incident and the distinction between the two expressions without requiring an invented speech. It concerns the group that performed the act, not every person sharing their religious identity. The moral fault lies in the intentional misuse of a greeting. A social sign normally offering safety has been turned into a vehicle for wishing harm while retaining enough resemblance to ordinary courtesy to evade straightforward acknowledgment.

**The Prophet Recognized the Insult Without Copying Its Excess**

The same report supplies a detail that changes how the confrontation should be understood: the Prophet's restrained reply was not caused by failure to hear. He understood and had already responded. His instruction to ʿĀʾishah concerning gentleness therefore joins awareness with self-command. Recognition of hostility does not require answering it with every expression anger makes available.

This is relevant to the verse's moral account, not a softening of the offense. The hostile greeting remains condemned even though the immediate human response is measured. Those who equate restraint with approval would misunderstand the incident. Those who equate zeal for the Prophet with uncontrolled abusive speech would also miss his own example. The checked report gives a reply that neither accepts the false courtesy nor allows the offender to dictate the responder's manners. The insult is returned without a wider eruption. That distinction is especially important because the verse reserves final judgment to Allah. A clear divine condemnation does not make every possible human reaction equally appropriate. The Prophet's conduct demonstrates that firmness and gentleness can operate together when the facts are understood. One need not choose between pretending the injury did not occur and reproducing the aggressor's moral disorder.

**Delayed Punishment Is Turned Into a False Test of Truth**

The speakers ask why Allah has not punished them for their words. Their question is not a humble request for guidance. It treats the absence of immediate punishment as evidence that their mockery is safe or that the Messenger's claim is false. They have invented a timetable and made it a condition that divine truth must satisfy. If no penalty arrives at once, they imagine themselves vindicated.

Ibn Kathīr explains the challenge in precisely this connection with their abuse of the greeting. Their reasoning moves from present survival to supposed immunity. The inference fails because divine judgment is not controlled by the interval they choose to observe. A person who commits a wrong and remains unharmed for the moment has learned something about the timing of consequences, not necessarily about innocence. The wording can be understood as what they say inwardly or among themselves; either way, the challenge exposes an assumption that strengthens their shared defiance. Its danger lies in self-confirmation. Every unpunished repetition seems to them another proof that the conduct carries no cost. The verse breaks that pattern by naming a consequence beyond the narrow span in which they have decided to look for one.

**The Destination Answers the Challenge Without Accepting Its Timetable**

Hell is declared sufficient for them. The answer does not promise an immediate spectacle arranged to satisfy their demand. It denies the premise that judgment must occur on their schedule to be real. The punishment named is not a minor correction they can dismiss after surviving an insult. It is a destination in which they will suffer, and the closing evaluation makes its evil unmistakable.

The warning also brings their words back into the realm of accountable deeds. They may regard a distorted greeting as too small to matter or too artful to count as open hostility. Revelation judges its intended meaning together with their persistent misconduct. The form of a polite word cannot neutralize the purpose of an injury. Their end is stated because the absence of immediate punishment has become part of the deception they practice upon themselves. No additional scene of worldly retaliation is needed to complete the verse's answer. It has identified deliberate return to forbidden counsel, hostility disguised as greeting, and a false inference from delay. Final judgment exposes the failure of that entire strategy.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:9

> O believers! When you converse privately, let it not be for sin, aggression, or disobedience to the Messenger, but let it be for goodness and righteousness. And fear Allah, to Whom you will ˹all˺ be gathered

**Expanded Commentary**

**Belief Places Private Deliberation Under a Moral Rule**

The address calls upon believers precisely where other people may not be able to inspect their conduct. Religious identity does not make every private conversation good. Those who believe must govern the purposes of their meetings by the guidance they profess. The verse therefore treats confidentiality as an area of responsibility, not an exemption granted to insiders who trust one another.

Its opening assumes that private conversation will occur. The command is not to abolish it but to direct it. People need settings in which they can ask advice, consider delicate matters, and organize help without making every fact public. The moral question concerns what the privacy serves. A closed meeting may protect someone who needs assistance, or it may protect speakers from accountability for harming him. The same outward arrangement can serve contrary ends. The verse resolves that ambiguity by specifying prohibited and approved purposes. Believers cannot simply ask whether their meeting is hidden; they must ask why it is hidden and what they are helping one another to accomplish. Faith becomes a rule for the use of confidentiality rather than a label that automatically validates whatever a believing circle decides to say.

**Sin, Aggression, and Disobedience Expose Different Failures**

The three prohibited directions identify wrong before Allah, harm directed toward others, and resistance to the Messenger's command. Naming them together prevents participants from evaluating a proposal by only one favorable description. A plan may appear useful to the group while injuring someone outside it. It may seem clever while refusing an established obligation. Group advantage is not enough to make its counsel righteous.

The distinction also reaches conversation before a completed public action. Planning, encouragement, and mutual approval matter. A person who supplies a harmful proposal with persuasive words is participating in its formation even if someone else would carry it out. The verse does not wait until aggression has fully materialized before addressing the speakers. It places a boundary at the stage when they can still refuse to organize it. That is particularly important in a private group, where the absence of an opposing voice can make a weak moral argument seem unanimously strong. The command introduces a standard beyond the room's agreement. A proposal does not become lawful because every participant supports it, and loyalty to companions cannot make disobedience to the Messenger a form of fidelity. The group must answer to a guidance it did not create.

**Goodness Gives Counsel a Purpose Beyond Avoiding Harm**

The positive command is not merely to stop speaking when a forbidden subject arises. Private conversation should serve goodness and righteousness. Birr, goodness expressed in right dealing and beneficial action, gives counsel constructive work to do. Taqwā, reverence that guards against disobedience, keeps that work within its proper limits. The two belong together: a useful result does not justify wrongful means, and caution about wrongdoing should not become refusal to help.

A concrete question follows for any deliberation: whose good is being served, and by what means? A meeting about assistance can preserve the recipient's dignity. Advice can prevent an angry person from acting unjustly. Reconciliation can begin with quiet conversations that allow each side to speak without public embarrassment. These are applications of the verse's stated direction, not claims that every confidential process succeeds. Their value depends on truthfulness and right purpose. The positive command refuses a purely defensive morality in which the best possible conversation is one that accomplishes nothing wrong because it accomplishes nothing at all. Believers are asked to put their capacity for planning and cooperation to work. The same privacy that could shelter aggression can instead make sensitive, beneficial action possible.

**A Parallel Passage Names Charity and Reconciliation**

Qur'an 4:114 identifies private counsel directed toward charity, kindness, and reconciliation as exceptions to much unprofitable secret speech. It also connects reward to seeking Allah's pleasure ([Qurʾān 4:114](https://quran.com/an-nisa/114)). This checked addition supplies concrete examples of the goodness required here. Confidentiality may be valuable precisely because a person in need should not have to exchange public exposure for help, and disputants may need space to retreat from hostile positions without losing face.

The parallel does not turn a benevolent label into a guarantee. A plan called reconciliation could pressure an injured person to accept injustice; a collection called charity could serve the collectors' reputation. The stated purpose must be real, and the means must agree with it. That is why goodness and reverence are stronger together than a vague intention to do something helpful. The present verse joins the content of the consultation to the participants' relation to Allah. The parallel adds that the desired divine approval, rather than applause for managing the affair, gives righteous counsel its proper aim. A quiet good deed is not a lesser deed for being quiet. In these cases, preserving privacy can belong to the good itself rather than merely hiding the people who perform it.

**The Final Gathering Places Every Private Circle Within One Accountability**

The closing command to fear Allah is grounded in being gathered to Him. A small group can control its membership now, but it cannot control the ultimate assembly before its Lord. The participants who have agreed among themselves must still answer beyond their own agreement. This gives the instruction more force than a recommendation for good social manners. Private counsel belongs to a life that will be judged.

The plural address also distributes responsibility. No participant can assume that the group's leader carries the whole moral burden of what all willingly support. The verse speaks to believers as persons capable of directing their own speech and refusing an improper purpose. Reverence therefore has a practical expression before a meeting ends: correct a false statement, resist an aggressive proposal, or turn the discussion toward a genuinely good outcome. These actions do not require knowing hidden intentions with certainty. They require responding honestly to the purpose and conduct that become apparent in the consultation. The final gathering does not make all present deliberation useless; it makes it consequential. Counsel worth bringing before Allah is counsel already ordered by His guidance. The verse preserves privacy while denying that privacy can become a separate moral territory ruled only by the preferences of those inside it.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:10

> Secret talks are only inspired by Satan to grieve the believers. Yet he cannot harm them whatsoever except by Allah’s Will. So in Allah let the believers put their trust

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Secret Talk in View Aims to Wound**

The verse identifies a use of private speech that serves Satan's purpose: it causes believers grief. Confidentiality is not condemned simply because a conversation is not public. The definite moral setting concerns talk used to disturb, isolate, or intimidate. Its secrecy becomes an instrument of harm when those outside the conversation are made to feel that something threatening is being arranged against them.

The distinction prevents the opening from swallowing every form of private advice. A trusted conversation can protect a person's dignity; the conduct described here works in the opposite direction. It turns limited access to information into a means of distress. The participants may not need to announce a complete plan. Their exclusion of others, gestures, or deliberately conspicuous secrecy can make uncertainty itself painful. The verse recognizes this injury without requiring the target to prove that every feared event will actually occur. Grief can be an intended result even where no further attack succeeds. Calling the conduct satanic therefore judges its direction. It does not relieve its human participants of responsibility, as though they were passive instruments with no choices. Their speech assists a purpose hostile to the well-being and steadiness of believers.

**Sorrow Is a Real Injury, Not a Sign That Faith Has Vanished**

The people grieved are called believers. The verse does not deny their faith because they feel sorrow, nor does it instruct them to pretend that malicious conduct is painless. It acknowledges an emotional injury and then teaches how to resist its claim over them. That order matters. Reassurance that ignores distress can become another form of dismissal; here the distress is named before the limit on the enemy's power is asserted.

The purpose of the harmful talk also helps distinguish experience from inference. A believer may truly feel excluded or threatened while still not knowing what the speakers intend in detail. The emotion is real; the imagined account of every danger may remain uncertain. The verse does not command the sufferer to fill those gaps with the worst possible story. Its movement is away from granting the hidden speakers unlimited control over what happens next. This is not a demand for emotional numbness. It is a correction to the belief that an opponent's ability to disturb the heart must mean an equal ability to determine the future. Faith can coexist with grief while refusing to make grief the measure of Allah's care or the enemy's reach.

**The Prophetic Rule Protects the Person Left Out**

A verified instruction makes the social concern concrete. Ibn Masʿūd reports that when three people are together, two should not hold a private exchange that excludes the third until they are among others, because such exclusion would grieve him. This is Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6290 ([Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6290](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6290)). The report adds a practical rule beyond the verse's general description of satanic counsel. It addresses behavior that can cause hurt even without a proven malicious plot.

The reason stated is the excluded person's sorrow. Good manners therefore require more than a speaker's private assurance that he means no harm. The arrangement of the conversation can communicate rejection regardless of its subject. Including the third person or postponing the private exchange prevents an avoidable wound. This also shows why the verse's concern should not be reduced to spectacular conspiracies. Everyday patterns of speech can cultivate the very anxiety that malicious actors exploit. The report does not require that every discussion be shared with every person present in every circumstance. It gives a defined case and its moral reason. That reason invites attentiveness to how confidentiality is practiced, not suspicion toward confidentiality itself. Protection from grief begins partly with refusing to produce needless exclusion.

**Satan's Reach Is Not an Independent Sovereignty**

The statement that he cannot harm except by Allah's will denies Satan autonomous power. His hostile purpose does not become a decree merely because he pursues it. The distance between intention and outcome remains under Allah's authority. This is the central reassurance to people whose uncertainty may make a hidden threat seem boundless. The adversary is active, but he is not sovereign.

Permission here must not be confused with moral approval. The verse calls the purpose satanic and then places its possible effects under Allah's will. Those two statements would contradict one another if every permitted occurrence were automatically a righteous act. Instead, the verse distinguishes the wrongness of the purpose from the ultimate governance of events. The enemy can choose malice without acquiring independent control over its result. The statement also does not promise that believers will never experience painful consequences. An absolute guarantee of worldly invulnerability would ignore the qualifying clause. What they are promised here is a true account of power: harm cannot escape Allah's rule. That truth leaves room for prudent action while removing the imagined need to treat every hostile whisper as a competing destiny. A creature's scheme remains a creature's scheme, however intimidating its concealment may feel.

**Trust Refuses to Let Hidden Speech Govern the Heart**

The conclusion directs believers to put their trust in Allah. Trust is not an instruction to admire the secrecy of their opponents or to surrender practical judgment. It is the response appropriate to knowing that the enemy lacks independent power. The believer may seek clarification, avoid an unsafe situation, or ask for help without treating those steps as evidence that reliance on Allah has failed.

What trust changes is the final location of confidence. Neither a hostile group's intentions nor one's own ability to discover every secret determines the whole future. This releases the believer from an impossible demand: becoming completely informed before being able to remain steady. Complete information belongs to Allah. Human action can proceed responsibly with limited knowledge while refusing panic's claim that every unknown detail must conceal disaster. The verse therefore ends with a positive allegiance rather than with fascination about Satan. It has named the malicious purpose, acknowledged the grief it seeks, and denied its independence. The heart is then directed beyond the source of anxiety to the Lord who governs its possible effects. Trust does not make the wrong harmless in moral terms; it prevents the wrongdoer from becoming the imagined master of the believer's life.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:11

> O believers! When you are told to make room in gatherings, then do so. Allah will make room for you ˹in His grace˺. And if you are told to rise, then do so. Allah will elevate those of you who are faithful, and ˹raise˺ those gifted with knowledge in rank. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do

**Expanded Commentary**

**Making Room Refuses to Turn a Seat Into a Private Privilege**

The command concerns an ordinary bodily action within a gathering. Those already seated must make room when asked. Prior arrival does not give them a right to occupy every available space as though the gathering existed only for their benefit. They are called to notice the people whose access depends on a small adjustment by those already comfortable.

The act has more than practical value. Refusing to move can express a claim to rank: one's closeness, comfort, or familiarity is treated as more important than another person's opportunity to participate. Making room challenges that claim without requiring a grand public gesture. A person can serve others by shifting slightly, arranging belongings differently, or accepting less space. The verse takes such conduct seriously because communal life is shaped by repeated small decisions about who is allowed to belong. Its application begins with actual gatherings, not an abstract metaphor about generosity. Broader lessons should grow from that physical act rather than replace it. A community that praises welcome while leaving newcomers standing unnecessarily has failed at the very point where its welcome could have become visible. The instruction gives inclusion a form simple enough to practice immediately.

**The Prophetic Rule Distinguishes Expansion From Displacement**

A checked report from Ibn ʿUmar states that the Prophet forbade making one person leave his seat merely so another could occupy it, while directing people to spread out and provide room. Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī places this report under the present verse: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6270 ([Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6270)). This additional evidence is important because making room could otherwise be misused as a slogan for displacing someone less influential.

The distinction is between widening access and transferring a favored position through humiliation. The command should not be read as a general license for a late arrival to demand another person's place. The report preserves the existing participant's dignity while still requiring flexibility. It also helps interpret the instruction to rise. A legitimate direction connected to the order or purpose of a gathering differs from a person's private demand to possess someone else's seat. The verse calls for responsive conduct, not competition to remove others in the name of obedience. These rules belong together: do not cling selfishly to unnecessary space, and do not solve crowding by treating another participant as disposable. Shared adjustment is the first remedy. Reverence becomes visible through consideration for both the person arriving and the person already present.

**Allah's Expansion Answers a Willingness to Ease Another's Situation**

The promise that Allah will make room for the obedient gives the small act a larger horizon. The believer gives up an unnecessary claim to space and receives a promise of divine generosity. The wording does not restrict that generosity to getting a larger seat at another gathering. The translation's reference to grace preserves the breadth without pretending to list every form the promise must take.

The relation is not a commercial exchange in which a measured bodily movement purchases a fixed quantity of reward. Allah's favor exceeds the action that occasions it. What the act reveals is willingness to ease another person's situation under His command. A verified report from Abū Hurayrah connects relieving difficulty and helping another believer with Allah's help: Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2699a ([Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)). That parallel supports a broad moral relation while leaving the promise's particular forms to Allah. It also prevents apparent smallness from becoming an excuse for neglect. The person who cannot solve another's whole difficulty may still remove one avoidable obstacle. In the gathering, that obstacle is literal lack of room. The promised divine response dignifies the action without allowing its performer to boast that another person's access now belongs to him as a favor owed.

**Knowledge Raises Rank Without Authorizing Arrogance**

Allah will elevate believers and those given knowledge. The phrase given knowledge presents learning as a gift and trust rather than self-created superiority. The elevation comes from Allah; it is not secured by forcing oneself into the most visible seat. Within a verse about making room and responding to direction, the promise unsettles the assumption that dignity depends on refusing to move.

Knowledge and faith are named together. The sentence does not praise information detached from obedience or skill used to make a gathering revolve around its possessor. A learned believer should understand more clearly why the command matters. The same report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2699a associates the pursuit of knowledge with a path made easy toward Paradise and describes the blessing of gathering to study Allah's Book. This checked connection gives the rank of knowledge a devotional and practical setting. Learning opens a way of service and nearness, not merely a claim to public recognition. The verse does not supply a numerical scale by which readers can rank every scholar or believer. It locates the authority to elevate in Allah and directs the participants to the conduct He commands. A person may rise from a place without falling in worth; obedience can be the very occasion of elevation.

**Divine Awareness Reaches the Manner in Which the Body Obeys**

The closing statement that Allah knows what people do gathers the visible acts and their moral quality. Two people may both move, while one does so gladly and the other makes the newcomer feel unwelcome. The outward instruction remains necessary, but its performance is not beyond scrutiny once the body has technically complied. Allah knows the adjustment and the resentment, the helpful intention and the display.

This does not authorize participants to inspect one another's hearts. It directs each person toward sincerity in his own conduct. The gathering is not improved by accusing everyone who hesitates of arrogance. People can have needs, limitations, or reasons not visible to others. The verse's practical direction should therefore be carried out with consideration rather than turned into another contest for status. Its assurances make that possible: making room does not impoverish the believer before Allah, rising does not destroy his dignity, and knowledge need not defend itself through possessiveness. True rank is not identical with occupying the best physical position. The person who yields space in obedience may receive a greater elevation than the one who successfully guards a place of honor.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:12

> O believers! When you consult the Messenger privately, give something in charity before your consultation. That is better and purer for you. But if you lack the means, then Allah is truly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful

**Expanded Commentary**

**Private Access Carries a Responsibility Beyond Personal Desire**

The verse places a charitable act before private consultation with the Messenger. A person who seeks his exclusive attention must first do something that benefits another. The order prevents private access from being treated as a privilege pursued only for the satisfaction of the person requesting it. A conversation with the Messenger belongs to a community whose needs extend beyond the immediate speaker.

The command concerns consultation in private, not the purchase of revelation or a general fee for hearing religious instruction. The object given is charity. Its direction is outward toward a beneficiary, rather than a payment defined as the Messenger's personal income. This difference is essential to the rule's moral character. The person seeking a private audience does not buy a more favorable judgment by offering money. He undertakes a good deed before receiving attention. The verse also does not state a tariff or fixed quantity. Its concern is the charitable act and the sequence in which it occurs. A commentary should not invent a standard payment where the translation supplies none. What is clear is that access is joined to responsibility, and the seeker of guidance is asked to express concern for someone other than himself before occupying the Messenger's time.

**The Gift Comes First So That Preparation Becomes an Act**

The instruction to give before the consultation makes preparation concrete. Good intentions alone are not the whole response. The believer is asked to perform an act that changes something outside his own desire to speak. By the time the private exchange begins, a benefit has already reached another person. The conversation therefore enters a setting shaped by generosity rather than mere competition for attention.

This order also encourages seriousness about the proposed consultation. A person must pause between wanting access and using it. That pause can help distinguish a genuine question from the wish to appear especially close to the Messenger. The verse does not accuse every seeker of an audience of vanity, but the structure of the rule checks a form of vanity that private access could invite. Ibn Kathīr explains the charity as cleansing the person in preparation for the consultation ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:12](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/12/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked explanation gives the sequence a devotional meaning: one approaches with an act of obedience already offered, not simply with a claim that one's concerns must take priority. The good deed does not replace the need for a truthful question. It helps form the person who will ask it.

**Better and Purer Describe the Good of the Giver**

The verse says the practice is better and purer for those addressed. The poor person's benefit is real, but the giver is also changed by responding. Charity loosens the attachment that treats one's resources and opportunities as instruments of self-advancement alone. In this setting, the person seeking special attention is reminded that drawing near to the Messenger should agree with the generosity his guidance calls forth.

Better and purer are not identical evaluations. Better names the good of the action; purer points to the removal of a moral impurity or the correction of a self-centered disposition. A gift can be outwardly useful while performed in a boastful spirit, so the description directs the giver toward more than visible transfer. He should not convert the charitable act into evidence that he now deserves to dominate the conversation. Such an attitude would reintroduce the very possessiveness the act can discipline. The rule's benefit lies in obedience and generosity, not in acquiring a badge of rank. Nor should the wording be turned into a claim that poor believers are impure because they cannot give. The verse itself immediately addresses inability with mercy. Purification through this act belongs to those able to perform it; poverty is not a moral stain.

**The Exception Protects Those Who Lack the Means**

The person without the means to give is not treated as a failed purchaser of access. Allah's forgiveness and mercy are named directly. Ibn Kathīr explains that the command to offer this charity concerns those financially able to do so. The qualification therefore prevents the rule from becoming an exclusion of the poor from necessary consultation. Capacity is part of the command's stated application, not an unofficial concession added against its spirit.

This matters because a requirement that involves resources can easily be misunderstood through the habits of a wealthy society. People may begin to assume that those who can give are more entitled to be heard, while those who cannot should remain silent. The verse does not permit that inference. Its charitable discipline is directed at the capable, and its ending explicitly makes room for the incapable. Genuine inability differs from unwillingness to part with anything, but the difference is not a warrant for humiliating a person who asks for help. The divine names place the matter under mercy rather than a demand to expose every private hardship. The aim is not to make the vulnerable prove their worth through a payment. It is to order private consultation in a way that produces good while honoring the limits under which people actually live.

**Respect for the Messenger Includes Respect for His Burden**

Ibn Kathīr preserves an explanation attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās that repeated private questioning had become burdensome for the Prophet. This is an exegetical report transmitted in the commentary, not a newly authenticated Prophetic saying. It supplies a practical setting for the command: a valued opportunity can be overused when every individual sees only his own wish for access. The combined burden is greater than any one request appears in isolation.

The charitable preparation makes that opportunity deliberate without declaring sincere consultation unwelcome. Respect is expressed not merely through affectionate words about the Messenger but through consideration for the demands placed upon him. A person can seek religious benefit and still need discipline in how much exclusive attention he asks another to provide. The verse's arrangement joins that discipline to help for the needy, so the remedy is not simply a closed door. Its own teaching is complete at this point: the capable person gives before consulting, the act is beneficial and purifying, and the person without means is met by divine mercy. This specific ruling should not be transferred into a universal right for later teachers or officials to sell private religious access. The named authority, the charitable purpose, and the explicit protection for poverty all belong to the command being explained.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:13

> Are you afraid of spending in charity before your private consultations ˹with him˺? Since you are unable to do so, and Allah has turned to you in mercy, then ˹continue to˺ establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and obey Allah and His Messenger. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question Exposes Anxiety About the Cost of Access**

The opening asks whether the believers have become afraid of giving charity before private consultation. Its concern is not simply the amount of money but the reluctance revealed by attaching generosity to a desired opportunity. People may value a private conversation highly until something must be surrendered before it. The question brings that difference into view without requiring a detailed account of every individual's means or motives.

Ibn Kathīr explains the apprehension as fear that the requirement would remain in force continually ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:13](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/12/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked explanation adds a useful dimension. A repeated obligation can be felt differently from a single gift, especially when a person does not know how often he will need consultation. The verse addresses that anxiety while directing attention away from self-justifying calculations. It does not invite the audience to argue that charity has no value because the condition was difficult. Nor does it erase the distinction between genuine inability and reluctance. Its question disciplines the heart, and its following statement grants relief. The two actions belong together: mercy can recognize human difficulty while allowing the experience of that difficulty to teach something about attachment to resources.

**Relief Is Granted by the Same Authority That Established the Rule**

Allah has turned to the believers in mercy. The requirement attached to private consultation is lifted in this verse; Ibn Kathīr identifies this as abrogation of the earlier obligation. The change is not represented as a successful human effort to make divine guidance irrelevant. It is a new divine ruling that grants ease. Obedience therefore includes receiving the relief on the terms Allah gives.

This explains how an earlier command can remain meaningful even when its continuing obligation is removed. It had required a real response and exposed a real disposition. Its educational force need not vanish with its legal duration. The verse does not supply the exact interval for which the requirement remained in operation. Reports assigning it a brief duration should not be turned into a precise chronology without adequate evidence. The essential legal movement is clear without that calculation: Allah grants relief, and the audience is directed toward duties that remain. Abrogation here concerns a particular obligation, not a claim that generosity has ceased to be good or that private consultation has become morally unregulated. The authority behind both command and relief is one. A believer cannot honor the first while rejecting the second, or welcome the second while denying that the first ever had a binding claim.

**Prayer Keeps Mercy From Becoming Spiritual Neglect**

The first continuing duty is to establish prayer. The verse does not leave relief as an empty space in which the believers need do nothing. Their relation to Allah still requires regular worship. Establishing prayer means sustaining it as a real practice rather than treating gratitude for mercy as a passing feeling. The easing of one specific burden returns attention to a central obligation already capable of ordering life.

The direction also prevents special access to the Messenger from becoming a substitute for ordinary obedience. A person might attach great importance to private conversations while neglecting the worship required of every believer. This verse places the continuing act of prayer before any claim to privileged intimacy. Religious seriousness is not measured only by how much exclusive attention a person receives from an authority. It is expressed through what he does when no private audience is taking place. Prayer gives that seriousness a repeated bodily and temporal form. The instruction is not punishment for having found the charitable condition difficult. It is a positive direction within mercy. Allah's turning toward the believers calls forth renewed attention, not distance. Relief should make obedience sustainable, rather than become evidence that obedience itself no longer matters.

**The Removal of One Charity Requirement Leaves Financial Duty Intact**

Alms-tax remains expressly commanded. The verse distinguishes the special charity associated with consultation from the continuing obligation of zakāh, the prescribed alms due under its conditions. These should not be confused. Relief from the first does not cancel the second, and the difficulty of one charitable arrangement does not turn all financial responsibility into optional goodwill.

This is a precise form of easing. It does not dismantle the community's care for material need; it removes a particular condition while reaffirming an established duty. A person who celebrates the relief but withholds the alms still due has misunderstood what was granted. The sentence also joins worship and wealth, so that devotion cannot be confined to prayer while resources are treated as a private territory beyond Allah's command. The two duties address different aspects of the same life. One orders the believer's repeated approach to Allah; the other governs a claim upon his possessions. Neither can simply stand in for the other. The verse supplies no detailed calculation of zakāh here, so its mention should not be made into a complete manual of thresholds and rates. Its point is continuity: mercy has modified one obligation without dissolving the believer's accountable use of wealth.

**Obedience and Divine Awareness Complete the Meaning of the Concession**

The general command to obey Allah and His Messenger follows the named duties. Prayer and alms are concrete expressions of that obedience, but they do not exhaust it. The believer cannot treat them as payments that purchase freedom to disregard every other command. The verse moves from particular acts to the larger allegiance that makes them coherent.

Allah's awareness of conduct closes the passage. The concession is not a loophole whose use escapes scrutiny. He knows whether relief produces gratitude, whether prayer is maintained, and whether the obligation upon wealth is honored. This awareness also prevents outward compliance from becoming a performance designed to preserve standing after the difficulty exposed by the opening question. The answer required is practical and sincere. A believer may accept ease without shame, since Allah grants it, while refusing to use ease as a pretext for indifference. The verse's final arrangement is balanced: apprehension is acknowledged, mercy is extended, continuing duties are named, and conduct remains known. Divine relief does not end the relationship of obedience. It makes clear where that relationship is now to be expressed.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:14

> Have you not seen those ˹hypocrites˺ who ally themselves with a people with whom Allah is displeased? They are neither with you nor with them. And they swear to lies knowingly

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Alliance Is Judged Together With Its Deception**

The verse identifies people whose declared place among believers does not match their concealed commitments. Their alliance with a people under Allah's displeasure is not an isolated social fact. It belongs to a larger pattern of divided allegiance and deliberate lying. The three clauses interpret one another. A reading that detaches the first clause from the false oaths would miss the particular moral disorder being exposed.

Ibn Kathīr explains the case as the hypocrites' covert aid to their allies while publicly presenting themselves as believers ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:14](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/14/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked explanation makes the relation more precise than an indiscriminate prohibition of ordinary kindness toward people of another religion. The conduct concerns support joined to deceit about one's own position. The verse does not condemn neighbors for dealing fairly across religious differences. It exposes people who seek the benefits of a declared allegiance while acting contrary to it and lying when challenged. Their problem is not honest recognition of multiple social relationships. It is the use of those relationships without truth. Allah's displeasure marks the seriousness of the alignment, and the knowingly false oath reveals the means by which its speakers try to conceal what they are doing.

**Neither With You Nor With Them Denies the Security of Double Belonging**

The hypocrites imagine that moving between groups can preserve an advantage with both. The verse denies the deeper belonging that their maneuver seeks. They are neither truly with the believers nor securely identified with those they assist. Tactical association has not produced honest commitment. Their location between the groups is not praised as an enlightened neutrality but exposed as an unstable relation founded on concealment.

This is not a claim that every person with friendships in more than one community lacks integrity. The sentence concerns the people already identified through hypocrisy and lying. Its point is that sincerity cannot be manufactured by accumulating contacts. A person can be useful to another group while not being faithful to it, just as he can speak the language of belief while withholding the allegiance it expresses. The verse's double denial cuts through both appearances. The hypocrites have not successfully become more secure by refusing a truthful position. They have weakened the basis on which either side could trust them. The outward ability to enter more than one gathering may look like influence, but access alone is not belonging. A relationship sustained through a false account of oneself carries its instability within it even before others discover the lie.

**A Parallel Verse Describes Wavering Rather Than Genuine Balance**

Ibn Kathīr connects this description with 4:143, where the hypocrites are portrayed as wavering between sides without belonging to either. That checked cross-reference adds a useful distinction. Balance is a principled ability to recognize different legitimate claims; wavering here is a refusal of a truthful allegiance while seeking advantage from incompatible appearances. The two should not be confused merely because both can place a person between groups.

The present verse gives the ethical evidence for the distinction: the speakers swear to what they know is false. Their intermediate position is maintained through deceit, not through an openly stated principle. They cannot describe their commitments honestly to those whose confidence they seek. This exposes why the strategy fails at a deeper level than simple indecision. Uncertainty can be acknowledged and examined; deliberate falsehood is used to prevent examination. A person who genuinely does not know where he stands may ask questions or suspend a judgment. These people instead produce emphatic assurances. Their apparent certainty covers an inward division. The parallel helps explain the double denial without inventing a psychological history for every individual. What revelation identifies is a pattern in which the flexibility of outward speech conceals the absence of faithful commitment.

**A True Statement Can Be Used Within a False Profession**

The oath concerns a claim the speakers know does not represent their own reality. Ibn Kathīr explains that they profess belief when meeting the believers while concealing what they actually hold. A proposition about the Messenger can be true in itself while the speaker's sworn claim to believe it is false. Truth of the religious words and truthfulness of the profession are not the same question.

This is a precise and important distinction. Someone can repeat a sound formula without honestly reporting his own commitment. The lie then lies not in the reality of Allah's message but in the claim that the speaker has submitted to it. An oath intensifies the deception by inviting others to place greater confidence in that claim. The verse does not make eloquence or correct terminology worthless; it denies that either can substitute for integrity. The speakers know the words expected of them and use that knowledge to conceal their stance. Their intelligence is therefore part of the deception rather than an excuse for it. The statement knowingly removes the possibility that the problem is a mistaken recollection or an innocent misunderstanding. They invoke certainty where they themselves know the account is false.

**Knowledge Makes the False Oath a Chosen Betrayal of Trust**

The final qualification locates responsibility in awareness. The speakers do not simply utter a false statement; they know it is false. Their action uses a form intended to guarantee trust while emptying it of the sincerity that makes it meaningful. They are not only withholding information but actively directing others toward an untrue conclusion about their loyalty.

That distinction protects the verse from being used recklessly against ordinary inconsistency. A believer can act weakly, struggle with mixed motives, or fail to live up to a genuine profession without necessarily being identical to these deliberate liars. The text describes a known inward falsehood, and human observers do not possess unrestricted access to that condition. Its warning should first correct one's own use of assurance: do not swear to an allegiance that conduct and intention deny. The verse ends with the exposure itself rather than a detailed human procedure for investigating every suspected hypocrite. It has made the moral situation clear. An alliance concealed by knowingly false oaths does not create secure membership on either side. It creates a form of speech in which the appearance of faith is used against the trust that faith ought to sustain.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:15

> Allah has prepared for them a severe punishment. Evil indeed is what they do

**Expanded Commentary**

**Prepared Punishment Is More Certain Than Present Concealment**

The verse declares that Allah has prepared a severe punishment. The identified hypocrites may succeed in concealing their conduct from people, but concealment has not left the matter undecided before Allah. The preparation belongs to His knowledge and judgment, not to a future investigation whose result remains uncertain. The sentence gives assurance of consequence without supplying a date that the offenders can use as another object of calculation.

This is why the perfect expression concerning preparation carries such force. The penalty is not introduced as a vague possibility that might matter only if someone eventually notices the wrong. Allah's response is already stated as established. That does not mean the reader possesses a detailed view of how the punishment is prepared. The verse supplies certainty, not a mechanism. Its immediate target is the confidence that successful management of appearances can keep accountability suspended. People may still dispute the facts among themselves, but their dispute does not determine whether Allah knows them. The warning separates human discovery from divine judgment. A hidden wrong need not become public knowledge before it is morally real, and a person who remains socially secure has not thereby proved that no serious consequence awaits him.

**Severity Corresponds to Conduct That Corrupts Trust**

The punishment is severe because the conduct under judgment is not a harmless difference between private feeling and public manners. Ibn Kathīr explains the verse in relation to the hypocrites' deceit, betrayal of believers, and support for hostile allies ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:15](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/14/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). These are actions that damage the trust on which communal life depends. People are induced to rely upon an appearance of fidelity while the appearance is being used against them.

The wrongdoing therefore has more than an inward dimension. A concealed stance becomes a practical injury when it shapes deceptive conduct. The verse does not require the commentator to multiply dramatic details in order to justify its gravity. Betrayal of trust is already serious. The judgment also belongs to Allah rather than to a listener's desire for revenge. Calling the punishment severe is not an instruction that every offended person should devise a severe penalty. It states the divine response to the identified evil. Keeping that distinction protects the moral warning from becoming an excuse for anger. The text neither treats deliberate treachery as trivial nor transfers unrestricted punitive authority to anyone who suspects that treachery has occurred. Its severity is grounded in knowledge that human resentment does not possess.

**The Final Evaluation Names Evil Rather Than Mere Failure**

The second sentence judges what they do as evil. Their conduct is not condemned simply because it is strategically unsuccessful or likely to be exposed. A lie would remain wrong even if it secured an advantage, and betrayal would remain a betrayal even if its target never discovered it. The verse establishes that moral judgment independently of the offender's calculation of success.

This matters because hypocrisy can be defended as cleverness. A person may call his concealment flexibility, his false assurance diplomacy, or his divided allegiance prudence. Such descriptions can identify a method while concealing its ethical content. The verse refuses to let a favorable description control the verdict. What matters is the act in its truth before Allah. The emphasis on what they do also prevents the warning from becoming a condemnation of an inherited identity. The named evil is conduct attributed to the people in question. Their responsibility is not supplied by ancestry, appearance, or a category invented by a hostile observer. Revelation identifies the action and gives its judgment. A reader who uses the verse to insult a group without examining the conduct it condemns has replaced its moral precision with a much weaker accusation.

**The Wider Qur'anic Warning Makes Hypocrisy Spiritually Grave**

A checked parallel in 4:145 places the hypocrites in the lowest depths of the Fire ([Qurʾān 4:145](https://quran.com/an-nisa/145)). This additional reference clarifies that the present warning is not merely about losing reputation within an earthly community. The severity belongs to the person's relation to Allah and the final judgment. An outward profession of religion does not automatically protect someone who knowingly uses that profession as a deception.

The parallel should be applied with the same care as the present verse. Every failing believer is not therefore a hypocrite in the full sense described. Weakness, forgetfulness, and a deliberate concealed rejection maintained through deception are not interchangeable conditions. The warning addresses a grave reality, and its gravity is one reason not to assign it carelessly to particular people. Its first practical effect should be self-examination concerning sincerity and truth. The person who knows he is exploiting others' confidence should not comfort himself merely because his language remains religiously correct. The Qur'anic comparison makes that danger unmistakable. Faith cannot serve as a protective name for a course of action whose purpose is to betray the trust associated with faith. The external profession and the internal reality must not be treated as permanently separable before Allah.

**The Two Sentences Join Certain Judgment to a Moral Reason**

The verse is brief, but its structure is complete. The first sentence declares the prepared consequence; the second declares the character of the conduct. Judgment is not presented as unexplained power. The actions are evil, and the punishment is severe. These claims are joined without a narrative that might allow the reader to become absorbed in colorful details while losing the central relation.

Its brevity also leaves no room for bargaining through appearances. The offenders' social position, persuasive ability, and access to trusted circles do not appear as mitigating guarantees. The issue is what they actually do. That focus does not erase the possibility of honest correction elsewhere in revelation; it refuses to call persistent deceit safe while it continues. The appropriate response to such a warning is to abandon the evil, not to perfect the means of concealing it. Nor should the observer answer with confident claims about every hidden motive around him. Allah has disclosed this judgment, and the reader must keep the distinction between disclosed knowledge and suspicion. The verse demands moral seriousness at both levels: deliberate treachery is not small, and a divine verdict must not be imitated by an accusation whose facts the accuser does not know.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:16

> They have made their ˹false˺ oaths as a shield, hindering ˹others˺ from the cause of Allah. So they will suffer a humiliating punishment

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Oath Protects a Lie Instead of Guaranteeing the Truth**

The verse describes a deliberate reversal of an oath's purpose. An oath should strengthen a truthful assurance by making the speaker answerable for it before Allah. These people use their oaths as a shield. The outward form invites confidence, while the inward purpose is to prevent their actual stance from being examined. A sign meant to support truth becomes protection for falsehood.

The shield image identifies more than a single inaccurate statement. It describes a practice with a defensive function. When suspicion threatens their position, they place a solemn assurance between themselves and scrutiny. The strategy depends on others taking oaths seriously. Their deception therefore exploits a moral good they do not themselves honor. If no one respected sworn speech, the device would lose its value. The hypocrites borrow the community's reverence in order to evade the responsibility that reverence should impose. This makes the wrong especially corrosive. They are not merely failing to live up to a social expectation; they are using that expectation against those who trust it. The verse exposes the protection as false without declaring all oaths worthless. The institution is not the evil. Its calculated use to conceal betrayal is.

**The Immediate Protection Serves a Wider Obstruction**

The consequence is hindering others from Allah's cause. The false oath does not end with preserving the speaker's reputation. It allows him to retain a position from which he can influence people. Trust obtained through deception becomes a means of diverting others from the guidance the speaker outwardly claims to respect.

Ibn Kathīr explains the sequence through concealed disbelief and the people deceived by its outward covering ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:16](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/14/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). That reading shows why the two clauses are joined. A person openly known to oppose a path may have less power to misrepresent it than someone accepted as belonging to it. The deceptive insider can make obstruction appear to be advice, caution, or concern. The verse does not list every technique by which the hindrance occurs, so a commentary should not invent a catalogue of incidents. Its structural claim is sufficient: false assurance creates cover, and the cover facilitates diversion. The harm extends beyond the oath-taker's own inward state. Others may form judgments about religion, trust, and obedience under an influence whose real allegiance has been concealed from them.

**Turning Away and Turning Others Away Are Related but Distinct**

The expression concerning the path can be understood in relation to the speakers' own withdrawal or their obstruction of others. The supplied translation explicitly takes the latter direction. That is the stronger immediate basis for this commentary, especially because the shield enables continued influence over those who accept the outward profession. The personal refusal remains part of the moral situation, but it does not exhaust the stated harm.

This distinction makes the verse more demanding than a warning about private inconsistency. A person can move away from obedience and also recruit others into that movement, whether through direct discouragement or through deceptive example. The two acts carry different relations to responsibility. One concerns the person's own response to guidance; the other concerns the effect he intentionally seeks upon another's response. The verse's focus on hindrance gives public consequence to concealed falsehood. It also guards against reducing religion to appearances. A formally convincing oath may preserve the speaker's place while his conduct works against the very cause his words appear to serve. The interpretation should therefore attend to the sequence rather than treating its clauses as independent labels: what the oath protects is what makes the obstruction possible.

**A False Oath Can Win an Earthly Advantage Without Making It Right**

A verified Prophetic report gives a concrete parallel. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6676–6677, Ibn Masʿūd transmits a warning about using a false oath to obtain another Muslim's property. The same report includes al-Ashʿath ibn Qays's account of a disputed well and the requirement of evidence or an opposing oath ([Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6676–6677](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6676)). This checked addition concerns property rather than the present obstruction of faith, but the misuse of solemn assurance is directly relevant.

The legal form can affect what people are able to establish in this world, yet it cannot make a knowingly false claim true before Allah. The distinction explains why the shield in the present verse is only apparent protection. It may delay exposure or influence human treatment; it cannot change the reality of the speaker's conduct. The report also preserves the importance of evidence. Human judgment operates through available testimony and procedures, not unrestricted knowledge of hidden intent. Exploiting those limits is the oath-taker's wrong. A person who succeeds within an outward process by lying has not converted the result into moral innocence. The advantage gained and the divine account remain different matters, and the verse insists that the second cannot be neutralized by manipulating the first.

**Humiliation Answers the False Honor Borrowed From Sacred Speech**

The punishment is described as humiliating. The offenders had used an honored form of speech to maintain a protected standing. Their end exposes the emptiness of that standing when separated from truth. The reversal is morally fitting: solemn words used to disguise betrayal do not secure lasting dignity before the One whose name gives those words their gravity.

This is not a command to treat every suspected liar with degrading conduct. The verse declares Allah's judgment on a known pattern of hypocrisy. Human beings must still distinguish evidence from suspicion and a deliberate false oath from mistake. Its direct ethical warning concerns one's own use of trust: do not invoke sacred assurance to gain protection for conduct that opposes the assurance's meaning. The three parts of the verse form a complete account of the wrong and its consequence. The oath becomes cover, the cover enables obstruction, and the offender faces humiliation rather than the safety he sought. Truthfulness is therefore not a decorative virtue added to effective religious speech. It is what prevents that speech from becoming an instrument of harm. Without it, the very forms that should bind a community to Allah can be exploited to lead people away from Him.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:17

> Neither their wealth nor children will be of any help to them against Allah whatsoever. It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ordinary Sources of Security Cannot Resist Divine Judgment**

Wealth and children can give a person considerable security among other human beings. Property supplies resources, while a strong household can provide companionship, representation, and practical support. The verse does not deny these ordinary benefits. It denies their power against Allah. The objects remain what they are, but the setting changes from social life to judgment, where neither can compel a favorable outcome.

This qualification gives the warning its precision. The problem is not simply possessing money or loving one's children. It is treating those possessions and relationships as protection from accountability. A person accustomed to resolving difficulty through payment or family influence may carry the same expectation into his understanding of religion. The verse breaks that expectation completely. No amount of material strength turns a creature into someone whom Allah cannot judge. Nor can a family create an exemption for one of its members merely through attachment to him. What works within a limited human order cannot be assumed to govern the Lord of that order. The sentence therefore corrects the meaning assigned to familiar blessings rather than declaring that those blessings have no rightful place in life. They are means entrusted to people, not a defense against the One who entrusted them.

**The Two Possessions Cover Different Forms of Human Power**

The pairing of wealth and children reaches beyond one kind of advantage. Money can secure goods and services; descendants can extend a person's social presence beyond his individual strength and lifetime. One represents accumulated resources, the other continuing relationship. Together they describe forms of support that often make a person feel less exposed to danger. The verse denies that either support can cross the boundary into immunity before Allah.

This also explains why material poverty is not the verse's proposed remedy. A person without wealth can still imagine that belonging to a powerful family settles his standing. Someone without children can make possessions the center of the same false confidence. The error concerns reliance, not merely a particular distribution of assets. Even possessing both does not create the missing authority. Their combined strength remains created strength. The words whatsoever exclude the thought that, although these advantages cannot secure everything, they might secure a small exemption from the judgment being described. There is no fraction of divine sovereignty available for purchase or familial negotiation. The human supports may remain meaningful within their proper sphere, but they cannot become a counterweight to Allah. Their limit must be understood before their abundance is mistaken for a guarantee.

**What Accompanies a Person Is Not Identical With What He Owns**

A verified report from Anas ibn Mālik offers a concrete parallel. The Prophet describes family, property, and deeds accompanying a dead person, after which family and property return while the deeds remain. This is Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2960 ([Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2960](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2960)). The report adds a vivid distinction beyond the verse's wording: what can gather around a person at departure is not necessarily what continues with him in his account.

The distinction does not make the proper use of wealth irrelevant. Giving, providing, and acting honestly with possessions are deeds rather than an attempted purchase of immunity. Likewise, the way one treats children belongs to conduct, not merely to the fact of having descendants. The warning therefore redirects attention from ownership to responsibility. A person may count what he possesses while neglecting what he is doing with it. The report's contrast exposes the weakness of that accounting. Material things have a history of use, and that history matters in a way mere possession cannot. The verse concerns those whose wealth and children are imagined as protection against Allah. Their mistake is to count the resources around them as though those resources could replace a truthful relation to Him. The account follows action, not the prestige attached to having much.

**Residence in the Fire Replaces the Security They Claimed**

The second sentence identifies the people concerned as residents of the Fire. Residence ordinarily suggests an established place in which life is settled. Here that language turns the expectation of security into its opposite. Those who depended on earthly means of protection do not merely undergo an inconvenience before returning to a protected position. The destination itself is one of punishment.

The verse does not supply a physical description of the Fire in this sentence. Its purpose is to establish the outcome and its relation to failed reliance. Adding imagined details would not strengthen that argument. The severe contrast already lies in the movement from wealth and family support to a destination neither can prevent. Nor is the pronoun a license to declare the same fate for every wealthy person, every parent, or everyone an observer dislikes. It refers to the people under judgment in the passage. Their possessions do not explain the condemnation by themselves; they fail to rescue those whose wrongdoing has brought them under it. This distinction keeps the warning morally exact. The Fire is not presented as the penalty for having a household. It is the stated end from which a household cannot shield the people concerned.

**Forever Denies an Escape Based on Another Waiting Period**

The closing statement declares that they remain there forever. The sentence removes the possibility of treating the destination as a temporary loss that wealth or kinship might eventually help reverse. Earthly influence often operates through delay: one waits for an ally, a payment, or a changed circumstance. The final clause denies that familiar pattern as an escape from this judgment.

The duration belongs to the specific verdict stated here. It should not be flattened into a claim that every believer who commits any sin has the identical fate. The verse speaks about its identified subjects and gives their end with unmistakable severity. Its practical force lies in the present correction of false confidence. A person must not let blessings become reasons to ignore the Lord who grants them. Wealth can be used faithfully, and children can be loved faithfully, only when neither is asked to do what no creature can do. The three movements of the verse establish that limit: no rescue through possessions or descendants, residence in the Fire, and no end to that residence for those described. The warning dismantles imagined protection so that worldly security is not mistaken for the final measure of a person's standing before Allah.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:18

> On the Day Allah resurrects them all, they will ˹falsely˺ swear to Him as they swear to you, thinking they have something to stand on. Indeed, it is they who are the ˹total˺ liars

**Expanded Commentary**

**Resurrection Removes Distance Without Removing Their Habit of Lying**

Allah raises all the people concerned, and they attempt the same kind of false assurance they used among human beings. The change of setting is absolute, yet their response repeats an old practice. The verse exposes the depth of a habit through that contrast. They confront divine judgment but still reach for speech designed to manage appearances.

The gathering leaves no member of the group beyond the account. Their previous circumstances may have differed, and some may have been more successful at concealment than others. Resurrection removes those practical differences as sources of escape. What remains visible in the sentence is their common resort to a lie. This does not mean that Allah has called them together because He needs their testimony to discover the facts. He is the One who raises them and already knows what their words attempt to hide. The oath therefore reveals their condition rather than supplying Him with necessary evidence. The opening scene is disturbing precisely because a practice that may once have seemed useful survives into the setting where its uselessness should be obvious. A life shaped by managing others' beliefs has not prepared its practitioners for truthful acknowledgment before their Lord.

**The Same Oath Is Directed to an Entirely Different Hearer**

They swear to Allah as they swear to the believers. The comparison concerns their method, not a similarity between divine and human knowledge. Human listeners can be deceived because they do not have direct access to another person's inward reality. Allah cannot be deceived because that reality is already known to Him. The speakers carry their old method across a boundary where the method loses its supposed power.

An oath ordinarily invokes Allah as witness to the truth of a claim. Here the speakers use false assurances in addressing the very One whose witness gives an oath its seriousness. The contradiction exposes more than poor strategy. It shows the extent to which sacred speech has become detached from reverence in their practice. They use the form because they associate it with obtaining favorable treatment, not because they acknowledge the truth it ought to bind them to. The verse does not suggest that the false words briefly confuse the divine judgment. Their falsehood is named from the beginning. What changes is the visibility of the failure. The hearer before whom they now stand is not limited by the uncertainty that their oaths once exploited. The comparison dismantles the assumption that a successful human performance can become a valid defense before Allah.

**An Outward Profession Had Been Mistaken for a Final Acquittal**

Ibn Kathīr explains their expectation through the outward treatment they received in earthly life: people dealt with them according to their apparent profession, while Allah knew what lay beneath it ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:18](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/18/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked addition supplies an important distinction beyond the bare fact of their lying. Human recognition of an outward status is not the same as Allah's final approval of a person's inward reality.

A social or legal community cannot operate by claiming unrestricted knowledge of hearts. Its treatment of a person may therefore rest on public evidence and profession. Exploiting that necessary limitation does not make the hidden falsehood true. The hypocrites mistake a rule governing human treatment for a verdict governing divine judgment. That mistake is what their expectation of having something to stand on expresses. They imagine that the form which secured standing among people must carry the same force before Allah. The verse refuses the inference without requiring human beings to abandon fair outward procedures. The defect lies in the deceiver's abuse of those procedures and his false confidence about what they accomplish. Being treated as a believer by people does not authorize someone knowingly to conceal rejection and then claim that the treatment has purified his actual stance.

**Confidence Can Become Part of Self-Deception**

The speakers think they possess a basis for their claim. Their confidence is therefore not merely a show directed at an audience; it belongs to the false judgment they make about their own position. Repeatedly obtaining an advantage through a method can encourage the belief that the method itself establishes right. The verse places that belief beside the plain identification of them as liars.

The contrast distinguishes assurance from evidence. A person can speak firmly, repeat a claim often, and feel supported by previous success while still lacking truth. The intensity of conviction does not create the missing foundation. Nor does calling the belief self-deception make the original falsehood innocent. The verse has identified deliberate lying in the pattern being repeated. A practice can begin as deception of others and become a distorted account of one's own security. This is one reason the warning matters before judgment. Habits of speech shape the responses a person reaches for under pressure. If every challenge is answered by preserving appearances, the capacity for confession and correction is steadily neglected. The verse does not present a clinical theory of the mind. It presents a concrete moral relation: false oaths coexist with imagined standing, while divine testimony denies the standing those oaths are supposed to provide.

**The Final Declaration Allows No Negotiation Over the Truth**

The ending emphatically names them as liars. It is not one competing allegation among others in the scene. Allah's statement settles the truth of the speech before any performance can make it ambiguous. The people may believe their oaths support them, but the divine declaration identifies precisely what those oaths cannot change.

This final contrast also limits the reader's role. The verse reveals a specific judgment; it does not give every observer permission to declare the inner faith of others on the basis of suspicion. Its force should be directed toward the danger it actually exposes: using religious profession as a substitute for sincerity and mistaking outward acceptance for final security. The proper remedy is truthfulness before the moment when false assurance has no possible usefulness. Confession of a wrong may cost standing among people, but a lie cannot secure standing before Allah. The passage joins universal resurrection, repeated false speech, imagined support, and decisive exposure in a single sequence. The old method persists, but the old opportunity to deceive does not. What the speakers considered a defense becomes evidence of the very condition from which they refuse to turn.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:19

> Satan has taken hold of them, causing them to forget the remembrance of Allah. They are the party of Satan. Surely Satan’s party is bound to lose

**Expanded Commentary**

**Domination Is Seen in What No Longer Governs Their Attention**

Satan's hold is described through a particular result: the people forget remembrance of Allah. The verse does not direct the reader toward an imagined visible possession or a hidden physical mechanism. It identifies a moral condition in which the awareness that should govern conduct has been displaced. Their decisions proceed without effective recollection of the Lord before whom they are answerable.

This is more serious than an ordinary lapse in memory. A person can forget a word or miss a detail without having adopted the allegiance condemned here. The verse concerns people whose established conduct reflects a deeper surrender of attention. Remembrance has ceased to act as a restraint upon falsehood or a summons toward obedience. Satan's success is therefore not measured merely by whether his own name appears in their speech. They may never describe themselves as serving him. What matters is whose purposes their conduct advances and what has been excluded from the heart's deliberation. The description challenges a narrow idea of spiritual danger in which only dramatic acts count. Persistent neglect can leave the governing place empty, and the conduct that fills it can reveal an allegiance the people would never openly claim.

**Remembrance Joins Speech to the Direction of Life**

Remembering Allah includes words of praise, but the verse's contrast concerns a whole moral orientation. Pronouncing His name while deliberately excluding His command from action would not undo the condition being exposed. The remembrance neglected here should keep the person conscious of truth, responsibility, and the authority of the One remembered. Its absence allows a false account of security to become habitual.

Abū Mūsā reports the Prophet's comparison between the person who remembers his Lord and the person who does not as the difference between the living and the dead. The report is verified in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6407 ([Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6407](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6407)). The comparison concerns spiritual vitality, not a denial that the neglectful person remains biologically alive. It explains why remembrance cannot be treated as an ornamental addition to an otherwise complete life. The heart needs a living relation to its Lord if knowledge of right is to become responsive conduct. The report also avoids reducing the remedy to speculation about Satan. Attention is turned toward the act that gives the heart life. The believer counters domination not by endlessly describing the enemy but by restoring the remembrance and obedience that the enemy seeks to remove.

**Shared Worship Resists the Isolation in Which Neglect Grows**

A checked report from Abū al-Dardāʾ warns about a group among whom prayer is not established and describes Satan gaining mastery over them. It then urges congregational prayer, using the image of a wolf taking an isolated animal. This is Sunan Abī Dāwūd 547, graded ḥasan by al-Albānī on the checked page ([Sunan Abī Dāwūd 547](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:547)). The report adds a practical communal dimension beyond the per-verse account of forgetfulness.

Prayer in company places remembrance into shared time and action. People call, gather, stand, and return to a common obligation rather than leaving every impulse to be judged in isolation. The report's image is not a license to label someone under Satan's control merely because illness or another legitimate obstacle keeps him from a gathering. Its warning concerns abandonment of the communal practice it names. The relation to the verse is constructive: domination through forgetfulness is opposed by habits that preserve recollection. The community does not replace an individual's responsibility, but it can help sustain the conditions in which that responsibility remains visible. A neglected act of worship can become a neglected relation; a maintained act can repeatedly interrupt the assumption that the day's desires are the only claims upon a person's life.

**Their Party Is Defined by Allegiance Rather Than a Name They Adopt**

The people are called Satan's party. The description identifies the direction of their loyalty and conduct, not a formal organization with a membership register. They belong to the pattern because they have become instruments of its purpose. Their own preferred title cannot settle the matter when their acts reveal a contrary allegiance.

The collective language also explains why the verse moves beyond isolated temptation. People can reinforce one another's neglect, reward the same falsehoods, and make disobedience seem normal within their circle. The group then helps preserve the very condition in which remembrance has lost its place. Yet party language must not be turned into an easy label for every contemporary opponent. The verse gives Allah's judgment on an identified moral reality. A human disagreement does not by itself establish the comprehensive condition described here. The useful question is what conduct and allegiance the group actually cultivates. A person can test his own associations by asking whether they help him remember Allah truthfully or teach him to explain away what he knows is wrong. The verse's collective description makes that examination necessary. Company has moral force because it can sustain either responsiveness to guidance or mutual confidence in neglect.

**Loss Is the True Result of an Allegiance That Appears Useful**

The final declaration calls Satan's party losers. Their tactics may have produced temporary advantages, but the verse judges the whole outcome rather than a selected moment. The ability to deceive others or preserve status is not success if it forms a life increasingly detached from Allah. The loss concerns what that life becomes and where its allegiance leads.

The ending also prevents fascination with the enemy from eclipsing the moral verdict. Satan has gained a hold, but his party has not gained a secure future. His success in misleading does not become success for those misled. A person who follows a harmful guide may assist the guide's purpose while ruining his own. The verse places those different perspectives side by side: domination from Satan's side, loss from the followers' side. That distinction makes the warning urgent without making the adversary sovereign. The believer's practical response remains within reach through remembrance, worship, and truthful conduct rather than through admiration of hidden power. The sequence is complete: an alien mastery displaces remembrance, displaced remembrance marks a corrupted allegiance, and that allegiance leads to loss. The apparent usefulness of the arrangement cannot change its final description before Allah.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:20

> ˹As for˺ those who defy Allah and His Messenger, they will definitely be among the most debased

**Expanded Commentary**

**Defiance Is a Position Taken Against Revealed Authority**

The verse identifies its subjects by their action: they defy Allah and His Messenger. The description concerns a stance of opposition, not merely the presence of a question, an incomplete understanding, or a difference in social identity. The gravity of the warning follows from the authority resisted. The Messenger conveys Allah's guidance; defiance of that mission cannot be reduced to an ordinary disagreement between private individuals.

Ibn Kathīr explains the opposition as a separation between the position of stubborn refusal and the position of guidance ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:20](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/20/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked explanation sharpens the sense of the verb without turning it into a slogan against every critic. The question is not whether a person has disagreed with a powerful believer. It is whether he has taken the stance toward Allah and His Messenger that the verse names. That distinction matters because human authority can err. A teacher or ruler cannot simply appropriate the Messenger's standing and make opposition to himself identical with opposition to Allah. The verse's severity must remain attached to its actual object. It judges rejection of divine guidance, not the wounded pride of anyone claiming to speak in its name.

**The Messenger Is Named Through His Relation to Allah**

Allah and His Messenger appear together, but not as two rival sovereigns. The Messenger's authority comes from being sent. His words and commands in that capacity convey a claim that does not originate in personal ambition. The pairing therefore identifies the full direction of the defiance: it reaches through the mission to the One whose mission it is.

This prevents an evasive profession of loyalty to Allah that treats His chosen messenger as optional whenever guidance becomes inconvenient. It also prevents an exaggeration that makes the Messenger independent of Allah. Both errors separate what the verse joins. Obedience is ordered through a revealed relation rather than negotiated between two competing powers. The statement is particularly forceful because the opponents may imagine that they can lower the Messenger's standing by rejecting him. Their response does not determine the reality of his commission. A message does not become false because its recipients refuse the authority it conveys. The verse places the resulting dishonor upon the defiant, not upon the mission they oppose. The account of rank therefore begins with truth and authorization rather than with the number or confidence of those willing to accept the Messenger in a given moment.

**Debasement Is a Verdict, Not Merely a Bad Reputation**

The promised condition is inclusion among the most debased. This is stronger than saying that opposition will sometimes be embarrassing. The wording assigns a place within a class whose standing has collapsed before divine judgment. Those who reject guidance as beneath them discover that their assumed superiority has no foundation.

Ibn Kathīr explains the condition through exclusion from good and humiliation in this life and the Hereafter. The explanation extends the meaning beyond a temporary loss of public prestige. Yet it should not be turned into a timetable requiring every opponent to appear socially defeated at every moment. A person can retain earthly influence while his conduct is already dishonorable before Allah. The verse states the final truth of the stance, not a promise that observers can always read that truth directly from current wealth or office. This distinction protects the warning from being measured by a shallow test of success. A powerful person is not vindicated merely because he remains powerful, and someone suffering oppression is not condemned merely because he appears lowly. Divine debasement concerns the judgment of defiance under a standard that human reputation cannot create or abolish.

**Firmness Against Defiance Does Not Cancel Kindness and Justice**

The Qur'anic distinction between hostile opposition and peaceful relations must remain intact. In 60:8, Allah permits kindness and fairness toward those who have not fought believers on account of religion or driven them from their homes ([Qurʾān 60:8](https://quran.com/en/al-mumtahanah/8/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This checked parallel prevents the present warning from becoming a general prohibition on decent relations with people of another faith. The command concerns the stance being judged, not an obligation to be unjust toward everyone outside the believing community.

Kindness does not require declaring all beliefs identical, and religious disagreement does not require abandoning fairness. Those distinctions allow a believer to maintain conviction without manufacturing hostility. The verse's announcement of divine debasement is also not a command to humiliate people. Allah judges the defiant; human beings remain responsible for the conduct actually required of them. A reader who answers the warning by abusing a peaceful neighbor has added an action the sentence does not authorize. The broader evidence keeps the interpretation disciplined. The severity of Allah's verdict and the believer's duty to act justly are not competitors. A sound reading preserves both rather than using one revealed truth as a reason to silence another.

**The Certainty of the Verdict Removes the Need for Borrowed Arrogance**

The verse speaks with certainty. Believers do not need to reproduce their opponents' arrogance in order to make the warning effective. The outcome rests on Allah's judgment, not on the volume of human denunciation. This gives firmness a different character from retaliation driven by insecurity. A person can refuse defiance without believing that the truth needs his excesses to survive.

The form among the most debased also strips the opponents of imagined uniqueness. Their resistance does not create a special rank above the ordinary relation of creatures to their Lord. They enter a condition defined by the moral character of their stance. The reader's task is therefore not to invent dramatic scenes of their downfall but to understand why the stance cannot produce true honor. A life opposed to guidance cannot acquire lasting dignity simply by gathering allies or controlling appearances. At the same time, the warning invites examination of one's own response to revealed authority. It is not safely confined to an admired story about other people's failure. The verse's claim is short and complete: defiance of Allah and His Messenger leads to debasement. The authority to state that verdict belongs to Allah, while the responsibility to receive guidance truthfully falls upon everyone who hears it.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:21

> Allah has decreed, “I and My messengers will certainly prevail.” Surely Allah is All-Powerful, Almighty

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Promise Begins With a Decree, Not a Calculation**

Allah's declaration is presented as decreed. Its certainty does not depend on a favorable balance of visible resources or on observers agreeing that success seems likely. Human forecasts begin with limited evidence and remain open to correction. This statement begins with the authority of the One who determines the outcome. The order of the sentence makes the source of confidence unmistakable.

The verb translated decreed can carry the sense of writing or prescribing. Ibn Kathīr explains it here as a settled divine determination recorded in the decree ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:21](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/20/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). The point is not that the reader must imagine a physical process of inscription before trusting the promise. It is that the matter is established beyond the changes that unsettle human intention. A ruler may issue a declaration and later lack the power to carry it out. Allah's decree is not exposed to that division between intention and capacity. The final divine names will make that explicit. At the opening, the believer is already directed away from treating current appearances as the highest authority over what can happen. The promise has a ground stronger than the calculations by which an anxious or confident audience might assess it.

**The Messengers Prevail Through the Authority of Their Sender**

Allah includes His messengers in the declaration of prevailing. Their triumph is not independent of Him, nor does their inclusion make them divine. The relation is that of sender and those commissioned to convey His message. Their cause is inseparable from the truth and authority of the mission entrusted to them. The promise therefore honors the messengers without making them rival sources of power.

The plural also gives the declaration a horizon broader than one immediate contest. Allah's messengers belong to a shared history of revelation, even though their circumstances and the forms of opposition they face differ. The truth of their mission is not created by its reception. A messenger rejected by an audience does not become false because that audience refuses him. This matters for understanding prevailing. The declaration cannot be reduced to the rule that every messenger must enjoy uninterrupted public dominance at every stage of his life. The verse names a divinely secured outcome; it does not erase the trials involved in conveying guidance. Its assurance is precisely valuable where the visible path is difficult. The messenger does not have to manufacture the ultimate authority of his message through force of personality or numbers of supporters. That authority belongs to Allah, who has joined His decree to the mission He sends.

**Prevailing Is Larger Than a Single Visible Encounter**

The text does not identify one instrument through which the promise must be fulfilled. Truth can be vindicated through evidence, falsehood can lose its claim to authority, and historical power can change hands. These aspects should not be confused, but neither should the broad wording be narrowed prematurely to only one of them. The essential affirmation is that opposition will not overturn Allah's determined end for His messengers.

Ibn Kathīr connects the verse with 40:51–52, which speaks of support for the messengers and believers in earthly life and at the standing of the witnesses. This checked parallel adds the final judgment explicitly to the horizon of the promise. It prevents a reader from measuring all vindication by one campaign, one lifetime, or one period of political power. The comparison does not make worldly events irrelevant; it places them within a larger account. An immediate setback cannot by itself refute a promise whose scope exceeds that moment. Equally, a temporary victory cannot justify whatever a victorious party happens to do. The truth of the mission remains the measure. Prevailing under Allah's decree is not a blank approval of every human act performed while claiming to defend religion. The promise concerns His messengers and His truth, not ambition borrowing their names.

**The Divine Names Explain Why the Decree Cannot Be Defeated**

All-Powerful and Almighty close the verse. The decree is not merely sincere; it is backed by complete ability and invincible authority. Human beings can know a good purpose yet fail to achieve it because strength, opportunity, or control is missing. No such deficiency separates Allah's determination from its fulfillment. His promise is not vulnerable to a stronger power that might veto it.

The two names therefore belong to the argument rather than serving as an interchangeable ending. All-Powerful answers the question of capacity. Almighty excludes subjection to a rival force. Together they explain why the certainty expressed earlier is sound. The believer is not asked to create confidence by ignoring evidence; he is taught which authority stands above the limited evidence available to him. That confidence should produce steadiness rather than carelessness. Allah's power does not make deceit or negligence a faithful response to His command. The messengers carry out their missions; their followers must still obey the guidance they receive. Trust concerns the ultimate source of success, not permission to abandon responsibility. The names free the heart from imagining that divine truth survives only because human beings are sufficiently clever or numerous to keep it alive.

**Certainty About Allah's Cause Is Not Certainty About Every Human Claim**

A group cannot simply attach this verse to its preferred project and declare every desired outcome guaranteed. The statement concerns Allah and His messengers. Later actors must establish that their conduct genuinely follows revealed guidance rather than assuming that religious language makes their plans identical with the divine decree. The promise calls for fidelity before it supplies grounds for confidence.

This distinction protects the assurance from becoming triumphalism. A person may sincerely hope for a particular result while admitting that his judgment is limited. He can remain certain that Allah's promise is true without claiming certainty that his own strategy is the promised means. Such humility does not weaken belief. It preserves the difference between revelation and interpretation. The verse's concise structure gives the proper order: a settled decree, the prevailing of Allah and His messengers, and the power that makes that decree secure. Human confidence should rest there rather than in an exaggerated account of its own role. The promise sustains obedience through uncertainty because it places the final outcome beyond the reach of opposition. It does not remove the need to act truthfully within the uncertainty that remains part of human life.

## Sūrah al-Mujādilah 58:22

> You will never find a people who ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day loyal to those who defy Allah and His Messenger, even if they were their parents, children, siblings, or extended family. For those ˹believers˺, Allah has instilled faith in their hearts and strengthened them with a spirit from Him. He will admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there forever. Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him. They are the party of Allah. Indeed, Allah’s party is bound to succeed

**Expanded Commentary**

**Faith Sets a Limit to Loyalty Even Within the Family**

The verse confronts the strongest ordinary bonds: parents, children, siblings, and the wider family. These ties can carry love, gratitude, duty, and a sense of shared identity. Their force is acknowledged by naming them. Yet none can make active defiance of Allah and His Messenger into something a believer must support. Faith establishes a limit that kinship cannot erase.

The wording concerns loyalty to those who defy, not an order to extinguish every natural affection toward a relative. A believer can love a parent while refusing the parent's demand to abandon faith. The distinction becomes explicit in 31:15, which forbids obeying parents who press for association of partners with Allah while still requiring courteous companionship in worldly life ([Qurʾān 31:15](https://quran.com/luqman/15)). This checked parallel adds a crucial practical boundary. Refusal of religious betrayal and continued good treatment can coexist. The present verse is not a license for private violence, cruelty, or indiscriminate severing of family obligations. It denies that family allegiance is ultimate. Where relatives seek support against divine guidance, the believer cannot make love an excuse for joining the defiance. The pressure is real, but it does not become a source of religious authority.

**The Last Day Gives Costly Fidelity Its Proper Horizon**

Belief in Allah is joined to belief in the Last Day. The second belief changes the scale on which family pressure is judged. A person may fear present loss of affection, protection, or belonging. Those costs can be profound, but they are not the whole future. The final account remains before Allah, whose approval cannot be replaced by the approval of even the closest relatives.

This does not trivialize the pain of a divided household. The verse's list makes the personal cost visible rather than hiding it beneath a general command. What faith supplies is a reason not to let that cost decide the truth. A person can seek reconciliation, speak respectfully, and preserve every lawful duty while refusing the particular allegiance that contradicts guidance. Such restraint may be harder than either surrendering completely or responding with anger. The Last Day gives that patient fidelity a horizon beyond immediate reaction. The believer is not required to win every family argument or obtain every relative's agreement before remaining faithful. He must keep his own conduct answerable to Allah. The verse therefore treats true belief as a relation that orders the strongest human attachments, not as an addition that applies only when those attachments happen to permit it.

**Faith Is Established in Hearts Rather Than Merely Displayed in Speech**

Allah has instilled faith in the hearts of those described. The expression presents inward firmness as His gift. Their loyalty is not sustained only by public pressure or a desire to appear uncompromising. It rests upon a faith that has become established within them. The location in the heart answers the temptation to make religious identity a performance whose content changes with the audience.

Ibn Kathīr explains the inscription of faith as Allah's establishing it and making it dear within their hearts ([Ibn Kathīr on 58:22](https://quran.com/en/al-mujadila/20/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). That explanation does not remove their action from the verse. They are the people whose conduct has been described, and their firmness is then identified as a blessing from Allah. Grace and responsibility are not offered as rival accounts. The believer acts faithfully while recognizing that the faith enabling fidelity is not a possession he created independently. This prevents the costly refusal of a wrong allegiance from becoming a new source of arrogance. One may have resisted pressure successfully without thereby becoming self-sufficient. The heart's firmness is a gift to preserve through obedience and gratitude, not a title that authorizes contempt for people still struggling with difficult attachments.

**A Spirit From Allah Supplies Strength Without Dividing His Being**

The believers are strengthened with a spirit from Him. The phrase names a divine gift of support; it does not mean that a portion of Allah's being enters human beings. Ibn Kathīr preserves an explanation attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās that emphasizes strength. That reading fits the immediate setting, where believers need support to remain faithful under powerful relational pressure.

The wider interpretive vocabulary can speak of guidance, light, or the life-giving effect of faith. These senses describe how divine help makes the heart capable of a response it might otherwise fail to sustain. The verse itself does not require identifying the spirit here with a particular angel or reducing it to a single created medium. The strongest immediate explanation is therefore the broad one supplied by the context: Allah reinforces the believers with support from Himself. It preserves the wording without asserting more than the evidence establishes. Their firmness does not become harshness for its own sake. The help serves fidelity to guidance, including the duties of fairness and kindness that guidance also commands. A person cannot appeal to spiritual strength as a reason to disregard the revealed limits on his own behavior. The support is for obedience, not for the unrestricted expression of personal hostility.

**The Promise Joins Permanent Abode to Mutual Contentment**

Allah promises Gardens with flowing rivers and lasting residence. The reward answers the fear that fidelity leaves the believer permanently deprived of belonging and security. The earthly ties may impose a cost, but the promised end is not homelessness before Allah. It is admission to an enduring good that does not depend on the approval withheld by hostile relatives.

The verse then names something beyond the place itself: Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him. The two directions are related but not identical. His pleasure is the approval of the Lord; theirs is the contentment of servants receiving His favor. They do not judge Allah from an independent superior position. Their hearts rest in the goodness He grants. Ibn Kathīr explains this contentment through the delight and encompassing favor given to them. The promise therefore is not merely compensation measured as a larger quantity of what was lost. It brings a healed relation to the One for whose sake the difficult choice was made. The verse's order moves from inward faith and support to a lasting abode and complete acceptance. The believer's future is defined by divine generosity rather than by the temporary isolation that obedience may have required.

**Allah's Party Is Known by Faithfulness, Not by Appropriating a Title**

The closing designation identifies these believers as Allah's party and declares their success. The description follows the qualities and gifts already named. It is not a title that any faction can seize and then use as proof that all its actions are right. The group belongs to Allah through the faith and fidelity the verse describes, under the judgment He gives.

This conclusion gathers the whole verse without reducing it to political membership. True allegiance survives pressure from the nearest relationships; faith is established in the heart; divine help sustains it; and Allah grants lasting reward and contentment. Success belongs to that complete relation, not merely to winning an argument against one's relatives. The closing chapter therefore leaves the believer with a measure of belonging deeper than ancestry and a promise stronger than social approval. Yet it also leaves humility intact. No reader can manufacture Allah's pleasure by loudly claiming the name of His party. The path is to receive His guidance and act within it, including where personal attachment makes obedience costly. The promised success is His gift to those He identifies, not a tool for exempting self-appointed representatives from examination.
