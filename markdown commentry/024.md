# Sūrah an-Nūr (Chapter 24) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**The Slander Against ʿĀ'ishah and the Crisis of the Commonwealth**

The historical furnace out of which Sūrah an-Nūr descended was the traumatic crisis of *Ḥadīth al-Ifk* (The Event of the Slander) in 5 or 6 AH, following the expedition of Banū al-Muṣṭaliq. ʿĀ'ishah, the young wife of the Prophet ﷺ and daughter of Abū Bakr, was accidentally left behind in the desert when her camel litter was carried away without anyone noticing her light weight, after she had stepped out to search for a dropped necklace. Found hours later by a young Companion, Ṣafwān ibn al-Muʿaṭṭal, who escorted her back to Madinah on his camel while walking respectfully ahead of her, their innocent arrival was seized upon by ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and the hypocrites to fabricate a vile rumor of adultery. When all earthly avenues of verification were exhausted, verses 11–26 of Sūrah an-Nūr descended from above the seven heavens: passionately clearing ʿĀ'ishah of all false charges, severely reprimanding the community for not instantly rejecting the rumor with righteous indignation (*“Why, when you heard it, did you not say: 'It is not for us to speak of this! Exalted are You [O Allah]; this is a monstrous slander!'”*, 24:16), and legislating the penal sentence for false accusations against chaste women (*qadhf*).

**The Structural Modulation from Law to Mystical Illumination**

The architecture of Sūrah an-Nūr is a breathtaking synthesis of exacting penal law, domestic etiquette, and sublime mystical metaphysics. The opening movement (1–26) codifies the legal penalties for fornication (*zinā*), establishes the mandatory requirement of four eyewitnesses for allegations of adultery to protect society from reckless character assassination, institutes the procedure of mutual swearing (*liʿān*) between spouses, details the divine exoneration of ʿĀ'ishah, and commands Abū Bakr to forgive and continue supporting his relative who participated in the slander: *“Do you not love that Allah should forgive you? And Allah is Forgiving and Merciful”* (24:22). The second movement (27–34) establishes the sanctity of private life: mandating that believers never enter homes other than their own without seeking permission (*isti'nās*) and greeting the occupants with peace, commanding believing men and women to lower their gazes and guard their modesty, legislating the outer garment (*khimār*) over the bosom, and commanding the community to help marriages for single men, women, and righteous slaves, assuring that God will enrich them from His bounty. The third movement (35–45) forms the luminous, metaphysical heart of the sūrah: the Verse of Light (24:35), the description of the mosques where the light is nurtured by men whom neither trade nor sale distracts from the remembrance of God, contrasted against the twin parables of disbelief—the mirage in the desert that the thirsty traveler imagines to be water until he reaches it and finds God there to pay him his account. The terrifying darkness in a fathomless ocean, where wave rides upon wave beneath black clouds, such that if a man stretches out his hand he can barely see it: *“And he to whom Allah has not granted light has no light!”* (24:40). The fourth movement (46–64) examines civic obedience: exposing the hypocrisy of those who demand secular arbitration when they are guilty but rush to divine courts when they have a claim, commanding reverence and respect for the assembly of the Messenger ﷺ, and concluding with the sovereign reminder: *“Unquestionably, to Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth.”*

## Verse 24:1

> ˹This is˺ a sûrah which We have revealed and made ˹its rulings˺ obligatory, and revealed in it clear commandments so that you may be mindful

**A Sūrah Given as a Duty**

The opening words establish a formal legislative charter without preamble or narrative preface: *sūratun anzalnāhā wa-faraḍnāhā*. Grammatically, the indefinite noun *sūratun* stands as the predicate of an omitted demonstrative pronoun, meaning: this is a magnificent chapter that We have sent down. Many chapters open with isolated letters, oaths sworn by cosmic phenomena, or historical chronicles. This sūrah begins instead with an immediate declaration of divine authorship and legal necessity. The verb *faraḍnāhā* conveys a decisive moral weight. In the old Arabic dictionaries, the root *f-r-ḍ* means notching, cutting, or fixing a definitive measure into a solid object. When applied to legal injunctions, it designates duties that are carved into the communal order with unyielding permanence. The revelation is not presented merely as wise counsel or recommended etiquette. Every ordinance established across the chapter carries the binding force of a divine decree that believers are required to enact within their personal conduct and their collective judicial institutions.

**The Grammar of Injunction: faraḍnā versus farraḍnā**

Scholars of the Qur'anic readings observed a meaningful distinction in the vocalization of the central verb. The vast majority of canonical reciters read *faraḍnāhā* with a single ungeminated letter, emphasizing the establishment of binding obligation across the entirety of the text. An alternative canonical reading transmitted from Ibn Kathīr vocalizes the verb with gemination as *farraḍnāhā*. This morphological intensification carries two distinct interpretive senses according to classical grammarians. First, it points to the detailed apportionment and distribution of many distinct legal rulings throughout the chapter. Each legal matter receives its own specific measure, form, and penalty. Second, gemination stresses the intensity and gravity of the obligations themselves. The two readings illuminate complementary aspects of the divine legislative will.

## Verse 24:2

> As for female and male fornicators, give each of them one hundred lashes, and do not let pity for them make you lenient in ˹enforcing˺ the law of Allah, if you ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. And let a number of believers witness their punishment

**The Penalty Named Exactly**

The statute opens with an exact numerical decree: *al-zāniyatu wa-al-zānī fajlidū kulla wāḥidin minhumā miʾata jaldah*. in Arabic phrasing, placing the female before the male marks a noticeable shift from the legal syntax of theft in 5:38, where the male is named first. Exegetes note that illicit intimacy involves mutual physical agency, but historical social vulnerability and the domestic consequences of lineage corruption fell with singular weight upon women. Placing the woman first stresses that the moral prohibition binds her directly as an autonomous moral agent, while immediately pairing her with the male ensures equal accountability under the whip. Neither partner is excused or treated as incidental to the crime.

**The Jurisprudential Debate Over Marital Status and Sanctions**

Conversely, groups among the early Khawārij and several independent jurists throughout Islamic history maintained that the clear, unambiguous text of the Qur'an must remain absolute and uncompromised. They argued that solitary historical reports cannot override or append capital penalties to an explicit Qur'anic statute that prescribes solely lashes. They also pointed to verses that halve the punishment for enslaved individuals in 4:25, reasoning that death by stoning is mathematically indivisible, whereas one hundred lashes can readily be reduced by half.

---

**The Two Clauses Around the Number**
The verse does not stop at the hundred lashes. It adds two instructions that keep the penalty from becoming a private or a spiteful act. The first is a warning to the community that carries it out: *and let not pity for them overcome you in the religion of God, if you believe in God and the Last Day*. The mercy a person feels for a wrongdoer is not to be allowed to cancel a law, which is why the sentence is tied to faith in God and the Last Day — the one place where sentiment cannot be used as a vote. The second clause requires a public act: *and let a group of the believers witness their punishment*. The sentence is carried out in front of people, so the community sees the law working and the offender is not destroyed in a corner by men who owe him money.
**The Ruling and the Differences Around It**
The verse states the penalty for the unmarried offender. The Prophet's practice, recorded in the two collections and applied by the caliphs who followed him, prescribed stoning for the person who had been married and the lashing here for the one who had not, and that is the position of the four schools. A minority held that the Qur'anic verse allows only the hundred lashes in every case, arguing that a statute stated this plainly cannot be extended; their reading is recorded in the books of law and answered by the reports of the Prophet's own judgements, and the disagreement belongs to the history of the law rather than to the wording of the verse. What the verse itself fixes is beyond dispute: the penalty is set by God, it is not reduced for a rich man or increased for a poor one, and no one may take the enforcement of it into his own hands. The proof required before it is applied is deliberately hard to produce, which is why the sūrah goes on to legislate the witnesses and to punish the accusation that cannot be proved.

## Verse 24:3

> A male fornicator would only marry a female fornicator or idolatress. And a female fornicator would only be married to a fornicator or idolater. This is ˹all˺ forbidden to the believers

**Why the Pairing Shocks**
The verse joins the one who persists in sexual sin with the idolater: a fornicator marries a fornicator or an idolatress, and a fornicatress marries a fornicator or an idolater. The pairing startled the commentators, and it should. Idolatry is a rejection of God altogether; unchastity is a major sin committed by someone who may still call himself a believer. The pairing does not put the two on the same level, nor does it take a Muslim out of the faith for one grave sin. It shows what a settled habit does to a person: a life built on satisfying the body makes a natural match with a life that has no place for God in it.
**The Occasion in Madinah**
The ruling answered a real situation in the young city. Many of the Emigrants had arrived with nothing, and some of the poorest Companions lived on the bench of the mosque, the *Aṣḥāb al-Ṣuffah*. At the same time, certain wealthy women of Madinah had made their fortunes from prostitution in the days before Islam and still kept their establishments, and there were men ready to urge marriage with them for the sake of the money. The verse closed the trade at the door of the community. It protected the poor believer from being drawn into a marriage arranged for profit and then made the arrangement a matter of belief rather than taste.
**Forbidden to the Believers**
The verse ends with *wa-ḥurrima dhālika ʿalā al-muʾminīn* — and that is forbidden to the believers. The prohibition is put on the community as a whole and attached to the name *muʾminīn*, which is the emphasis: this is not a rule for the weak or the poor. Anyone who carries the name carries the standard with it. The reply to a market that sells bodies is not a slogan but a boundary, and the boundary is drawn around the household, where the next generation is raised.

## Verse 24:4

> Those who accuse chaste women ˹of adultery˺ and fail to produce four witnesses, give them eighty lashes ˹each˺. And do not ever accept any testimony from them—for they are indeed the rebellious—

**Three Penalties at Once**

The law imposes a comprehensive threefold penalty upon the unsubstantiated accuser: eighty lashes, permanent disenfranchisement from legal testimony, and public branding as moral rebels. The first punishment strikes the body: *fajlidūhum thamānīna jaldah*. Administering eighty lashes inflicts severe corporal humiliation upon those who tried to inflict social shame upon an innocent soul. The physical pain mirrors the social laceration they sought to cause.

**The Sanctity of Honor and the Crime of Qadhf**

The revelation erects an unyielding fortress around personal dignity through the legislation about unproven accusation: *wa-alladhīna yarmūna al-muḥṣanāti thumma lam yaʾtū bi-arbaʿati shuhadāʾ*. The Arabic verb *yarmūna* literally means hurling stones or shooting arrows at a target. By selecting this visceral metaphor, the Qur'an portrays defamation not as mere careless speech, but as a ballistic assault designed to shatter an innocent person's social standing. The tongue becomes a weapon capable of inflicting permanent psychic and social devastation upon its victim.

**Three Penalties at Once**

The second sanction dismantles their civic authority: *wa-lā taqbalū lahum shahādatan abadā*. Stripping an individual of testamentary capacity in a society reliant upon personal reputation for commerce and justice is a profound civil demotion. The third consequence defines their spiritual status: *wa-ulāʾika humu al-fāsiqūn*. The label *fāsiq* means one who has ruptured the bounds of moral decency. Together, these measures neutralize the defamer physically, socially, and spiritually, rendering them incapable of poisoning the community further. The punishment matches the many-sided damage wrought by defamation.

## Verse 24:5

> except those who repent afterwards and mend their ways, then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful

**The Door Left Open**
The verse follows the penalty for accusing a chaste woman without witnesses — eighty lashes, and testimony that will never be accepted — and it names the one exception: *except those who repent after that and put things right*. Then it states the outcome: God is forgiving, most merciful. The sūrah is careful about where it places the mercy, because it has just spent a verse closing a door on a man's word.
**What Putting Things Right Requires**
Al-Ṭabarī and al-Qurṭubī report that the early jurists would not accept a bare apology. A slanderer who wanted the exception had to stand where he had done the harm and say aloud that what he had attributed to the person was false. Stopping the talk was not enough. Mending meant the name was repaired in front of the same people who had heard it damaged, and it meant the victim's honour was handed back rather than merely left alone.
**The Majority Position**
Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal read the exception as covering the whole verse. Repentance, in their view, restores the man both before God and before the court: he has admitted the lie publicly, and his testimony becomes admissible again, and the penalty of the Hereafter is lifted from him. Their argument is grammatical as much as legal — the exception is attached to the sentence as a whole and not to one phrase in it.
**The Ḥanafī Position**
Abū Ḥanīfah and his school read it differently. In their view the exception reaches only the last clause of the previous verse: the slanderer who repents is no longer classed as a rebel and is forgiven by God, but his testimony remains permanently unacceptable in a court. On this reading the public interest in trustworthy testimony cannot be repaired by the same act that mends the man's soul, and the community keeps its record of who broke a person's name.
**What the Verse Settles**
The two positions agree on the main thing. A slanderer is not sealed off from God, and the repair he owes is public and specific rather than a private feeling. The sūrah has been building the apparatus for the protection of honour — witnesses, oaths, penalties — and this verse adds the only part of the law a court cannot enforce: the return of a person who did wrong.

## Verse 24:6

> And those who accuse their wives ˹of adultery˺ but have no witness except themselves, the accuser must testify, swearing four times by Allah that he is telling the truth

**When a Husband Accuses His Wife**

The revelation addresses an unique domestic predicament that ruptures the ordinary rules of evidence: *wa-alladhīna yarmūna azwājahum wa-lam yakun lahum shuhadāʾu illā anfusuhum*. The law of slander in 24:4 established an unyielding barrier against defamation, requiring four eyewitnesses and sentencing unproven accusers to eighty lashes. In typical public interactions, that standard shields innocent citizens from malice. Marriage, however, introduces an intimate sphere of life shielded from the public gaze. An act of marital infidelity occurs in private spaces where four independent observers are virtually never present.

A husband who discovers his wife in betrayal faces an agonizing dilemma. If he remains silent, he endures psychological devastation, risks raising a child not his own, and tolerates a desecrated marriage bed. If he speaks publicly without four external witnesses, the general statute of 24:4 would command that he be flogged with eighty lashes as a slanderer. This verse resolves the crisis by introducing the procedure of *liʿān* (mutual imprecation), providing a distinct judicial pathway tailored strictly to the spousal relationship. The law balances personal grief with institutional equity, acknowledging domestic vulnerability while preventing unchecked slander.

**The Jurisprudential Distinction Between Spouses and Strangers**

Islamic jurisprudence strictly confines the procedure of *liʿān* to legally married couples. Scholars across the major schools—including Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, and Abū Ḥanīfah—emphasized that a stranger who accuses a married woman without four eyewitnesses receives no exemption and is immediately sentenced to eighty lashes for *qadhf*. The exception is granted exclusively to a husband because of the existential interests at stake.

**The Historical Context: The Dilemma of Hilāl ibn Umayyah**

The historical occasion for this legislation is preserved in authoritative traditions, including the report in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4747 about Hilāl ibn Umayyah. Hilāl came before the Prophet ﷺ after witnessing his wife in compromise with another man. Upon hearing the claim, the Prophet ﷺ held firmly to the established law of slander, declaring: Either bring your proof or face the whip upon your back.

**The Moral Gravity of the Public Assembly**

Classical jurists stated that the administration of these oaths must occur in a public and venerated setting. Imām al-Shāfiʿī and Imām Mālik recommended that the ceremony be conducted in the central congregational mosque immediately following the afternoon prayer, surrounded by a gathering of upright believers.

## Verse 24:7

> and a fifth oath that Allah may condemn him if he is lying

**The Judicial Admonition Before the Fifth Oath**

The profound gravity of this final oath is reflected in the procedural safeguards instituted by the Prophet ﷺ. In authentic traditions, such as the narration in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4748, the Prophet ﷺ instructed the judicial authority to intervene directly before the fifth declaration. The judge places a hand over the man's mouth or issues a stern verbal admonition: Pause, for this fifth oath brings the inescapable consequence of God's wrath, and the punishment of the Hereafter is far more severe than the shame of this world.

In the Qur'anic lexicon, the term *laʿnah* means far more than temporary anger or verbal rebuke; it means absolute expulsion and banishment from divine mercy. To be cursed by God is to be stripped of spiritual protection, abandoned in this worldly life, and consigned to everlasting torment in the Hereafter.

**The Legal Consequences of Completing the Husband's Oaths**

The formal utterance of the fifth oath triggers immediate legal consequences. First, the husband is officially shielded from the statutory punishment of eighty lashes decreed in 24:4 for unproven slander. His sworn imprecations legally satisfy his obligation of proof as an accusing spouse. Second, if the husband accompanied his accusation with a formal renunciation of paternity (*nafy al-walad*), the child is legally detached from his lineage.

**The Irrevocable Dissolution of the Domestic Bond**

Imām Mālik, Imām al-Shāfiʿī, and Imām Aḥmad maintained that the separation resulting from *liʿān* creates an eternal prohibition (*taḥrīm muʾabbad*). The couple can never remarry under any circumstances, even if both later retract their oaths or express mutual forgiveness. Abū Ḥanīfah held that the dissolution requires a formal decree from the judge, but concurred that the marital bond is irreparably severed. The permanence of the separation serves as a sobering reminder of the finality of marital slander, permanently guarding the home against toxic reconciliation and ongoing recrimination.

## Verse 24:8

> For her to be spared the punishment, she must swear four times by Allah that he is telling a lie

**The Definition of al-ʿadhāb in the Context of Liʿān**

Classical jurists engaged in thorough analysis about the precise nature of the punishment (*al-ʿadhāb*) that the wife's oaths avert. The majority of scholars, including Imām al-Shāfiʿī, Imām Mālik, and Imām Aḥmad, held that *al-ʿadhāb* refers to the statutory corporal punishment for adultery established in 24:2. If the husband has completed his oaths and the wife refuses to swear her counter-oaths, she is deemed to have implicitly conceded the accusation, by this facing the prescribed penalty.

**The Parity of Defense: Averting the Statutory Punishment**

The revelation establishes an immediate procedural defense for the accused woman: *wa-yadraʾu ʿanhā al-ʿadhāba a tashhada arbaʿa shahādātin bi-Allāh*. The verb *yadraʾu* means repelling, warding off, or deflecting an impending blow. Once the husband completes his five solemn oaths, a grave legal presumption arises against the wife. In many ancient and contemporary legal traditions, a husband's formal accusation was enough to condemn a woman without granting her equal recourse.

**The Symmetry of Four Oaths of Denial**

In classical Islamic evidentiary jurisprudence, the wife's sworn word carries legal power identical to that of the husband. Her testimony is not discounted on the basis of gender or social standing. Each of her four oaths systematically neutralizes one of his four oaths.

## Verse 24:9

> and a fifth oath that Allah may be displeased with her if he is telling the truth

**The Prophetic Interruption and the Moment of Trembling**

The Sunnah preserves a moving record of the psychological gravity that attended this fifth oath. In authoritative traditions, including the report in Sunan Abī Dāwūd 2256 and related accounts in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, the Prophet ﷺ commanded the presiding authority to pause the woman before she uttered the final formula. The judge warned her: Fear God, for this fifth oath brings the inescapable consequence of divine retribution, and the punishment of the Hereafter is far more severe than worldly disgrace.

**The Theological Distinction Between laʿnah and ghaḍab**

the commentators, including al-Zamakhsharī and Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, observed a profound linguistic and religious distinction in the divine choice of words. In verse 7, the husband invokes *laʿnah* (the curse of God), whereas in verse 9, the wife invokes *ghaḍab* (the wrath of God). While both terms denote severe divine displeasure, *ghaḍab* represents an even more intense and immediate manifestation of divine retribution than *laʿnah*.

**The Lineage and Custody of the Child**

When the process of *liʿān* involves a husband disowning a child born of the marriage, the completion of the oaths establishes definitive legal rulings about lineage and custody. The Prophet ﷺ decreed that the child is attributed exclusively to the mother: *al-walad li-ummih*. The child inherits from the mother and her heirs, and the mother inherits from the child, while all legal ties to the former husband are severed.

Crucially, the law erects a formidable shield around the mother and child against future slander. Anyone who dares cast aspersions on the child's legitimacy or defames the mother based upon the severed marriage is subjected to the full penalty of eighty lashes for *qadhf*. Divine law ensures that the child is not rendered an outcast or subjected to social stigmatization, preserving their dignity through legitimate maternal kinship, guaranteeing that no innocent newborn bears the stigma of parental conflict.

## Verse 24:10

> ˹You would have suffered,˺ had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy upon you, and had Allah not been Accepting of Repentance, All-Wise

**The Two Names That Close the Passage**
The verse ends the section on false accusation with two names of God. *Al-Tawwāb* is the One who keeps turning towards His servants, accepting their return each time they come back. *Al-Ḥakīm* is the Wise, and the verse ties the wisdom to the rules just given: the witnesses, the oaths, the eighty lashes, the refusal to hear a slanderer's word. The law is strict because reputations cannot be unbroken by an apology, and the law ends with mercy because the community is made of people who fall.
**Why the Names Come Here**
The verses before this one describe the very faults that produce slander: haste, suspicion, anger, jealousy inside a household, panic in a crowd. A person who has read the penalty list needs to know that the door is not nailed shut, and the verse supplies it before the sūrah moves on. The same two names recur at the end of the passage on the great slander itself, where the sūrah tells the Muslims not to keep repeating the story once the truth has come (24:20), and the connection is deliberate: a community that has been given strict rules is being taught to live by them without turning cruel.
**Where the Sūrah Goes Next**
The next verse opens the account of the slander against the Prophet's household, the hardest test the young Muslim community had faced in Madinah (24:11). This verse stands immediately before it, a reminder of who God is placed in front of a story about what people do. Whoever reads the two together is meant to carry the names into the account that follows.

## Verse 24:11

> Indeed, those who came up with that ˹outrageous˺ slander are a group of you. Do not think this is bad for you. Rather, it is good for you. They will be punished, each according to their share of the sin. As for their mastermind, he will suffer a tremendous punishment

**The Mastermind and the Tremendous Punishment**

Ibn Ubayy orchestrated the campaign to fracture the Muslim community, discredit the prophetic household, and destabilize the state. Because his crime was rooted in cynical hypocrisy and unrepentant enmity against God and His Messenger, the verse promises him a *ʿadhābun ʿaẓīm*—a tremendous, overwhelming punishment in the lowest depths of the Hellfire, where no intercession or mitigation will reach him.

**The Anatomy of the Outrageous Slander (al-Ifk)**

The revelation confronts the most severe crisis of reputation in early Islamic history: *inna alladhīna jāʾū bi-al-ifki ʿuṣbatun minkum*. In the Arabic language, *al-ifk* means the most heinous, inverted falsehood—a lie that completely turns reality upside down. The crisis occurred following the military expedition of Banū al-Muṣṭaliq. The Prophet's wife ʿĀʾishah was inadvertently left behind in the desert when her enclosed litter was mounted onto her camel without attendants noticing her absence, as she had stepped aside to search for a lost necklace.

When the pious and valorous companion Ṣafwān ibn al-Muʿaṭṭal, tasked with following the army to collect fallen belongings, discovered the Mother of the Believers asleep in the desert, he lowered his camel, turned his face in modest reverence, and led her back to Madinah in broad daylight. Enemies of the faith seized upon this innocent occurrence, fabricating a monstrous accusation of infidelity that circulated through the city for an entire month, wounding the Prophet ﷺ and throwing the community into profound turmoil.

**The Betrayal from Within: A Faction Among You**

The verse identifies the perpetrators with an unsettling phrase: *ʿuṣbatun minkum*—a cohesive group or faction among you. The assault did not come from pagan Quraysh marching across the sands, nor from hostile empires on distant borders; it was engineered within the civic body of Madinah itself. While the chief instigators were hypocrites (*munāfiqūn*) seeking to assassinate the moral authority of the Prophet ﷺ, several sincere believers fell victim to the hysteria and participated in circulating the rumor.

**The Divine Vindication of Innocence**

The resolution of the crisis shows the ultimate sovereignty of divine advocacy. In her famous narration in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4750, ʿĀʾishah remarked with profound humility that she hoped the Prophet ﷺ would see a dream in which God would clear her, but she considered herself far too insignificant for God to send down verses of the Qur'an that would be recited in prayers forever.

## Verse 24:12

> If only the believing men and women had thought well of one another, when you heard this ˹rumour˺, and said, “This is clearly ˹an outrageous˺ slander!”

**The Organic Unity of the Community: bi-anfusihim**

The verse employs an extraordinary phrase to describe the social bond of the faithful: *ẓanna al-muʾminūna wa-al-muʾminātu bi-anfusihim khayrā*—literally, that they should think well of their own selves. the commentators, including Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī, emphasized that the Qur'an portrays the community of believers as an indivisible, living organism. When a believer considers a fellow believer, they are gazing upon an extension of their own spiritual selfhood.

This principle echoes the authentic prophetic teaching in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2586: The believers in their mutual love, mercy, and compassion are like a single body; when one limb aches, the entire body responds with sleeplessness and fever. To entertain malicious rumors about a brother or sister in faith is to harbor poison against one's own flesh. True solidarity demands that an attack on the honor of another believer be felt and resisted with the exact same protective instinct one would deploy to defend one's personal reputation, shielding one's brethren as one would shield oneself against disgrace.

**The Obligation of Ḥusn al-Ẓann (Positive Presumption)**

The verse codifies the foundational ethical duty of *ḥusn al-ẓann*—assuming the best of fellow believers. In Islamic legal and moral philosophy, innocence and integrity constitute the baseline reality of every Muslim (*al-aṣl barāʾat al-dhimmah*). Suspicion without definitive proof is strictly forbidden, as emphasized in 49:12. When an outrageous accusation is leveled against individuals whose lives are characterized by piety and moral excellence, the default assumption must always favor their purity.

## Verse 24:13

> Why did they not produce four witnesses? Now, since they have failed to produce witnesses, they are ˹truly˺ liars in the sight of Allah

**The Demand for Witnesses**
*Lawlā jāʾū ʿalayhi bi-arbaʿati shuhadāʾ* — why did they not bring four witnesses to it? The sūrah has fixed the rule a few verses earlier: a person who accuses a chaste woman must produce four witnesses, or take eighty lashes and lose his testimony (24:4). The verse here applies the rule to the great slander itself, and the answer comes in the same sentence: since they did not bring the witnesses, they are the liars in the sight of God. The matter is settled by evidence rather than by feeling, and the sūrah is careful to say so before it describes what the accusation cost the household it was aimed at.
**What "in the Sight of God" Means**
Al-Rāzī took up the question of the phrase *ʿinda Allāh* — before God, in His sight. Does it mean the court has classified them as liars, or that God is calling the statement false in fact? His conclusion is that the two come together here. A person who speaks about the honour of another Muslim without the evidence God required has said something about God's religion that he cannot support, and the Book's word for that is lying. Even a man who thought he was repeating the truth is included, because the law was given to stop the mouth, not only to correct the mind.
**The Household and the Community**
The verse stands inside the passage on the slander against the Prophet's wife, the hardest test the young Muslim community faced in Madinah, and it is answered at the end of the passage with the promise that the sūrah will keep returning to: the accused is innocent, and the talkers were the ones at fault (24:16; 24:26). The verse also gives the community the habit that would keep it safe in every later crisis: when a claim about someone's honour arrives, ask for the evidence or say nothing. The sūrah's teaching is that a rumour is a legal claim, and a claim that cannot be proved in front of witnesses is a lie in front of God.

## Verse 24:14

> Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy upon you in this world and the Hereafter, you would have certainly been touched with a tremendous punishment for what you plunged into—

**The Second Evocation of Transcendent Grace**

The divine warning returns to the grand rhetorical structure of conditional grace: *wa-lawlā faḍlu Allāhi ʿalaykum wa-raḥmatuhū fī al-dunyā wa-al-ākhirah*. This marks the second time in the chapter that the phrase *lawlā faḍlu Allāhi* appears, but here the divine voice expands the scope to explicitly encompass both temporal existence (*fī al-dunyā*) and eternal reality (*al-ākhirah*).

In this earthly life, divine grace shielded the community from catastrophic social fracture, violent internal vendettas, and physical destruction. In the Hereafter, divine mercy shields the penitent believers from eternal damnation in the Hellfire. The repetition of this formula stresses that humanity lives perpetually on borrowed divine favor. Were God to hold human beings to strict, unmitigated accounting for every moral failure and careless utterance, no community could survive on the face of the earth for a single day. Divine benevolence acts as an invisible shield preserving human existence from its own folly and self-destruction, granting mortal creatures continuous opportunities to realign themselves with truth.

**The Metaphor of Plunging into Sin: afaḍtum fīh**

The verse employs a vivid and devastating verbal metaphor to describe the community's participation in the scandal: *fīmā afaḍtum fīh*, about that which you plunged into. The Arabic verb *afāḍa* derives from the root *f-y-ḍ*, which means an overflowing torrent, an inundating flood, or diving heedlessly into deep water.

---

## Verse 24:15

> when you passed it from one tongue to the other, and said with your mouths what you had no knowledge of, taking it lightly while it is ˹extremely˺ serious in the sight of Allah

**Alternative Readings: talaqqawna versus taliqūna**

Linguistic analysis of the central verb reveals deep layers of meaning across the classical readings. The standard canonical reading *talaqqawnahū* derives from the root *l-q-y*, meaning the eager reception and rapid handover of an object from one person to another, capturing the swift transmission of rumors across private gatherings.

A celebrated alternative reading transmitted from ʿĀʾishah herself vocalizes the verb as *taliqūnahū* from the root *w-l-q*. In the old Arabic dictionaries, *al-walq* means a rapid, darting strike, like a snake striking its prey, or a needle penetrating fabric in continuous, unbroken succession. In this reading, the verse describes people darting forward with their tongues, driving sharp verbal blades into the honor of the innocent with reckless speed. Both vocalizations capture the lethal velocity with which gossip spreads when restraint is abandoned, turning the tongue into a destructive weapon that inflicts deep psychic wounds upon the community and poisons the social well.

**Empty Mouths and Baseless Speech**

In the account of knowledge of the Qur'an, speech that lacks knowledge (*ʿilm*) is an abomination. God explicitly commands in 17:36: *“Do not pursue that of which you have no knowledge”* (17:36). To express claims about an individual's private life without verified eyewitness knowledge is to commit intellectual and spiritual fraud. The verse strips away all excuses: repeating what someone else claimed does not grant the speaker knowledge; it merely transforms the speaker into an active agent of falsehood, disseminating unverified claims without moral justification or factual foundation, compounding personal guilt.

**The Cosmic Weight of Honor: wa-huwa ʿinda Allāhi ʿaẓīm**

In the sight of God, the reputation and dignity of a believing soul are sacred. Classical traditions record that the Prophet ﷺ once looked upon the Kaʿbah and remarked that while its sanctity is immense, the sanctity of a believer's honor, blood, and property is even greater in the sight of God. To casually assault that sacred honor through unverified rumors is to commit an offense that shakes the moral order of creation, inviting divine wrath upon all who participated in its trivialization, demonstrating that human measures of sin are profoundly defective when measured against the divine standard of absolute justice.

## Verse 24:16

> If only you had said upon hearing it, “How can we speak about such a thing! Glory be to You ˹O Lord˺! This is a heinous slander!”

**The Exclamation of Transcendent Sanctification: subḥānak**

The commanded verbal response incorporates the sublime formula of glorification: *subḥānak*—Glory be to You, O Lord! the commentators, including Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī, explored why divine transcendence (*tasbīḥ*) is invoked upon hearing a human rumor. They identified two profound spiritual dimensions behind this exclamation.

First, *subḥānak* serves as an expression of sheer cosmic shock and awe at the monstrosity of the lie, recoiling to the purity of God as a spiritual refuge from earthly filth. Second, it serves as an affirmation of divine providence: God is far too exalted, wise, and protective of His chosen Messenger ﷺ to allow the Mother of the Believers to fall into such moral degradation. Exclaiming *subḥānak* affirms that God preserves the domestic sanctuary of His Prophet ﷺ from contamination, turning religious conviction into an immediate defense against suspicion, honoring the divine majesty above all earthly doubts, human whispers, and sinister plots devised by hypocrites across time.

**The Unambiguous Denunciation: hādhā buhtānun ʿaẓīm**

The verse demands that the reaction culminate in an explicit, fearless denunciation: *hādhā buhtānun ʿaẓīm*, this is a heinous, monstrous calumny. In the old Arabic dictionaries, *buhtān* is a specialized category of falsehood meaning a fabrication so shocking that it confounds (*yabhatu*) and paralyzes the innocent victim with disbelief.

**The Prophetic Standard of Verbal Safety**

The demanded reaction corresponds directly to the core definition of Islamic identity expressed in the Sunnah. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 10, the Prophet ﷺ established the gold standard of faith: The Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand other Muslims are safe. Verse 16 shows that verbal safety is not only passive silence; it is active communal protection.

---

## Verse 24:17

> Allah forbids you from ever doing something like this again, if you are ˹true˺ believers

**Never Again**
The verse's prohibition is built on a comparison: do not return *to the like of it* — *li-mithlihī*. The phrase widens the rule past the events of Madinah and past the person at the centre of them. That accusation had its own circumstances, and the community might have told itself the case was closed: a rumour about the Prophet's household, and a sūrah that cleared her name. The verse removes the exemption. What it forbids is the practice, and the practice finds a new target in every generation: a story carried by people who do not know, hardened by people who do, and repeated by men whose own sins are not the subject.
**The Word the Verse Chooses**
God does not merely order here; He admonishes: *yaʿiẓukum Allāh*, and the verb carries the sense of advice offered with concern, the way a father warns a son he does not want to see ruined. The advice is not to go near the like of it *ever* — *abadan*, a word that closes the door of a return. The slander was not to become one of the community's old stories, told and retold with the sharp edge worn off. The verse sets a permanent rule, and it sets it as counsel given for the community's own good rather than as a penalty recorded after the fact. What the sūrah will go on to explain is what the rule protects: a Muslim community whose reputation for truth is part of its religion, and a household that has already suffered enough.

## Verse 24:18

> And Allah makes ˹His˺ commandments clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise

**The Divine Attribute of Omniscience: Allāhu ʿAlīm**

The verse pairs legal clarification with the divine attribute *al-ʿAlīm*—the All-Knowing. Divine omniscience provides the metaphysical foundation upon which all divine law operates. God knows the deepest recesses of the human psyche, the secret motives that drive gossip, the unspoken conspiracies of hypocrites, and the silent suffering of the slandered.

Connecting the clarification of laws to divine knowledge serves as a profound reminder to the believer. Human courts can only judge outward actions and documented evidence; they cannot penetrate the hidden chambers of the heart. The knowledge that God is *ʿAlīm* introduces an internal, spiritual policing mechanism within every individual conscience. Even if a slanderer manages to evade earthly detection through clever rhetoric or procedural loopholes, they know with terrifying certainty that their secret malice is fully witnessed by the All-Knowing Creator, who forgets nothing, overlooks no injustice. Leaves no deed unaccounted for on the Day of Reckoning, when all secrets are laid bare before the divine throne.

**The Divine Attribute of Absolute Wisdom: Allāhu Ḥakīm**

The text couples omniscience with absolute wisdom: *wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm*. The attribute *al-Ḥakīm* means that every commandment, prohibition, and procedural threshold decreed by God is situated in its perfect, optimal place. Divine wisdom does not impose arbitrary burdens; every statute serves a precise, necessary purpose in preserving human welfare.

## Verse 24:19

> Indeed, those who love to see indecency spread among the believers will suffer a painful punishment in this life and the Hereafter. Allah knows and you do not know

**The Inner Affection for Corruption: yuḥibbūna an tashīʿa al-fāḥishah**

The divine warning penetrates beyond overt external actions to indict the hidden psychological and emotional motivations of the human heart: *inna alladhīna yuḥibbūna a tashīʿa al-fāḥishatu fī alladhīna āmanū*. The verb *yuḥibbūn* means loving, desiring, delighting in, and craving. The Qur'an does not merely condemn those who actively manufacture or broadcast indecency; it condemns those who harbor an internal affection for seeing moral corruption circulate.

**The Dual Realm of Agonizing Torment: lahum ʿadhābun alīm**

The retribution decreed against those who desire the spread of indecency spans both planes of existence: *lahum ʿadhābun alīmun fī al-dunyā wa-al-ākhirah*. The punishment is described as *alīm*—intensely agonizing and piercing. In this earthly life (*fī al-dunyā*), the purveyors of scandal face statutory corporal punishment of eighty lashes, public disgrace, the loss of civil testimony rights, and divine humiliation.

**The Prophetic Warning Against Searching for Faults**

The ethical philosophy underpinning this verse is powerfully expressed across authoritative traditions of the Sunnah. In a famous and piercing sermon recorded in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2032 and Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4880, the Prophet ﷺ mounted the pulpit and called out with a raised voice: O you who have accepted Islam with your tongues while true faith has not entered your hearts! Do not harm Muslims, do not revile them, and do not track down their private faults.

The Prophet ﷺ then delivered an unforgettable cosmic warning: For whoever seeks out the private faults of his Muslim brother, God will seek out his faults, and whomever God pursues, He will expose and humiliate him, even if he remains in the deepest interior of his own house. This prophetic warning shows that actively seeking, publicizing, and celebrating the moral shortcomings of believers invites immediate divine pursuit and earthly exposure, turning the pursuer's own weapon against his domestic peace, shattering his reputation, and stripping him of all divine concealment.

---

## Verse 24:20

> ˹You would have suffered,˺ had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy upon you, and had Allah not been Ever Gracious, Most Merciful

**A Change in the Names**
The sūrah's refrain appears in four verses, and each time it closes with a different pair of God's names. The first time, after the law and the declaration, it ends on *Tawwāb, Ḥakīm* — the Accepter of Repentance, the Wise (24:10). Here it ends on *Raʾūf, Raḥīm* — the Ever Gracious, the Most Merciful (24:20). The commentators noticed the shift, and the shift is the point of the verse.
**What Raʾūf Adds**
*Raʾfah* and *raḥmah* are both mercy, and the scholars who weighed the words separated them carefully. *Raḥmah* is the wide mercy: provision, forgiveness, the rain and the harvest and the second chance. *Raʾfah* is the tender kind: the mercy that cannot bear to see the servant suffer, that moves to remove a burden because it hurts him to carry it. When the verse wants to name what the community was given after the slander had run through Madinah, it chooses the tenderness rather than the breadth.
**Why the Community Needed It**
The passage has just described the slander in a series of questions: why did they not, when they heard it, think well of one another? why did they not say, this is a clear lie? (24:12-16). By the time the last of the admonitions arrives, the community had been shaken from top to bottom, a household had been dragged through a month of it, and the men who had spread it were known. The mercy named at the end of the passage is not the mercy of acquittal; it is the mercy of a body that has been through a fever and is being set back on its feet. The verse's own words before the names say so: *had it not been for the grace of God upon you and His mercy, and that God is Ever Gracious, Most Merciful*, you would have been left with what the whispering had made of you. The next verse tells the Muslim community what to do with the resolve the mercy has given them: do not let the wrongdoers be a model to follow (24:21).

## Verse 24:21

> O believers! Do not follow the footsteps of Satan. Whoever follows Satan’s footsteps, then ˹let them know that˺ he surely bids ˹all to˺ immorality and wickedness. Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy upon you, none of you would have ever been purified. But Allah purifies whoever He wills. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing

**Grace First**
*Wa-lawlā faḍlu Allāhi ʿalaykum wa-raḥmatuhu mā zakā minkum min aḥadin* — and were it not for God's grace upon you and His mercy, not one of you would be pure. The verse follows a section on following the steps of Satan, on indecency and on spreading talk among the believers, and it answers the question of how a person actually escapes all of that. The answer is placed before any moral instruction: not one of you would be clean by his own strength. The sūrah says the same thing in another key when it tells the believers not to imagine themselves purified, since God knows best who is mindful of Him (53:32). The law in this chapter sets the boundary; the grace described here is what gets a person across it.
**What Purity Means in the Book**
The word here is *tazkiyah*, and the Book uses it for a state a person seeks and receives at the same time. The Sūrah of the Sun says that the one who purifies his soul succeeds and the one who buries it fails (91:9-10), and the sūrah of the Most High gives the same promise to whoever purifies himself (87:14). What the verse in front of us adds is where the ability comes from: God purifies whom He wills. The command and the gift are not rivals. The command tells a person what to do, and the gift is what makes it possible, and the sūrah of the Light is demonstrating both on the same page.
**The Two Names and the Two Reminders**
The verse ends with God hearing and knowing. That pair is the sūrah's answer to the slander that has been running through the passage: every sentence spoken about somebody in his absence, and every reason behind it, is already heard and already known (24:14-16). The same pair returns at the close of the verses on modesty and the protection of honour, where the Book tells the believers not to force their servants and daughters into anything (24:33). The chapter's law is strict with the tongue and gentle with the person, and this verse is the one that explains why both are needed.

## Verse 24:22

> Do not let the people of virtue and affluence among you swear to suspend donations to their relatives, the needy, and the emigrants in the cause of Allah. Let them pardon and forgive. Do you not love to be forgiven by Allah? And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful

**The Two Words and the Difference Between Them**
The verse commands two things together: *wa-l-yaʿfū wa-l-yaṣfaḥū* — let them pardon and let them overlook. *Al-ʿafw* is the cancellation of a penalty: the man who had a right to claim something releases it. *Al-ṣafḥ* goes further, and the scholars describe the difference carefully: it comes from a word for the wide flat face of a thing, and the sense is drawing a hand across the page and starting a new one — not merely giving up the demand but clearing the bitterness out of the heart so that the offence is no longer standing between the two people. A person can forgive without reaching the second stage, and the verse asks for both.
**The Case Behind It**
The verse was given for a particular household, and the story is preserved in the reports. Abū Bakr had been supporting a poor relative, Misṭaḥ ibn Uthāthah, who was among the men who let the slander of ʿĀ'ishah run through Madinah; when the truth came out, Abū Bakr swore that he would never help the man again. This verse answered him: *let them pardon and overlook — do you not wish for God to forgive you?* He returned to the support and, on some reports, to more than he had given before. That is the pattern the verse names: a wrong done to the person closest to the quarrel, and a command that removes the quarrel before it becomes a family's permanent arrangement.
**Who Is Being Addressed**
The verse addresses *ulū al-faḍli wa-al-saʿah* — those who possess the surplus and the means — and the naming matters. The command is given to the people who can afford to act on it, the ones whose response will set the temperature of the community, which is why the argument hits home when it is put as a question: would you not want God to forgive you? The one asking God for forgiveness daily is asked what his own ledger looks like, and the verse leaves him to answer it himself. The sūrah's next verse carries the same demand into a different register, and the community that had nearly torn itself apart over a rumour is asked to close the year with its poor still fed.

## Verse 24:23

> Surely those who accuse chaste, unsuspecting, believing women are cursed in this life and the Hereafter. And they will suffer a tremendous punishment

**Three Words for the Women Who Are Slandered**
The verse is short and its subject is the gravest kind of accusation made against the most innocent kind of person: *those who cast the accusation at chaste women who are unaware and believing*. Three adjectives do the work, and they are worth taking one at a time. *Al-muḥṣanāt* is the word for women who are guarded and known to be chaste, protected within a household and under recognized law so that an accusation against them is an attack on that protection. *Al-ghāfilāt* describes the one who is unaware — the woman who has no idea that a rumour is circulating, because she is not the sort of person whose name would be in one. *Al-muʾmināt* places her, finally, inside the community of faith. The Book names all three so that the verse's protection reads as a general rule rather than a single case, and it states the consequence in the same sentence: they are cursed in this world and the next, and there is a great punishment waiting.
**What the Commentators Said About the Occasion**
Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿIkrimah held that the verses of this passage were given about the wives of the Prophet in particular, and that an attack on that household was not an ordinary piece of gossip but an assault on the Messenger himself. The plain words of the verse are wider than one house, and the jurists read the threatening clause as a general law whose application to a particular household was the first and most serious instance. What the passage protects is not only a reputation but the standing of a community that has decided that a Muslim's honour is a thing no one may help himself to, and the verses before and after it in the sūrah build the apparatus — witnesses, oaths, penalties — that makes the protection enforceable in a court rather than only in a sermon.

## Verse 24:24

> on the Day their tongues, hands, and feet will testify against them for what they used to do

**The Paradox of the Testifying Tongue**

A profound and terrifying paradox defines the first witness: *tashhadu ʿalayhim alsinatuhum*, their own tongues will testify against them. In this world, the tongue is the principal weapon of the slanderer, an agile muscle capable of formulating eloquent excuses, feigning injured innocence, and weaving intricate webs of deception to deflect blame onto others.

**The Witness of the Hands and Feet**

The feet testify to every journey undertaken in the service of falsehood: the steps taken toward gatherings of gossip, the paths trodden to spy upon private domestic sanctuaries, and the physical presence maintained in assemblies where the honor of the righteous was shredded. Every cell, muscle, and nerve of the human body bears an indelible empirical record of moral agency, preserved by the angels and unlocked by the All-Hearing, All-Seeing Creator on the Day of Truth, leaving no room for denial, evasion, or obfuscation.

**Parallel Qur'anic Accounts of Bodily Testimony**

This striking imagery of physical testimony recurs across several pivotal passages of the Qur'an, illustrating an unified Last-Day doctrine. In Sūrah Yā-Sīn, God describes the sealing of speech: *“On this Day We will seal their mouths, their hands will speak to Us, and their feet will testify to what they used to earn”* (36:65). In Sūrah Fuṣṣilat, the resurrected confront their own bodies in sheer astonishment: *“‘Why did you testify against us?’ They will say, ‘Allah made us speak—the One Who makes everything speak’”* (41:21).

---

## Verse 24:25

> On that Day, Allah will give them their just penalty in full, and they will ˹come to˺ know that Allah ˹alone˺ is the Ultimate Truth

**They Will Know the Truth**

The verse culminates in one of the most magnificent religious proclamations in the entire Qur'an: *wa-yaʿlamūna anna Allāha huwa al-Ḥaqqu al-Mubīn*, and they will know that Allah is the Manifest Truth. In this fleeting worldly life, the hypocrites, cynics, and scandalmongers lived within a thick fog of delusion. They convinced themselves that truth is merely a social construct, that morality is whatever the powerful can enforce, and that God's warnings were idle rhetoric.

On that Day, the veil of mortal illusion is incinerated by the brilliance of cosmic reality. They will confront the undeniable of being fact that God is *al-Ḥaqq*—the Supreme Reality, the Source of all existence, whose promises are absolute—and *al-Mubīn*, the Manifest Truth who clarifies all obscurities and exposes all falsehoods. The reality of the divine sovereignty will stand before their eyes with terrifying, unavoidable clarity, reducing every earthly pretense to dust and vindicating the divine governance of creation across the cosmos for all eternity.

**The Semantic Unity of dīn as Religion, Law, and Recompense**

A rich religious insight emerges from the many-sided semantic range of the Arabic root *d-y-n*. In Qur'anic discourse, the word *dīn* encompasses the religion revealed by God, the legal statutes commanding human obedience, and the final Last-Day recompense administered on the Day of Judgment (*Yawm al-Dīn*).

---

The verses immediately before this one describe the day on which their own tongues, hands and feet will testify to what they did (24:24), so the "just penalty" here is the sentence passed on evidence they themselves supplied. The Book states the same doctrine in the sūrah of the Troops, where God is the truth and what they invoke besides Him is falsehood (31:30), and in the sūrah of the Pilgrimage, where the truth about the Hour is stated as the one fact no one can dispute (22:6–7). The verse's point for its first hearers is the slander that began the sūrah: the punishment for accusing without proof will be as exact as the crime.

## Verse 24:26

> Wicked women are for wicked men, and wicked men are for wicked women. And virtuous women are for virtuous men, and virtuous men are for virtuous women. The virtuous are innocent of what the wicked say. They will have forgiveness and an honourable provision

**The Pairing in the Verse**
*Al-khabīthātu lil-khabīthīna* — corrupt women belong with corrupt men, corrupt men with corrupt women, and clean women with clean men, clean men with clean women. The commentators read the sentence in two ways, and both are on record. On the first, it is about people and marriage: a person's character finds its like, and the pairing is the ordinary working of attraction between those who live by the same standard. On the second, the words are about speech rather than persons, and the verse answers the slander directly: the filthy accusation belongs to the filthy accuser, and the clean words are for the clean people who were slandered. The two readings agree on the principle that runs through the sūrah: character is visible in what a person says about others.
**The Exoneration**
Then the verse gives the verdict on the household at the centre of the affair: *those are free of what they say* — *mubarraʾūn*, cleared. The word is a passive participle, and it matters that it is God who pronounces it and not the community. No court examined the case, no apology was extracted from the men who spread the story, and the woman's name was cleared by a sentence in the Book that will be recited until the end of time. She is described as innocent of what they said, and then the verse adds what belongs to innocence: for them is forgiveness and a generous provision.
**Why the Sūrah Needed the Sentence**
The sūrah has just spent verses on the punishment of the accusers and on the character they displayed. This verse is the other side of the account. When the Book clears a person, it does not merely drop the charge; it names the slanderers as what they are and attaches a reward to the person wronged. The reader is left with the two verdicts side by side, and the warning the sūrah then spells out is what the Muslim community has carried since: the same accusation, made against anyone, is still a sin of the tongue and an offence against the innocent.

## Verse 24:27

> O believers! Do not enter any house other than your own until you have asked for permission and greeted its occupants. This is best for you, so perhaps you will be mindful

**A Word of Warmth, Not of Procedure**
The verse tells the believers not to enter houses that are not their own until they have asked and greeted the people in them, and the choice of word is worth noticing. The Qur'an does not use the legal term for seeking permission; it uses *tastaʾnisū*, from the root that gives *uns* — familiarity, ease, friendliness. The picture is not of a claimant presenting a case at a door but of a visitor announcing himself in a way that lets the household relax. That is why the greeting comes first in the same sentence. The person inside hears a voice at the door and knows who it is and what he wants before anything is opened.
**The Manners That Were Built on It**
The Prophet turned this into practice. Abū Mūsā relates that he asked permission three times to enter ʿUmar's presence, and being given no answer, he went away; when ʿUmar later asked why he had left, he explained that the Prophet had instructed them: when one of you asks permission three times and is not given it, he should return (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6245). The three attempts give the people inside time, and the departing gives them the freedom of a door left unanswered. The same instruction explains the next verse, where the believer is told that if he is sent away, he should go back without offence: it is purer for him (24:28).
**What the Rule Was Replacing**
The ruling was given to a community whose doorways had been open doors in the old manner. In the camp and in the town, a man walked in on his neighbour, a child walked in on his parents, and the habits of the desert had taught people that privacy was a thing only the wealthy could afford. The sūrah's rules on this subject — this verse, the ones that follow about the home, and the later passage about children and servants — build a household in which a person may be himself within his own walls. The word chosen at the start says what the whole arrangement is for: so that no one has to compose his face before the sound of a knock has even reached him.

## Verse 24:28

> If you find no one at home, do not enter it until you have been given permission. And if you are asked to leave, then leave. That is purer for you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of what you do

**Purity of Soul Through Respecting Boundaries: huwa azkā lakum**

The divine voice provides a profound spiritual evaluation of this etiquette: *huwa azkā lakum*, that is purer for you. Turning back gracefully upon refusal cultivates deep spiritual purity in both parties.

For the visitor, turning back without offense purifies the ego (*tazkiyat al-nafs*) of pride, entitlement, and intrusive curiosity. A famous scholar from the righteous predecessors remarked: I spent my entire life hoping that someone would say to me at their door, “Turn back,” so that I could turn back with a joyous heart and attain the divine purity mentioned in this verse!

**The Prophetic Protocols of Seeking Permission**

The practical application of this verse is closely detailed across authoritative traditions of the Sunnah. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6245 and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2153, the Prophet ﷺ established the golden rule of visitation: Permission should be sought three times; if you are granted permission, enter; otherwise, depart. A visitor knocks or calls out with the greeting of peace at reasonable intervals, allowing the occupants time to respond.

If no response is received after the third try, the visitor must walk away without lingering, peering, or expressing frustration. and, the Prophet ﷺ commanded that visitors never stand directly in front of the center of a doorway, but position themselves to the right or the left side of the entrance. This protocol ensures that when the door is opened, the interior of the home and its female occupants remain completely shielded from the visitor's initial gaze, fulfilling the spirit of modesty.

**The Criminalization of Peeping and Voyeurism**

Islamic jurisprudence treats the visual violation of domestic privacy with extraordinary severity. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6888, the Prophet ﷺ expressed the foundational principle: The seeking of permission was legislated completely for the sake of the gaze. An individual who stands outside a home and deliberately peers through windows, cracks, or door keyholes commits a major legal and moral transgression.

When a man once peered through an opening into the Prophet's ﷺ private quarters while he was combing his hair, the Prophet ﷺ pointed an iron instrument toward him, declaring: Had I known you were looking, I would have pierced your eye with it! Classical jurists across all four schools of law concluded from this and related traditions that if an intruder deliberately peeps into a private residence without permission. The occupants throw a stone or object that injures or blinds the intruder's eye, the occupants bear no financial or legal liability (*lā diyata lah*), because the intruder stripped himself of legal protection by violating the domestic sanctuary.

---

## Verse 24:29

> There is no blame on you if you enter public places where there is something of benefit for you. And Allah knows what you reveal and what you conceal

**Houses That Are Not Lived In**
The two verses before this one built a wall of privacy around the home: do not enter a house until you have asked and greeted its people (24:27), and if you are sent away, go back (24:28). This verse opens the door again — but only for buildings that are not homes. *Laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun a tadkhulū buyūtan ghayra maskūnah* — there is no blame on you for entering houses that are not lived in. *Buyūtan ghayra maskūnah* means places not kept as private residences. The commentators list what they had in mind: markets, inns and caravanserais, public storehouses, the roadside lodges called *ribāṭāt*, government offices, and empty ruins that travellers shelter in.
**The Balance the Law Keeps**
Islamic law guards the home closely — *ḥaram al-bayt*, the sanctity of the household, makes it a place of modesty and rest. Yet the same law has to let a working society move. A market cannot function if every stall needs an invitation, and a traveller cannot wait outside a ruin for permission from an owner who is not there. So the rule protecting the home stops at the door of buildings where no one lives. Privacy is protected where privacy exists; movement is left free where it does not.
**Why the Companions Were Relieved**
Al-Ṭabarī records, from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Qatādah, that when the two warning verses came down, the Companions were badly shaken. They had entered such places freely all their lives, and now they feared every doorway they had ever crossed — the anxiety of people who take a command seriously and cannot see where its edge lies. This verse drew that edge. It told them their past was not sin and their future was not a maze, because God does not legislate to trap the people who obey Him. The exemption is small in words and large in effect: it turns an anxious conscience back into a clear path.

## Verse 24:30

> ˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. That is purer for them. Surely Allah is All-Aware of what they do

**Guarding the Private Parts: wa-yaḥfaẓū furūjahum**

The verse establishes an immediate structural connection between the discipline of the eyes and the protection of chastity: *wa-yaḥfaẓū furūjahum*, and let them guard their private parts. In the Qur'anic moral anatomy, the eye and the sexual organs are linked in an unbreakable chain of cause and effect. Guarding one's chastity against fornication, adultery, and perverse desires is an impossible fantasy if the eyes are granted unrestricted freedom to feast upon forbidden sights.

**The Prophetic Protocol of the Accidental Glance**

The practical application of this divine statute is illuminated through authoritative traditions of the Sunnah. In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2159, the noble companion Jarīr ibn ʿAbdillāh inquired directly of the Prophet ﷺ about the sudden, accidental glance (*naẓar al-fajʾah*) that falls upon an uncovered person without premeditation. The Prophet ﷺ gave a concise, definitive command: Avert your gaze immediately!

In another well-known narration, the Prophet ﷺ advised ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib: O ʿAlī, do not allow a second glance to follow the first; for the first glance is for you (unintentional and forgiven), but the second glance is against you (deliberate and sinful). Righteousness does not demand that a human being have supernatural control over the initial sensory encounter in a bustling world; it demands the moral courage to immediately tear one's gaze away rather than indulging the desire to linger and inspect.

**The Rights of the Public Road in the Sunnah**

The communal dimension of this verse is vividly preserved in the prophetic teachings about public spaces. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2465, the Prophet ﷺ cautioned his companions: Beware of sitting on the pathways and roadsides! When the companions explained that they had legitimate social and commercial needs to gather on the roads, the Prophet ﷺ instructed: If you insist on sitting there, then give the road its rightful due!

When the companions asked what constituted the rightful due of the road, the Prophet ﷺ enumerated its fundamental duties: Lowering the gaze, removing harmful obstacles from the path, returning the greeting of peace, enjoining the good, and forbidding the wrong. This prophetic charter shows that visual modesty is an indispensable prerequisite for public civility, transforming the shared streets into a safe and honorable space where women, men, and children can move without fear of harassment or predatory stares.

**The Divine Awareness of Visual Treachery: inna Allāha khabīrun bimā yaṣnaʿūn**

The verse concludes with an awe-inspiring divine signature: *inna Allāha khabīrun bimā yaṣnaʿūn*, surely Allah is All-Aware of what they do. The choice of the attribute *al-Khabīr* means intimate, exhaustive, and penetrating knowledge of the most subtle and concealed intricacies of human action. and, the text uses the verb *yaṣnaʿūn* (what they craft or manufacture) rather than the standard *yaʿmalūn* (what they do).

The word *yaṣnaʿūn* points to the subtle internal craftsmanship of visual sin: the stolen glances stolen from the corner of the eye, the feigned innocence, the deliberate positioning of the body to catch an illicit glimpse, and the internal fantasies constructed behind lowered eyelashes.

---

## Verse 24:31

> And tell the believing women to lower their gaze and guard their chastity, and not to reveal their adornments except what normally appears. Let them draw their veils over their chests, and not reveal their ˹hidden˺ adornments except to their husbands, their fathers, their fathers-in-law, their sons, their stepsons, their brothers, their brothers’ sons or sisters’ sons, their fellow women, those ˹bondwomen˺ in their possession, male attendants with no desire, or children who are still unaware of women’s nakedness. Let them not stomp their feet, drawing attention to their hidden adornments. Turn to Allah in repentance all together, O believers, so that you may be successful

**What May Be Shown in Dress**

The verse establishes a foundational rule governing female dress: *wa-lā yubdīna zīnatahunna illā mā ẓahara minhā*, and not to reveal their adornment except that which normally appears. The term *zīnah* encompasses both natural bodily beauty and acquired embellishments such as clothing, jewelry, henna, and cosmetics. The precise scope of the exception *illā mā ẓahara minhā* generated rich and extensive legal discourse among early authorities.

**The Architectural Decree: Khumur over the Juyūb**

The text delivers a specific, practical instruction about the arrangement of head coverings: *wa-lyadrīna bi-khumurihinna ʿalā juyūbihinn*. In pre-Islamic Arabia, women often wore the *khimār* (head veil), but customary fashion involved draping the fabric backward over the shoulders, leaving the throat, neck, ear ornaments, and the upper chest opening (*al-jayb*) completely exposed to the public gaze.

The Qur'anic command intervened with architectural precision, instructing believing women to draw the fabric of their head veils forward, fastening them securely over their chest openings. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4758, ʿĀʾishah praised the Anṣārī women who, the very moment this verse descended, tore pieces from their thick outer garments to cover their chests and heads immediately. This historic response showed how the early community embraced divine modesty not as an oppressive imposition, but as a dignified expression of sacred identity.

---

The jurists built the law of dress on this verse and drew the phrase "what normally appears" out of it, differing over whether it means the face and hands, as most held, or the clothing itself, and the difference runs through the schools. The list of those before whom the adornment may be shown is given in the verse's own list — husbands, fathers, sons, brothers and the rest — and it ends with the instruction not to stamp the feet so as to reveal what is hidden, which the commentators read as a rule about the manner of movement rather than only about the garment. The verse closes with the call to turn to God in repentance together, which puts the rule in the frame of a common obligation rather than a charge against women alone.

## Verse 24:32

> Marry off the ˹free˺ singles among you, as well as the righteous of your bondmen and bondwomen. If they are poor, Allah will enrich them out of His bounty. For Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing

**Help the Unmarried to Marry**

The divine command establishes a collective social obligation: *wa-ankiḥū al-ayāmā minkum*. The imperative form *ankiḥū* (marry off / arrange marriages for) addresses the entire Muslim community, legal guardians, and communal leaders. The term *al-ayāmā* is the plural of *ayyim*, designating any individual who lacks a spouse—whether male or female, never-married, widowed, or divorced.

**The Divine Promise of Economic Enrichment: yughnihimu Allāh**

The verse addresses the universal anxiety that frequently delays marriage: financial poverty. The divine promise intervenes with astonishing economic clarity: *in yakūnū fuqarāʾa yughnihimu Allāhu min faḍlih*, if they are poor, Allah will enrich them out of His bounty. Human prudence routinely counsels delay, advising youth to postpone marriage until substantial wealth, property, and career security are accumulated.

**The Divine Horizon: Allāhu Wāsiʿun ʿAlīm**

The verse seals the matrimonial charter with two majestic divine names: *wa-Allāhu wāsiʿun ʿalīm*, and Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing. The attribute *al-Wāsiʿ* means the One whose capacity, generosity, and treasures are utterly boundless, incapable of being depleted by providing sustenance to every creature in existence.

---

**The Instruction the Prophet Gave**
The community was told to marry off its unmarried, and the Prophet made the same instruction personal. Ibn Masʿūd reports his words to young men: whoever among you can afford it, let him marry, for it lowers the gaze and guards chastity; and whoever cannot should fast, for that will be a restraint for him (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5065). Read beside the verse, the two belong together: the community is asked to arrange and to help, and the individual is asked to want it. The Book answers the fear of poverty in the same verse — if they are poor, God will enrich them from His bounty — which is a promise about provision, not a licence to be careless. The practice of the Companions showed both sides: Abū Bakr married off his daughter to a man with nothing but what he had memorised for a wedding portion, and others were given help by the community to set up a home.
**Who the Verse Names**
*Al-ayāmā* is the word for those without spouses, and it covers men and women, never married, widowed, or divorced, which is worth stating in a topic where the burden usually falls on women alone. The verse continues to the servants and the poor, and addresses the whole community rather than the family. That is why the jurists read *ankiḥū* as a communal duty in the proper sense: a town where no one helps its single people to marry has left a command of the Book undone.

## Verse 24:33

> And let those who do not have the means to marry keep themselves chaste until Allah enriches them out of His bounty. And if any of those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession desires a deed of emancipation, make it possible for them, if you find goodness in them. And give them some of Allah’s wealth which He has granted you. Do not force your ˹slave˺ girls into prostitution for your own worldly gains while they wish to remain chaste. And if someone coerces them, then after such a coercion Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful ˹to them˺

**The Injunction to Active Chastity: wa-lyastaʿfif**

The verse provides clear ethical guidance for those who genuinely cannot attain the financial or social means to marry: *wa-lyastaʿfif alladhīna lā yajidūna nikāḥan ḥattā yughniyahumu Allāhu min faḍlih*. The grammatical form *yastaʿfif* means active, determined striving for chastity. The individual who faces unavoidable bachelorhood is not granted a moral license to indulge in illicit avenues, pornography, or casual compromises under the pretext of biological frustration.

They are commanded to wage an active, conscious struggle to protect their spiritual and physical purity. The Prophet ﷺ operationalized this divine command in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5066, advising: O young people, whoever among you has the means to marry, let him marry, for it lowers the gaze and protects chastity; and whoever cannot, let him fast, for fasting serves as a shield for him. Chastity must be maintained through discipline, prayer, fasting, and patience until divine bounty opens the door to honorable marriage, preserving the integrity of the soul before God and society.

**The Redistribution of Divine Wealth: wa-ātūhum min māli Allāh**

Classical jurists interpreted this financial mandate in two complementary ways. Under the rule of *al-waḍīʿah*, the master is legally required to forgive or return a substantial portion (typically one-quarter or one-fifth) of the agreed contract price to the newly freed individual, providing them with initial startup capital. Simultaneously, Islamic law designated contracting slaves (*al-mukātabūn*) as one of the eight mandatory categories entitled to public zakāh funds in 9:60 (*wa-fī al-riqāb*). The state treasury and the private master were both compelled to finance the liberty of the formerly enslaved, ensuring their lasting economic dignity.

The Qur'an did not treat emancipation as a theoretical abstraction or an empty slogan.

---

## Verse 24:34

> Indeed, We have sent down to you clear revelations, along with examples of those who had gone before you, and a lesson to the God-fearing

**Who the Admonition Reaches**
The verse names what the sūrah has been giving and who receives it: clear revelations, and a lesson and an admonition for those mindful of God. *Mawʿiẓah* is advice that reaches the heart and moves it; *taqwā* is the awareness of God that keeps a person watchful. The Book speaks to everyone, but the two conditions in the sentence meet only in some listeners. A man who has decided in advance that the rule will not touch his household can hear the clearest verse and find a way around it, which is why the verse ends by naming the quality that opens a person to advice rather than by naming the quality of the advice itself.
**The Example From Earlier Peoples**
The verse adds the phrase *and an example from those who passed away before you*. The troubles of the young Muslim community in Madinah — the false accusation against ʿĀ'ishah, the whispering of the hypocrites, the strain of keeping chastity in a new town — were not the invention of that generation. Every community that has tried to live by revelation has met the same resistance, from inside as much as from outside, and the Book keeps the earlier records so that no generation can read its own difficulties as proof that God has abandoned it.
**The Doorway to the Light Verse**
The verse's position tells the reader what to expect. It ends the run of laws and warnings that has filled the first half of the sūrah — the punishment, the accusation, the privacy rules, the household etiquette — and it comes immediately before the sūrah's great picture of God's light in the niche with the lamp (24:35). The pause is deliberate: the reader is given a summary of what the laws have been teaching and then taken to the source of the light that made them necessary.

## Verse 24:35

> Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. His light is like a niche in which there is a lamp, the lamp is in a crystal, the crystal is like a shining star, lit from ˹the oil of˺ a blessed olive tree, ˹located˺ neither to the east nor the west, whose oil would almost glow, even without being touched by fire. Light upon light! Allah guides whoever He wills to His light. And Allah sets forth parables for humanity. For Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things

**The Supreme Of being Declaration: Allāhu Nūru al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ**

The revelation ascends to the absolute zenith of metaphysical contemplation: *Allāhu nūru al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ*, Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. In classical Islamic ontology and philosophical theology, light (*al-nūr*) is defined as that which is manifest in itself and brings all other things into manifestation (*al-ẓāhiru bi-nafsih al-muẓhiru li-ghayrih*).

**The Anatomy of the Parable: Niche, Lamp, and Crystal**

The verse expresses an allegorical masterpiece describing the vessel of divine guidance: *mathalu nūrihī ka-mishkātin fīhā miṣbāḥ al-miṣbāḥu fī zujājah al-zujājatu ka-annahā kawkabun durrīy*. The *mishkāh* is a hollow, non-perforated niche set into a thick wall, functioning to gather, reflect, and amplify light rather than allowing it to dissipate into space. The *miṣbāḥ* is the luminous burning wick, and the *zujājah* is an exquisitely clear crystal enclosure that protects and refracts the flame like a radiant, pearlescent star (*kawkab durrīy*).

Classical authorities, tracing their insight to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb and ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās, explained that this parable represents the light of divine guidance residing within the human being. The niche is the chest of the faithful servant; the transparent crystal glass is their pure, untainted heart; and the radiant lamp is the uncreated light of faith and the Qur'an burning within. The crystal heart gathers the divine light, radiating clarity into every thought, emotion, and bodily action.

---

## Verse 24:36

> ˹That light shines˺ through houses ˹of worship˺ which Allah has ordered to be raised, and where His Name is mentioned. He is glorified there morning and evening

**Houses Where He Is Remembered**

Syntactically and conceptually, verse 36 flows seamlessly from the sublime metaphysical vision of verse 35: *fī buyūtin adhina Allāhu a turfaʿa wa-yudhkara fīhā ismuh*. The cosmic light (*al-nūr*) described as an unquenchable celestial lamp inside an exquisite crystal niche is not an ethereal concept confined to abstract heavens.

It finds tangible, earthly grounding in designated sacred physical spaces: *buyūt* (houses). the commentators such as al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr explained that these houses refer mainly to mosques (*masājid*) established on earth for the sole worship of the Creator. While mosques are the primary manifestation, the term embraces all sanctuaries dedicated to the unadulterated remembrance of God, including the Kaʿbah in Makkah, the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah, and the sacred sanctuary of Jerusalem. These terrestrial nodes serve as spiritual beacons radiating divine light into the surrounding human landscape, providing a perpetual refuge where weary human hearts can find shelter against the blinding distractions and moral chaos of worldly life, renewing their spiritual strength.

**Cosmic Cadence of Glorification: bi-al-ghuduwwi wa-al-āṣāl**

The text establishes a perpetual rhythm of spiritual devotion: *yusabbiḥu lahū fīhā bi-al-ghuduwwi wa-al-āṣāl*, He is glorified there morning and evening. The term *al-ghuduww* refers to the early morning hours, the period of dawn when the world awakens, while *al-āṣāl* is the plural of *aṣīl*, meaning the late afternoon hours preceding sunset.

**Syntactic Continuity with the Verse of Light**

The structural placement of verse 36 immediately following verse 35 reveals a vital pedagogical truth: illumination requires dedicated discipline and sacred communal space. The magnificent metaphysical parabola of the Light of the heavens and earth does not conclude with solitary mystical withdrawal or abstract religious musing.

---

## Verse 24:37

> by men who are not distracted—either by buying or selling—from Allah’s remembrance, or performing prayer, or paying alms-tax. They fear a Day when hearts and eyes will tremble

**Trade That Does Not Distract Them**

The revelation distinguishes between two forms of economic transaction: *tijārah* (general trade or business ventures) and *bayʿ* (immediate buying and selling contracts). Classical commentators noted the profound subtlety of mentioning both terms.

*Tijārah* represents large-scale, ongoing business enterprises that consume long-term cognitive focus, planning, and capital investment. *Bayʿ* means individual, quick retail transactions where immediate profit is exchanged hands-on. By declaring that neither long-term commercial ambition nor immediate cash profit distracts them, the text covers the full spectrum of economic temptation. These individuals operate within the commercial world without allowing the world to occupy their hearts. They handle silver and gold with their hands, but keep their souls completely consecrated to the transcendent remembrance of God, remaining upright in both large contracts and petty exchanges, refusing to let material gain compromise ethical virtue, degrade personal integrity, or dim their inner spiritual vision, living as shining examples of righteousness.

**The Courage of Spiritual Detachment**

The early companions exemplified this sublime detachment. Commentators recorded that when the blacksmiths among them raised their hammers to strike iron and heard the *adhān*, they would not even bring the hammer down upon the anvil; they would lay the tool aside immediately to hasten to prayer. Similarly, merchants weighing goods would leave their scales balanced and walk directly to the mosque. This unyielding prioritization of the eternal over the ephemeral transformed an obscure group of desert traders into the moral leaders of civilization, showing that worldly success reaches its peak when subordinated to sacred obedience, elevating commerce into an arena of spiritual integrity and selfless service, lighting up the marketplace with divine remembrance and ethical distinction.

---

## Verse 24:38

> ˹hoping˺ that Allah may reward them according to the best of their deeds, and increase them out of His grace. And Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit

**Divine Evaluation by the Best Standard: li-yajziyahumu Allāh**

The divine decree unveils the ultimate reward awaiting those noble souls who refused to let worldly commerce distract them from divine worship: *li-yajziyahumu Allāhu aḥsana mā ʿamilū*. The prepositional prefix *lām* in *li-yajziyahum* indicates both purpose and consequence; their disciplined devotion was oriented toward this singular divine reckoning.

The phrase *aḥsana mā ʿamilū* (the best of what they did) reveals an astonishing principle of divine justice and mercy. Classical commentators, including al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Qurṭubī, explained that God does not evaluate the believer through a harsh, hyper-critical scrutiny that exposes every minor flaw and human imperfection. Instead, He takes the finest, most sincere, and most beautiful deeds performed by the servant and uses them as the standard measure by which the rest of their righteous conduct is judged and elevated. Minor shortcomings are graciously pardoned and overshadowed by the excellence of their highest devotions, filling the believer with profound hope, enduring gratitude, and unshakeable spiritual tranquility that sustains the heart across every trial, inspiring deeper love for God.

**Transcendent Grace Beyond Merit: wa-yazīdahum min faḍlih**

Divine reward is not confined to a strict one-to-one transactional equivalence: *wa-yazīdahum min faḍlih*, and increase them out of His grace. In the religious framework of the Qur'an, human salvation is never purchased merely by the mechanical weight of mortal deeds.

Even the most devout servant, having spent a lifetime in prayer, fasting, and charity, could never balance the scales against the infinite blessings of life, sight, intellect, and guidance bestowed by God. Therefore, while the *jazāʾ* (recompense) corresponds to the excellence of their deeds, the *ziyādah* (increase) flows completely from unearned divine bounty (*faḍl*).

---

## Verse 24:39

> As for the disbelievers, their deeds are like a mirage in a desert, which the thirsty perceive as water, but when they approach it, they find it to be nothing. Instead, they find Allah there ˹in the Hereafter, ready˺ to settle their account. And Allah is swift in reckoning

**Deeds Like a Mirage**

The Qur'an selects an exquisite natural phenomenon to illustrate the tragic illusion of unanchored deeds: *ka-sarābin bi-qīʿatin*. The *sarāb* (mirage) occurs on flat, sun-baked desert plains (*qīʿah*), where intense thermal gradients refract light rays upward, creating the deceptive optical appearance of shimmering lakes and expansive bodies of cool water.

**The Dramatic Shift: From Luminous Reality to Desert Delusion**

The revelation executes a dramatic pedagogical contrast: having illuminated the radiant pathway of believers whose hearts are niches of divine light (vv. 35–38), the text turns abruptly to the tragic reality of disbelief: *wa-alladhīna kafarū aʿmāluhum ka-sarābin bi-qīʿah*. Disbelief is not only a lack of intellectual assent; it is an of being dislocation from truth that invalidates human exertion.

The verse addresses the ostensible "good deeds" performed by disbelievers—their philanthropic donations, scientific discoveries, civic building projects, and acts of social compassion. While these actions appear noble, impressive, and beneficial on the surface of temporal history, they lack the foundational anchor of divine faith. Without connection to the transcendent Creator, human deeds become weightless phantoms, devoid of eternal substance and doomed to evaporate like desert mist when confronted with ultimate reality, leaving the soul impoverished and spiritually barren.

---

The mirage is followed in the sūrah by a second and darker image: deeds like the darkness of a deep sea under waves and clouds, where a person cannot see his own hand (24:40). The Book uses the same comparison for the works of the disbelievers elsewhere: like scattered dust blown about on a windy day, with nothing of it left in their hands (14:18; 25:23), and like a crop ruined by a freezing wind before it is harvested (3:117). What the two images in this sūrah have in common is the moment of arrival: the thirsty man reaches the place where the water seemed to be, and finds nothing, and the swimmer in the dark finds no light at all.

## Verse 24:40

> Or ˹their deeds are˺ like the darkness in a deep sea, covered by waves upon waves, topped by ˹dark˺ clouds. Darkness upon darkness! If one stretches out their hand, they can hardly see it. And whoever Allah does not bless with light will have no light

**Layers of Darkness**

Just as divine illumination compounds upon itself through pure human intuition, sacred scripture, and righteous deeds, spiritual blindness compounds upon itself through doubt, transgression, and willful obstinacy. The classical companion Ubayy ibn Kaʿb commented that the disbeliever is trapped in five darknesses: his speech is dark, his actions are dark, his entrance into the grave is dark, his resurrection is dark, and his ultimate destiny in the Fire is dark. It is a total, suffocating occlusion of spiritual perception that leaves no room for truth to enter, transforming the soul into a frozen landscape of negation.

**The Abyssal Sea: Stratification of Profound Darkness**

The revelation presents an alternative, even more harrowing cosmic allegory: *aw ka-ẓulumātin fī baḥrin lujjiyyin yaghshāhu mawjun min fawqihi mawjun min fawqihi saḥāb*. While verse 39 depicted the active, deceptive works of the disbeliever who mistakenly anticipates reward (the desert mirage), verse 40 portrays the inner epistemic state of the disbeliever who is completely submerged in existential despair, skepticism, and nihilism.

**Layers of Darkness**

The culmination of these obscuring layers is captured in an emphatic exclamation: *ẓulumātun baʿḍuhā fawqa baʿḍ*, darknesses, one above the other! This phrase stands in direct, breathtaking antithesis to the proclamation in verse 35: *nūrun ʿalā nūr* (Light upon light!).

---

## Verse 24:41

> Do you not see that Allah is glorified by all those in the heavens and the earth, even the birds as they soar? Each ˹instinctively˺ knows their manner of prayer and glorification. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all they do

**Everything in the Heavens and the Earth Glorifies Him**

The divine speech opens with an awakening rhetorical question: *a-lam tara anna Allāha yusabbiḥu lahū man fī al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ*, do you not see that Allah is glorified by all those in the heavens and the earth? The visual verb *tara* (do you not see) invites both sensory observation of the natural cosmos and deep intellectual reflection upon its underlying order and harmony.

Angels in celestial spheres, stars in galactic orbits, oceans, mountains, trees, and microscopic organisms all participate in continuous glorification (*tasbīḥ*).

**Aerial Hymns: wa-al-ṭayru ṣāffāt**

The verse highlights a particularly breathtaking manifestation of terrestrial worship: *wa-al-ṭayru ṣāffāt*, even the birds as they soar with outspread wings. The word *ṣāffāt* depicts birds gliding high in the sky, extending their wings in effortless aerial suspension without frantic flapping, sustained completely by divine aerodynamic decrees that govern the atmosphere.

Classical commentators, including al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Rāzī, noted that the outstretched wings of soaring birds mirror the outspread arms and bowed postures of human worshippers in prayer. As birds glide through the atmosphere, their rhythmic ascent and aerial maneuvers constitute their own distinctive liturgy of adoration. Their flight is an open sign of complete dependence upon the Sustainer (*al-Qayyūm*), who holds them aloft in the thin air, transforming the skies into a soaring temple of praise where every flight path testifies to divine balance and providence, inspiring awe in human onlookers.

---

The Book makes the same statement in the sūrah of the Night Journey, where the seven heavens and the earth and everything in them glorify God, and there is nothing that does not glorify Him, though the community does not understand their praise (17:44), and in the sūrah of the Pilgrimage, where the birds held in the air and the mountains themselves are said to make the same prostration (22:18). The closing clause of this verse, that God knows what every creature does, is the base on which the passage continues: the glorification of the creation and the accounting of deeds are one theme here, since the same knowledge that hears the praise records the acts of the free.

## Verse 24:42

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. And to Allah is the final return

**The Kingdom, and Where Everything Returns**
The verse states an ownership in one sentence: *wa-lillāhi mulku al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ* — and to God belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth, and to God is the return. Two claims stand in that single line. *Al-mulk* is rule, the authority that decides what happens in a territory; the verse says the territory and the rule over it are both His, and it puts the name first — *lillāh* — so that the reader hears possession before he hears what is possessed. Then it adds the destination of every matter: the return is to God, which means every case left unresolved in this world is not left unresolved.
**What the Sentence Does to the Reader**
The passage it stands in is about worship and glory: the verse before describes everything in the heavens and the earth declaring God's praise, and the verses after describe the clouds and the rain and the lightning as His doing. In the middle of a picture that wide, the ownership sentence does a particular kind of work for a human reader. It takes the ordinary material of pride — a house, a piece of land, a body, an income — and puts it on loan. Nothing in the verse is a rebuke of having; the rebuke is aimed at the frame of mind that treats the loan as a title. And the return at the end of the sentence is the part that keeps a person steady: the tyrant's ledger closes, and the man who was cheated has a place where his case is still open.

## Verse 24:43

> Do you not see that Allah gently drives the clouds, then joins them together, piling them up into masses, from which you see raindrops come forth? And He sends down from the sky mountains ˹of clouds˺ loaded with hail, pouring it on whoever He wills and averting it from whoever He wills. The flash of the clouds’ lightning nearly takes away eyesight

**Mountains of Cloud and Hail**

The discourse ascends to the violent, frigid heights of the storm cloud: *wa-yunazzilu mina al-samāʾi min jibālin fīhā min barad*, and He sends down from the sky mountains loaded with hail. the commentators noted two profound interpretations of *jibāl* (mountains).

**Dynamic Meteorology: yuzjī saḥāban**

The Qur'an directs human contemplation toward the dynamic, awe-inspiring processes of atmospheric physics: *a-lam tara anna Allāha yuzjī saḥāban thumma yuʾallifu baynahū thumma yajʿaluhū rukāmā*. The verb *yuzjī* depicts gentle, gradual driving; winds are steered by divine decree to push isolated, scattered fragments of water vapor across vast atmospheric expanses.

**Emergence of Life-Giving Rain: yakhruju min khilālih**

The verse traces the miraculous consequence of this atmospheric architecture: *fa-tarā al-wadqa yakhruju min khilālih*, from which you see raindrops come forth. The term *al-wadq* specifically designates gentle, penetrating raindrops emerging from the interior recesses (*khilāl*) of the cloud mass.

**Mountains of Cloud and Hail**

First, looking upward at cumulonimbus clouds, or looking down upon them from aircraft, they present the literal visual appearance of immense, snow-capped mountain ranges rising miles into the sky. Second, inside these atmospheric "mountains," supercooled water droplets circulate through violent vertical currents, freezing onto ice pellets layer by layer to form destructive hail (*barad*). When these frozen missiles plummet to earth, they have formidable destructive force, capable of flattening crops, shattering roofs, injuring livestock, and devastating agricultural economies in minutes, revealing the dual nature of weather as both mercy and trial, warning human hearts of latent power and reminding them of divine majesty.

## Verse 24:44

> Allah alternates the day and night. Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight

**A Lesson for People of Insight**

The verse declares the pedagogical essence of this astronomical cycle: *inna fī dhālika la-ʿibratan*, surely in this is an instructive lesson. The noun *ʿibrah* derives from the root *ʿ-b-r*, meaning to cross over, traverse, or bridge from one bank of a river to the other.

**The Requisite Quality of Vision: li-ulī al-abṣār**

The divine text specifies who is capable of crossing this cognitive bridge: *li-ulī al-abṣār*, for people of insight. The term *abṣār* is the plural of *baṣar*, which means both physical eyesight and the interior perceptual faculty of the heart (*baṣīrah*).

**Harmonizing the Human Heart with the Cosmic Cycle**

The ultimate spiritual invitation of verse 44 is to harmonize human life with the celestial cadence established by God. The alternation of night and day was engineered to align with the rhythms of worship revealed in sacred law.

The alternation of light and darkness also mirrors the spiritual journey of the believer, who experiences seasons of radiant spiritual expansion followed by periods of sobering contraction.

---

The verse closes a short passage of astronomical proofs that runs from 24:43: the clouds, the rain, the hail, the lightning, the mountains of cloud. The Book uses the same evidence in the sūrah of the Family of ʿImrān, where the alternation of day and night is a sign for people of understanding (3:190), and in the sūrah of the Heifer with the same phrase (2:164). The word for the lesson, *ʿibrah*, is used in the Book of the historical ruins as well — the gravel pits of the towns of Lūṭ were left as a sign (15:75) — so the alternation of the day and the night and the ruins of the past are the same kind of evidence, one read in the sky and one in the earth.

## Verse 24:45

> And Allah has created from water every living creature. Some of them crawl on their bellies, some walk on two legs, and some walk on four. Allah creates whatever He wills. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything

**Every Living Thing From Water**

The revelation directs human inquiry into the biological foundation of terrestrial life: *wa-Allāhu khalaqa kulla dābbatin min māʾ*, and Allah has created from water every living creature. The noun *dābbah* designates any sentient, ambulating creature that moves across the earth's surface.

**Sovereign Creative Liberty: yakhluqu Allāhu mā yashāʾ**

The verse explains the philosophical cause behind this biological diversity: *yakhluqu Allāhu mā yashāʾ*, Allah creates whatever He wills. Creation is not constrained by deterministic necessity, mechanical algorithms, or rigid natural laws that bind the Creator.

The laws of physics and biology are themselves creations of God, sustained by His active will and subject to His sovereign discretion. God is not an impersonal first cause who set an automated clockwork universe in motion only to withdraw into cosmic detachment. He is the Living Creator (*al-Khāliq*) who continuously innovates, designs, and actualizes whatever forms He chooses. Mentioning crawling, two-legged, and four-legged creatures serves as a representative taxonomy that hints at still greater wonders—creatures with six legs, eight legs, or centipedes with dozens of limbs, as well as marine creatures and invisible microorganisms that populate every ecological sphere.

The verse seals its biological exposition with a definitive cosmic formula: *inna Allāha ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr*, surely Allah is Most Capable of everything. The divine attribute *al-Qadīr* means absolute, untrammeled omnipotence.

---

## Verse 24:46

> We have indeed sent down revelations clarifying ˹the truth˺. But Allah ˹only˺ guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path

**Clarifying Signs: āyātin mubayyināt**

The divine voice reasserts the clarity and efficacy of its revealed guidance: *la-qad anzalnā āyātin mubayyināt*, We have indeed sent down revelations clarifying the truth. The term *mubayyināt* stresses active, radiant illumination that leaves no room for moral ambiguity.

**The Dual Nature of Guidance: Universal Sign vs. Special Favor**

A crucial religious distinction emerges in the contrast between the first and second halves of the verse: *wa-Allāhu yahdī man yashāʾu ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*, but Allah only guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path. The revelation itself is sent down universally to all humanity (*anzalnā ilaykum*), accessible to any reader or listener without restriction.

However, the actual attainment of transformative guidance (*al-hidāyah*) is an exclusive divine favor granted according to divine wisdom and human sincerity. Classical theologians, including al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Bayḍāwī, differentiated between two levels of guidance: *hidāyat al-dalālah* (the external pointing out of the path through revealed scripture and prophetic instruction, which is universal) and *hidāyat al-tawfīq* (the internal divine facilitation that opens the heart, illuminates the conscience, and grants steadfastness upon the truth). While all human beings have access to the clarifying signs, only those who approach the revelation with humility and moral sincerity receive the inner illumination necessary to traverse the Straight Path safely.

Verse 46 serves as a pivotal hinge within the architecture of Sūrah an-Nūr. It bridges the grand cosmic contemplation of verses 41–45 with the incisive psychological diagnosis of hypocrisy that follows in verses 47–50.

---

## Verse 24:47

> And the hypocrites say, “We believe in Allah and the Messenger, and we obey.” Then a group of them turns away soon after that. These are not ˹true˺ believers

**What They Say**
The verse begins with a sentence the hypocrites of Madinah were repeating: *we believe in God and in the Messenger, and we obey*. Three declarations in one breath, and each of them is the sort of thing a person says when the group he wants to stay in says it. The sūrah is pointing at the gap that appears one clause later — *then a group of them turns away after that* — because the turning away is the evidence that the first sentence was not a description of anything.
**The Verdict**
The verse does not argue with the claim; it withdraws what the claim was asking for: *those are not believers*. The Book does this with care. It does not say they were never Muslims in the legal sense, nor does it expel anyone from the community on the strength of a sentence. It says that the thing they were claiming — the standing of a believer — is not theirs to hold while they behave this way, and it leaves open the door of return that the sūrah names later in the passage. Faith, in the Book's usage, is not a badge that a man can pick up by saying the words; it is a commitment that shows itself when the commitment costs something.
**The Refusal Behind It**
The verses that follow describe what the turn was actually about: they were invited to come to what God revealed and to the Messenger for judgement, and a group of them walked out (24:48-50). The quarrel was not about the words of the creed; it was about who was going to decide the case they had in hand. That is why the verse says *after that* — after the declaration, when the moment arrived to hand the decision over, the declaration was tested and failed. The sūrah records the test rather than the slogan, which is the only way to tell a believer from a man who has learned the vocabulary.

## Verse 24:48

> And as soon as they are called to Allah and His Messenger so he may judge between them, a group of them turns away

**When They Are Called to the Prophet**

The syntactic phrasing of the purpose clause contains an exquisite religious nuance: *li-yaḥkuma baynahum* (so he may judge between them). The singular masculine pronoun *yaḥkum* (he judges) refers grammatically to the Messenger ﷺ, yet the summons was explicitly to *Allah and His Messenger*.

Classical commentators, including al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, observed that this syntactic fusion affirms the absolute inseparability of divine law and prophetic judgment. The Messenger ﷺ does not judge according to personal whim, cultural convention, or subjective bias; his rulings are direct applications and embodiments of divine revelation. To accept the judgment of the Prophet is to accept the judgment of God; conversely, to reject or evade prophetic adjudication is an explicit rejection of divine sovereignty itself. The court of the Messenger was the terrestrial manifestation of cosmic justice, leaving no space for selective allegiance, tribal evasion, or opportunistic obedience.

**Historical Incidents of Evasion in Madinah**

Classical exegesis records several historical disputes that illuminate the concrete context of this revelation. Exegetes such as al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ṭabarī document a dispute over land between a hypocrite named Bishr and a Jewish resident of Madinah.

The Jewish claimant, fully confident in the legendary fairness and incorruptibility of the Prophet ﷺ, insisted: Let us take our dispute to Muḥammad! The hypocrite Bishr, knowing that his claim was fraudulent and that the Prophet would never rule in favor of injustice regardless of religious identity, adamantly refused, insisting instead: Let us go to Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf (a corrupt tribal chieftain who took bribes). Other accounts narrate a property dispute between Mughīrah ibn Wāʾil and ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, where Mughīrah openly expressed fear that the Prophet would judge against him. These incidents showed that religious minorities often had greater trust in prophetic equity than hypocrites who claimed to be Muslims, shaming the duplicity of the disaffected.

## Verse 24:49

> But if the truth is in their favour, they come to him, fully submitting

**The Antithesis of Authentic Faith**

The authentic believer approaches divine arbitration with the spirit expressed in 4:65: they find within themselves no resistance whatsoever against prophetic judgment, submitting with complete, wholehearted acceptance (*wa-yusallimū taslīmā*). If the court rules against the true believer, they accept the verdict with dignified humility, grateful that an injustice was prevented in this world rather than punished in the next. The hypocrite, conversely, experiences religion as a zero-sum commercial negotiation. They worship their own ego (*al-hawā*); God and His Messenger are acknowledged only as far as they serve as useful advocates for the ego's insatiable desires, creating an idolatry of self that poisons the spirit.

**The Psychology of Opportunistic Adjudication: wa-in yakun lahumu al-ḥaqq**

The Qur'an delivers a penetrating psychological exposure of hypocritical opportunism: *wa-in yakun lahumu al-ḥaqqu yaʾtū ilayhi mudhʿinīn*. Having exposed how the hypocrites reflexively turned away from prophetic judgment when they feared their fraudulent claims would be dismissed (v. 48), the text now reveals their radically opposite behavior when circumstances reversed.

**The Antithesis of Authentic Faith**

and, this opportunistic posture reveals a total absence of the fear of God (*taqwā*). A person who genuinely believes in the Day of Judgment understands that acquiring property or wealth through a biased or fraudulent legal judgment in this world is akin to swallowing burning coals of hellfire into their belly. The Prophet ﷺ famously cautioned litigants in his court: I am only a human being, and you bring your disputes to me; perhaps one of you is more eloquent in his argument than the other, and I rule in his favor based on what I hear. Whoever I grant a right that belongs to his brother, let him not take it, for I am only granting him a piece of the Fire. The hypocrite ignores this terrifying prophetic warning because they care nothing for the Hereafter, obsessed exclusively with immediate material acquisition.

## Verse 24:50

> Is there a sickness in their hearts? Or are they in doubt? Or do they fear that Allah and His Messenger will be unjust to them? In fact, it is they who are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers

**Do They Fear Injustice?**
The third diagnostic query reaches the outer limits of moral absurdity: *am yakhāfūna a yaḥīfa Allāhu ʿalayhim wa-rasūluh*, or do they fear that Allah and His Messenger will be unjust to them? The verb *yaḥīf* means judicial bias, partiality, tipping the scales of justice, and defrauding a litigant of their rightful entitlement.
To even contemplate that the Creator of all things—the Sovereign Lord who created the heavens with mathematical balance and commands universal justice—could act unjustly toward a mortal creature is an outrageous religious impossibility and grave sin. Similarly, to suspect the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ—who was renowned even before revelation as *al-Amīn* (the Trustworthy) and sent as a mercy to all worlds—of defrauding a citizen in a property dispute is grotesque slander. By hesitating to enter the prophetic courtroom, the hypocrites committed the ultimate psychological projection: being unjust themselves, they projected their own corrupt instincts onto God and His Messenger, exposing their own dark inclinations.

**The Three Questions and the Word at the End**
The verse asks what is really behind the refusal to come for judgement: is there a sickness in their hearts, or a doubt, or a fear that God and His Messenger will treat them unjustly? Then it gives the answer itself — no, it is they who are the wrongdoers. The sūrah has just described the behaviour: when the matter is decided in their favour they come running, and when it is decided against them they turn away (24:48-49). The three possibilities the verse names are the only ones available, and the verdict at the end of the sentence closes all of them. A question that ends with its own answer is the sūrah's way of showing that the reader already knows the reason.
**The Authority the Passage Is Defending**
The passage establishes that being judged by God's Messenger is part of faith, not a favour being granted. The Sūrah of Women states the rule in its strongest form, saying that people do not truly believe until they accept the Prophet's judgement in the disputes among them and then find no discomfort in it (4:65), and the same sūrah describes those who turn away to other judges while claiming to believe (4:60-61). The sūrah of the Light adds that the only real safety for a community is a justice it accepts even when it goes against it. Judgment in a favouritism-driven society is the test the verse is applying.
**What It Is Like to Be on the Other Side**
The verse's closing verdict is one of the sūrah's hardest, and it is aimed at a specific attitude rather than at a group of people. A man who accepts a ruling he likes and walks away from one he does not has already decided that the law is his tool. The Sūrah of the Light is a sūrah of rules — about marriage, about accusation, about entering houses, about modesty — and this verse sits among them to make a simple point: the rules are not the society's property, and refusing the judge is the wrong itself. The next verses give the alternative, describing those who say they hear and obey and who prosper (24:51-52).

## Verse 24:51

> The only response of the ˹true˺ believers, when they are called to Allah and His Messenger so he may judge between them, is to say, “We hear and obey.” It is they who will ˹truly˺ succeed

**The Readiness of Submission: samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā**

The verbal response of the faithful encapsulates the entire ethos of Islam: *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā*, we hear and we obey! This two-part formula represents the complete synthesis of cognitive reception and physical execution.

First, *samiʿnā* (we hear) is not passive auditory reception, but attentive, respectful, and unbiased listening that seeks to understand divine commands without prejudice or resistance. Second, *aṭaʿnā* (we obey) is instantaneous, active conformity of the will and limbs to the judicial ruling, executed with cheerful readiness. This response echoes the covenant of the righteous companions described in 2:285, in stark contrast to the rebellious defiance of ancient Israel recounted in 2:93 (*samiʿnā wa-ʿaṣaynā*—we hear and we disobey). For the believer, hearing and obeying occur as an unified spiritual reflex, closing the dangerous gap between hearing truth and executing duty, leaving no room for hypocrisy.

**The Dignified Demeanor Before Judicial Authority**

If the Prophet ﷺ rules in their favor, they praise God for vindicating their legitimate rights and remain humble; if the Prophet rules against them, ordering them to surrender disputed land or repay a debt, they accept the ruling with serenity and immediate compliance. They realize that losing a temporary worldly asset under divine justice is an infinitely lighter burden than carrying a stolen penny into the terrifying scrutiny of the Day of Judgment. Their submission before the bench is an act of high worship, transforming a civil litigation into a profound manifestation of monotheistic piety, honoring God's justice above mortal desires and temporary worldly wealth.

**True Prosperity Defined: wa-ulāʾika humu al-mufliḥūn**

The verse culminates in an emphatic Last-Day and worldly verdict: *wa-ulāʾika humu al-mufliḥūn*, and it is they who will truly succeed. The noun *mufliḥūn* derives from the root *f-l-ḥ*, which mainly means agricultural flourishing, cultivation, and harvesting abundance (*falāḥ*).

---

## Verse 24:52

> For whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger, and fears Allah and is mindful of Him, then it is they who will ˹truly˺ triumph

**The Subtle Distinction Between Khashyah and Taqwā**

The second pair of conditions addresses the internal spiritual realities of the soul: *wa-yakhsha Allāha wa-yattaqh*, and fears Allah and is mindful of Him. Classical Arabic lexicographers and theologians closely distinguished between *khashyah* and *taqwā*.

*Khashyah* is awe-inspired dread born of profound knowledge and contemplation of divine majesty, power, and transcendence. It is an intellectual and spiritual trembling that seizes the soul when it perceives the boundless glory of God compared to the utter insignificance of mortal creation. As 35:28 declares: Only those among His servants who have sacred knowledge truly fear Him (*innamā yakhshā Allāha min ʿibādihi al-ʿulamāʾ*). In contrast, *taqwā* is the active, vigilant erection of a protective shield (*wiqāyah*) between oneself and divine wrath through the avoidance of sins and conscientious fulfillment of obligations. *Khashyah* is the interior emotional awe; *taqwā* is the practical ethical discipline that awe generates across every circumstance of life, preserving the believer from moral ruin.

The structural sequencing of these four conditions reveals profound pedagogical wisdom. Exterior obedience (*al-ṭāʿah*) without internal fear and mindfulness degenerates into hypocritical theatricality—precisely the disease that crippled the hypocrites exposed in verses 47–50.

## Verse 24:53

> They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that if you ˹O Prophet˺ were to command them, they would certainly march forth ˹in Allah’s cause˺. Say, “˹You˺ do not ˹have to˺ swear; your obedience is well known!” Surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do.”

**Their Strongest Oaths**

The divine text exposes another psychological defense mechanism employed by the hypocrites: *wa-aqsamū bi-Allāhi jahda aymānihim la-in amartahum la-yakhrujunna*. The expression *jahda aymānihim* (their most solemn, strenuous oaths) means the swearing of aggressive, hyperbolic oaths invoking the supreme majesty and holiness of God.

Whenever their lack of commitment was unmasked by their refusal to submit to prophetic arbitration (vv. 48–50), the hypocrites felt an urgent need to restore their social credibility within the Madinan community. Rather than offering humble repentance or rectifying their daily ethical failures, they resorted to bombastic, theatrical oaths to impress onlookers. They swore that if the Prophet ﷺ were to issue a supreme military mobilization, they would immediately abandon homes, families, and fortunes to march forth into the most dangerous battlefronts. They used the sacred Name of God as an emotional shield, hoping that the sheer volume and solemnity of their oaths would silence suspicion, deceive the innocent, and intimidate doubters into accepting their loyalty.

---

**The Answer the Oaths Received**
The verse does not end with the hypocrites' swearing. The divine reply cuts the performance short in one line: *qul lā tuqsimū* — say: do not swear. The oaths were being offered as a currency, in a transaction where the price was credibility, and the verse refuses the payment. What comes next names the only thing that was ever being examined: *your obedience is known* — known to the One who sees the intention behind the performance and the empty room behind the closed door. The verse closes with the sentence that makes the whole scene readable: God is aware of what you do. The men were swearing for an audience; the answer names a different audience, and the audience is the one that cannot be deceived.
**What the Men Were Covering**
The strongest oath in their repertoire was spent on the promise to march if they were ordered to. The Book has already described their actual conduct in the previous verses, where the summons to judgement and the call to fight met with hesitation, and it will go on to describe their conduct further. Their oath was not a statement of fact but a repair: every refusal had been seen, and each one had to be answered with something louder. That is how the language of the strongest oath becomes, in this sūrah, a sign of the weakest position.

## Verse 24:54

> Say, “Obey Allah and obey the Messenger. But if you turn away, then he is only responsible for his duty and you are responsible for yours. And if you obey him, you will be ˹rightly˺ guided. The Messenger’s duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺ clearly.”

**The Functional Limits of Prophetic Mission: mā ʿalā al-rasūli illā al-balāgh**

The verse defines the constitutional perimeter of prophetic authority: *wa-mā ʿalā al-rasūli illā al-balāghu al-mubīn*, and naught is incumbent upon the Messenger save the clear delivery. The noun *al-balāgh* means conveying a message so thoroughly, lucidly, and fully that it reaches the core of the recipient's consciousness.

The qualifier *al-mubīn* indicates that the proclamation is clear, transparent, and completely devoid of obscurity. The Prophet ﷺ was sent as an inviter (*dāʿī*), a warner (*nadhīr*), and a bearer of glad tidings (*bashīr*); he was not commissioned as an authoritarian tyrant who forces internal belief upon unwilling minds (*lasta ʿalayhim bi-muṣayṭir*). By stating that the Messenger's sole duty is clear delivery, the revelation relieves the Prophet of the crushing psychological grief he felt when observing the obstinacy of the hypocrites, reassuring him that human hearts belong exclusively to God and will be judged by Him alone.

When the message has been delivered with immaculate clarity, rejection is stripped of every plausible defense. In the early Madinan community, the citizens lived in the physical presence of the Messenger of God ﷺ.

---

## Verse 24:55

> Allah has promised those of you who believe and do good that He will certainly make them successors in the land, as He did with those before them; and will surely establish for them their faith which He has chosen for them; and will indeed change their fear into security—˹provided that˺ they worship Me, associating nothing with Me. But whoever disbelieves after this ˹promise˺, it is they who will be the rebellious

**A Promise Stated in the Past Tense of Certainty**
The promise opens with *waʿada Allāhu* — God has promised — and the form is deliberate. What God promises is not a hope that may fail but a fact waiting for its date. The promise belongs to those who believe and do good works, and it holds three things together: succession in the land, establishment of the religion, and safety in place of fear.
**Succession in the Land**
*La-yastakhlifannahum fī al-arḍ*, and *kamā istakhlafa alladhīna min qablihim* — as He gave succession to those before them. The clause points back through the prophetic line: the Children of Israel, kept as slaves under Pharaoh and then handed the land of tyrants. The early community in Madinah knew the comparison exactly, because it was their own story being told before it happened. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr all read the verse as fulfilled in the era of the rightly guided caliphs, who ruled from North Africa to Central Asia within a few decades of the Prophet's death. A promise made to a hunted minority, and the map changed.
**Establishment and Safety**
*Wa-la-yumakkinanna lahum dīnahum alladhī irtaḍā lahum* — the religion He has approved for them will be made firm; the phrase echoes the completed favour of 5:3. The faith was not meant to stay a secret minority religion hiding in cellars. Then comes the third gift: *wa-la-yubaddilannahum min baʿdi khawfihim amnan* — their fear will be exchanged for security. The condition attached is worship of God alone, with nothing joined to Him. And the verse closes with a warning for whoever disbelieves after all this: they are the ones who leave the path. The promise is a covenant in two directions, and both doors are stated.

## Verse 24:56

> Moreover, establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and obey the Messenger, so you may be shown mercy

**Three Duties That Hold Up a Nation**
Verse 55 made a promise, and verse 56 names what the promise is for. *Wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa-ātū al-zakāta wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūla laʿallakum turḥamūn* — establish the prayer, give the alms, obey the Messenger, so that mercy may reach you. The three duties cover the whole shape of a life together. Prayer sets the person right with God. Alms sets people right with one another. Obeying the Messenger sets the community right in law and public order.
**No Promise Without Work**
The order matters. The verses that follow a promise of victory are never luxury; they are duty, because a promise of this size cannot be met by people who expect to be carried. Divine help comes to a community that builds something worth helping: worship that is actually maintained, not wished for; wealth that actually circulates as charity; a Messenger actually obeyed, not merely praised. *Aqīmū* — establish — is a word of structure, used of setting something upright and keeping it up. It asks for the congregation kept, the times kept, the habit kept, across a whole society, not for a private feeling.
**The End of the Command Is Mercy**
The verse ends with *laʿallakum turḥamūn*, perhaps you will be shown mercy. That is the target of the whole sentence: not victory as an end in itself, not statehood as an end in itself, but a community that has become the kind of community mercy arrives in. The order given here is the order of the Prophet's Madinah in miniature — worship, welfare, and law — and every generation that inherits the promise inherits the same three instructions with it.

## Verse 24:57

> Do not think ˹O Prophet˺ that the disbelievers can escape in the land. The Fire will be their home. Indeed, what an evil destination

**Do Not Think They Have Escaped**
*Lā taḥsabanna alladhīna kafarū muʿjizīna fī al-arḍ* — do not think that those who disbelieve can escape in the land. The verb is emphatic, and the thought it forbids is one the reader is likely to have had. The verse was revealed at a time when the men of Makkah who had hounded the Muslims were wealthy, secure, and successful, and the community's own position looked precarious; the sentence is addressed to the person looking at that picture and drawing the obvious conclusion. Nothing here says the delay is an accident or a failure of attention. The Book calls the same respite elsewhere a postponement that lets a people run further (3:178), and it ends the thought the verse forbids with the reason it is wrong: they are not outside the reach of the One who is waiting.
**The Word for Home**
The second clause uses *maʾwā*, and the choice is pointed. *Maʾwā* is a shelter — the place a person returns to for safety, the roof he seeks when the weather turns, the refuge a tired man lies down in. The verse gives them the Fire as their *maʾwā*, and the word carries the whole judgment in it: not a punishment from outside, but the shelter they arrive at. The verse then ends with the Book's strongest form of the exclamation: *what an evil destination*. The sūrah's third use of the same closing comes a few verses later, where the verse about the Day when their own tongues, hands and feet testify ends on the same phrase — so that the reader carries the phrase through the whole scene.

## Verse 24:58

> O believers! Let those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession and those of you who are still under age ask for your permission ˹to come in˺ at three times: before dawn prayer, when you take off your ˹outer˺ clothes at noon, and after the late evening prayer. ˹These are˺ three times of privacy for you. Other than these times, there is no blame on you or them to move freely, attending to one another. This is how Allah makes the revelations clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise

**Asking Permission at Home**

The divine law returns from political treaties (vv. 55–57) to the everyday ethics of the home, showing the reach of Qur'anic guidance: *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū li-yastaʾdhinkumu alladhīna malakat aymānukum*. The imperative verb *li-yastaʾdhin* sets up the duty of seeking permission (*istiʾdhān*) before entering private chambers.

**The Three Temporal Nodes of Intimate Privacy: thalāthu ʿawrāt**

The verse identifies three critical times of privacy, calling them *thalāthu ʿawrāt* (three times of privacy/nakedness): *min qabli ṣalāti al-fajri wa-ḥīna taḍaʿūna thiyābakum mina al-ẓahīrati wa-min baʿdi ṣalāti al-ʿishāʾ*.

The first node is before the dawn prayer (*min qabli ṣalāt al-fajr*), when people awaken from sleep, rise from marital beds, and change out of nightwear to perform ablution. The second node is the midday rest (*al-ẓahīrah*), when the intense desert heat prompts individuals to remove heavy outer garments for the customary midday nap (*al-qaylūlah*), seeking bodily refreshment. The third node is after the night prayer (*min baʿdi ṣalāt al-ʿishāʾ*), when the day's labor ends, clothes are shed, and spouses retire to bed for rest and physical intimacy. During these three sensitive windows, bedrooms become private sanctuaries; no servant or child may cross the threshold without knocking and receiving explicit verbal permission from the occupants within.

The verse seals its domestic ordinances with a closing statement about God: *ka-dhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakumu al-āyāti wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm*, this is how Allah makes the revelations clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

---

The three daily times of privacy are the rule's core, and the verse that follows extends the same discipline to the children when they reach puberty (24:59). The jurists held that the requirement to ask permission applies especially to those who are not past the age of childhood and to servants who have business in the house, and they read the closing words of the verse — that God knows what is done openly and in secret — as the sanction for a household rule rather than a public law. The practice at the time of the Prophet ﷺ is preserved in the reports of the Companions about children coming in on their mothers, and the sūrah's rule is placed among the other regulations in the chapter on modesty and household life, the same group that includes the dress of women (24:31) and the sharing of meals (24:61).

## Verse 24:59

> And when your children reach the age of puberty, let them seek permission ˹to come in˺, as their seniors do. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise

**When the Children Come of Age**
Verse 58 arranged the etiquette of the house for those not yet grown: children and servants come in politely at three times. This verse hands the young their adult status. *Wa-idhā balagha al-aṭfālu minkumu al-ḥuluma fa-lyastaʾdhinū* — when your children reach puberty, they must ask permission. *Balagha al-ḥulum* is the coming of age: the signs the law recognises are nocturnal discharge for a boy, menstruation for a girl, or any other mark of full physical maturity. At that point childhood ends and the same rule that binds adults binds them.
**The Household as It Actually Was**
The reason the rule matters is the shape of the home it was given to. In the Near East of that time, and in many Muslim households still, three generations share one compound: uncles, aunts, grown cousins, married brothers and sisters, all moving through shared rooms. In such a house, privacy is not a luxury but the thing that keeps a large family comfortable together, and the grown son who walks freely into his parents' or his sister's room may see what no one in the household wants seen. Islam does not dissolve the extended family to solve this. It teaches it courtesy.
**The Seal at the End**
The verse closes with the same pair that closed verse 58: *ka-dhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakum āyātihī wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm* — this is how God makes His signs clear to you, and God is All-Knowing, All-Wise. The repetition is itself a lesson; these household rules are not minor business but part of revelation's curriculum. *Al-ʿAlīm* knows how children grow and when awareness wakes. *Al-Ḥakīm* times the duty to the body's own clock, asking nothing of a child and asking everything of a grown man. The result is a home where honour is not enforced by suspicion but built by habit.

## Verse 24:60

> As for elderly women past the age of marriage, there is no blame on them if they take off their ˹outer˺ garments, without revealing their adornments. But it is better for them if they avoid this ˹altogether˺. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing

**The Older Women and the Outer Garment**
The verse gives a concession, and it names exactly who receives it: *al-qawāʿid*, the women who have stopped expecting marriage, whose age has released them from the years of attraction and of childbearing. The root of the word is *qaʿada*, to sit, and the picture is of the woman who has come to sit at home. For these women there is no blame *if they put aside their outer garments* — the cloak worn over the house clothes — provided they are not displaying themselves or the adornment that would draw attention.
**The Better Choice Named With It**
The verse does not stop at permission. It adds the sentence that sets the higher standard beside it: *and that they stay modest in it is better for them*. The jurists made this pair a subject of their own, calling the permission a *rukhṣah*, an easing granted where it is needed, and the voluntary modesty an *ʿazīmah*, the stronger course that a person takes when nothing forces the ease on her. The two are not in competition; the verse states the concession so that the older woman who finds the cloak heavy in the heat is not carrying sin for it, and then says that keeping it off the floor of necessity is better.
**Why the Names Close It**
The verse ends with *God is All-Hearing, All-Knowing*, and the pairing sets the whole rule in its place. All-Hearing covers what is said in the house when no one outside is listening; All-Knowing covers the reason a woman gives for what she wears — whether the cloak comes off because of age and heat, or because the heart is still asking for attention. The law states the boundary; the names say who is actually watching the doorway. No one states in court what she intended, and no one has to.

## Verse 24:61

> There is no restriction on the blind, or the disabled, or the sick. Nor on yourselves if you eat from your homes, or the homes of your fathers, or your mothers, or your brothers, or your sisters, or your paternal uncles, or your paternal aunts, or your maternal uncles, or your maternal aunts, or from the homes in your trust, or ˹the homes of˺ your friends. There is no blame on you eating together or separately. However, when you enter houses, greet one another with a greeting ˹of peace˺ from Allah, blessed and good. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will understand

**Eating in the Homes of Family and Friends**

The verse systematically maps out an expansive network of domestic kinship where food may be shared freely without formal invitations: *wa-lā ʿalā anfusikum a taʾkulū min buyūtikum aw buyūti ābāʾikum aw buyūti ummahātikum*. The text enumerates eleven categories of households: parents, siblings, paternal and maternal uncles and aunts, homes held in trust, and intimate friends.

**The Liturgy of Domestic Entry: taḥiyyatan min ʿindi Allāh**

Even if a house is empty, classical commentators like Ibn ʿAbbās noted that one should say: *al-salāmu ʿalaynā wa-ʿalā ʿibād Allāh al-ṣāliḥīn* (Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of God). The Islamic greeting of peace (*al-Salām*) is not a secular pleasantry; it is a sacred invocation descending from God (*min ʿindi Allāh*), characterized as *mubārakah* (blessed, attracting divine increase) and *ṭayyibah* (wholesome, pleasing to the soul). By initiating every domestic encounter with this divine greeting, the believer sanctifies the space, banishes demonic discord, and establishes an atmosphere of mutual security.

**Cognitive Maturity and the Ethic of Inclusion: laʿallakum taʿqilūn**

The verse concludes with a profound pedagogical seal: *ka-dhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakumu al-āyāti laʿallakum taʿqilūn*, this is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will understand. The verb *taʿqilūn* calls upon the human intellect to grasp the profound ethical coherence of divine law.

---

The verse answers scruples the community had raised: the blind, the lame and the sick feared they would be thought to be taking food uninvited, and the verse removes the restriction for them together with the wider permission to eat in the houses of family and friends. The commentators explained the practice behind it — that the Anṣār and the Emigrants shared houses and property, and a person did not wish to eat from a relative's larder without being formally offered — and noted that the clause about eating together or separately covers the community's own preference for gathering or eating apart. The verse closes with the command to greet one another, which is the household form of the same welcome that the sharers of the meal are being given.

## Verse 24:62

> The ˹true˺ believers are only those who believe in Allah and His Messenger, and when they are with him on a public matter, they do not leave without his permission. Indeed, those who ask your permission ˹O Prophet˺ are the ones who ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and His Messenger. So when they ask your permission for a private matter, grant permission to whoever you wish and ask Allah’s forgiveness for them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful

**What the Community Meets For**
*Amrin jāmiʿ* means a matter that gathers people — a public affair, not a private errand: a march, a consultation, a crisis that needs the whole community standing together. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāzī read the phrase broadly, as any common business of the Muslims that requires joint effort and joint decision.
**Leave Only With Permission**
The verse lays down the etiquette for such gatherings. A believer who is with the Prophet on a shared matter should not slip away quietly to see to his own business; when there is a genuine need, he asks. And permission is not his to take: *fa-ʾdhan li-man shiʾta minhum* — grant leave to whom you will. The Prophet has the wider view, and he knows which man is needed at his post and which one has a real errand at home. A duty is not abandoned by asking; it is abandoned by leaving without asking.
**The Names at the End**
The verse closes with *inna Allāha ghafūrun raḥīm* — God is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. That mercy is the reason the rule can be firm without being harsh. Discipline in public affairs and kindness in private need are not opposites here; the same law that says the line must be held says that the man who asks is answered with forgiveness rather than shame.

**Who Asks and Who Does Not**
The verse names the men who ask permission at the end of the passage, and it names them so that the rule is not read as a licence for the lazy: *those who ask your permission are the ones who believe in God and His Messenger* (24:62). The men who slipped away quietly were the men whose faith was thin; the man who asks, states a need, and waits on the answer is the man whose conduct shows what his faith is worth. The verse then supplies the other half of the exchange — for the Prophet, the instruction to grant leave when he sees fit and to ask forgiveness for the men he excuses. So the permission is followed by a prayer, and no one leaves the gathering carrying a mark against him. The whole passage ends where the earlier verses on oaths and obedience ended: the community is held together by a chain of requests, permissions and forgiveness, not by silence.

## Verse 24:63

> Do not treat the Messenger’s summons to you ˹as lightly˺ as your summons to one another. Allah certainly knows those of you who slip away, hiding behind others. So let those who disobey his orders beware, for an affliction may befall them, or a painful torment may overtake them

**Last-Day Retribution: aw yuṣībahum ʿadhābun alīm**

The adjective *alīm* conveys excruciating, agonizing pain that pierces the soul and body in the blazing fires of Hell. The hypocrites who sneaked away behind their companions to avoid a few hours of physical labor or military service will find that their brief worldly comfort was purchased at the cost of unimaginable, eternal torment. Verse 63 stands as a monumental pillar of Sūrah an-Nūr, establishing that true civilizational survival, moral sanity, and spiritual salvation are found solely in unwavering, reverent submission to the commands of the Messenger of God ﷺ, honoring his prophetic station across all generations.

**Revering the Prophetic Summons: lā tajʿalū duʿāʾa al-rasūl**

The divine speech issues an urgent constitutional command governing decorum and reverence toward prophetic leadership: *lā tajʿalū duʿāʾa al-rasūli baynakum ka-duʿāʾi baʿḍikum baʿḍā*. Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Qurṭubī, expressed two complementary and mutually reinforcing interpretations of *duʿāʾ al-rasūl*.

First, it refers to the community addressing the Prophet ﷺ: believers are strictly forbidden from calling out to him casually by his bare personal name—such as saying "O Muḥammad!" as they would address a peer in the marketplace. Instead, they must address him with reverent titles of honor, saying "O Messenger of God!" or "O Prophet of God!" Second, it refers to the Prophet calling the community to action: when the Messenger of God summons the people to an assembly, a defensive mobilization, or a judicial hearing, his summons carries absolute, divine authority. It cannot be treated casually, postponed for convenience, or ignored like an ordinary social invitation from an acquaintance. To heed the Prophet's call is an immediate, non-negotiable religious obligation.

**The Anatomy of Cowardly Evasion: yatasallalūna minkum liwādhā**

The adverbial noun *liwādhā* derives from *lādha bi-al-shayʾ* (to take shelter or seek cover behind something). During critical public assemblies or difficult defensive labors—such as digging the trench in freezing winds—the hypocrites lacked the courage to ask the Prophet ﷺ for permission, knowing their excuses were fraudulent.

**The All-Seeing Eye of Divine Surveillance: qad yaʿlamu Allāh**

The conspirators imagined that by shielding their bodies behind others, they had successfully deceived the Prophet ﷺ and protected themselves from embarrassment. The Qur'an reminds them that while they might conceal their movements from human eyesight, they stand completely exposed before the Lord of the worlds.

## Verse 24:64

> Surely to Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth. He knows well what you stand for. And ˹on˺ the Day all will be returned to Him, He will inform them of what they did. For Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things

**Divine Cognizance of Human States: qad yaʿlamu mā antum ʿalayh**

The divine gaze narrows from cosmic expanse to the immediate reality of human conscience: *qad yaʿlamu mā antum ʿalayh*, He knows well what you stand for / He certainly knows the state you are in. The particle *qad* combined with the present-tense verb *yaʿlam* communicates absolute, intimate, and continuous knowledge.

The phrase *mā antum ʿalayh* encompasses the totality of human orientation: beliefs, hidden intentions, hypocritical vacillations, secret sins, quiet sacrifices, and moral struggles. God knows whether an individual stands upon authentic faith, humble obedience, and sincere love for truth, or whether they stand upon cynical hypocrisy, deceitful evasion, and self-serving opportunism. Nothing in the human psyche remains hidden. This intimate awareness dismantles the superficial facades constructed by human beings to impress their peers, calling every servant to rigorous internal honesty before the Lord who perceives every pulse of the heart.

**Transcendent Omniscience as the Ultimate Seal: Allāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿAlīm**

The sūrah concludes with its final, majestic religious seal: *wa-Allāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm*, for Allah has perfect knowledge of all things. The attribute *al-ʿAlīm* encompasses infinite, all-embracing knowledge that spans past, present, future, and all counterfactual realities.

**The Architectural Coherence of Sūrah an-Nūr**

Reflecting upon the entire span of Sūrah an-Nūr reveals an architectural masterpiece of divine wisdom. The chapter opened in verse 1 with an uncompromising declaration of legal authority: *Sūratun anzalnāhā wa-faraḍnāhā* (A sūrah which We have revealed and made obligatory). It ends in verse 64 with the cosmic proclamation of divine ownership and omniscience: *inna lillāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ*.
