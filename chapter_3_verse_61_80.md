## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:61

> Now, whoever disputes with you ˹O Prophet˺ concerning Jesus after full knowledge has come to you, say, “Come! Let us gather our children and your children, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves— then let us sincerely invoke Allah’s curse upon the liars.”

**Commentary:**

### The Challenge of Mutual Invocation

After presenting clear evidence about Jesus, the Prophet ﷺ is instructed to invite those who continue disputing to *mubāhalah*: each side gathers its closest family and sincerely asks Allah to place His curse upon whoever is lying. This is an exceptionally serious procedure, not an ordinary debating technique.

The invitation comes only “after full knowledge has come.” It is not permissible to invoke Allah’s curse because of a minor disagreement, incomplete research, or anger. The truth must first be explained with evidence, and the parties must understand the issue. A believer should prefer patient dialogue and prayer for guidance whenever possible.

### Family as a Witness

The instruction mentions children, women, and the people themselves. The gathered family represents the sincerity and seriousness of the claim. A person who is willing to place his loved ones under Allah’s judgement is declaring that he is not hiding behind rhetoric.

The family should not be treated as a shield or weapon. Its mention highlights the gravity of the matter and the communal consequences of religious claims. Falsehood can harm generations, while truth can guide them.

### The Event with Najrān

Classical tafsīr and hadith collections connect this verse with the Christian delegation from Najrān that disputed with the Prophet ﷺ about Jesus. Reports in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim mention that the Prophet ﷺ called ʿAlī, Fāṭimah, al-Ḥasan, and al-Ḥusayn when the invitation was considered. The delegation ultimately did not proceed with the mutual invocation.

These reports do not make family members objects of worship or proof independent of revelation. Their presence represents the Prophet’s household in a solemn appeal to Allah.

### Do Not Use Mubahalah Casually

Mutual invocation of Allah’s curse is a last resort under legitimate authority and after clear proof. It is not a way for online disputants to intimidate one another or to claim certainty about Allah’s judgement. The Prophet ﷺ was commanded to it in a matter of central creed after revelation had settled the evidence.

A person should fear lying when invoking Allah, but he should also fear falsely accusing another person. The safer course in most disagreements is to say that Allah knows best and continue seeking truth.

### Truth and Consequence

The verse shows that religious truth matters. Jesus’ identity is not a harmless detail if it changes the object of worship. Yet the severity of the proposed procedure is matched by the seriousness of the prior evidence and the need to prevent endless argument after clarity has arrived.

The purpose is not to glorify conflict. It is to end a dispute by placing the final matter before Allah when one side refuses to accept decisive evidence.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:45–60 on Jesus’ birth, miracles, servanthood, and comparison with Adam.
- Qur’an 10:94 and 17:36 on verifying knowledge before making claims.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports and avoiding rash judgement.
- Qur’an 16:125 and 29:46 on inviting and debating with wisdom and the best manner.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the report of the Prophet’s invitation to the Christians of Najrān and the gathering of his family.
- For *mubāhalah*, the condition of prior clarity, the seriousness of invoking Allah’s curse, and the Najrān delegation, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:61.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:62

> Certainly, this is the true narrative, and there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Allah. And indeed, Allah ˹alone˺ is the Almighty, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### The True Narrative

After presenting the account of Jesus and the challenge to those who disputed it, Allah declares that this is the true narrative. The Qur’an does not present Jesus through legend, political propaganda, or inherited speculation. It presents the truth known by Allah and revealed for human guidance.

The word *qaṣaṣ* can carry the sense of following a matter carefully and relating it accurately. The Qur’an’s narrative selects what people need to know: Jesus’ miraculous birth, his prophetic mission, his signs, his call to tawḥīd, and the error of making him divine.

### Tawḥīd as the Conclusion

The truth of the narrative culminates in the declaration that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah. This is the decisive conclusion of the entire passage. The discussion of Jesus is not complete until it answers the question of worship.

A person may acknowledge Jesus as a noble prophet yet still misunderstand the purpose of his mission if he directs worship to Jesus or another created being. The prophets call people away from themselves and toward Allah.

### Allah’s Might and Wisdom

Allah is *al-ʿAzīz*, the Almighty, and *al-Ḥakīm*, the All-Wise. His power made the miraculous birth and signs of Jesus possible, while His wisdom placed Jesus within a precise prophetic mission. The miracle does not violate wisdom; it reveals wisdom when read in context.

Allah’s might also means that no theological dispute can alter reality. Human claims may change through generations, but Allah’s truth remains stable. His wisdom means that revelation corrects error in a way suited to the spiritual needs of each community.

### Truth beyond Argument

The preceding verse offered a final solemn appeal after argument had continued despite knowledge. Here Allah closes the matter. Once truth has been clearly conveyed, the responsibility shifts to the hearer. A person may continue disputing, but repetition does not make falsehood true.

The believer should learn both firmness and restraint. Firmness means not compromising tawḥīd; restraint means not turning every disagreement into a personal quarrel. Allah is the final judge of hearts.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:45–61 on the true account of Jesus, his signs, and the challenge to disputants.
- Qur’an 5:72–75 on Jesus’ call to worship Allah and his created dependence.
- Qur’an 19:30–36 on Jesus as servant and prophet.
- Qur’an 4:171 and 112:1–4 on avoiding excess and affirming Allah’s uniqueness.
- Qur’an 18:13 and 12:3 on Qur’anic narratives as truth and guidance.
- For *qaṣaṣ al-ḥaqq*, tawḥīd, *al-ʿAzīz*, and *al-Ḥakīm*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:62.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:63

> If they turn away, then surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the corruptors.

**Commentary:**

### The Limit of the Messenger’s Duty

If the disputants turn away after truth has been explained, the Prophet ﷺ is reminded that Allah knows the corruptors. The messenger must convey, clarify, warn, and invite, but he does not control another person’s heart. This principle protects the caller from despair and from attempting to force belief.

Turning away after knowledge is not the same as needing more time or asking a sincere question. The verse concerns those who refuse truth and continue creating corruption after the evidence has been made clear.

### Allah Knows the Corruptors

Allah’s knowledge includes outward actions and hidden motives. He knows whether a person turns away because of confusion, coercion, pride, envy, or a desire to protect power. Human beings judge what is apparent; Allah alone knows the complete moral reality.

The word “corruptors” is *mufsidūn*. Corruption includes spreading falsehood, distorting revelation, inciting hostility, violating rights, and using religious dispute to damage people. It is not limited to physical destruction. A lie that confuses a community can be a form of *fasād*.

### No Need for Revenge

Because Allah knows the corruptors, the Prophet and believers do not need to take revenge merely because someone rejects their argument. They may protect themselves from harm and uphold justice, but they leave final judgement to Allah. This keeps religious disagreement from becoming personal vengeance.

The Qur’an commands Muslims to respond to evil with what is better when that response is wise and safe (41:34). It also permits resistance to aggression and requires justice. Patience is not surrender to oppression, and firmness is not permission for cruelty.

### The Comfort of Divine Knowledge

A sincere caller may feel that his effort has failed when people reject guidance. This verse provides comfort: Allah saw the effort and knows the result. A good word may bear fruit later, even when the speaker does not see it. The caller’s reward is with Allah, not with immediate acceptance.

Likewise, those harmed by distortion can trust that no falsehood escapes Allah. He knows who sought truth and who deliberately manufactured confusion.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 28:56 and 5:99 on the messenger’s duty to convey and Allah’s control of guidance.
- Qur’an 2:256 and 10:99 on no compulsion in religion.
- Qur’an 41:34 and 16:125 on responding with wisdom and the best manner.
- Qur’an 2:205 and 7:56 on corruption and not spreading *fasād* on earth.
- Qur’an 6:108 and 49:11–12 on avoiding abuse, insult, and conduct that creates further corruption.
- For *tawallī*, *mufsidūn*, divine knowledge, and the limits of prophetic responsibility, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:63.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:64

> Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “O People of the Book! Let us come to common terms: that we will worship none but Allah, associate none with Him, nor take one another as lords instead of Allah.” But if they turn away, then say, “Bear witness that we have submitted ˹to Allah alone˺.”

**Commentary:**

### A Common Word

The Prophet ﷺ is told to invite the People of the Book to *kalimatin sawāʾ*, a common and equitable word. The invitation begins with shared truth rather than immediate insult. Jews and Christians already affirmed revelation, prophecy, and accountability, so the Qur’an calls them back to the foundation shared by all prophets: worship Allah alone.

The common word is not a compromise that asks Muslims to abandon their creed. It is a clear basis for dialogue: one God, no partners, and no human beings treated as divine authorities.

### Worship Allah Alone

The first principle is exclusive worship. Allah is not one deity among many, nor a tribal possession. He is the Lord of all. Worship includes prayer, supplication, sacrifice, ultimate reliance, and obedience in matters of divine law.

The invitation also applies to Muslims. A person may verbally affirm tawḥīd while allowing wealth, nationalism, political power, or personal desire to rule his life. The common word becomes meaningful only when it shapes conduct.

### No Partners with Allah

The second principle rejects *shirk*, associating partners with Allah. The Qur’an honours prophets and righteous people but does not permit worship to be directed toward them. Jesus, Mary, angels, saints, and religious symbols remain created servants, however honoured.

This is why the discussion of Jesus in this sūrah leads to this invitation. The miraculous birth and signs of Jesus are affirmed, but they cannot justify giving him Allah’s exclusive rights.

### No Human Lords

The third principle rejects taking people as lords instead of Allah. This does not prohibit respecting parents, teachers, scholars, or lawful rulers. It condemns treating human beings as having an independent right to legislate worship and morality against Allah’s revelation, or obeying them in clear disobedience while considering their command superior.

When ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim heard this verse and said they did not worship their rabbis and monks, the Prophet ﷺ explained that they followed them in declaring lawful what Allah had forbidden and forbidden what Allah had made lawful. The report is recorded in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī. Authority becomes lordship when people surrender Allah’s prerogative to it.

### Dialogue without Compromise

The verse models principled interfaith dialogue. Muslims can identify shared values—worship of God, truthfulness, mercy, justice, and accountability—without pretending that theological differences do not exist. Courtesy does not require false agreement, and firmness does not require insult.

The Prophet ﷺ used this principle in his letters to rulers and religious communities. He invited them to a clear foundation and left their response to Allah.

### If They Turn Away

If they reject the invitation, the Prophet says, “Bear witness that we have submitted.” This is not a threat; it is a public clarification of the Muslim position. Muslims cannot compel acceptance, but they must not hide what they believe.

The closing declaration also protects believers from dependence on another community’s approval. Submission to Allah is the source of identity and guidance.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:64–65 and 29:46 on dialogue with the People of the Book.
- Qur’an 2:256 and 16:125 on no compulsion and inviting with wisdom.
- Qur’an 4:171 and 5:72–75 on Jesus, shirk, and exclusive worship of Allah.
- Qur’an 9:31 on taking religious authorities as lords through unlawful obedience.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, the Prophet’s letter to Heraclius containing the invitation to a common word; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, the report of ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim about obeying religious leaders in making lawful and unlawful.
- For *kalimah sawāʾ*, *shirk*, human lordship, and principled interfaith dialogue, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:64.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:65

> O People of the Book! Why do you argue about Abraham, while the Torah and the Gospel were not revealed until long after him? Do you not understand?

**Commentary:**

### An Anachronistic Dispute

The People of the Book disputed over Abraham, each community trying to claim him exclusively. Allah points out the obvious historical problem: the Torah and Gospel came after Abraham. He therefore could not have been a Jew or Christian in the later communal and textual sense.

The verse does not deny the spiritual relationship between Abraham and the later prophets. It rejects the attempt to define him through labels that arose after his lifetime. Abraham’s true identity is determined by his faith and submission, not by later group ownership.

### Abraham’s Place in Revelation

Abraham stands before the later divisions as a model of *tawḥīd*. He rejected idols, challenged inherited polytheism, and submitted to the Lord of the heavens and earth. Both Jews and Christians should honour him by following his devotion to Allah rather than by turning his legacy into a source of rivalry.

The Qur’an also teaches Muslims not to claim Abraham as a possession in a tribal sense. His legacy belongs to those who follow his faith and uphold his principles (3:68), regardless of ancestry.

### History and Reason

The rhetorical question “Do you not understand?” calls the disputants to use reason. Religious claims should not contradict basic historical sequence. If a label belongs to a community formed after Abraham, it cannot describe Abraham’s own religious identity.

The verse teaches a broader method: examine claims in their historical context, distinguish original revelation from later community identity, and do not let inherited slogans replace knowledge.

### Unity before Division

The prophets belong to one chain of revelation. Moses and Jesus came after Abraham and confirmed his call to worship Allah. Muhammad ﷺ came later and restored the same essential message. The unity lies in tawḥīd, while laws and community circumstances may differ.

This allows Muslims to honour Jewish and Christian prophets without accepting every later theological claim. It also calls Jews and Christians to examine whether their present claims preserve or contradict the monotheism of Abraham.

### The Danger of Possessive Religion

A community may become so attached to its historical identity that it forgets the truth its founder taught. Claiming Abraham while opposing his rejection of idols or his willingness to submit to Allah is a contradiction. Religious history should produce humility and obedience, not superiority over others.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:130–141 and 6:74–83 on Abraham’s faith and rejection of idolatry.
- Qur’an 3:67–68 on Abraham’s identity and those most entitled to claim connection with him.
- Qur’an 16:120–123 on Abraham as a nation, a *ḥanīf*, and a model of gratitude.
- Qur’an 42:13 on the shared religion entrusted to Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad.
- Qur’an 2:135 on the false demand to become Jew or Christian and the call to follow Abraham’s upright way.
- For chronology, *ḥanīfiyyah*, Abraham’s legacy, and the danger of communal ownership of revelation, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:65.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:66

> Here you are! You disputed about what you have ˹little˺ knowledge of,[140] but why do you now argue about what you have no knowledge of?[141] Allah knows and you do not know.

**Commentary:**

### The Limits of Knowledge

Allah exposes the inconsistency of the disputants. They argued about matters in which they had some knowledge, though even that knowledge was limited, and then argued about Abraham despite having no sound basis for assigning him later identities. The verse is a lesson in intellectual honesty: having partial information does not authorize certainty about what one does not know.

People often move from a small amount of evidence to a large conclusion. They know a name, a report, or a fragment of history and then construct an entire doctrine. Revelation teaches them to stop where the evidence stops.

### Questions Are Not the Problem

The Qur’an does not condemn every question or discussion. It condemns arguing without knowledge and using dispute to protect pride. Sincere questions can lead to understanding, while argumentative disputation seeks victory even after the evidence is clear.

A learner should distinguish between *baḥth*, serious investigation, and quarrelsome *jidāl*. The first seeks truth and is willing to revise an opinion; the second treats truth as secondary to winning.

### Allah Knows

The declaration “Allah knows and you do not know” is not an insult to human intelligence. It places human knowledge in its proper relationship to divine knowledge. Allah knows history, intention, unseen reality, and final consequences completely. Human beings know partially and often mistakenly.

This admission is the beginning of wisdom. A scholar who says “I do not know” may be more reliable than one who answers every question with confidence. The believer asks Allah to increase him in knowledge while remaining humble about what he lacks.

### Applying the Principle to Scripture

The verse has a special relevance to religious interpretation. A reader must not make the Qur’an say what he wants, claim knowledge of the unseen without evidence, or use an isolated phrase against the clear meaning of the whole Book. Context, language, authentic Sunnah, and qualified scholarship are necessary.

Disputes about Abraham, Jesus, or any prophet should be governed by revelation rather than by inherited propaganda. The same standard applies to disputes among Muslims.

### Humility in Public Debate

Public religious speech carries responsibility. A person may have an audience, credentials, or a confident style while lacking evidence. The believer verifies claims, identifies the strength of reports, and distinguishes established doctrine from interpretation.

When disagreement remains within legitimate scholarly boundaries, it should be expressed with courtesy. When a claim has no basis, it should be rejected clearly without humiliating the person who made it.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 17:36, 49:6, and 2:111 on not following or asserting what one does not know.
- Qur’an 12:76 and 20:114 on Allah’s superior knowledge and asking for increase in knowledge.
- Qur’an 16:43 and 21:7 on asking people of knowledge.
- Qur’an 58:11 on Allah raising those who believe and those given knowledge.
- Qur’an 29:46 on debating the People of the Book in the best manner.
- For *ḥujjah*, partial knowledge, *jidāl*, epistemic humility, and “Allah knows and you do not know,” see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:66.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:67

> Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness[142] and was not a polytheist.

**Commentary:**

### Abraham before Later Labels

Abraham lived before the Torah and Gospel and therefore was neither a Jew nor a Christian in the historical sense of those later communities. He was an upright submitter, a *ḥanīf Muslim*, and he did not associate partners with Allah. The verse defines his religion by worship and orientation, not by a later communal label.

“Muslim” here means one who submits to Allah. All prophets were Muslims in this essential sense, even though the final law and community of Muhammad ﷺ came later. The verse is not erasing history; it is identifying the unity of the prophetic faith.

### Hanīf: Turning Away from Idolatry

A *ḥanīf* is one who inclines away from falsehood toward truth. Abraham turned away from the idols of his people and from the authority of inherited custom when it contradicted revelation. His uprightness was active: he investigated, questioned, rejected false gods, and submitted to Allah.

The word also implies steadiness and balance. Abraham was not a polytheist, nor did he replace one idol with another. He directed worship and ultimate reliance to Allah alone.

### Not a Polytheist

The explicit negation of *shirk* is central. Abraham’s story demonstrates that tawḥīd requires rejecting all created beings as objects of worship. Stars, moon, sun, rulers, ancestors, and religious personalities cannot share Allah’s rights.

This does not mean that a person cannot respect creation or use created means. Abraham travelled, reasoned, spoke to people, and took practical steps, while his heart remained attached to Allah.

### Faith before Community Rivalry

The verse corrects the rivalry of communities that claim exclusive ownership of Abraham. The true connection is not inherited genealogy but shared faith. Muslims are asked to follow Abraham’s upright path, while Jews and Christians are invited to return to the monotheism represented by their prophetic ancestor.

A religious community should ask whether it is preserving its founder’s submission or merely claiming his name. The same warning applies to Muslims who invoke Abraham while tolerating idolatry of wealth, nation, or personality.

### Abraham as a Model

Abraham’s life includes sacrifice, migration, hospitality, prayer for his descendants, and opposition to tyranny. His *ḥanīfiyyah* is not withdrawal from the world. It is a pure orientation to Allah that shapes action in the world.

The Prophet ﷺ and the Muslim community inherit Abraham’s path through tawḥīd and obedience. This inheritance is a trust rather than a certificate of automatic salvation.

- Qur’an 2:135 and 22:78 on following Abraham’s *ḥanīf* way and the meaning of submission.
- Qur’an 42:13 on the shared essential religion of the prophets.
- Qur’an 4:125 on the excellence of following Abraham as a *ḥanīf*.
- For *ḥanīf*, *Islām*, *shirk*, and Abraham’s position before later communal labels, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:67.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:68

> Indeed, those who have the best claim to Abraham are his followers, this Prophet,[143] and the believers. And Allah is the Guardian of those who believe.

**Commentary:**

### The Best Claim to Abraham

The best claim to Abraham belongs to those who follow his faith, this Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and the believers. The word “best claim” indicates closeness through truth and obedience, not merely biological descent. A person can be related to Abraham and still oppose his message, while a believer from another people can be close to him in faith.

Muhammad ﷺ is connected to Abraham through the line of Ismāʿīl, and his message restores Abraham’s central call to worship Allah alone. The Muslim community follows Abraham not through racial pride but through *tawḥīd*, prayer, sacrifice, and obedience.

### Faith Is the True Inheritance

The verse corrects the idea that ancestry automatically transfers spiritual honour. Family descent may be a historical fact, but faith is the inheritance that Allah values. The Qur’an gives examples of the son of Nūḥ and the father of Abraham to show that relatives can reject truth.

A community that claims Abraham must demonstrate his qualities: rejecting idolatry, standing against falsehood, keeping covenants, showing hospitality, and submitting to Allah’s command even when sacrifice is difficult.

### The Prophet’s Connection

The mention of “this Prophet” honours Muhammad ﷺ as the final messenger within the Abrahamic chain. His message does not create a rival God or a separate Lord. It confirms the essential faith of Abraham and corrects distortions that had entered later communities.

Following the Prophet therefore includes following his mercy, honesty, patience, justice, and worship. It is not enough to use Abrahamic language while abandoning the prophetic character.

### Allah as Guardian

Allah is the Guardian, *walī*, of those who believe. His guardianship includes guidance, protection from misguidance, support in hardship, and care in the Hereafter. It does not mean believers are never tested. Abraham himself was tested through migration, family separation, and the command of sacrifice.

Divine guardianship gives the believer security that human approval cannot provide. If people dispute his religious identity, Allah knows the reality. If worldly allies fail, Allah remains the true Protector.

### Solidarity of Believers

The verse joins the Prophet ﷺ and believers together as the true followers of Abraham. This shows that faith creates a community across generations and cultures. Believers should support one another in truth and patience, while refusing to turn this solidarity into ethnic superiority.

The community’s claim to Abraham is renewed through its conduct. If it spreads injustice or worships worldly power, it has moved away from the path it claims.

- Qur’an 2:133 and 11:42–46 on lineage not guaranteeing faith.
- Qur’an 22:78 on the heritage of Abraham and the name “Muslims.”
- Qur’an 2:257 and 42:9 on Allah as the Guardian of believers.
- For *awlā*, following Abraham, Muhammad’s place in the prophetic chain, and Allah’s guardianship, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:68.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:69

> Some of the People of the Book wish to mislead you ˹believers˺. They mislead none but themselves, yet they fail to perceive it.

**Commentary:**

### A Warning about Malicious Influence

The verse warns that some among the People of the Book desired to mislead the believers. The word “some” is important. The Qur’an does not condemn every Jew or Christian, and it later praises upright people among the People of the Book (3:113–115). It identifies a particular attitude: an effort to turn believers away from faith and truth.

The warning applies beyond one historical group. Anyone who deliberately distorts religion, spreads doubt for personal gain, or leads people away from Allah falls under the moral principle.

### Misguidance Returns to the Misleader

“They mislead none but themselves” means that the ultimate harm returns to the one who chooses to mislead. He may influence others temporarily, but he accumulates responsibility for the deception. The victim may be forgiven or guided, while the manipulator carries the burden of his intention and impact.

The verse does not mean that no other person is harmed. False teaching can cause real damage. It means that the misleader cannot escape the consequences of his own choice by pretending that he only affected others.

### Failure to Perceive

The phrase “they fail to perceive it” points to moral blindness. A person may think he is preserving his group’s status while destroying his own integrity. He may imagine that suppressing truth protects his community, yet Allah sees the corruption produced by concealment and manipulation.

This is why spiritual diseases often become self-deception. Pride prevents a person from recognizing that he has become the very thing he warns others against.

### Protecting Faith with Knowledge

The proper response is not paranoia or hostility. Believers protect themselves through knowledge, sound scholarship, verification, and sincere companionship. The Qur’an commands them to verify reports (49:6), ask people of knowledge (16:43), and hold firmly to Allah’s guidance.

A believer should listen carefully and think critically, but not allow every doubt to uproot faith. He should distinguish a sincere question from an intentional attempt to confuse.

### Calling with Justice

The verse does not permit Muslims to return misguidance with injustice. Even when warned about hostile influence, Muslims must speak truthfully and deal fairly. The Qur’an praises those among the People of the Book who are upright, worship Allah, and do good.

A just response identifies falsehood without making false generalizations about an entire people.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:109 and 3:118–120 on hostility, envy, and protecting believers from harmful influence.
- Qur’an 3:113–115 on upright people among the People of the Book.
- Qur’an 49:6 and 16:43 on verification and asking people of knowledge.
- Qur’an 16:25 and 29:13 on the burden carried by those who mislead others.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 60:8 on justice and kindness despite religious difference.
- For *yudillūn*, self-deception, malicious influence, and the importance of the word “some,” see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:69.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:70

> O People of the Book! Why do you reject the signs of Allah while you bear witness ˹to their truth˺?

**Commentary:**

### Knowledge and Responsibility

The verse challenges those who reject Allah’s signs while bearing witness to their truth. Their knowledge increases responsibility. A person who has never encountered clear evidence is not like one who recognizes truth and rejects it because of pride, envy, or worldly interest.

The phrase “People of the Book” addresses those who received scripture and therefore possessed a framework for recognizing revelation. They knew that Allah sends prophets, commands justice, and calls to worship Him alone. The arrival of Muhammad ﷺ challenged their group expectations, but it did not invalidate the truth they already possessed.

### What Are Allah’s Signs?

Allah’s signs include Qur’anic revelation, the miracles of prophets, the evidence of creation, and the signs within a person’s own life. In context, they include the truth of Muhammad’s message and the continuity between the Qur’an and earlier revelation.

A sign is not merely a supernatural event. A clear moral command, a truthful messenger, and the coherence of revelation can all be signs. The believer asks Allah to make him receptive to them rather than proud of his own assumptions.

### Bearing Witness

To bear witness means to know and recognize. A person may carry scripture that points to truths about Allah and His messengers, then refuse the implications when a new messenger appears. This is a contradiction between intellectual recognition and moral submission.

The warning also applies to Muslims. A Muslim may bear witness that the Qur’an is true and still reject a command when it conflicts with wealth, status, desire, or tribal loyalty. Verbal testimony must be followed by obedience.

### A Call to Self-Examination

The verse is addressed publicly, but it should be heard personally. What truth do I recognize yet avoid? Do I acknowledge the prohibition of injustice while benefiting from it? Do I believe in accountability while concealing dishonesty? The Qur’an’s questions are meant to expose the distance between knowledge and action.

Self-examination is safer than using the verse only to condemn another group. The believer asks Allah to make knowledge a proof for him rather than against him.

### Justice in Addressing Communities

The Qur’an’s use of “some” in nearby verses and its praise of upright People of the Book show that this criticism is not a blanket condemnation. It addresses those who reject signs despite knowledge. Muslims must preserve this distinction and avoid attributing the behaviour of some to every member of a faith community.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:146 and 6:20 on recognizing truth while some conceal it.
- Qur’an 3:113–115 on righteous People of the Book.
- Qur’an 7:157 and 10:94 on recognition of the final message through earlier revelation.
- Qur’an 61:5 and 2:44 on knowledge rejected in practice.
- Qur’an 17:36 and 49:6 on responsibility for knowledge and verified claims.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:71

> O People of the Book! Why do you mix the truth with falsehood and hide the truth knowingly?

**Commentary:**

### Mixing Truth with Falsehood

The verse condemns *talbīs al-ḥaqq bi-al-bāṭil*, covering truth with falsehood. This may occur by adding a lie to a true statement, removing a necessary qualification, taking a verse out of context, or presenting a partial truth as the whole truth. The result confuses people because the falsehood is disguised by something genuine.

A person may also mix truth and falsehood in his own life: he speaks about worship while pursuing injustice, praises scripture while concealing its commands, or uses a true principle to justify a false conclusion. Truth must be accepted as a whole, not used selectively.

### Concealing Truth Knowingly

Hiding truth is more serious when the person knows it. The Arabic wording emphasizes deliberate concealment. Ignorance can be corrected through teaching; intentional concealment is a betrayal of trust and of the people who depend on accurate guidance.

The Qur’an commands those who possess knowledge to make it clear and not conceal it (2:159; 3:187). This applies to scholars, teachers, public officials, witnesses, media workers, and anyone who possesses information affecting another person’s rights.

### The Religious Context

In the historical context, the verse addresses scripture-bearers who knew principles of revelation yet concealed or distorted evidence relating to the Prophet ﷺ and tawḥīd. The criticism is directed at those who acted this way, not at every member of the People of the Book. The Qur’an’s praise of upright people among them must remain part of the reading.

The same warning applies to Muslims who distort verses, conceal uncomfortable truths, or use selective quotations to manipulate their audience. The standard of honesty is universal.

### The Ethics of Communication

Modern communication makes the warning especially relevant. Falsehood can be spread through edited clips, misleading headlines, selective statistics, fabricated quotations, and religious claims detached from context. A person may not have invented the original lie, yet still become responsible if he forwards it carelessly after having reason to doubt it.

The believer should verify, give context, distinguish fact from interpretation, and admit uncertainty. Silence may be appropriate when one lacks knowledge, but concealment to protect status or profit is condemned.

### Truth as a Trust

Knowledge is a trust from Allah. The trustworthy person does not change the message to please a patron, preserve a faction, or avoid criticism. He speaks with wisdom and proportion, avoiding both concealment and reckless disclosure that causes unjust harm.

Truthful communication includes correcting one’s own error. A person who discovers that he misled people should clarify the correction rather than quietly moving on.

- Qur’an 17:36 and 49:6 on verification and responsibility for information.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on truthful testimony even against one’s interests.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith “Whoever among you sees an evil…”; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, reports on truthful speech and the prohibition of false testimony.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:72

> A group among the People of the Book said ˹to one another˺, “Believe in what has been revealed to the believers in the morning and reject it in the evening, so they may abandon their faith.

**Commentary:**

### A Plan to Create Confusion

The verse describes a group that proposed a deliberate psychological tactic: pretend to believe in the morning, then reject the revelation in the evening, hoping that believers would become uncertain and abandon faith. The plan treats truth as a performance and other people’s faith as something to manipulate.

The word “group” is important. The Qur’an does not attribute this scheme to every Jew or Christian. It exposes a particular plan and teaches believers how to recognize manipulative behaviour in any community.

### Manufactured Doubt

The strategy depends on the assumption that people judge truth by the reaction of others. If respected or apparently informed people accept something and then quickly reject it, observers may conclude that the revelation is unreliable. The conspirators hoped to turn their own staged reversal into evidence against the Qur’an.

This remains a danger in public life. Coordinated misinformation, false endorsements, deceptive criticism, and manufactured scandals can be used to make people distrust truth. The believer should examine evidence rather than allowing another person’s performance to determine his convictions.

### Faith Requires Knowledge

The remedy is not blind stubbornness. Believers should learn the Qur’an, understand its evidence, and ask qualified people when confused. A faith built only on social approval can be shaken by every rumour. A faith built upon knowledge, worship, and sincere reflection is more stable.

The verse also warns religious teachers not to use doubt as a tactic. Deliberately confusing people to preserve influence is a serious betrayal of knowledge.

### Do Not Generalize

The Qur’an’s moral precision must be respected. It says “a group,” while elsewhere praising upright People of the Book who worship Allah and do good (3:113–115). Muslims should not transform this verse into a claim that all Jews or Christians are deceptive. The criticism concerns those who deliberately attempted to mislead believers.

The same standard requires Muslims to criticize their own manipulators. A Muslim organization, preacher, or government can use the same tactic and deserves the same moral warning.

### Protecting the Vulnerable

Manipulation often targets people with little access to information or who are already anxious. Teachers and leaders must not exploit this vulnerability. They should give evidence, acknowledge complexity, and allow people to ask questions without humiliation.

A believer should also avoid forwarding claims merely because they attack an opposing group. Falsehood remains false even when it appears to support Islam.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:73

> And only believe those who follow your religion.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Surely, ˹the only˺ true guidance is Allah’s guidance.” ˹They also said,˺ “Do not believe that someone will receive ˹revealed˺ knowledge similar to yours or argue against you before your Lord.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Indeed, all bounty is in the Hands of Allah— He grants it to whoever He wills. **Commentary:**

### Exclusive Gatekeeping

The verse records the speech of a group that wanted to restrict belief and knowledge to their own religious circle. They said, in effect, that truth should remain within their group and that no one outside it should receive revelation comparable to what they had received. This is religious gatekeeping driven by pride rather than submission.

The Qur’an does not condemn preserving authentic revelation or maintaining a sincere community. It condemns treating Allah’s guidance as private property and rejecting truth merely because it came through another people.

### Guidance Belongs to Allah

The Prophet ﷺ is instructed to respond that true guidance is Allah’s guidance. Guidance cannot be monopolized by ancestry, institution, language, or religious office. Allah chooses whom He wills to guide and sends revelation according to His wisdom.

This statement protects believers from being dependent upon the approval of religious elites. It also prevents Muslims from claiming that guidance is their personal possession. Whoever receives truth should respond with gratitude and humility.

### Revelation Is a Divine Gift

The objection that another person might receive revealed knowledge like theirs is answered by the declaration that all bounty is in Allah’s hands. Prophethood, scripture, understanding, and faith are divine gifts. Allah is not restricted by human expectations about who may be chosen.

The final revelation being sent through an Arab prophet was a test for communities who had become attached to ethnic or institutional privilege. The same test remains for anyone who rejects truth because the messenger, scholar, or community does not belong to his group.

### Allah’s Bounty Is Not Limited

Allah is *dhū al-faḍl al-ʿaẓīm*, the Lord of immense bounty. His gifts do not diminish when given to others. A person who envies another’s guidance behaves as though divine generosity were a limited resource. Gratitude, by contrast, asks Allah to increase guidance for oneself and others.

The verse also shows that Allah’s gift is paired with knowledge. He gives with perfect wisdom and knows who is sincere, who seeks truth, and who would misuse a privilege.

### Do Not Let Group Pride Judge Truth

The plan described in the previous verse attempted to make believers doubt the Qur’an. This verse exposes the motive: the group wanted to retain exclusive status and avoid being challenged before Allah. They feared that another community’s revelation would establish an argument against them.

Truth should not be evaluated according to who benefits from it. A believer accepts evidence even when it corrects his group, while a hypocrite rejects it even when he recognizes it because it threatens his standing.

### A Warning to Muslims

Muslims can repeat this error by claiming that learning, spiritual insight, or religious service belongs only to one ethnic group, school, family, or institution. No one can prevent Allah from granting understanding to a sincere servant. At the same time, a claim to knowledge must be tested by evidence, sound scholarship, and character.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:74

> He chooses whoever He wills to receive His mercy. And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty.

**Commentary:**

### Allah Chooses for Mercy

Allah chooses whom He wills to receive His mercy. His mercy includes faith, revelation, prophethood, understanding, forgiveness, and the opportunity to serve. The verse follows the discussion of people who resented the possibility that Allah would give revelation and honour to another community.

Divine choice is not injustice or arbitrary favouritism. Allah knows the visible and hidden realities of people and chooses according to perfect wisdom. Prophethood is not earned through family lobbying or worldly status, and guidance is not something a person can demand as a right over Allah.

### Mercy and Responsibility

Being chosen for mercy is an honour and a responsibility. A messenger receives revelation to convey it; a scholar receives knowledge to teach it; a believer receives faith to live by it and invite with wisdom. A gift that produces arrogance has not been understood properly.

The Qur’an presents previous communities as favoured when they upheld the covenant and censured when they betrayed it. Privilege without obedience is a test, not a guarantee.

### Infinite Bounty

Allah is the Lord of immense bounty, *dhū al-faḍl al-ʿaẓīm*. His gifts are not limited by the number of recipients. He can guide an entire community without reducing anyone else’s share, forgive many sinners, and grant many people knowledge and mercy.

This truth cures envy. When another person receives a blessing, the believer says, “This is from Allah,” asks for blessing, and seeks Allah’s bounty rather than wishing that the gift disappear.

### Choice and Human Effort

Allah’s choice does not make human effort irrelevant. People are commanded to seek knowledge, repent, pray, and respond to truth. The gift of guidance may reach a person through a teacher, a verse, a hardship, or a sincere conversation. The servant’s duty is to respond with humility and perseverance.

Nor should a person use divine choice as an excuse for prejudice. Humans cannot know the hidden basis of Allah’s choice, and they must judge one another by what is apparent with justice.

### The Final Revelation

In context, the verse prepares for the claim that Muhammad ﷺ was chosen as the final messenger. This choice does not diminish Moses, Jesus, or the earlier prophets. It completes the chain of revelation and extends guidance to humanity.

The correct response is not envy but recognition of Allah’s bounty. People who love Allah should welcome truth wherever Allah places it.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:75

> There are some among the People of the Book who, if entrusted with a stack of gold, will readily return it. Yet there are others who, if entrusted with a single coin, will not repay it unless you constantly demand it. This is because they say, “We are not accountable for ˹exploiting˺ the Gentiles.”[144] And ˹so˺ they attribute lies to Allah knowingly.

**Commentary:**

### Moral Differences within a Community

The verse begins with an important qualification: there are some among the People of the Book who honour a large trust, while others betray even a small one. The Qur’an refuses to treat a whole religious community as morally identical. Individuals are judged by their conduct, and the verse itself recognizes trustworthy and untrustworthy people.

This is a general Qur’anic principle. A person should not praise or condemn an entire people without justice. Trustworthiness and betrayal are individual and institutional behaviours, not qualities assigned to every member of an ethnicity or religion.

### The Large and the Small Trust

The contrast between a stack of gold and a single coin shows that integrity is not measured by the size of the temptation. Some people can be trusted with great wealth because they fear Allah; others betray a small amount because they do not recognize the moral seriousness of any trust.

A believer should return deposits, repay debts, preserve confidential information, and honour contracts whether the amount is large or small. Allah knows the value of a trust even when people consider it insignificant.

### False Religious Excuses

The untrustworthy claimed that they were not accountable for exploiting those outside their community. The Qur’an rejects this excuse as a lie attributed to Allah. Divine law does not permit fraud merely because the victim belongs to another religion, nation, class, or group.

Justice is universal. The Qur’an commands believers to fulfil trusts to their owners and to judge with justice (4:58), and it praises those who keep their covenant even when they dislike the other party (5:8). Religious difference never makes theft lawful.

### Trust and Faith

The Arabic word *amānah* includes property, promises, public office, knowledge, and responsibility. The Prophet ﷺ said that the signs of hypocrisy include betraying a trust, breaking a promise, and lying in speech, in the hadith recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

Trustworthiness is therefore part of faith’s visible character. A person cannot excuse financial fraud by claiming religious knowledge. His conduct contradicts the religion he claims to represent.

### Business Ethics

The verse has direct relevance to trade and finance. Agreements should specify rights clearly, debts should be recorded, and repayment should not depend upon the creditor’s ability to pressure the debtor. Exploiting a person who is poor, foreign, socially weak, or unfamiliar with a system is forbidden.

The creditor may demand his right, but he should avoid humiliation and show patience where appropriate. The debtor must not delay payment while able to repay.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:76

> Absolutely! Those who honour their trusts and shun evil—surely Allah loves those who are mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Commentary:**

### The True Standard

“Absolutely!” rejects the claim that exploitation is permissible against people outside one’s group. Allah loves those who fulfil their trusts and practise *taqwā*. The standard of righteousness is not communal advantage but faithfulness to Allah’s command.

The verse follows the distinction between trustworthy and untrustworthy People of the Book. It shows that religious communities are not judged as undifferentiated blocs. The righteous person is the one who honours obligations and avoids wrongdoing.

### Honouring Covenants and Trusts

The expression “honour their trusts” includes fulfilling promises, contracts, deposits, debts, oaths, and public responsibilities. It also includes preserving another person’s dignity and confidential information. A trust remains binding even when the other party is weak or different in faith.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that Muslims must fulfil conditions and agreements, and he warned that betrayal is a sign of hypocrisy. Trustworthiness is therefore a practical expression of faith, not merely a social courtesy.

### Shunning Evil

“Shun evil” includes avoiding *taqwā*’s opposite: exploiting, lying, cheating, oppressing, and persisting in sin. The verse does not limit evil to ritual violations. Financial dishonesty and broken commitments can be grave sins because they injure people and corrupt society.

Taqwā means placing a protective barrier between oneself and Allah’s displeasure. It includes carefulness before acting, repentance after falling, and a willingness to give people their rights.

### Allah’s Love

Allah’s love is an honour that brings acceptance, guidance, and reward. The Qur’an names qualities beloved to Him: repentance, purification, patience, excellence, justice, and reliance. This verse adds trustworthiness and taqwā.

A person seeking Allah’s love should ask not only whether an action appears religious but whether he fulfils the rights entrusted to him. A neglected family, unpaid worker, deceived customer, or betrayed friend can reveal a spiritual problem hidden behind public worship.

### Justice beyond Group Loyalty

The verse destroys the idea that one community may exploit another without accountability. The believer must be more, not less, careful with the rights of people who cannot easily defend themselves. The Prophet ﷺ and his Companions dealt with non-Muslims through contracts, trade, and justice.

This principle also applies within the Muslim community. A powerful Muslim who exploits a poor Muslim has not fulfilled the verse merely because both share a religious label.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:77

> Indeed, those who trade Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a fleeting gain will have no share in the Hereafter. Allah will neither speak to them, nor look at them, nor purify them on the Day of Judgment. And they will suffer a painful punishment.

**Commentary:**

### Selling a Covenant

The verse describes people who exchange Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a small worldly gain. The language of trade shows how lightly they value a sacred obligation. They treat truth, faithfulness, and promises as commodities to be sold whenever profit or status is offered.

Allah’s covenant includes belief, obedience, and the obligations revealed to a community. It also includes promises made in Allah’s name. A person who uses religious language to deceive people commits a double wrong: he harms the victim and abuses Allah’s name.

### The Danger of False Oaths

A false oath can make a dishonest claim appear trustworthy. A seller may swear that goods are sound, a witness may swear to a lie, or a leader may invoke Allah while secretly planning betrayal. The Prophet ﷺ counted false testimony among the gravest major sins and warned against using oaths as a tool of commerce.

An oath does not make falsehood true. The person who swears falsely may gain money for a moment, but he loses blessing and faces accountability before Allah.

### A Fleeting Gain

The gain is described as *thaman qalīl*, a small price, even if it appears large in worldly terms. Any amount is small when compared with the Hereafter. Wealth, office, praise, or temporary safety cannot compensate for the loss of Allah’s acceptance.

The phrase is also a call to examine decisions. Would a person sell honesty for a contract, betray a trust for promotion, or conceal truth for a donation? The Qur’an asks him to see the true scale of the exchange.

### Severe Spiritual Deprivation

Allah will neither speak to them, look at them, nor purify them on the Day of Judgement. Classical scholars explain these expressions as deprivation of honour, mercy, and purification—not that Allah lacks the ability to address or see them. Their punishment includes being excluded from the grace reserved for the righteous.

The warning is especially severe because they used the language of covenant and faith while deliberately betraying it. Hypocrisy turns sacred words into evidence against the speaker.

### The Prophetic Warning

The Prophet ﷺ mentioned among those whom Allah will not speak to or purify, and toward whom He will not look with mercy, an old adulterer, a lying king, and a poor person who is arrogant, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Other authentic reports mention the one who sells goods through false oaths. These hadith show that the form of the sin varies, but the disease is the same: knowingly using Allah’s name or blessings for deception and pride.

### Repentance and Restoration

The door of repentance remains open before death. The wrongdoer must stop the deception, repent sincerely, return unlawful gains, and repair the rights of those harmed. Verbal regret without restitution is incomplete when another person’s property or reputation was taken.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:78

> There are some among them who distort the Book with their tongues to make you think this ˹distortion˺ is from the Book—but it is not what the Book says. They say, “It is from Allah”—but it is not from Allah. And ˹so˺ they attribute lies to Allah knowingly.

**Commentary:**

### Distorting Revelation

The verse describes some people who distort the Book with their tongues so that listeners believe the distortion is revelation. This can involve changing words, mispronouncing or rearranging a recitation, inventing an interpretation, or presenting a personal opinion as though Allah commanded it.

The phrase “some among them” preserves the Qur’an’s precision. The criticism is directed at those who knowingly distort, not at every member of the People of the Book. The same moral warning applies to anyone who speaks falsely in Allah’s name.

### The Tongue as an Instrument

The tongue can convey truth or disguise falsehood. A skilled speaker may use pauses, emphasis, selective quotation, or confident delivery to make an invented claim sound sacred. The listener may assume that eloquence proves authenticity, but Allah judges the content and intention.

This is especially dangerous when the audience cannot check the source. Teachers and public speakers must distinguish revelation, prophetic hadith, scholarly interpretation, personal reflection, and uncertainty.

### Saying “Allah Says”

Attributing a statement to Allah is a grave responsibility. A person who says “Allah has forbidden” or “Allah guarantees” without evidence has spoken about Allah without knowledge. The Qur’an warns against declaring lawful and unlawful based on invention (16:116).

Religious authority must therefore be handled with humility. The trustworthy scholar cites evidence and says “I do not know” when necessary. The dishonest speaker uses divine attribution to strengthen personal power.

### Distortion by Interpretation

Distortion is not limited to changing letters. A person can preserve the words while emptying them of their meaning, ignoring context, or using a verse to reverse a clear principle of the Qur’an. Sound interpretation gathers the evidence, respects Arabic usage, and reads unclear passages through the clear foundations of revelation (3:7).

This does not mean that every difference of interpretation is distortion. Qualified scholars may disagree sincerely about secondary matters. The accusation of falsifying Allah’s Book should not be made simply because another Muslim reaches a different legitimate conclusion.

### Modern Responsibilities

The warning applies to books, sermons, social media, translations, and educational material. A person should verify quotations, correct errors, and avoid circulating claims merely because they support his preferred position. A shortened quote can distort as effectively as an altered word.

The listener also has responsibility. He should ask for sources, compare interpretations, and avoid treating every confident speaker as an authority.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:79

> It is not appropriate for someone who Allah has blessed with the Scripture, wisdom, and prophethood to say to people, “Worship me instead of Allah.” Rather, he would say, “Be devoted to the worship of your Lord ˹alone˺”—in accordance with what these prophets read in the Scripture and what they taught.

### Prophets Call to Allah

It is impossible for a true prophet to call people to worship him instead of Allah. The three gifts named in the verse establish the foundations of religious leadership. The phrase “be devoted to the worship of your Lord” is connected in the Arabic with becoming *rabbāniyyūn*: people devoted to the Lord, who nurture themselves and others through knowledge and worship. This concept joins education with spiritual formation. A teacher does not merely fill minds with information; he helps students become truthful, humble, disciplined, and beneficial.

The verse describes people who read the Scripture and teach it. Learning and teaching create a chain of transmission. The teacher must preserve the revealed message, distinguish it from personal opinion, and show how it applies with justice and mercy.

In the context of the sūrah, this verse corrects claims that Jesus invited people to worship him. Jesus’ own Qur’anic declaration is that Allah is his Lord and the Lord of his people (3:51). His miracles and titles do not change his status as a servant and messenger.

The principle applies to all later claims made in the name of prophets. The measure is the prophet’s revealed message, not the popularity of a later doctrine.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:80

> And he would never ask you to take angels and prophets as lords. Would he ask you to disbelieve after you have submitted?

**Commentary:**

### No Prophet Makes Rivals for Allah

A true prophet would never ask people to treat angels or prophets as lords instead of Allah. Angels and prophets are honoured servants, but they remain created and dependent. The worship and ultimate obedience due to Allah cannot be transferred to them.

This verse completes the previous one. A prophet who receives scripture, wisdom, and prophethood uses those gifts to establish tawḥīd. Any teaching that turns a messenger into an object of worship has departed from the messenger’s own mission.

### What Does “Lords” Mean?

The word *arbāb* is the plural of *rabb*. Taking someone as a lord can mean worshipping him, believing he controls the unseen independently, or granting him an unquestionable right to declare lawful and unlawful against Allah’s revelation. Respecting a scholar or obeying a lawful authority is not automatically this kind of lordship.

The danger appears when people surrender their conscience completely to a leader, institution, or religious class. The Qur’an calls them back to Allah as the only ultimate authority.

### Angels and Prophets

Angels are created from light and carry out Allah’s commands, but they do not share His divinity. Prophets receive revelation and convey it, but they do not own revelation or control forgiveness, provision, or the unseen independently. The Qur’an commands believers to honour both without worshipping either.

Jesus’ miraculous birth and signs therefore cannot justify worshipping him. Muhammad’s miracles and rank cannot justify worshipping him. Their greatness lies in servanthood and obedience.

### Submission and Disbelief

The rhetorical question asks whether a prophet would call people to disbelieve after they had submitted. The answer is no. True submission is incompatible with giving Allah’s rights to a created being. If a later doctrine conflicts with the prophet’s call to worship Allah alone, the doctrine must be rejected regardless of its popularity.

The verse also warns that religious exaggeration can gradually reverse faith. People may begin with respect, then turn respect into invocation, absolute authority, or worship. The believer keeps every created being in its proper place.

### Honour without Worship

Islam’s position is balanced: love prophets, follow them, send blessings upon Muhammad ﷺ, defend their honour, and learn from their examples—but direct prayer, sacrifice, ultimate reliance, and divine attributes to Allah alone.

This balance protects both truth and human dignity. It prevents religious leaders from becoming tyrants and prevents followers from abandoning responsibility to a human intermediary.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:64, 3:79–80, and 4:171 on tawḥīd and rejecting human or angelic lordship.
- Qur’an 9:31 on taking religious authorities as lords through unlawful obedience.
- Qur’an 5:72–75 on Jesus’ servanthood and his call to worship Allah.
- Qur’an 2:255 and 16:49–50 on angels’ created servanthood.
- Qur’an 18:110 and 41:6 on Muhammad ﷺ as a human messenger.
- Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, the report of ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim explaining religious authority that makes lawful and unlawful; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, the warning against exaggerating concerning prophets.
- For *arbāb*, honouring without worship, obedience, and the preservation of tawḥīd, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:80.
