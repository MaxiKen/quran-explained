## Verse 221

Verse 221 says: *"Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe; for a believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you. And do not marry your women to polytheistic men until they believe, for a believing slave-man is better than a free polytheist, even though he may look pleasant to you. They invite ˹you˺ to the Fire while Allah invites ˹you˺ to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace. He makes His revelations clear to the people so perhaps they will be mindful."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on marriage with the polytheists: believers may not marry idolatrous women, nor give their daughters to idolatrous men, until the idolaters believe; the faith of a believing servant outweighs the status and charm of a free idolater; the idolaters call towards the Fire while Allah calls towards Paradise and forgiveness.

The verse's first command: the prohibition of marrying the polytheist (the mushrik) women until they believe. Ibn Kathir: Allah forbade the marriage of the idolatresses until they believe — and notes that this ruling excludes the women of the People of the Scripture, who are permitted by the later verse (5:5) — the explanation of Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Makhul, al-Hasan, ad-Dahhak, Zayd ibn Aslam and ar-Rabi' ibn Anas and others. The verse's *mushrikat* — the polytheist women, the idolatresses — the marriage is barred until the faith: the marriage is not only the union of the bodies; the marriage is the union of the worships, the creed, the path. The verse's second: the marriage of the believing women to the polytheist men — do not give your daughters to the mushrik men until they believe. The prohibition is symmetrical: the wife's faith and the husband's faith — the marriage of the believer and the idolater is the contradiction of the worship.

"A believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you" — the verse's scale: not the beauty, not the lineage, not the freedom — the faith. A believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist though the polytheist pleases you, Ibn Kathir: this is the scale of the marriage: the faith outweighs the beauty, the wealth, the lineage. The verse's context: the companions at the beginning — some years Muslims were still looking forward to marry the idolatresses and marry their daughters to the idolaters out of the desire for their noble lineages; and the story of the believer who married a slave-woman and was reproached — and the verse was revealed about them. The verse's point: the pleasure of the appearance is not the standard; the standard is the faith, and the believing slave-woman is better than the free polytheist.

"They invite you to the Fire while Allah invites you to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace" — the verse's comparison: the spouses are invitations. The polytheist spouse invites to the Fire — the way of life, the creed, the worship, the house of the disbelief; and Allah invites to the Paradise and the forgiveness by His grace. Ibn Kathir quotes the hadith (in the Two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayrah): the life of this world is but a delight, and the best of the delights of this earthly life is the righteous wife. The verse's frame: the marriage is the invitation, and the believer chooses the invitation of the Paradise over the invitation of the Fire.

"In modern life" — the verse's message: the marriage is the companionship of the faith. The modern person is tempted by the appearance, the wealth, the charm, the status — and the verse says: the faith is the first measure; the believing partner, even of the humbler status, is better than the attractive non-believer — because the marriage is the invitation: the spouses invite each other to the path, the values, the worship, the life. The believer's marriage is the companion of the hereafter: not only the beauty of the wedding, but the invitation of the path. The verse's close: the revelations are clear so the people may be mindful: the choice of the spouse is the choice of the invitation — and the believer chooses the invitation of the Paradise by His grace.

## Verse 222

Verse 222 says: *"They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about menstruation. Say, "Beware of its harm! So keep away, and do not have intercourse with your wives during their monthly cycles until they are purified. When they purify themselves, then you may approach them in the manner specified by Allah. Surely Allah loves those who always turn to Him in repentance and those who purify themselves.""*

In simple terms, the verse answers the question of menstruation: it is a state of harm; husbands must abstain from intercourse during the monthly cycle, may resume when the wife is purified, and Allah loves the repentant and the purifiers.

"They ask you, O Prophet, about menstruation" — the verse's context: the companions asked about the monthly cycles, and the answer came. Ibn Kathir: Imam Ahmad recorded that Anas said the Jews used to avoid their menstruating women — they would not eat or even mingle with them in the house — and the companions asked about this matter and Allah revealed the verse. The verse's *mahid* — menstruation, the monthly cycle — and the answer: the *adha* — the harm, the ailment, the state of hurt — the verse's description of the menses: it is the harm, the harm for the marital intercourse.

"Beware of its harm! So keep away, and do not have intercourse with your wives during their monthly cycles until they are purified" — the verse's *fa'tazilu al-nisa'* — keep away from the women during the menses — the verse's command: the abstinence from the sexual intercourse during the cycle. Ibn Kathir: "keep away from women during menses" means avoid the sexual organ; the Prophet said, "Do everything you wish, except having sexual intercourse" (Muslim) — and this is why most of the scholars said it is allowed to fondle the wife except for the intercourse (during the menses). Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah reported from 'Abdullah ibn Sa'd al-Ansari: he asked the Prophet, "What am I allowed of my wife while she is having her menses?" — "What is above the Izar." The verse's *hatta yat-hurna* — until they are purified — the end of the cycle; and *fa-idha tat-hahrana* — when they have purified — the bath; the purification is the end of the menses and the bath — and then the approach.

"Then you may approach them in the manner specified by Allah" — the verse's *min haythu amarakum Allah* — from where Allah has commanded you: the approach is in the manner of the lawful — the verse: the normal marital relations, in the place of the lawful, not the forbidden. Ibn Kathir: Abu Razin, 'Ikrimah and ad-Dahhak and others said "when they are pure, and not during the menses." And the verse's close: "Surely Allah loves those who always turn to Him in repentance and those who purify themselves" — the verse's *tawwabin* — the constantly repentant; *muttahhirin* — the purifiers — the love of Allah for the repentant even for the repeated sin, and for the purified from the impurity. Ibn Kathir: those who purify themselves from the impurity and the filth that include having sexual intercourse with the wife during the menses and the anal sex.

In modern life, the verse is the divine law of the marital gentleness. The modern person treats the body as the object of the convenience, and the verse says: the marriage has the divine limits; the monthly cycle has the divine command — the abstinence in the period of the harm, the purification, the return. The verse's mercy: the Jews of the time avoided the woman completely — no eating, no mingling — and the verse gives the middle: the companionship continues, the love continues, only the intercourse is withheld; "do everything you wish, except the intercourse." The verse's message: the marital law is not the law of the harshness or the taboo; it is the law of the divine purity — the repentance and the purification — and the Lord loves the repentant and the purifiers.

## Verse 223

Verse 223 says: *"Your wives are like farmland for you, so approach them ˹consensually˺ as you please. And send forth something good for yourselves. Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him. And give good news to the believers."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the law of the marital approach: the wives are the tillage of the husbands, who may approach in the lawful manner they wish; the believers are to send ahead good deeds, be mindful of Allah, and receive the good news.

"Your wives are like farmland for you" — the verse's *harth* — the farmland, the tillage, the field — the image of the marriage: the wife is the field, the husband is the one who approaches the field — the marital relation as the sowing: the marriage is not the play; the marriage is the field — the seed, the harvest, the offspring. The verse's frame: the marriage's purpose — the lawful pleasure within the divine limits, and the harvest of the offspring.

"So approach them consensually as you please" — the verse's *fa'tu harthakum anna shi'tum* — so go to your tillage as you will, in the manner you wish. The verse's point: the approach is permitted in the usual manner — the front, in the lawful place; Ibn Kathir (from Ibn Jurayj's report): the Prophet said "in the front or the back" — and the commentators: the approach is in the law's limits — the front (the vaginal), not the forbidden (the anal, the menses) — per the understanding of Ibn Abbas (in Tanwir al-Miqbas: "fear Allah regarding penetrating your wives in their anus or having sex with them during menstruation"). The verse's *anna shi'tum* — as you will — the manner of the lawful: the freedom within the divine bounds.

"And send forth something good for yourselves" — the verse's *qaddimu li-anfusikum* — send forward something for yourselves: the good deed — the children (the righteous offspring), the kindness, the remembrance — the marriage's fare for the hereafter. Ibn Kathir (Tanwir): send forth good — righteous children. The verse's frame: the marriage is not only the present; the marriage is the future — the soul's provision: the righteous child, the mutual kindness, the obedience — the send-forth.

"Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him. And give good news to the believers" — the verse's close: the taqwa and the meeting — the marriage is under the mindfulness, and the meeting with the Lord is the horizon; and the good news to the believers — the glad tidings of the Paradise for the ones who observe the limits. Ibn Kathir (with Ibn Abbas, Tanwir): the good news — the believers who avoid the anal and the menses — theirs is the Paradise.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the marital tenderness and the marital purpose. The modern marriage is often the law of the individual pleasure and the power; and the verse's image: the wife is the field — the partnership of the sowing and the harvest, not the object of the consumption; and the husband's approach is the lawful tenderness, within the limits, in the consensuality. The verse's message: the marriage is the field: the kind approach, the good send-forth — the children, the kindness, the faith — the marriage's harvest is the soul's provision. And the close: the mindfulness and the meeting — the believers' marriage is the marriage under the Lord, and the good news of the believers.

## Verse 224

Verse 224 says: *"Do not use Allah's Name in your oaths as an excuse for not doing good, not guarding against evil, or not making peace between people. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the oaths: Allah's Name must not be turned into a pretext for avoiding the good, the guarding against evil, or the reconciliation of people — for Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

"Use Allah's Name in your oaths as an excuse" — the verse's *la taj'alu Allah 'urdatan li-aymanikum* — do not make Allah a hindrance, an excuse, an object of your oaths: the oath by Allah turned into the barrier — "I swore by Allah, so I will not do it." Ibn Kathir: Allah commands — do not implement your vows in Allah's Name to refrain from pious acts and severing the relations with the relatives, if you swear to abandon such causes. Ibn Abbas (via 'Ali ibn Abi Talhah): the meaning — do not vow to refrain from doing good works; pay the kaffarah and perform the better deed. The verse's *'urda* — the shield, the pretext, the obstacle: the oath becomes the shield against the good — "I have sworn" as the excuse for the neglect of the good.

"Doing good, not guarding against evil, or not making peace between people" — the verse's three: the *birr* — the good, the piety, the kindness; the *taqwa* — the guarding, the fear; the *islah* — the reconciliation of the people. The verse's context: Tanwir al-Miqbas — the verse was revealed about 'Abdullah ibn Rawahah who swore not to show kindness to his sister and her husband, not to speak with them or make peace between them — and Allah warned him: do not make Allah the reason. The verse's sin: the oath against the good, the oath against the reconciliation, the oath against the peace — the oath is not the license of the harm; the oath is the breach of the divine command.

The verse's remedy: the oath that blocks the good is broken — the kaffarah and the better deed. Ibn Kathir: the hadith (Muslim, Ahmad): "Whoever makes a vow and then finds what is better than his vow should break his vow, pay the kaffarah and perform the better deed"; and "It is more sinful to Allah that one of you implements his vow regarding (severing the relations with) his relatives than (breaking his promise and) paying the kaffarah that Allah has required in such cases." And the hadith (Abu Dawud): "No vow (is valid) to disobey the Lord, cut the relations of the womb or dispose of what you do not own."

"And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing" — the verse's close: the Heard and the Knowing — the oath heard by the Lord; the intention known by the Lord. The verse's frame: the oath is not the escape; the Lord heard the oath and knows the motive — and the oath against the good is the oath against the command of the One who hears and knows.

In modern life, the verse is the warning of the oath-as-excuse. The modern person makes the promises, the vows, the "I swear" — and the verse says: the oath is not the shield of the neglect. The modern forms: "I swore I would never speak to him" — and the reconciliation is blocked; "I promised myself I would never give" — and the charity is blocked; "I gave my word" — and the good is abandoned. The verse's message: the oath cannot be the excuse for the harm; the good, the taqwa, the reconciliation are above the oath; and the oath that blocks the good is broken — with the kaffarah and the better deed. The believer's oath is the servant of the good, not the barrier: the Lord who hears the oath and knows the intention is the One who accepts the better deed.

## Verse 225

Verse 225 says: *"Allah will not hold you accountable for unintentional oaths, but for what you intended in your hearts. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing."*

In simple terms, the verse distinguishes the oaths: Allah will not hold the believers for the idle and unintentional oaths, but will hold them for the oaths deliberately intended in their hearts — He is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

"Allah will not hold you accountable for unintentional oaths" — the verse's *laghw* — the unintentional, the inadvertent, the idle: the oaths of the tongue's habit, the careless utterance, the "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah" of the everyday talk. Ibn Kathir: Allah does not punish or hold you accountable for the laghw vows that you make — the unintentional vows are just like the habitual statements that the tongue repeats without really intending them — for instance (in the Two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayrah): whoever swore and unintentionally mentioned al-Lat and al-'Uzza in his vow, should then say "There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah"; and the laghw includes what the man says in his house, such as "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah"; Ibn Abi Hatim from Ibn Abbas — the laghw vow includes vowing while angry.

"But for what you intended in your hearts" — the verse's *wa lakin yu'akhidhukum bima kasabat qulubukum* — but He will hold you accountable for what your hearts have earned: the deliberate oath. Ibn Kathir: Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and several others said this means swearing about a matter while knowing that he is lying — the deliberate lie; and the parallel of 5:89 — "He will punish you for your deliberate oaths." The verse's line: the unintentional is forgiven; the intended is accounted. The verse's *kasaba* — earned — the heart's earning: the intention, the deliberation, the knowing falsehood — the heart earns the accountability.

The verse's *kasabat qulubukum* — the hearts have earned — the verse's principle: the accountability follows the intention. Ibn Kathir (with 2:286): although Allah will question His servants and judge them, He will only punish for what one is able to protect himself from; as for what one cannot protect himself from — the passing thoughts, the inner suggestions — they will not be punished; to dislike the evil thoughts that cross one's mind is part of the faith.

"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing" — the verse's close: the *Ghafur* — the All-Forgiving — the forgiveness of the laghw, the unintentional, the careless; the *Halim* — the Most Forbearing, the Clement — the forbearance toward the servants who slip. Ibn Kathir: He is Oft-Forgiving to His servants and Most Forbearing. The verse's frame: the accidental oath is forgiven; the intended is judged; and the Lord's character is the forgiveness and the forbearance — the believer trusts the mercy of the Lord of the oaths.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the tongue and the heart. The modern person speaks: the swear words, the casual oaths, the "I swear to God" of the routine — and the verse says: the Lord does not hold you for the careless tongue, but He holds you for the heart's intent — the deliberate oath, the knowing lie, the heart consumed. The verse's mercy: the everyday slips of the tongue are under the Lord's forbearance; the verse's seriousness: the deliberate oath is under the Lord's accountability. The believer's discipline: the tongue is guarded, the oath is rare, and the heart is pure; and the believer's trust: the All-Forgiving, the Most Forbearing — the Lord who forgives the careless and accounts the intended, and who is the mercy of the repentant.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 226

Verse 226 says: *"Those who swear not to have intercourse with their wives must wait for four months. If they change their mind, then Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the oath of abstention (ila): the man who swears not to approach his wife is given four months; if he returns to her, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

The verse's *ila* — the oath of the husband swearing not to have intercourse with his wife, the abstention, the abandonment of the wife by the oath. Ibn Kathir: if a man swears not to have intercourse with his wife for a period — less than four months: he may wait the end of the period and then have intercourse; the wife must be patient and cannot demand the return during this period; as is established in the Two Sahihs from 'A'ishah: the Prophet made ila' (swore to abstain) from his wives for a month, and came down after twenty-nine nights — "The month is twenty-nine" (also from 'Umar, similar). If the period exceeds four months: at the end of the four months the wife may demand — either the husband returns (fi'a — has intercourse) or he divorces; and the judge forces him to one or the other, so that the wife is not harmed.

"Must wait for four months" — the verse's *tarabbus arba'at ashhur* — the waiting of four months: the limit of the oath. Ibn Kathir: the husband waits four months from the time of the vow and then ends the ila if the vow was for four or more months and is required to either return or divorce. The verse's purpose: the four months is the limit of the coercion — the wife is not left in the limbo of the indefinite oath; the limit forces the decision: the return or the release. Maududi: for this kind of abnormal discord and estrangement God has fixed a limit of four months during which the spouses are required either to settle their difference, or to break the tie of wedlock so that each becomes free to contract marriage with someone with whom a harmonious matrimonial relationship appears more likely.

"If they change their mind, then Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" — the verse's *fa'in fa'u* — if they return — the return to the marital relations, the repeal of the oath. Ibn Kathir (Ibn Abbas, Masruq, ash-Sha'bi, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and others): the fa' — the return — is a metaphor for the intercourse. The verse's *fainna Allah Ghafur Rahim* — Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — the forgiveness for what has passed of the neglect of the wife's right; the mercy of the wives. Ibn Kathir: the verse's ending — Allah is Forgiving of what has passed of the shortcoming in respect of the wives because of the oath, and Merciful to them.

In modern life, the verse is the law against the silent cruelty of the marriage. The modern marriage has its forms of the cold abandonment: the spouse withheld, the love withdrawn, the "punishment" of the silence, the indefinite neglect. The verse says: the oath can be the cruelty, and the limit is fixed — four months — and then the decision: the return to the marriage or the release of the wife. The verse's message: the marriage is not the weapon; a spouse cannot be held in the limbo of the indefinite coldness; the vow is limited, the return is the mercy, and the Lord is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — for the wife, and for the husband who returns.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 227

Verse 227 says: *"But if they settle on divorce, then Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the alternative: if the couple settles on divorce rather than the return, then Allah is indeed All-Hearing and All-Knowing of the matter.

The verse is the second half of the ila ruling of 2:226: the oath of abstention ends at the four months — and the husband chooses: the return (fi'a) or the divorce. The verse's *in 'azamu al-talaq* — if they decide upon the divorce — the resolution, the intention, the firm decision of the divorce. Ibn Kathir: "if they decide upon divorce" indicates that divorce does not occur by merely passing the four-month mark during the ila; Malik reported from Nafi' that Ibn 'Umar said: if the man swears to ila from his wife, then divorce does not occur automatically even after the four months have passed — when he stops at the four months mark, he should either divorce or return (al-Bukhari); Ibn Jarir reported from Suhayl ibn Abi Salih from his father: he asked twelve Companions about the man who does ila with his wife — they all stated that he does not have to do anything until the four months have passed and then has to either retain or divorce her (also ad-Daraqutni); reported from 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali, Abu ad-Darda', 'A'ishah, Ibn 'Umar and Ibn 'Abbas.

The verse's point: the divorce is the decision, not the automatic result of the time. The verse's *'azm* — the determination, the firm resolve — the decision is the matter of the deliberate will, not the mechanical passage of the months. The verse's frame: the ila — the oath; the four months — the limit; the return or the divorce — the two options; and the divorce — the decision — is the deliberate.

"And Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing" — the verse's close: the Hearing and the Knowing — the Lord heard the oath and the intention, the arguments and the harms; the Lord knows the divorce's motive and the marriage's condition. The verse's *Sam'i' 'Alim* — the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing — the same close as 2:224 (the oaths): the Lord of the oaths is the Lord of the divorce — He heard the vow and knows the decision; the marriage's breaking is before the One who hears and knows. Ibn Kathir: the ayah — the All-Hearing of the statements, the All-Knowing of the intentions.

In modern life, the verse is the seriousness of the marriage's end. The modern divorce is often the impulsive, the angry, the sudden — and the verse says: the divorce is the decision, not the accident; the months of the limit, and the deliberate resolution. The verse's message: the marriage is not ended by the careless oath or the automatic time; the marriage is ended by the deliberate decision before the One who is All-Hearing, All-Knowing — the One who heard the marriage's vows and knows the divorce's motives. The believer's marriage and the believer's divorce are both before the Hearing and Knowing Lord: the oath sworn, the decision made, the accounting of the Lord.

## Verse 228

Verse 228 says: *"Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can re-marry˺. It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation. Women have rights similar to those of men equitably, although men have a degree ˹of responsibility˺ above them. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise."*

In simple terms, the verse rules the divorced woman's waiting: three cycles; no concealment of the womb's contents for the believer; the husband's better right of return for the reconciliation; the rights are mutual and the men have a degree of responsibility — Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

"Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles before they can re-marry" — the verse's *quru'* — the three cycles, the monthly periods: the waiting period of the divorced woman — the iddah. Ibn Kathir: the quru' — the menstruation periods; the verse's waiting: the divorced woman waits the three cycles — the purpose: the certainty of the womb (the absence of the pregnancy), and the period of the reconsideration. The verse's *yatarabbasna bi-anfusihinna* — they keep themselves in the wait — the waiting of the divorcee: the three cycles, the purity, the basis of the remarriage.

"It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they truly believe in Allah and the Last Day" — the verse's prohibition: the concealment of the pregnancy — the womb's creation. The verse's frame: the waiting's purpose is the clarity of the womb; and the wife's concealment of the pregnancy (the false claim of the cycle's end, the hiding of the child's existence) is the breach of the faith. Ibn Kathir: the wife cannot conceal the pregnancy or the menses — the concealment defeats the purpose of the iddah, and harms the child, the husband, the lineage; and the verse's condition: if they believe in Allah and the Last Day — the belief is the motive of the truth.

"And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation" — the verse's *bu'ulatuhunna ahaqqu bi-raddihinna* — their husbands have the better right to take them back in that period: the husband may take back the wife within the iddah if the divorce is the first or second (the revocable divorce) — if he desires the reconciliation. Ibn Kathir: the husband's right is the reconciliation's door: the divorce is not final until the third; the husband has the right during the waiting to return — on the condition of the *islah* — the reconciliation. The verse's *islah* — the making right: the return is the return of the repair, not the harm.

"Women have rights similar to those of men equitably, although men have a degree of responsibility above them" — the verse's balance: the *mithl* — the similar — the rights of the wife over the husband (the maintenance, the companionship, the kindness) similar to the rights of the husband over her (the reputation, the obedience, the conduct) — the reciprocity of the marriage; and the *darajah* — the degree — the husband's degree of the responsibility. Ibn Kathir: the wife has certain rights on her husband just as he has certain rights on her (reported by Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim). Ma'arif: the men's degree — physically, in mannerism, status, obedience (of women to them), spending, taking care of the affairs, and in general in this life and the Hereafter; Ibn Abbas (Tanwir): the degree — in the reasoning faculty, inheritance, blood money, as witnesses in court. The verse's frame: the equality of the rights and the degree of the responsibility — the marriage's balance is not the tyranny of the degree but the reciprocity of the rights and the degree of the responsibility, before the Almighty, the All-Wise.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the divorce's dignity and the marriage's justice. The modern divorce is often the chaos: the wife's status unclear, the husband's rights and duties vague, the fairness forgotten. The verse gives the structure: the waiting of the three cycles — the clarity of the womb and the space of the reconsideration; the truth of the pregnancy — no concealment against the faith; the husband's right of the return — the door of the reconciliation; and the balance: the rights similar, and the degree of the responsibility — the reciprocity and the responsibility, not the mastery and the servitude. The verse's message: the divorce is the law's order: the waiting, the truth, the reconciliation, and the justice — before the Almighty, the All-Wise.

## Verse 229

Verse 229 says: *"Divorce may be retracted twice, then the husband must retain ˹his wife˺ with honour or separate ˹from her˺ with grace. It is not lawful for husbands to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives, unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah. So if you fear they will not be able to keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame if the wife compensates the husband to obtain divorce. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers."*

In simple terms, the verse sets the boundaries of the divorce: two retractions, then the honour of retention or the grace of release; the dowry is not recoverable except in the khul' — the wife's compensation when the couple fears the limits; and the transgressors of the limits are the wrongdoers.

"Divorce may be retracted twice" — the verse's *al-talaq marratani* — the divorce is twice: the revocable divorces — the first and the second. Ibn Kathir: the man used to have the right to take back his wife even if he had divorced her thrice; Allah abrogated this and said the divorce is twice. The verse's context (Ibn Abi Hatim, from 'Urwah): a man said to his wife, "I will neither divorce you nor take you back" — "I will divorce you and when your term of iddah nears its end, I will take you back" — the harm of the endless retraction; and the verse was revealed: the divorce is twice. Ibn Jarir: the ayah was revealed about Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas and his wife Habibah bint 'Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul.

"Then the husband must retain his wife with honour or separate from her with grace" — the verse's *imsak bi-ma'ruf aw tasrih bi-ihsan* — the retention with the good (the kindness, the honor) or the release with the excellence (the grace): the two options after the second divorce. Ibn Kathir: if you divorce her once or twice, you have the choice to take her back as long as she is in her iddah, intending to be kind to her and to mend differences; otherwise await the end of her iddah, when the divorce becomes final, and let her go in peace without the harm or the injustice. The verse's frame: the law's minimum (the compensation is the duty) and the excellence's standard (the good-doers' honor).

In modern life, the verse is the law of the honorable parting. The modern breakup is often the cold: the dismissal, the silent end, the "goodbye" without the dignity. The verse says: the end can be the honor: the compensation — the gift of the farewell — the rich by the ability, the poor by the ability; and the obligation is on the good-doers — the kindness of the parting is the mark of the good. The verse's message: the divorce's honor is not only the letter of the law but the gift of the grace: the woman is not left with nothing and no word; the ending has the dignity — the compensation, the kindness, the good.

## Verse 237

Verse 237 says: *"And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry, pay half of the dowry, unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full. Graciousness is closer to righteousness. And do not forget kindness among yourselves. Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the half-dowry: when the divorce comes before the consummation but after the dowry was fixed, half is due, unless the wife waives or the husband pays the whole — the forgiveness is nearer to the righteousness; the kindness is not forgotten.

"And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry, pay half of the dowry" — the verse's case: the dowry was fixed and the marriage was not consummated — and the divorce: the half of the fixed dowry. Ibn Kathir: this honorable ayah requires the husband to relinquish half of the appointed mahr if he divorces his wife before the marriage is consummated — the giving away of half of the bridal-money in this case is the agreed practice according to the scholars. The verse's *nisf ma faradtum* — the half of what you appointed: the fixed dowry and the half — the divorce's cost: the husband pays the half, the wife's right of the half.

"Unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full" — the verse's two releases: the wife's forgiveness (the waiver — she forgives the half, or the whole) or the husband's forgiveness (the payment in full — the one in whose hand is the marriage knot pays the whole). Ibn Kathir: the wife may remit, and the husband may pay the full. The verse's phrase — the one in whose hand is the marriage tie, per the hadith (Ibn Marduwyah, chosen by Ibn Jarir): the husband is the one who holds the marriage tie; the wife's wali is not allowed to give away any of her rightful dues without her permission, especially the dowry.

"Graciousness is closer to righteousness" — the verse's *an ta'fuq aqrab li al-taqwa* — that you forgive (the waiver, the full payment) is nearer to the taqwa: the forgiveness — the wife's waiver, the husband's full payment — is nearer to the righteousness. Ibn Kathir (ash-Sha'bi and several scholars): the one who forgives is nearer to the taqwa. Ma'arif: the forgiveness symbolically indicates that the severance of the bond of the marriage was also done with magnanimity and good grace — the objective of the Shari'ah — the forgiveness could come from the woman or the man. The verse's scale: the law's minimum (the half) and the excellence (the waiver/full) — the forgiveness is nearer to the righteousness.

"And do not forget kindness among yourselves" — the verse's *la tansaw al-fadl baynakum* — do not forget the favor, the kindness, the generosity among yourselves: the marriage's end is not the end of the decency; the couple's parting keeps the memory of the kindness. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *fadl* — the generosity, the kindness — do not forget it between you; the marriage's kindness remains in the parting.

"Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do" — the verse's close: the *Basar* — the All-Seeing — the Lord sees the parting's generosity, the waiver, the full payment, the kindness, the greed. The verse's frame: the divorce's settlement is before the All-Seeing — the one who sees the forgiveness and the one who sees the withholding.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the fair ending. The modern separation often fights over the money: the dowry's battle, the "take back", the greed's ledger. The verse's structure: the half — the law's fair minimum; the waiver — the wife's grace; the full — the husband's grace; the forgiveness nearer to the righteousness; and the kindness not forgotten. The verse's message: the divorce's money is settled by the law's fairness and the couple's grace — the woman's right is the half, and the grace above the half is the righteousness; the man's right is the half's return, and the full payment is the grace. The parting's kindness is not forgotten, and the All-Seeing sees the generosity and the greed of the settlement.

## Verse 238

Verse 238 says: *"Observe the ˹five obligatory˺ prayers—especially the middle prayer—and stand in true devotion to Allah."*

In simple terms, the verse commands the prayers: the five prayers are to be guarded, especially the middle prayer, and the standing before Allah is with the true devotion.

"Observe the five obligatory prayers" — the verse's *hafizu 'ala al-salawat* — guard, preserve, observe the prayers: the command of the preservation — the prayers' times, the prayers' conditions, the prayers' constancy. Ibn Kathir: the ayah commands the preservation of the prayers — the five daily prayers, the times, the perfection. The verse's *hifz* — the guarding — the prayer is not only the performance but the preservation: the watch of the time, the conditions, the quality.

"Especially the middle prayer" — the verse's *al-salat al-wusta* — the middle prayer. Ibn Kathir: at-Tirmidhi reported from Ibn Mas'ud — the Prophet said, "The 'Asr prayer is the middle prayer" (at-Tirmidhi: hasan sahih); Muslim reported from the hadith of the battle of the ditch (al-Khandaq): "They (disbelievers) busied us from performing the middle prayer, the 'Asr prayer"; and the authentic hadith (Ibn 'Umar): "Whoever misses the 'Asr prayer, it is as if he lost his family and his wealth." The verse's *wusta* — the middle — the commentators: the 'Asr (the majority, per the hadiths) — the middle of the five, and the emphasized. The verse's note: the middle prayer's emphasis — the prayer of the middle — the guarding of it is the sign of the constancy.

"And stand in true devotion to Allah" — the verse's *qum li Allah qanitin* — stand for Allah in obedience, in devotion, in humble obedience — the *qunut* — the standing of the devotion, the obedience, the humility. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *qanitin* — the obedient, the devoted, and (in some readings) the standing in the prayer. The verse's frame: the prayer's outward (the standing, the times) and the prayer's inward (the qunut — the devotion, the humility, the obedience) — the prayer is the standing before the Lord with the true devotion.

In modern life, the verse is the standard of the prayer's priority. The modern life is the schedule of the busy: the work, the meetings, the tasks — and the prayer is the first to be dropped. The verse says: guard the prayers — the watch of the times, the constancy of the schedule; especially the middle — the prayer of the middle's emphasis; and the stand with the true devotion — the quality of the presence, not only the motion of the body. The believer's life: the prayer is the anchor of the day — the five, and the middle, and the standing with the heart — the true devotion before the Lord. The verse's message: the prayer is the preserved; the middle is the emphasized; and the stand is the devotion — the believer's day is ordered by the prayer, not the prayer by the day.

## Verse 239

Verse 239 says: *"If you are in danger, pray on foot or while riding. But when you are safe, ˹take time to˺ remember Allah for teaching you what you did not know."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the prayer in the danger: the prayer is offered on foot or riding when the believer fears; when safe, the believer remembers Allah in the manner He taught.

"If you are in danger, pray on foot or while riding" — the verse's *fa-in khiftum fa-rijalan aw rukbanan* — if you fear, then on foot or riding: the prayer in the danger — the prayer is not abandoned in the fear. Ibn Kathir: after Allah commanded His servants to perform the prayer perfectly and emphasized this commandment, He mentioned the situation where the person might not be able to perform the prayer perfectly, during battle and combat — pray in the appropriate manner under these circumstances, whether on foot or riding and whether facing the qiblah or otherwise. Ibn Kathir (Imam Malik from Nafi' from Ibn 'Umar): if there is intense fear, pray on foot, riding, facing the qiblah and otherwise — the description of the fear prayer — al-Bukhari and Muslim. Al-Jalalayn (Ibn Abbas, Tanwir): the fear — the enemy, the torrent, the predatory animal — the prayer on foot or riding. The verse's point: the prayer is not suspended in the danger; the prayer is adapted: walking, riding, the direction, the posture — the prayer's core (the remembrance) continues.

"But when you are safe, take time to remember Allah for teaching you what you did not know" — the verse's *fa-idha amintum fa-udhkuru Allah kama 'allamakum ma lam takunu ta'lamun* — when you are secure, then remember Allah as He has taught you what you did not know: the return to the full prayer — the normal, the complete, the taught — at the safety. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *kama 'allamakum* — the manner He has taught you — the full prayer, the complete rites; the safety's return to the perfect prayer. The verse's frame: the danger's adaptation, the safety's completion — the prayer is the constant: adapted in the danger, perfected in the safety.

The verse's close — the gratitude: *kama 'allamakum ma lam takunu ta'lamun* — as He has taught you what you did not know: the prayer's knowledge, the manner, the rites — the teaching of the Lord — and the remembrance is the gratitude: the believer prays because the Lord taught; the believer remembers because the Lord taught the remembrance. The verse's frame: the prayer's knowledge is the divine gift: the one who did not know the prayer, the one who was astray — now knows — and the prayer is the gratitude of the teaching.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the faith in the emergency. The modern person says: "in the emergency I cannot pray, I cannot worship, I cannot think of the faith." The verse says: the danger is not the suspension — the prayer in the fear, on foot, riding; the faith's core survives the emergency. And the modern lesson: the safety is the completion: the return to the full worship when safe — the danger's reduced prayer is not the replacement but the adaptation; the safety's full prayer is the taught. The verse's message: the Lord taught you what you did not know — the prayer's knowledge is the gift — and the believer remembers: in the danger, the adapted prayer; in the safety, the complete prayer — the remembrance in both.

## Verse 240

Verse 240 says: *"Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year's maintenance without forcing them out. But if they choose to leave, you are not accountable for what they reasonably decide for themselves. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise."*

In simple terms, the verse instructs the bequest for the widows: a year's maintenance and residence without the expulsion; if the widow leaves, the family is not accountable for her reasonable decisions — and Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

"Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year's maintenance without forcing them out" — the verse's bequest: the year's maintenance — the provision and the residence for the widow — without forcing her out: the commanding of the husband's will: the widow's year — the provision and the home. Ibn Kathir: the widow used to reside and have her provisions provided for her for a year, in her deceased husband's house. The verse's *wasiyyah* — the bequest — the command of the will: the dying husband's provision — the year's maintenance, the residence, the no-expulsion. The verse's frame: the widow's protection is the dying husband's command: the year's provision and the home's security.

"But if they choose to leave, you are not accountable for what they reasonably decide for themselves" — the verse's *fa-in kharajna fala junaha 'alaykum* — if they leave (of their own choice), there is no sin on you for what they do of themselves in the known good: the widow may leave (the residence), and the family is not blameworthy for her decision — the leave is the widow's choice; her lawful decisions at the departure are her own. The verse's frame: the widow's freedom: she may stay (the year's provision) or leave (her own course) — and the family's responsibility is only the wrongful expulsion, not the widow's own choice.

"And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise" — the verse's close: the Almighty, the All-Wise — the power and the wisdom of the legislation: the widow's protection, the year's provision, the fair balance. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *Aziz Hakim* — the Almighty, the All-Wise. The verse's frame: the widow's law is the law of the Almighty — the power behind the protection — and the All-Wise — the wisdom of the year's measure.

The verse's history — the abrogation: Ibn Kathir: the majority of the scholars said that this ayah was abrogated by the ayah 2:234 — the widow's iddah of four months and ten days — and by the inheritance verses (4:12): the widow inherits the quarter or the eighth; the ayah's year of the maintenance was abrogated, and the widow's residence (per the iddah's statement — Ibn Abbas via 'Ata) — the ayah (2:240) abrogated the requirement that the widow spends the iddah with the deceased's family — she spends it wherever she wants. Ibn Kathir questions — the famous exchange of Ibn al-Zubayr and 'Uthman about the abrogated ayah's presence in the Qur'an: "I shall not change any part of the Qur'an from its place." The verse's presence: the abrogated ruling's text remains: the Qur'an's record.

In modern life, the verse is the memory of the widow's protection. The modern widow often faces the sudden: the death, the loss, the home's uncertainty, the family's pressure. The verse's legacy: the widow was protected: the year's maintenance, the residence, the no-expulsion — then the law refined: the four months and ten days, the inheritance's share — the widow's rights. The verse's message: the death does not end the responsibility; the widow's dignity is the law's care — and the modern lesson: the widow's provision, the widow's choice, the widow's freedom — under the Almighty, the All-Wise, who legislates the protection of the widow, and the All-Wise who balances the rights.
