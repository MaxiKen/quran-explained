## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:221

> Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe. A believing bondwoman is better than a polytheistic woman, even if she impresses you. And do not marry polytheistic men until they believe. A believing bondman is better than a polytheist, even if he impresses you. They invite to the Fire, while Allah invites to Paradise and forgiveness by His permission. He makes His revelations clear to people so that they may remember.

**Commentary:**

### Faith as the Foundation of Marriage

This verse addresses marriage to those who worship other than Allah. It places *īmān* before attraction, social status, wealth, and lineage. Marriage is not only companionship; it is a covenant that shapes worship, family identity, children, household decisions, and moral priorities. If the spouses disagree about the Lord to whom they owe ultimate obedience, the conflict can reach the foundation of the home.

The prohibition concerns polytheists—*mushrikūn*—and should not be carelessly applied to every non-Muslim category without studying the Qur’an’s distinctions. Sūrah al-Māʾidah (5:5) gives a specific ruling concerning chaste women from the People of the Book, while this āyah addresses those who associate partners with Allah. Juristic conditions and contemporary circumstances require qualified guidance.

### Dignity Is Not Measured by Status

The verse says that a believing bondwoman is better than a polytheistic free woman, and a believing bondman better than a polytheist. In the social world in which slavery existed, this was a radical reordering of value. Social freedom, beauty, wealth, and lineage do not make a person spiritually superior to a believer who has been oppressed and possesses none of those advantages.

The verse does not praise slavery or encourage Muslims to enslave people. The Qur’an repeatedly opens the path to emancipation, commands freeing slaves as expiation, and praises the freeing of a neck (90:13; 4:92; 5:89). The comparison exposes the error of judging marriage by social prestige while ignoring faith. A person’s human dignity remains real, and Islam’s ethical trajectory is toward liberation and justice.

### Attraction and Long-Term Direction

“Even if she impresses you” and “even if he impresses you” acknowledge that attraction is powerful. Islam does not deny beauty, personality, or compatibility. It asks whether attraction will lead a person to compromise the foundation of his faith. A charming partner may be good in worldly manners yet invite the believer toward practices and values that conflict with tawḥīd.

Marriage should therefore be evaluated through faith, character, responsibility, safety, and compatibility, not only appearance or social excitement. The Prophet ﷺ advised considering religion and character when choosing a spouse. This does not justify judging people harshly or treating a convert as morally superior in every matter; it identifies the foundation that must guide the covenant.

### “They Invite to the Fire”

A spouse who worships other than Allah can, without malicious intention, invite the family toward a worldview that leads away from the Hereafter. Children may be confused about worship, festivals, prayer, diet, identity, and moral authority. The phrase is about the direction of a life, not an instruction to insult or dehumanize a person of another faith.

The believer continues to treat non-Muslim relatives and neighbours with justice and kindness. The marital prohibition protects the religious covenant; it does not erase the rights of people. Qur’an 60:8 commands kindness and justice toward those who do not fight Muslims because of religion.

### Allah Invites to Paradise

Allah’s invitation to Paradise and forgiveness is an invitation through revelation, repentance, worship, and righteous action. Marriage should help the spouses answer that invitation, not make obedience increasingly difficult. A believing spouse can remind, support, and comfort, though no spouse controls another’s final faith.

The phrase “by His permission” teaches humility. Guidance is a gift from Allah, not a possession produced by personal superiority. A Muslim asks Allah to guide himself, his spouse, children, and all people, while respecting the limits Allah has set.

### Marriage and Freedom of Choice

The verse prohibits the Muslim from entering a particular marriage; it does not license forced marriage. Consent is required, and the Prophet ﷺ rejected marriages imposed upon women. Families may advise, but they may not imprison, threaten, or marry someone without genuine agreement. Faith cannot be produced by coercion, and a marriage entered through coercion is a violation of Islamic ethics.

The ruling also does not make the believer a judge of every interfaith friendship. Social kindness, neighbourly cooperation, and respectful conversation are distinct from a marriage covenant that creates intimate and generational obligations.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:221 on marriage, tawḥīd, attraction, status, and the invitation to Paradise.
- Qur’an 5:5 on the specific ruling concerning chaste women from the People of the Book, and Qur’an 60:8 on justice and kindness toward peaceful non-Muslims.
- Qur’an 90:13, 4:92, and 5:89 on emancipation and the moral restriction of slavery.
- Qur’an 30:21 and 49:13 on marriage as tranquillity and human worth beyond lineage.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith advising consideration of religion and character in marriage and reports affirming consent in marriage.
- For *shirk*, *īmān*, the status comparison, and the relation between marriage and religious formation, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 2:221.
## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:222

> They ask you about menstruation. Say, “It is a state of discomfort, so keep away from women during menstruation and do not approach them until they are pure. When they have purified themselves, then go to them from where Allah has ordained for you.” Surely Allah loves those who constantly repent and those who purify themselves.

**Commentary:**

### Menstruation Is Not a Stigma

The question about menstruation—*ḥayḍ*—is answered with practical guidance and moral dignity. It is described as *adhā*, a condition involving discomfort, bleeding, and physical vulnerability. The verse does not call menstruating women impure as people, cursed, or unfit for companionship. It gives a temporary sexual boundary while preserving their honour and ordinary human relationships.

The phrase “keep away from women” refers to avoiding sexual intercourse during menstruation, not abandoning, isolating, or refusing to touch women. The Prophet ﷺ ate with his menstruating wives, drank from the same place they drank, rested with them, and instructed them in worship. ʿĀʾishah رضي الله عنها reported that he would ask her to hand him something while she was menstruating, demonstrating that her person was not contaminated.

### A Temporary Physical Boundary

The prohibition concerns intercourse through the vaginal passage during menstruation. It protects the woman from increased discomfort and possible harm, and it gives the body a period of rest. The restriction is not a judgement of moral fault. Menstruation is a natural function created by Allah, and a woman is not blamed for missing prayer or fasting during it; she makes up missed Ramadan fasts but not missed prayers, according to the established Sunnah.

The husband must not use the verse to punish or humiliate his wife. Affection, conversation, sleeping together, eating together, and emotional support remain lawful. The marriage continues; only a particular act is paused until the condition ends and purification is completed.

### Purity and Ghusl

“Until they are pure” and “when they have purified themselves” refer to the end of the menstrual period and the *ghusl* required before intercourse and the return to ritual prayer. The exact signs of the end of menstruation, irregular bleeding, and the rules for postpartum bleeding require study from qualified jurists. Women should not be forced into worship based on guesswork or shamed for asking detailed questions.

*Ṭahārah* is both physical and spiritual. Ghusl cleanses the body for ritual worship, while repentance cleanses the heart. The verse’s closing praise joins the two without treating the menstruating woman as sinful. She is excused from certain acts by Allah’s mercy, not excluded from remembering Him, making supplication, learning, giving charity, or remaining part of the community.

### “From Where Allah Has Ordained”

The phrase directs marital intimacy to the lawful place and lawful relationship ordained by Allah. It confirms that sexual intimacy within marriage can be a good and grateful act, but it must remain within the boundaries of consent, bodily dignity, and the prohibition of intercourse during menstruation. It is not permission for coercion or for any practice that harms a spouse.

The Qur’an places this verse after the discussion of marriage and before verses concerning divorce and family rights. The sequence shows that sexuality is part of a wider covenant. Desire is recognized, but it is regulated by mercy, responsibility, and reverence for Allah’s limits.

### Allah Loves Repentance and Purity

Allah loves *al-tawwābīn*, those who repeatedly return to Him, and *al-mutaṭahhirīn*, those who purify themselves. Repentance is not only for dramatic sins. It includes returning after carelessness, repairing harsh speech, and renewing obedience. Physical cleanliness, wudūʾ, ghusl, clean clothing, and a clean environment also matter in Islamic worship.

The pairing prevents two errors. A person may maintain outward cleanliness while refusing to repent from arrogance or injustice. Another may feel spiritually ashamed because of a natural bodily process. Allah’s love is for those who pursue both inward repentance and outward purification according to His guidance.

### Menstruation and Family Compassion

The husband and family should understand the practical realities of menstruation. Pain, fatigue, mood changes, and heavy bleeding may require medical care and rest. Religious law should be taught in a way that helps women worship confidently rather than making them feel dirty or burdensome. The Prophet ﷺ showed affection and patience, and his household was a school in humane conduct.

If bleeding is abnormal, persistent, or accompanied by severe symptoms, a woman should seek qualified medical care. Spiritual guidance and medical expertise cooperate; one does not replace the other.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:221–223 on marriage, menstruation, intimacy, and the boundaries of the marital relationship.
- Qur’an 5:6 and 4:43 on ritual purification and the concessions in worship.
- Qur’an 9:108 on loving those who purify themselves, and 2:222 on repentance and purity.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the reports of ʿĀʾishah that the Prophet ﷺ interacted normally with menstruating wives and that menstruating women made up fasts but not prayers.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith describing menstruation as a natural matter and the Prophet’s instruction to avoid intercourse while maintaining other affection.
- For *ḥayḍ*, *adhā*, *ghusl*, and the meaning of “keep away,” see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 2:222.
## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:223

> Your wives are a place of cultivation for you, so approach your place of cultivation as you wish, and send ahead something good for yourselves. Be mindful of Allah and know that you will meet Him. Give good news to the believers.

**Commentary:**

### The Metaphor of Cultivation

The verse uses the metaphor of a field or place of cultivation—*ḥarth*—for the marital relationship. A field is approached carefully, tended, and connected with the possibility of life. The metaphor highlights intimacy, fruitfulness, responsibility, and the continuation of family. It does not reduce a wife to property or erase her feelings. The surrounding Qur’anic teaching describes spouses as garments for one another, sources of tranquillity, affection, and mercy (2:187; 30:21).

The metaphor also places intimacy within lawful marriage. Sexual relations are not treated as a casual transaction detached from trust, consequences, or rights. Spouses owe each other kindness, privacy, and consideration, and any intimacy must be free from coercion and harm.

### “As You Wish” within Allah’s Limits

“Approach your place of cultivation as you wish” allows lawful variation in the manner and timing of marital intimacy, provided the relationship remains within the boundaries Allah established. The phrase does not permit intercourse during menstruation, which was prohibited in the preceding verse, nor anal intercourse, which the prophetic teachings explicitly forbid. It does not permit intimacy without consent or practices that cause injury.

The verse answers a question about whether different positions were allowed. The Prophet ﷺ clarified that the permission concerns the manner of approach to the lawful place, not a change of lawful place. A translation without the surrounding explanation can be misunderstood, so Qur’anic language is read alongside the authentic Sunnah and the interpretations of the jurists.

### Mutual Rights and Kindness

The verse’s address to husbands in its historical context does not cancel the wife’s rights. The Prophet ﷺ said, “The best of you are the best to their wives,” and he never treated marriage as a licence for abuse. Mutual intimacy should consider health, emotional readiness, privacy, and the dignity of both spouses. A person who cites the verse to pressure or frighten a spouse is violating the Qur’an’s wider ethic of mercy.

Marriage includes duties beyond physical intimacy: financial support, companionship, honesty, protection, consultation, and patience. The metaphor of cultivation implies care over time. A field cannot be exploited endlessly without care, and a marriage cannot flourish when one person’s needs are treated as the only needs that matter.

### Sending Ahead for the Hereafter

“Send ahead something good for yourselves” has been explained as seeking children through lawful marriage, preparing good deeds for the Hereafter, and approaching intimacy with the intention of preserving chastity and strengthening the family. A child is not a trophy or an entitlement; he is a trust whose rights begin before birth and continue through upbringing.

The phrase also broadens the meaning of intimacy. A lawful act can become worship when accompanied by a sincere intention and humane conduct. The Prophet ﷺ taught that even marital intimacy can be charity when a person fulfils it lawfully rather than pursuing a prohibited outlet, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

### Taqwā in Private Life

“Be mindful of Allah” is especially meaningful in an intimate context, where people are hidden from public observation. Taqwā protects spouses from betrayal, coercion, exposure of private details, and using the relationship to retaliate. The Prophet ﷺ described among the worst trusts a person who discloses the private matters of his spouse after intimacy.

The believer remembers that Allah sees the private room as clearly as the public mosque. This does not make intimacy fearful; it makes it safe, honourable, and accountable. Privacy becomes a trust rather than a space for abuse.

### Meeting Allah

The reminder that spouses will meet Allah places marriage within the Hereafter. Each person will answer for how he used power, how he treated a partner, and whether he fulfilled the covenant. A marriage that looks successful publicly may be corrupt privately, while a quiet household of patience and mercy may be greatly honoured by Allah.

The promise of good news to believers shows that lawful family life can be a path to joy and reward. The believer asks Allah to bless intimacy with affection, righteous children, protection from sin, and a household that remembers Him.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:187, 2:222–223, and 30:21 on marital intimacy, limits, tranquillity, affection, and mercy.
- Qur’an 4:19 and 2:228 on kind treatment and mutual rights in marriage.
- Qur’an 23:5–7 and 70:29–31 on guarding chastity within lawful marriage.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the reports explaining the permissible manner of approaching the “place of cultivation,” and the hadith that marital intimacy can be charity.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith warning against exposing the private matters of spouses, and Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī on the best character toward one’s family.
- For *ḥarth*, the scope of “as you wish,” *tawqā*, and the balance between lawful intimacy and mutual dignity, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 2:223.
## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:224

> Do not make Allah, by your oaths, an obstacle to being righteous, mindful, or making peace between people. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Commentary:**

### Do Not Use an Oath to Block Good

The verse warns against swearing an oath and then treating it as a reason not to do good. A person may say, “By Allah, I will never give charity,” “I will never speak to that relative,” or “By Allah, I will not reconcile with my spouse.” He then imagines that keeping the oath requires continuing in miserliness, estrangement, or conflict. Allah forbids making His name an obstacle to righteousness.

An oath is serious, but it is not stronger than an explicit duty or a good act Allah loves. The person should not casually swear, and he should not use the name of Allah to give emotional force to a harmful decision. When an oath blocks a better and lawful action, the Sunnah provides a way to break it and offer the prescribed expiation.

### Righteousness, Taqwā, and Reconciliation

The three examples—righteousness, taqwā, and reconciliation—cover private worship and social repair. *Birr* includes charity, kinship, justice, and service. *Taqwā* includes obeying Allah and avoiding sin. *Iṣlāḥ* means restoring peace between people. A stubborn oath can obstruct all three when it turns anger into a permanent moral rule.

The verse especially protects family and community relationships. A person may be deeply hurt and need boundaries, but he should not make a sacred oath that prevents every possibility of forgiveness or necessary communication. Reconciliation does not mean returning to an abusive situation without protection. It means not allowing a personal oath to make reform impossible.

### The Prophetic Guidance on Oaths

The Prophet ﷺ taught that when a person swears an oath and then sees something better, he should do the better act and expiate the oath, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The expiation is explained in 5:89: feeding ten needy people from the average food one gives one’s family, clothing them, or freeing a slave; whoever cannot do these fasts three days.

The hadith does not encourage careless breaking of promises. It teaches that the oath is not allowed to trap a person in sin or prevent a superior good. The better act should be genuinely better according to revelation, not merely more convenient.

### Allah’s Name Must Be Honoured

“Do not make Allah” means do not place the name of Allah in the service of stubbornness. People sometimes swear to win a dispute, pressure a family member, sell a product, or make a doubtful claim appear certain. Excessive oaths weaken reverence and can make the tongue careless about truth.

Allah hears the oath and knows whether it was sincere, mistaken, manipulative, or false. A person may deceive people with solemn language, but he cannot deceive Allah. Truthfulness in speech is part of honouring the divine name.

### Boundaries and Forgiveness

A person who has been harmed may need distance, counselling, legal protection, or a carefully mediated conversation. The verse does not require him to remove every boundary or trust an unsafe person. It prohibits using a vow as a blanket excuse to refuse every act of goodness. Sometimes the best reconciliation is a just settlement with continued distance; sometimes it is restoring a relationship. Wisdom determines the form.

The Prophet ﷺ forgave many personal injuries while also protecting the community from repeated aggression. His example teaches that forgiveness and accountability can coexist.

### False Oaths Are Worse

The verse’s immediate concern is an oath that blocks good, but false oaths are even more serious. A person who swears falsely to consume another’s wealth commits a major sin, as warned in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. An oath cannot turn stolen property into lawful property or make a lie true.

If someone has taken a false oath, repentance includes admitting the truth, restoring rights, and expiating where applicable. The public harm may require public correction.

- Qur’an 2:224–225 on oaths, intention, righteousness, and Allah’s knowledge.
- Qur’an 5:89 on the expiation of oaths and the prohibition of treating vows carelessly.
- Qur’an 4:35 and 49:9–10 on reconciliation, mediation, and justice.
- Qur’an 24:22 on forgiving and overlooking relatives and the needy.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith on doing what is better and expiating an oath, and the warnings against false oaths and consuming people’s wealth.
- For *aymān*, *birr*, *taqwā*, *iṣlāḥ*, and the relationship between an oath and a superior good, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 2:224.
## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:225

> Allah does not hold you accountable for unintentional oaths, but He holds you accountable for what your hearts have earned. Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

**Commentary:**

### Unintentional Oaths

This verse distinguishes careless or unintentional expressions from deliberate commitments. People often say “By Allah” as a habitual phrase without intending an oath, or they swear about something while honestly believing it to be true and later discover that they were mistaken. Such speech is not treated as though the heart consciously entered a binding covenant.

The jurists call this category *laghw al-yamīn*, an idle or unintended oath. Its precise examples differ among the schools, but the principle is that Allah judges intention and awareness, not isolated sounds detached from meaning. This is mercy for ordinary speech and human error.

### What the Heart Has Earned

Allah holds people accountable for what their hearts have earned—*kasabat qulūbukum*. This refers to deliberate intention, conscious commitment, and the falsehood or harm a person chooses. A person who knowingly swears a lie, uses an oath to consume another’s wealth, or makes a vow and then breaks it without proper reason has acted from the heart, not from a slip of the tongue.

The heart in Qur’anic language is the centre of intention, understanding, and moral choice. Accountability is not limited to visible action, nor is it based on thoughts that a person rejects. A passing temptation is not the same as a settled decision. Allah knows the difference between involuntary mental noise and chosen intention.

### Oath, Error, and Expiation

A deliberate oath that is broken requires the expiation detailed in 5:89: feeding ten needy people, clothing them, or freeing a slave; if unable, fasting three days. A false oath concerning a past or present matter is a grave sin and requires sincere repentance and restoration of rights, not a mechanical payment that allows the lie to remain.

If an oath prevents a better action, the person follows the better action and offers expiation, as taught by the Prophet ﷺ. This balances the seriousness of vows with the priority of righteousness. The believer does not make many oaths and then treat them as harmless, but he also does not believe that every habitual phrase creates a legal burden.

### Allah’s Forgiveness

Allah is *Ghafūr*, forgiving, and *Ḥalīm*, forbearing. His forbearance means that He does not rush to punish every error and gives servants time to correct themselves. This encourages the person who discovers that he spoke carelessly to repent and repair without falling into despair.

Forbearance is not permission to continue false oaths. It is an opportunity for the heart to become truthful. A person who repeatedly invokes Allah’s name in deception should fear that the delay of punishment is a test, not approval.

### Speech Reveals Reverence

Oaths make speech weightier, so a believer uses them sparingly. He avoids swearing to pressure a spouse, persuade a customer, frighten a child, or win a dispute. If he is uncertain, he says he is uncertain. If he made a mistake, he corrects it plainly. Reverence for Allah’s name produces calm truthfulness rather than theatrical certainty.

The Prophet ﷺ warned against false oaths that take a Muslim’s property, even when the oath is persuasive. He taught that a person who swears and then sees something better should do the better act and expiate. These teachings show that words are connected to rights and conduct.

### The Inner Life and Accountability

The verse does not make human beings responsible for every involuntary thought or emotional reaction. A person may feel anger and still choose not to act unjustly; he may experience doubt and still seek knowledge; he may have a passing desire and reject it. Allah judges what the heart intentionally adopts and what the person brings into speech and action.

This distinction offers hope to people troubled by intrusive thoughts or unwanted impulses. They should not treat involuntary mental events as chosen disbelief. They seek help, ignore the whisper, and return to deliberate faith and action.

- Qur’an 17:36 and 50:16–18 on accountability for speech, inner knowledge, and recorded deeds.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:226

> Those who swear to abstain from their wives must wait four months. If they return, then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### The Oath of Īlāʾ

This verse addresses *īlāʾ*, an oath by which a husband commits himself to abstaining from marital intimacy with his wife. In pre-Islamic practice, such an oath could leave a woman suspended indefinitely: neither enjoying the rights of marriage nor being released through divorce. The Qur’an sets a limit of four months and then requires a decision.

The rule protects the wife from being used as a prisoner of anger. A husband may need temporary distance after a dispute, but he cannot turn an oath into permanent emotional or physical abandonment. Marriage is a covenant of rights, and a person cannot use the name of Allah to destroy those rights without consequence.

### The Four-Month Period

The four months provide time for reflection, reconciliation, and the cooling of anger. They are not an invitation to delay until the final day while offering no communication or support. During the period, the husband remains responsible for maintenance and respectful treatment, and the couple may seek mediation or counselling.

Classical jurists discuss whether the period begins with the oath, how it applies to different forms of intimacy, and what happens when the wife is pregnant, ill, or otherwise affected. The exact legal procedure is handled by a qualified judge or scholar. The moral boundary is clear: the wife must not be left indefinitely in uncertainty.

### Returning to the Marriage

“If they return”—*fa-in fāʾū*—means that the husband resumes marital relations and ends the harmful abstention within the permitted time. Allah’s forgiveness and mercy welcome the return. The husband should also repent for the harm caused, fulfil his wife’s rights, and not repeat the oath as a form of control.

Depending on the juristic analysis, breaking the oath may require the expiation described in 5:89, while the procedure of īlāʾ has its own legal consequences. A person should not use a legal technicality to avoid the spirit of the ruling. Repentance includes restoring emotional safety and trust.

### A Boundary against Suspension

The verse prepares for 2:227, which says that if the husband resolves upon divorce, Allah hears and knows. The husband therefore has two responsible paths after the period: return honourably or proceed toward divorce through lawful procedure. He may not keep a wife suspended simply because he refuses to decide.

This principle appears elsewhere in the prohibition against retaining a divorced woman to harm her (2:231). The Qur’an repeatedly closes the door to using marriage status as a weapon. A spouse is not property to be held or released according to one person’s anger.

### Anger and Oaths

Many oaths of abstention are made during anger. Islam recognizes anger but does not let a moment of emotion govern another person’s life indefinitely. The Prophet ﷺ taught that true strength is controlling oneself in anger, and he encouraged silence, changing position, ablution, and seeking refuge in Allah.

A person who frequently threatens divorce or vows to abandon intimacy should seek help. Repeated threats damage the household even when no legal divorce occurs. Taqwā requires choosing words carefully and not making Allah’s name a weapon.

### Mediation and Reconciliation

The waiting period can be used for sincere mediation. Trusted relatives, counsellors, or judges may hear both spouses, protect against abuse, and help determine whether return is safe and meaningful. Reconciliation must not be forced upon a person facing violence. Mercy includes protecting the vulnerable and ensuring that return does not simply renew harm.

Where the relationship can be repaired, returning can be an act of forgiveness and growth. The husband who returns should not demand that the wife forget everything immediately. He takes responsibility, listens, and rebuilds trust through consistent conduct.

### Allah’s Mercy and Marital Rights

The closing names of Allah show that legal limits are not merely punitive. They create a path to mercy. Allah forgives the person who abandons the harmful oath and returns to justice. The wife’s right is protected, the husband’s repentance is accepted, and the family has an opportunity to heal.

The verse also warns that Allah’s mercy should not be manipulated. A person who repeatedly harms a spouse and then cites forgiveness without reform has not fulfilled the requirement of return.

- Qur’an 4:35 on appointing arbiters when a marriage is in serious conflict.
- Qur’an 65:1–2 on divorce procedure, time limits, and not transgressing Allah’s boundaries.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:227

> But if they resolve to divorce, then surely Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Commentary:**

### A Decision after the Waiting Period

Following the rule of īlāʾ, this verse addresses the husband who resolves upon divorce rather than returning to marital intimacy. Divorce—*ṭalāq*—is permitted in Islamic law but is not treated as a casual weapon. It ends a covenant, affects children and families, and creates financial and emotional consequences. The decision must therefore be deliberate, not a passing threat made in anger.

The verse says “if they resolve”—*wa-in ʿazamū al-ṭalāq*. Resolution means a settled decision, not every intrusive thought or sentence uttered during rage. The jurists discuss the legal effect of different expressions, intentions, and circumstances. Ordinary Muslims should seek qualified guidance rather than assuming that a threat, text message, or angry phrase has one universal ruling.

### Divorce Is a Lawful Last Resort

The Qur’an does not deny that some marriages cannot safely or honestly continue. Abuse, persistent betrayal, irreconcilable conflict, or serious harm may make separation better than endless hostility. At the same time, divorce should follow attempts at counsel, mediation, and reform when these are safe and appropriate (4:35).

The Prophet ﷺ said that the most hateful of lawful things to Allah is divorce, in a report whose chain is discussed by hadith scholars; the meaning is consistent with the Qur’an’s treatment of divorce as permissible but serious. This should not be used to trap victims in harm. It teaches that ending a marriage should not be the first response to every disappointment.

### Allah Hears the Declaration

Allah is *Samīʿ*, All-Hearing. He hears the divorce declaration, the private threat, the wife’s unanswered plea, the children’s fear, and the mediation that others may never know about. A person may manipulate witnesses or exploit legal ambiguity, but Allah knows the words and circumstances completely.

He is also *ʿAlīm*, All-Knowing. He knows whether a husband intends release with dignity or seeks to punish his wife, whether a wife is seeking safety or being coerced, and whether relatives are mediating sincerely or protecting their own interest. The reminder places divorce under divine accountability rather than private power.

### Divorce Must Be Honourable

The following verses provide rules for waiting periods, taking a wife back, compensation, and fair treatment. Divorce is not a moment when ordinary ethics disappear. The husband must not insult, expose private matters, withhold property, manipulate children, or use financial dependence to force compliance. The wife must also avoid false claims and injustice, while both parties remain responsible for their own conduct.

Allah says elsewhere either retain a woman honourably or release her honourably (2:229, 2:231). This is the moral framework of the decision. A legal divorce obtained through cruelty may be technically effective while still leaving a grave sin before Allah.

### Children and Continuing Responsibilities

Divorce changes the marital relationship but does not erase parenthood. Children retain rights to maintenance, safety, affection, education, and contact according to what is beneficial. Neither parent may use children as messengers, weapons, or spies. The Qur’anic commands concerning breastfeeding and maintenance in 2:233 show that parental duties continue after separation.

A person who chooses divorce should plan for housing, finances, custody, and emotional support. Seeking counselling and legal advice is not a lack of faith. It is part of fulfilling the trust Allah has placed in the family.

### No Suspension or Retaliation

The verse ends the possibility of indefinite suspension created by īlāʾ. If the husband will not return, he must proceed toward divorce rather than keep the wife in uncertainty. This limit also applies to other forms of abandonment. A spouse may not withhold support, intimacy, documentation, or communication to force the other person into emotional submission.

Where divorce is necessary, releasing the other person can be an act of mercy. The aim is not to win the final argument but to prevent further harm and preserve the rights of everyone affected.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:228

> Divorced women shall wait for three menstrual cycles, and it is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they truly believe in Allah and the Last Day. Their husbands have a greater right to take them back during that period if they genuinely seek reconciliation. Women have rights similar to the obligations upon them, according to what is fair, though men have a degree of responsibility over them. Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise.

**Commentary:**

### The Waiting Period

This verse establishes the waiting period—*ʿiddah*—for a divorced woman who menstruates and whose marriage was consummated. She waits three *qurūʾ*, a term that classical jurists understood either as menstrual cycles or periods of purity, producing a recognized difference among the legal schools. The practical calculation should be obtained from qualified scholarship, especially for irregular bleeding, pregnancy, and medical conditions.

The waiting period protects several interests. It clarifies whether pregnancy exists, creates time for possible reconciliation in a revocable divorce, allows emotions to settle, and preserves the seriousness of the marital covenant. It is not a punishment imposed on the woman. It is a legal and social protection during a vulnerable transition.

### Truth about Pregnancy

The woman must not conceal what Allah has created in her womb. Concealing a pregnancy or denying a menstrual condition can affect lineage, maintenance, inheritance, custody, and the rights of the child. Honesty is therefore a religious and legal trust. The command does not permit intrusive public investigation into a woman’s body; it places the responsibility of truthful disclosure upon her while preserving privacy.

If a woman is pregnant, her waiting period continues until she gives birth, as explained in 65:4. The father remains responsible for maintenance during pregnancy and for the child’s needs. Any dispute should be addressed through evidence, medical expertise, and lawful authority rather than accusation or public shame.

### Reconciliation during a Revocable Divorce

The husband has a right to take his wife back during the waiting period of a first or second revocable divorce if he genuinely seeks *iṣlāḥ*, reconciliation and repair. This is not a licence to reclaim her in order to prolong suffering, prevent remarriage, or use the waiting period as emotional punishment. The Qur’an later prohibits retaining a woman to harm her (2:231).

Taking back a wife should involve a clear declaration, honest intention, and restoration of marital rights. Families and witnesses may be involved according to the legal school and local law. If abuse or serious danger exists, “reconciliation” cannot be used to force a woman back into harm. Protection and due process are part of justice.

### Mutual Rights and Duties

“Women have rights similar to the obligations upon them, according to what is fair”—*wa-lahunna mithlu alladhī ʿalayhinna bi-l-maʿrūf*—is a foundational statement of reciprocity. Marriage is not structured around one person’s rights and the other person’s service alone. Both spouses owe honesty, respect, fidelity, maintenance or cooperation according to their roles, privacy, and protection from harm.

The word *maʿrūf* means what is recognized as fair, sound, and consistent with revelation and good custom. Rights are not reduced to money. They include kind speech, emotional safety, reasonable intimacy, housing, child care, consultation, and freedom from violence. The specific legal allocation of duties requires the Qur’an, Sunnah, and juristic detail, but the moral foundation is mutuality.

### The Degree of Responsibility

“Men have a degree over them”—*wa-li-l-rijāli ʿalayhinna darajah*—has been explained by classical exegetes in relation to the husband’s responsibility for maintenance, protection, leadership of the household, and the authority to initiate revocable divorce and reconciliation within the limits of law. It is not a declaration that men are inherently more valuable before Allah or that women are inferior human beings.

The degree is a burden of accountability, not a crown of unrestricted power. A husband who claims authority while refusing maintenance, consultation, mercy, and justice has betrayed its meaning. The Prophet ﷺ said that the best believers are those with the best character and that the best men are those best to their wives. Any interpretation that permits cruelty contradicts the Qur’an’s commands to live with wives in kindness (4:19).

### Equality before Allah

Men and women are equally accountable before Allah and equally capable of faith, worship, knowledge, charity, patience, and Paradise. The Qur’an praises believing men and women together (33:35) and measures honour through taqwā, not sex or lineage (49:13). Differences in family roles and legal responsibilities do not justify contempt, exclusion from education, or violence.

The verse’s structure first establishes mutual rights, then mentions a degree of responsibility. It should not be reversed into a culture in which one party’s claimed authority erases the other’s rights.

### Divorce and Emotional Protection

The waiting period can be emotionally difficult. The divorcing couple should avoid public humiliation, gossip, and involving children in the conflict. The husband must maintain lawful obligations, and the woman must be free to observe the period without harassment. Both should seek mediation where safe and maintain truthful records of declarations and dates.

A community should support divorced women rather than treating divorce as proof of moral failure. The Prophet ﷺ instructed kindness toward women and protected their rights in marriage and separation.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:229

> Divorce is twice. Then retain the wife honourably or release her with good treatment. It is not lawful for you to take back anything of what you have given them, unless both fear that they cannot keep within Allah’s limits. If you fear that they cannot keep within Allah’s limits, then there is no blame on either of them if she gives something for her release. These are Allah’s limits, so do not transgress them. Whoever transgresses Allah’s limits, those are the wrongdoers.

**Commentary:**

### Limiting Repeated Divorce

The verse reforms a system in which a husband could repeatedly divorce and take back his wife, leaving her permanently suspended. “Divorce is twice” means that the first and second pronouncements can be followed by lawful reconciliation during the waiting period, while a third divorce produces the final separation described in the next verse. The rule gives the marriage a chance to recover but prevents endless manipulation.

Divorce is therefore a process with consequences, not a phrase used in every argument. A person should seek counsel, calm, and mediation before pronouncing it. The Prophet ﷺ was angered when a divorce was issued contrary to the proper procedure, and he taught the community to observe the waiting period and not harm the wife.

### Retain Honourably or Release Well

After a revocable divorce, the husband has two ethical paths: *imsāk bi-maʿrūf*, retaining her honourably, or *tasrīḥ bi-iḥsān*, releasing her with good treatment. Retaining means genuine reconciliation, restored rights, and a willingness to live without abuse. Releasing means ending the marriage without insults, withheld property, public exposure, or manipulation of children.

The alternatives exclude a third path—retaining a woman only to harm, delay her remarriage, or punish her. The Qur’an repeats this prohibition in 2:231. A legal status that remains technically intact while the spouse is tortured emotionally or financially is a violation of the verse.

### The Dowry Is Not a Weapon

“It is not lawful for you to take back anything of what you have given them.” The husband may not reclaim the *mahr*, gifts, property, or financial rights as a price for allowing the wife to leave. Divorce is not a business transaction in which the stronger party recovers every benefit and leaves the weaker party destitute.

The exception concerns a situation in which both spouses fear that they cannot keep Allah’s limits and the wife offers compensation for release. This is the basis of *khulʿ*, a separation sought by the wife with compensation under the conditions of Islamic law. It must be voluntary and fair, not demanded by a husband who wants to profit from forcing his wife out.

### Khulʿ and Marital Breakdown

The Sunnah records the case of the wife of Thābit ibn Qays, who did not accuse him of bad character or religion but feared she could not continue the marriage. The Prophet ﷺ instructed that she return the garden he had given her and directed Thābit to separate from her, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī. The report illustrates that a woman need not prove that her husband is a monster in order to seek release when the marriage has become unworkable.

The details of khulʿ—whether the husband’s consent is required, the amount of compensation, and the role of a judge—are discussed by the legal schools. The moral safeguards remain: no coercion, no exploitation, and no use of the process to trap or impoverish either party.

### Keeping Allah’s Limits

Allah’s limits—*ḥudūd Allāh*—include the number of divorces, the waiting period, maintenance, the prohibition of harm, the return of property, and the rights of children. A couple may feel emotionally overwhelmed, but their anger does not suspend these boundaries. They must document declarations, seek qualified advice, and act with fairness.

When a relationship is unsafe, preserving the limits may require separation rather than forced reconciliation. When conflict is temporary, the limits may create space for repair. The purpose is not to preserve every marriage at any cost; it is to preserve dignity, justice, and obedience in whichever lawful outcome occurs.

### Do Not Transgress

Transgression includes pronouncing divorce repeatedly to frighten a wife, preventing her from leaving, demanding compensation beyond what is fair, concealing assets, spreading private information, or using children to maintain control. It also includes making false claims against the other spouse or exploiting the legal process to obtain what one knows is not rightfully his or hers.

The final description—“those are the wrongdoers”—reminds both genders that rights come with accountability. The verse is addressed primarily to husbands in its immediate legal context, but every party must respect Allah’s limits.

### Justice and Emotional Wisdom

The community should not pressure a couple to remain together merely to preserve appearances, nor should it celebrate divorce as revenge. Counsellors, families, and judges should protect the vulnerable, listen to both sides, and distinguish ordinary conflict from abuse. A fair process is part of mercy.

The believer remembers that a marriage is a trust before it is a possession. If it ends, the former spouses can still act with *iḥsān* and preserve a relationship of co-parenting and basic respect.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:230

> If he divorces her for the third time, she is not lawful for him afterward until she marries another husband. If the latter husband divorces her, there is no blame on the two of them for returning to one another if they think they can keep within Allah’s limits. These are Allah’s limits, which He makes clear to people who know.

**Commentary:**

### The Third Divorce Is Final

This verse completes the limit introduced in 2:229. After a third valid divorce, the former spouses cannot simply resume their marriage during or after the waiting period. The rule prevents a husband from treating divorce and return as a cycle of threats, punishment, and control. A marriage cannot be repeatedly destroyed and restored without a point at which the decision becomes final.

The third divorce is not meant to be pronounced impulsively. The Qur’an gives time for reflection, mediation, and reconciliation before finality. The husband who uses divorce as a game may eventually reach a consequence he did not expect. Legal rulings concerning one utterance, repeated utterances, anger, and written statements require qualified scholarship and should not be settled by internet slogans.

### A Genuine Subsequent Marriage

The former wife becomes lawful to the first husband only after she marries another husband in a genuine marriage. This condition prevents the first couple from treating the third divorce as though it had never happened. The second marriage must be a real covenant with its own intention, rights, and possibility of continuing—not a temporary arrangement designed solely to return the woman to the first husband.

The Prophet ﷺ condemned the *muḥallil*, the man who enters a marriage only to make the woman lawful for her former husband, and the *muḥallal lahu*, the first husband who arranges it, in reports recorded by Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, Sunan Abī Dāwūd, and Ibn Mājah. Planned *taḥlīl* or “halālah” is a manipulation of the Qur’anic limit and an abuse of the woman.

### Marriage Must Be Consummated Naturally

The Sunnah clarifies that a merely formal contract is not enough. In the report of the wife of Rifāʿah, the Prophet ﷺ explained that she could not return to her first husband until the second marriage had been a real marital relationship, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. This is not a procedure to be arranged. It is a consequence that may occur only through an authentic marriage entered without a plan to dissolve it.

The requirement should never be used to pressure a woman into a second marriage or to treat her body as a legal tool. Her consent, safety, and dignity remain essential. If the second marriage ends naturally through divorce or death, she observes its waiting period before considering any future marriage.

### Returning Only if Limits Can Be Kept

If the second husband later divorces her, the former spouses may marry again if they think they can keep Allah’s limits. The permission is conditional upon a realistic prospect of *iṣlāḥ*, not merely nostalgia or loneliness. They should examine what caused the earlier breakdown, seek counsel, protect children, and agree to a relationship free from the old patterns of harm.

The condition also protects the woman from being reclaimed as property. She is free to decline the former husband. No person has a right to force her into a new marriage or back into an old one. The Qur’anic language of “there is no blame” removes the previous barrier; it does not create an obligation.

### Allah Makes the Limits Clear

The verse ends by calling the people to knowledge. Family law cannot be governed by anger, custom, or selective quotations. The rules of divorce, waiting periods, witnesses, maintenance, and reconciliation need learning and careful application. A person should consult a trustworthy scholar and the civil legal system where relevant, especially when children, abuse, or property are involved.

Clarity is a mercy. Without the limit, a woman could be suspended indefinitely and a man could repeatedly manipulate the marital bond. With the limit, both parties know that words have consequences and that marriage is a trust.

### No Space for Humiliation

Some communities misuse this verse by humiliating a divorced woman or arranging a degrading “return” procedure. That behaviour violates the verse’s purpose. The Qur’an protects the woman from being treated as an object of male rivalry. It condemns planned halālah and commands honourable treatment throughout divorce.

The correct response to a third divorce is not panic or public shame. It is to accept the legal consequence, protect the woman’s rights, support any children, and leave future decisions to a genuine and voluntary process.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:231

> When you divorce women and they have nearly reached the end of their waiting period, then either retain them honourably or release them honourably. Do not retain them to harm them and thereby transgress. Whoever does that has wronged himself. Do not take Allah’s revelations as a joke. Remember Allah’s favour upon you and what He sent down to you of the Book and wisdom, by which He instructs you. Be mindful of Allah and know that Allah knows all things.

**Commentary:**

### The Critical End of the Waiting Period

The verse addresses the moment when a revocable divorce’s waiting period is nearly complete. The husband must choose: return to the marriage honourably or allow the separation to finish honourably. This time can be emotionally vulnerable because a return may still be legally possible, but the relationship is also close to ending. Allah prevents the husband from using the final days to create uncertainty and pain.

A return—*imsāk*—must be based on genuine reconciliation, restored rights, and a willingness to live with kindness. Release—*tasrīḥ*—means allowing the woman to leave without harassment, withholding property, slander, or manipulation. Neither option permits keeping a spouse suspended while refusing the responsibilities of marriage.

### Retaining to Cause Harm

The prohibition “do not retain them to harm them” addresses a practice in which a husband would take a wife back shortly before the waiting period ended, then divorce her again, repeatedly extending the ordeal. This was a form of control. The Qur’an calls it transgression because it uses a lawful mechanism for an unlawful purpose.

Harm can take many forms: threatening the wife, preventing her remarriage, withholding maintenance, exposing private matters, manipulating children, refusing documentation, or using financial dependence to force obedience. The general Islamic legal principle is that harm must not be inflicted or returned. A person cannot defend cruelty by saying that a technical form was available to him.

### Wronging Oneself

“Whoever does that has wronged himself.” The oppressor may think he has power over the woman, but he is actually placing himself under divine accountability. Every act of humiliation, every withheld right, and every calculated delay is recorded. The person also damages his own soul by turning marriage into a tool of revenge.

The phrase reminds believers that injustice against another eventually returns to the wrongdoer. A person may escape family criticism or manipulate a court, but he cannot escape Allah’s knowledge. Repentance requires ending the harm, restoring rights, and allowing the former spouse to live safely.

### Do Not Take Revelation as a Joke

Taking Allah’s revelations as a joke—*huzuwan*—includes treating legal commands as toys that can be activated and deactivated for amusement or control. It includes mocking the limits, using pious words to hide abuse, and invoking divorce repeatedly without seriousness. Revelation is not a collection of loopholes for emotional games.

The command does not forbid light-heartedness in ordinary life. It forbids turning sacred obligations into a means of harm. A person who is unsure about a divorce should pause and consult qualified scholarship before speaking, rather than experimenting with words whose legal consequences may be irreversible.

### Remembering Allah’s Favour

Allah reminds the believers of His favour and of the Book and wisdom He sent down. The favour includes guidance that replaced tribal customs with clear rights and limits. The Book is the Qur’an, and wisdom is explained by many scholars as the prophetic Sunnah and sound judgement. Together they teach how to preserve families while protecting people from abuse.

Remembering this favour means studying the law, applying it with mercy, and recognizing that a spouse is a servant of Allah before being a person under one’s authority. The Prophet ﷺ was the best model of family conduct: he listened, consulted, forgave, and never used revelation to excuse cruelty.

### Taqwā and Legal Power

The command to be mindful of Allah is directed especially to the person who has procedural power. Taqwā restrains a husband, judge, guardian, family elder, and community leader from using a legal role for private revenge. It also protects the wife from responding to harm with false accusation or unlawful retaliation.

Divorce should be documented, witnessed, and handled with qualified advice. Modern legal protections can help enforce maintenance, custody, and property rights. Using civil law to preserve justice is not opposed to religion; it can be part of fulfilling the Qur’anic trust.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:232

> When you divorce women and they have completed their waiting period, do not prevent them from marrying their former husbands if they agree among themselves in a lawful and honourable manner. **Commentary:**

### Protecting a Woman’s Choice after Divorce

This verse addresses families and guardians who might obstruct a divorced woman from remarrying her former husband after the waiting period, even when both parties agree to return in a lawful and honourable manner. The prohibition of obstruction protects her agency. Divorce does not make her permanently controlled by relatives, nor does anger give a guardian the right to decide her future for selfish reasons.

The verse does not command a woman to return to her former husband. It protects the possibility when she and the man freely agree and when the marriage can be safe and sound. If she does not want to return, no family member may force her. Consent and the ability to assess harm remain central to marriage.

### The Occasion of Revelation

The Companions reported an incident involving Maʿqil ibn Yasār, whose sister had been divorced and later wished to return to her former husband. Maʿqil resisted out of anger, and this verse was revealed; he then accepted Allah’s instruction. The report, recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, shows that even a protective brother could allow personal hurt to become an unjust barrier.

The lesson is not that every former marriage should be restored. It is that guardians must distinguish genuine concern from revenge, status anxiety, tribal pride, or the desire to punish the former husband. They should advise honestly and then respect a lawful decision made by the adults concerned.

### Agreement in a Honourable Manner

The phrase *tarāḍaw baynahum bi-l-maʿrūf* requires mutual agreement and a lawful, recognized process. Both former spouses must consent without coercion. They should discuss the causes of the earlier divorce, expectations about finances and relatives, safety, and the welfare of children. Returning without addressing the prior harm can repeat the same cycle.

“According to what is acceptable” excludes secret arrangements that violate legal rights, deception, or the use of marriage to regain control. It includes a valid contract, proper witnesses and guardianship according to the applicable legal school, and protection of the woman’s financial and personal rights.

### The Role of Guardians

A *walī* has a protective and supportive role, not a licence for arbitrary obstruction. If a guardian prevents a suitable marriage out of spite, the matter can be taken to a qualified authority, who may transfer the guardianship according to the rules of Islamic law. The Prophet ﷺ said that when someone whose religion and character are acceptable seeks marriage, people should facilitate it rather than create corruption and fitnah.

At the same time, guardians may raise genuine concerns about abuse, addiction, dishonesty, or inability to maintain obligations. Advice must be based on evidence, not rumour or class prejudice. The goal is the woman’s welfare, not family control.

### Purer and More Virtuous

Allah calls non-obstruction “purer” and “more virtuous.” Removing resentment, allowing forgiveness, and respecting lawful choice purifies the community from prolonged feud. Sometimes a former couple can rebuild the marriage with maturity; sometimes the discussion itself closes old hostility. In both cases, justice is better than family revenge.

The language does not romanticize reconciliation. A return is good only if the couple can keep Allah’s limits. Where there was serious abuse, stalking, or danger, preventing renewed contact may be protection rather than obstruction. Expert counselling and legal safeguards can help distinguish the cases.

### Belief and Family Ethics

The admonition is directed to those who believe in Allah and the Last Day. Faith should change how family members exercise power. A person who remembers the Hereafter does not treat a daughter, sister, or divorced woman as an instrument for honour. He knows that he will answer for every barrier he placed before a lawful choice.

The verse also warns the woman and former husband not to use the permission as a way to deceive relatives or evade responsibilities. All parties remain under Allah’s knowledge.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:233

> Mothers may breastfeed their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete the nursing. The father is responsible for their provision and clothing according to what is reasonable. No soul is burdened beyond its capacity. No mother should be harmed through her child, nor a father through his child. The heir has a similar responsibility. If both parents decide, through mutual consent and consultation, to wean the child, there is no blame upon them. If you decide to have your children nursed by another woman, there is no blame upon you provided you pay what you have agreed in a fair manner. Be mindful of Allah and know that Allah sees what you do.

**Commentary:**

### Nursing as a Shared Responsibility

This āyah gives detailed guidance about breastfeeding, parental maintenance, and the welfare of a young child. It recognizes breastfeeding for two complete years as the period for whoever wishes to complete nursing. The wording allows parents to make informed decisions according to the child’s health, the mother’s condition, and professional advice. It does not make every family identical or turn a compassionate recommendation into a reason for shame.

The child’s welfare is central. Breastfeeding can provide nourishment and closeness, but the mother’s health, consent, ability, and circumstances also matter. A woman must not be insulted if she cannot breastfeed, and a child must not be deprived of proper nourishment because adults are fighting. Qualified medical guidance helps apply the general command safely.

### The Father’s Financial Responsibility

The father is responsible for the mother’s provision and clothing according to what is reasonable—*rizquhunna wa kiswatuhunna bi-l-maʿrūf*. In context, this concerns the nursing mother and child, including the expenses required for care. The father’s responsibility does not end because the parents are divorced. He must provide food, clothing, housing, medical needs, and other reasonable necessities according to his means and the child’s needs.

*Maʿrūf* means fairness recognized by sound custom and revelation. A wealthy father cannot provide the minimum while spending extravagantly on himself, and a struggling father is not required to give what he genuinely cannot afford. He should be transparent about income, avoid hiding assets, and seek fair legal arrangements when parents separate.

### No Parent Is Harmed through the Child

“No mother should be harmed through her child, nor a father through his child” is a comprehensive rule. A husband must not prevent a mother from nursing in order to punish her, and a mother must not use the child to extort, threaten, or cut off the father’s lawful relationship. Neither parent may manipulate custody, visitation, maintenance, or breastfeeding as a weapon.

The child must not be made a messenger in adult disputes. Parents should not interrogate the child about the other parent, make promises they cannot keep, or expose him to violence and humiliation. Where there is abuse, the priority is safety and lawful protection; “shared parenting” cannot be used to return a child to danger.

### The Heir’s Responsibility

If the father dies, “the heir has a similar responsibility.” The child’s maintenance does not disappear with the father. The surviving family or lawful estate bears responsibility according to the rules of inheritance and support. Classical exegetes discuss whether this refers to the heir’s maintenance obligation, not harming the mother, or both. In every reading, a vulnerable child must not be abandoned because the original provider has died.

The estate must first settle debts and valid obligations, then distribute inheritance according to Allah’s law. Relatives who receive wealth should remember that inheritance creates responsibilities as well as benefits. Modern guardianship and state support can assist, but relatives must not assume that an orphan is someone else’s problem.

### Weaning through Consent and Consultation

If both parents decide to wean before two years, there is no blame when they do so by mutual consent and consultation—*tarāḍin minhumā wa tashāwur*. The two conditions protect the child and prevent one parent from making a unilateral decision out of anger. They discuss health, nourishment, emotional readiness, and practical circumstances.

Consultation is a Qur’anic family principle. Parents can seek a paediatrician, lactation specialist, counsellor, or trusted scholar. The decision should not be dictated by relatives who ignore the mother’s health or the child’s needs. Consent must be real and free from threat.

### Employing a Wet Nurse

The verse permits arranging for another woman to nurse the child, provided the payment is made fairly and according to agreement. The wet nurse’s labour is valuable and must not be taken for granted. The agreement should clarify payment, duration, care, and any relevant health information.

Breastfeeding also creates a relationship of *riḍāʿah* in Islamic law, with consequences for marriage eligibility and family ties. Parents must record the identity of the nursing woman and the child’s nursing relationship so that future generations do not unknowingly enter a prohibited marriage. The legal details of the amount and conditions of nursing require qualified guidance.

### The Ethics of Agreement

The āyah repeatedly invokes fairness, capacity, mutual consent, and consultation. This protects the family from rigid formulas that ignore real life. A mother may need paid work, medical treatment, rest, or support. A father may face unemployment or illness. The answer is cooperation and honest assessment, not accusation.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the best spending includes what a person spends on his family and warned against neglecting dependants. Supporting a child is worship when done for Allah and with kindness, not merely a financial burden imposed by court.

### Taqwā and Hidden Conduct

The verse closes with taqwā and the reminder that Allah sees. Parents may deceive courts, hide income, or manipulate a child while appearing respectable to relatives. Allah sees the private arrangement, the missed payment, the exhausted mother, and the frightened child. Taqwā makes parents choose justice even when no one can enforce it.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:234

> Those of you who die and leave wives behind, the wives shall wait by themselves for four months and ten days. When they have completed their waiting period, there is no blame upon you for what they do with themselves in a reasonable manner. Allah is fully aware of what you do.

**Commentary:**

### The Widow’s Waiting Period

This verse establishes the *ʿiddah* of a widow as four months and ten days. It is a period of mourning, legal clarity, and transition after the death of a husband. It differs from the waiting period of a divorced woman and from the waiting period of a pregnant woman, whose term ends at childbirth according to 65:4.

The period is not a punishment or a statement that a widow loses her independence. It gives time for grief, protects lineage, allows immediate legal matters to be organized, and acknowledges the seriousness of a marriage covenant. The widow remains a person with rights, property, work, education, and lawful social contact.

### “By Themselves”

The phrase *yatarabbaṣna bi-anfusihinna* means that the women wait on their own behalf and observe the prescribed period. It does not mean that relatives may imprison them or make every decision for them. A widow may seek help, leave the home for genuine needs, receive visitors, and manage her affairs within the rules of modesty and safety.

The Sunnah clarifies that a widow may go out for necessary work or matters and return to her home, as reported in the account of a woman whose husband died. The pre-Islamic practice of isolating a widow for a full year and subjecting her to degrading treatment was replaced with a defined, dignified period.

### Mourning with Dignity

The waiting period includes *iḥdād*, mourning restrictions concerning adornment and perfume for the prescribed time. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a woman should not mourn a person beyond three days, except for a husband, for whom the period is four months and ten days, in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. This is a defined act of mourning, not a command to erase grief on the forty-first day.

A widow may continue prayer, remembrance, learning, conversation, and necessary work. She should be treated with compassion rather than suspicion. Families should provide food, childcare, financial support, and emotional presence, while respecting her decisions.

### After the Waiting Period

“When they have completed their waiting period, there is no blame upon you for what they do with themselves in a reasonable manner.” After the period, a widow is free to remarry, work, travel, manage property, and make lawful decisions. The phrase *fī anfusihinna bi-l-maʿrūf* emphasizes her agency and the requirement that her choices remain within what is lawful and socially dignified.

Relatives may advise but may not force her into a marriage, prevent a suitable proposal for inheritance reasons, or seize her property. The Qur’an forbids inheriting women against their will and commands kind treatment (4:19). A widow’s grief does not make her a possession of the husband’s family.

### Pregnancy and Other Circumstances

If the widow is pregnant, her waiting period is until she gives birth, as established by Qur’an 65:4 and the report of Subayʿah al-Aslamiyyah in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The four months and ten days apply to the non-pregnant widow. Cases of miscarriage, uncertain pregnancy, irregular bleeding, or simultaneous divorce and death require qualified legal advice.

The husband’s estate must also address her inheritance, deferred dowry, debts, housing, and maintenance according to the law. The waiting period does not cancel her financial rights.

### Allah Is Aware

Allah knows how the widow is treated by relatives, the contents of proposals, hidden pressure, and the true condition of the mourning heart. Human beings may see a family observing outward custom while privately forcing or abusing a widow. The closing reminder protects her from invisible injustice and warns guardians against exploiting grief.

It also comforts the widow who feels unseen. Allah knows the loss, the patience, the private tears, and the choices made under pressure. No act of dignified endurance is lost.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:235

> There is no blame upon you for hinting at a proposal to women or for keeping such an intention to yourselves. Allah knows that you will think of them. But do not make a secret promise with them, except that you speak in an honourable manner. Do not resolve to conclude a marriage contract until the waiting period is complete. Know that Allah knows what is within yourselves, so beware of Him, and know that Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

**Commentary:**

### Proposals during a Waiting Period

This verse addresses approaching a widow or divorced woman during her waiting period. It permits an indirect hint—*taʿrīḍ*—or keeping the intention private, especially because a direct proposal or contract may violate her right to complete the *ʿiddah*. A respectful hint might be a general statement about wishing for marriage or praising a person’s character without demanding a commitment.

The permission recognizes human emotion. A person may think about marriage with a widow, and the widow may consider her future, without being blamed for the mere intention. Allah does not punish involuntary thoughts. He regulates what people say and do so that a vulnerable woman is not pressured or publicly claimed before her waiting period ends.

### Honourable Speech

“Do not make a secret promise with them, except that you speak in an honourable manner.” This allows appropriate, dignified speech while prohibiting a hidden engagement that treats the waiting period as though it has already ended. A secret promise can create jealousy, pressure, false hope, and conflict with the deceased husband’s family or a former spouse.

Honourable speech—*qawl maʿrūf*—is clear enough to avoid deception and restrained enough to avoid emotional manipulation. A person should not exploit grief by promising wealth, protection, or rescue. He should not ask for secrecy that isolates the woman from trusted family or legal support.

### No Marriage Contract before the Period Ends

A marriage contract may not be concluded until the *ʿiddah* is complete. The waiting period belongs to the woman and protects her legal status. A contract made before it ends is invalid or unlawful according to the details of the case and legal school. If a person is unsure whether a period has ended, he should wait and seek qualified advice.

This rule also prevents confusion about lineage and preserves the seriousness of marriage. A new marriage should begin after the previous relationship’s legal consequences have been completed, not in the middle of grief, pregnancy uncertainty, or revocable divorce.

### Respect for the Widow’s Agency

The verse does not make the woman passive. She may consider a proposal, refuse it, seek advice, and plan her future. The restriction is on the manner and timing of approach, not on her ability to think. Relatives must not use the waiting period to claim her inheritance, promise her to a cousin, or prevent a lawful future marriage.

The same principle applies to a divorced woman whose waiting period remains. If the divorce is revocable, the former husband’s rights and the possibility of return must be respected, while other men must not compete through secret promises. If the divorce is final, indirect discussion may still be governed by the rules of the specific waiting period.

### Allah Knows the Heart

Allah knows what people conceal within themselves. This is a comfort because sincere intentions are visible to Him, and a warning because hidden manipulation is also visible. A person may speak in carefully chosen words while planning to exploit a woman’s grief or property. Allah knows the difference between compassion and predation.

The verse does not treat every private thought as a sin. The warning concerns the transition from thought to secret promises, pressure, and premature contract. Faith teaches people to manage desire with honesty rather than pretending that human emotions do not exist.

### Forgiveness and Forbearance

Allah is *Ghafūr* and *Ḥalīm*. He forgives mistakes and is forbearing with servants who are learning how to navigate grief, divorce, and desire. A person who crossed a boundary should stop, clarify matters, and seek forgiveness. Forbearance gives time to correct before harm becomes permanent.

Mercy is shown through clear rules. A woman is protected from being claimed while vulnerable, a man is protected from an unlawful contract, and families are protected from disputes about timing and lineage.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:236

> There is no blame upon you if you divorce women before you have touched them or fixed a bridal gift for them. But provide for them a suitable gift: the wealthy according to his means and the poor according to his means—a reasonable provision, a duty upon those who do good.

**Commentary:**

### A Marriage May End before Consummation

This verse addresses a marriage that ends before sexual consummation and before a specific *mahr*, or bridal gift, has been fixed. It acknowledges that a marriage contract can fail early and removes the assumption that divorce itself is always a sin. At the same time, it commands the husband to provide a suitable gift—*mutʿah*—so that the woman is not left humiliated or unsupported after the relationship ends.

The verse’s compassion matters because an early divorce can still cause emotional pain, social embarrassment, and financial loss. A woman should not be treated as though nothing happened merely because the marriage was not consummated. The husband owes honourable conduct even when a specific dowry is not legally due in the same way as after consummation.

### The Mutʿah Gift

*Mutʿah* here means a compensatory provision appropriate to the husband’s means and the recognized standard of fairness. It can include money, clothing, household items, or another useful gift. The amount should not be insulting or so extravagant that it creates a new injustice. The wealthy give according to their abundance, while the poor give according to what they can genuinely afford.

The verse rejects a single fixed amount that ignores circumstances. Wealth, local custom, the length of the engagement or marriage, the woman’s needs, and the reason for separation may all be relevant. The gift is not a purchase of the woman’s silence or a payment that permits cruelty. It is an expression of responsibility and good treatment.

### No Blame Does Not Mean No Ethics

“There is no blame upon you” removes blame for the divorce under the specified conditions, not for lying, insult, coercion, or broken promises. The couple must still preserve privacy, return property, and avoid spreading accusations. A person cannot say that no dowry was fixed and therefore nothing at all is owed in kindness.

Likewise, the wife should not be treated as morally defective because the marriage ended early. Families and communities must resist gossip and protect her dignity. A broken marriage is not proof that one person carries every fault.

### Divorce before Touching

The Qur’an uses “before you have touched them”—*mā lam tamassūhunna*—as a modest expression for consummation. If a marriage ends before consummation and no dowry was fixed, the mutʿah is required or strongly emphasized according to the legal details, while the moral command of kindness is clear. If a specified dowry was fixed, 2:237 gives the rule of half the dowry, subject to waiver and the circumstances described there.

The waiting period also differs. A woman divorced before consummation does not observe the ordinary waiting period, as stated in 33:49, though legal advice is needed when pregnancy or other complications are possible.

### Ability and Fairness

The difference between the wealthy and poor gives a general principle of family law: obligations are measured by ability and fairness. Islam does not demand that a poor man give what would destroy his ability to eat or support dependants, but it does not permit the wealthy to hide behind a token gift while spending freely elsewhere.

The command also encourages documentation. A couple should clarify the dowry and financial terms at the beginning of marriage, while understanding that unclear or missing documentation does not erase Allah’s rights. Transparent agreements prevent disputes and protect both parties.

### A Duty upon the Doers of Good

The final phrase calls the provision a duty upon the *muḥsinīn*, those who do good. Even where the minimum legal obligation is debated, *iḥsān* calls for generosity, apology, and a dignified conclusion. A husband may provide more than the minimum because he wants to close the relationship without bitterness.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that no believer should despise a believing woman because of one disliked quality, and he encouraged kindness in family relationships. When divorce is necessary, the departing spouses should remember the good that existed and avoid rewriting the entire marriage as evil.

### A Broader Ethical Lesson

The verse teaches that ending a contract does not erase the moral relationship formed through it. Employers owe wages after employment ends, business partners settle accounts, and spouses retain obligations after divorce. Lawfulness is the floor; *iḥsān* is the higher standard that turns a painful conclusion into a just one.

A community can help by providing mediation, legal advice, financial support, and protection from stigma. The woman’s future should not be destroyed by an early divorce.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:237

> If you divorce them before you have touched them but after a bridal gift has been fixed, then half of the specified gift is due, unless the women waive it or the one in whose hand is the marriage contract waives it. To waive it is closer to taqwā. Do not forget graciousness between yourselves. Surely Allah sees what you do.

**Commentary:**

### The Half-Dowry Rule

This verse gives the financial rule when a marriage ends before consummation but after a specific *mahr* has been agreed. The woman is entitled to half of the specified dowry. The rule recognizes the contract and the emotional reality of the marriage while also accounting for the fact that consummation did not occur.

The dowry is the woman’s right, not a purchase price and not a gift that the husband can reclaim at will. Once specified, it belongs to her according to the contract. If the husband has already paid the full amount, the legal details concerning return of half are handled through fairness and consent; he may choose to leave the whole amount with her. If it was deferred, the due portion must be settled.

### Voluntary Waiver

The woman may waive her right voluntarily, and the husband may choose to pay more than the required half or release any claim to a return. The verse does not permit pressure. A family, guardian, or husband must not frighten or shame a woman into surrendering her dowry. A waiver obtained through coercion is not the graciousness praised by the verse.

Classical exegetes discussed the phrase “the one in whose hand is the marriage contract”—*alladhī bi-yadihi ʿuqdatu al-nikāḥ*. Many identified him as the husband, who can choose to pay the full amount and complete the gift. Some jurists discussed the role of a guardian in particular circumstances. These details require qualified legal advice; the ethical principle is that the woman’s financial right is protected and any generosity is voluntary.

### Generosity Is Closer to Taqwā

“To waive it is closer to taqwā” encourages the more generous course. The woman may leave the half without resentment, and the husband may pay the full amount without using the law’s minimum to impoverish her. Generosity is not the same as surrender under pressure. It is an act chosen for Allah after one’s right and safety have been recognized.

The verse thus prevents legal entitlement from becoming the whole measure of conduct. A person may be within the minimum rule yet act harshly. *Taqwā* asks what is fair, compassionate, and likely to reduce harm. It also recognizes that the poorer spouse may need the full amount and should not be expected to perform generosity for public approval.

### Do Not Forget Graciousness

“Do not forget graciousness between yourselves”—*wa-lā tansaw al-faḍl baynakum*—is a beautiful principle for separation. The couple may remember the good that existed, refrain from exposing secrets, return belongings, speak fairly about one another, and protect any children from the conflict. Even when love has ended, basic honour can remain.

Graciousness does not require denying abuse or concealing a danger from those who need protection. It means that truth is spoken without revenge and rights are pursued without unnecessary humiliation. A person can make a lawful claim and still show *faḍl*, excellence and generosity.

### Divorce and Financial Justice

The verse belongs with a series that prevents economic abuse. A husband may not divorce a wife before consummation and use the unsettled dowry to punish her. A wife may not falsely claim payment or seize property beyond her right. Both parties should document what was paid, what remains due, and what each voluntarily waives.

Modern contracts may include a prompt and deferred dowry, gifts, and shared property. The parties should seek legal and religious advice at the beginning rather than waiting for anger. Clear records are a protection, not a sign of mistrust.

### Allah Sees the Hidden Agreement

Allah sees whether the waiver was voluntary, whether the husband hid assets, whether the family pressured the woman, and whether the couple preserved each other’s dignity. Human witnesses may hear the formal agreement, but Allah knows the private threat or manipulation behind it.

This closing reminder also comforts the person whose generosity was not appreciated. Allah sees the full gift even when people speak as though the giver did nothing. It restrains the person who boasts about waiving a right or humiliates the other for receiving it.

### A Culture of Kind Separation

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the best people are those best in character, and he displayed kindness even in disagreement. A Muslim community should make it possible for marriages to end without turning divorce into social death. Mediation, fair financial settlement, counselling, and protection from gossip are part of the Qur’anic ideal.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:238

> Guard strictly the prayers, especially the middle prayer, and stand before Allah devoutly.

**Commentary:**

### Guarding the Prayers

After a long passage on marriage, divorce, and family rights, the Qur’an returns the heart to its central covenant: prayer. “Guard the prayers”—*ḥāfiẓū ʿalā al-ṣalawāt*—means preserving their times, conditions, purification, direction, movements, recitation, and inward attention. Prayer is not an occasional refuge from life’s problems; it is the regular structure that keeps life oriented toward Allah.

The plural “prayers” refers to the five obligatory prayers established by the Sunnah. Guarding them includes planning work, travel, school, and family responsibilities around prayer rather than treating prayer as whatever remains after every other demand. A person who misses through genuine sleep or forgetfulness prays when he remembers, while deliberate neglect requires repentance and practical change.

### The Middle Prayer

Allah gives special emphasis to the middle prayer—*al-ṣalāt al-wusṭā*. The strongest transmitted interpretation identifies it as ʿAṣr, though the word “middle” has generated discussion among the exegetes. The special emphasis teaches that a prayer placed in the middle of work and daylight can be neglected precisely because worldly activity is intense.

The Prophet ﷺ warned that whoever misses the ʿAṣr prayer is as though his deeds have been nullified, in the authentic reports of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The warning highlights its importance; it does not invite believers to neglect the other prayers. Every ṣalāh is obligatory and precious.

### Standing before Allah

“Stand before Allah devoutly”—*qūmū lillāhi qānitīn*—describes humble obedience, quiet attentiveness, and sincere submission. The body stands, but the heart recognizes that it is in the presence of its Lord. *Qunūt* can mean obedience, humility, prolonged devotion, and silence where the prayer requires it.

Prayer is not a performance for people nearby. A person may stand in a beautiful row while his mind is chasing reputation, profit, or resentment. The believer continually returns his attention to the recitation, the meanings of the words, and the reality of standing before Allah.

### Prayer and Family Law

The placement of the verse shows that divine guidance about family does not replace worship, and worship does not excuse family injustice. Prayer should make people more truthful in divorce, more patient in conflict, and more careful with the rights of children and spouses. If a person prays while continuing deliberate abuse without repentance, he has not allowed prayer to perform its moral work.

The Prophet ﷺ was deeply devoted in prayer and also the most merciful in his household. He did not use worship to avoid practical responsibilities. His example joins private devotion with public fairness.

### Guarding Prayer in Difficulty

The next verse gives concessions during danger, showing that prayer is not abandoned even in crisis. Its form may change according to safety, illness, and ability, but the obligation remains. This teaches that prayer anchors the believer in ordinary life and in emergency.

Guarding prayer can require practical preparation: learning the times, keeping wudūʾ, identifying a clean place, arranging work breaks, and asking for reasonable accommodation. Parents teach children gradually, and communities should make mosques safe and accessible for women, elderly people, disabled people, and travellers.

### The Inner and Outer Prayer

The prayer has outward conditions and an inward spirit. Correct timing and movement matter, while *khushūʿ*—humble attentiveness—deepens its effect. A person should not abandon the outward form because concentration is difficult; he patiently trains the heart. Recitation with understanding, slowing down, remembering death, and reducing distractions can help.

The Qur’an says prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing (29:45). This is a promise and a test. The servant keeps returning to prayer even when he has fallen, then works to let the prayer reshape his conduct.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:239

> If you are in fear, then pray while walking or riding. When you are secure, remember Allah as He has taught you what you did not know.

**Commentary:**

### Prayer in Danger

The previous verse commanded guarding the prayers, and this verse explains that danger changes the form but does not erase the obligation. If a person is under attack, fleeing a threat, travelling through immediate danger, or unable to stand safely, he prays while walking or riding according to his ability. This is part of *ṣalāt al-khawf*, the prayer of fear, and part of the wider principle that Allah does not burden a soul beyond capacity.

The concession protects both life and worship. A person does not stand exposed to danger merely to preserve an ordinary posture, and he does not abandon prayer because circumstances are difficult. He turns toward the qiblah where possible, performs the movements he can, and signals or adapts when necessary according to reliable legal guidance.

### The Prophet’s Practice

The Qur’an gives a detailed form of the prayer of fear in 4:102, and the Prophet ﷺ performed it in more than one way depending on the battlefield. The variations teach flexibility within revelation. The essential purpose is to remember Allah while protecting the community and maintaining vigilance.

These rules should not be turned into a performance by untrained individuals in dangerous settings. A person follows the established prayer of fear for his circumstances and consults qualified scholars where possible. The principle is clear even when the details vary.

### Walking or Riding

Walking and riding represent movement under pressure. The worshipper may be unable to face the qiblah, bow, or prostrate normally. Allah accepts the prayer according to ability. A person in a vehicle, aircraft, boat, emergency shelter, or medical transport applies the relevant concessions without abandoning the prayer.

The concession is not for ordinary laziness. When security returns, the normal form returns as well. Islam distinguishes genuine incapacity from convenience while refusing to make genuine incapacity a cause of guilt.

### When Secure, Remember Allah as Taught

“When you are secure, remember Allah as He has taught you.” Security brings the obligation to restore the ordinary prayer, not merely to continue the emergency form. The phrase reminds believers that they did not invent the prayer in fear; Allah taught it through revelation and the Messenger’s practice.

The instruction also includes gratitude after danger. A person who escapes an accident, conflict, illness, or fear remembers Allah for protection. Security should increase obedience, not make the servant forget the Lord who carried him through danger.

### Prayer and Practical Precaution

The verse does not oppose security planning. A person may assign guards, seek shelter, travel with companions, delay movement, or use communication tools while praying. The Prophet ﷺ took military precautions and arranged the prayer of fear with attention to real threats. Tawakkul includes using means while relying upon Allah.

In personal danger, protecting life is important. A victim of violence should seek safety, call for help, and pray in the way possible. No one should be shamed for using a concession during a medical or security emergency.

### Knowledge Is a Gift

Allah says He taught believers what they did not know. The forms of worship are not products of cultural invention. The community learns through the Qur’an, the Sunnah, and the transmission of qualified scholarship. This should produce gratitude and humility rather than argument over personal preference.

The verse also establishes that knowledge has a practical purpose. The details of prayer are learned so that the servant can worship in health, travel, fear, and illness. A Muslim should learn the essentials before an emergency, just as he prepares emergency supplies before a crisis.

### Reassurance in Crisis

Prayer in danger tells the frightened heart that Allah remains near even when ordinary order has collapsed. The worshipper may not be able to pray beautifully or at length, but the smallest sincere act of remembrance matters. Allah sees the fear, the effort, and the intention.

Once security returns, the believer continues to remember Allah and to repair any missed or incomplete obligations according to qualified guidance. The crisis may become a source of greater reliance and gratitude.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:238–239 on guarding prayer, fear, movement, and restoring normal worship after security.
- Qur’an 4:101–103 on shortening prayer during travel and the detailed prayer of fear.
- Qur’an 2:286 and 64:16 on capacity and responsibility.
- Qur’an 8:60 on taking precautions while remaining within justice.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports describing the Prophet’s different forms of ṣalāt al-khawf and prayer while travelling or riding.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:240

> Those of you who die and leave wives behind should make a bequest for their wives: maintenance for one year without expelling them. But if they leave, there is no blame upon you for what they do with themselves in a reasonable manner. Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise.

**Commentary:**

### Security for Widows

This āyah addresses the financial and residential security of widows after a husband’s death. It instructs that a bequest should provide maintenance for one year and that the widow should not be expelled from the marital home during that period. The command recognizes that bereavement can combine emotional loss with sudden economic vulnerability. A widow should not be left without shelter while relatives divide the estate.

The instruction is a protection, not a reason to control the widow. She may remain in the home if she chooses and if it is safe, but the verse also says that if she leaves, there is no blame upon her for making lawful choices. Relatives must not imprison her, seize her property, or treat her as a burden attached to the estate.

### Relationship to the Four-Month Waiting Period

The previous verse establishes the widow’s waiting period as four months and ten days, while this verse mentions maintenance and residence for one year. Classical scholars carefully discussed the relationship between the two passages. The later legal framework, including 2:234 and the inheritance verses in Sūrah al-Nisāʾ (4:12), establishes the widow’s waiting period and her fixed inheritance share. Many exegetes considered the earlier one-year residence instruction abrogated or limited in its legal application by these later revelations; others preserved aspects of the right to residence and maintenance through different legal reasoning.

The enduring principle is certain even where the historical legal sequence is discussed: a widow’s inheritance, housing, and support must be protected, and no relative may expel or impoverish her unlawfully. Current application should follow qualified Islamic legal advice and the law of the country, especially where property and children are involved.

### Maintenance and Inheritance

Maintenance is not the same as inheritance. A widow may receive her prescribed share of the estate under 4:12, and she may also have rights to deferred dowry, debts owed to her, housing, and support. The estate must be administered transparently. Relatives cannot say, “You have inherited, so we owe you nothing,” while taking control of assets or refusing the rights established by law.

Where the deceased left young children, the widow may need additional support as their mother and caregiver. The community, relatives, charitable funds, and lawful public systems should help. A widow should not be forced to sell her home or marry a relative in order to survive.

### No Expulsion

“Without expelling them” condemns a common form of injustice. A husband’s family may attempt to remove the widow immediately, change locks, seize documents, or force her to leave with nothing. Such conduct violates the dignity and security the verse protects. If the home is unsafe or legally belongs to another person under valid law, the solution is secure alternative housing and fair settlement—not abandonment.

The prohibition also has a moral dimension. The widow should not be expelled from the family’s care because the husband has died. She remains a human being with honour and rights, not a person whose value ended with the marriage.

### Freedom after Bereavement

“If they leave, there is no blame upon you for what they do with themselves in a reasonable manner.” The widow may leave the residence after the relevant waiting period or when the legal framework permits, remarry, work, travel for need, manage wealth, and make other lawful decisions. Relatives may advise but may not dictate her future.

The phrase *fī anfusihinna bi-l-maʿrūf* includes dignified adornment, lawful social activity, and remarriage after the waiting period. It does not mean that a widow must remain permanently in mourning or that the husband’s family owns her choices.

### Allah’s Might and Wisdom

Allah is *ʿAzīz* and *Ḥakīm*. His might protects the rights of people whom human power may ignore, and His wisdom appears in the gradual development of family law. The community may debate the historical relation between verses, but it may not use technical disagreement as an excuse to exploit widows.

The divine law also recognizes different family structures, property arrangements, and circumstances. The executor must seek guidance rather than applying a rigid formula without considering safety, inheritance, debts, children, and the widow’s consent.

### Community Responsibility

The verse’s instruction begins with those who die and leave wives, but the wider community must help fulfil its purpose. Relatives should provide food, shelter, companionship, legal support, and protection from harassment. Charitable institutions should establish transparent widow and orphan support. Leaders must enforce property rights and protect women from coercion.

The Prophet ﷺ praised the person who strives to support widows and the poor, comparing that effort to great worship in the authentic reports of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Caring for a widow is not charity that creates superiority; it is a duty and honour before Allah.
