## Verse 1

Verse 1 says: *Alif-Lãm-Mĩm.*

In simple terms, the verse opens the surah with the detached letters — Alif, Lam, Mim — the Qur'an's known openings whose true meaning is with Allah.

"Alif-Lãm-Mĩm" — the verse's letters: the huruf muqatta'at — the detached, the disjointed letters at the openers of twenty-nine surahs of the Qur'an. Ibn Kathir: we already explained the tafsir of Alif-Lam-Mim at the beginning of Surat Al-Baqarah — the letters are among the surah's openers; their reality is known to Allah, and their recitation is the faith's test: the Arabs' own letters, the same ones they used for their speech, and the Qur'an of these same letters is the challenge that they could not meet. The verse's frame: the letters — the Qur'an's alphabet — the challenge in the very tongue of the objectors: the speech of these letters is the inimitable.

The verse's place: the opening of Surat Al 'Imran — revealed in Al-Madinah (Ibn Kathir: as evident from the fact that the first eighty-three ayat relate to the delegation from Najran that arrived in Al-Madinah in the ninth year of the Hijrah). The verse's frame: the surah's opening with the letters and the divine names (3:2) — the same pattern as Al-Baqarah: the challenge and the creed.

The verse's lesson: the letters are the sign — the revelation's miracle in the people's own alphabet. And the reader's part: recite them as revealed — their full meaning is with the One who revealed them.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 2

Verse 2 says: *Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him —the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining.*

In simple terms, the verse declares the divine Oneness: no god except Allah — the Ever-Living, the All-Sustaining.

"Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him" — the verse's shahada: la ilaha illa Huwa — the negation and the affirmation: no god, except Him. Ibn Kathir: he explained the meaning of "Allah! La ilaha illa Huwa, Al-Hayyul-Qayyum" in the tafsir of Ayat al-Kursi (2:255) — the same formula that opens the greatest verse of the Qur'an. The verse's frame: the surah's foundation: the Oneness before the revelation's story and the battles' lessons: the creed of the whole surah.

"—the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining" — the verse's two names: al-Hayy — the Ever-Living — al-Qayyum — the All-Sustaining — the Self-Subsisting, the One by Whom all subsists. The verse's frame: the same names of 2:255: the Life that never dies and the Sustenance that never fails: the One who lives and keeps all living.

The verse's lesson: the Oneness is the surah's key — the Living and the Sustaining are the first knowledge. And the reader's part: begin with the Oneness — every verse and every story of Al 'Imran is the unfolding of the no-god-except-Him.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 3

Verse 3 says: *He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth, confirming what came before it, as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel*

In simple terms, the verse speaks of the revelation: the Book was sent down in truth, confirming what came before it — as the Torah and the Gospel were revealed.

"He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth" — the verse's revelation: the nazzala — the sent down — the Kitab — the Book — the bi al-haqq — in truth. Ibn Kathir: "It is He Who has sent down the Book to you with truth" — means, revealed the Qur'an to you, O Muhammad, in truth — there is no doubt or suspicion that it is revealed from Allah — Allah revealed the Qur'an with His knowledge, and the angels testify to this fact — Allah is sufficient as a Witness. Al-Jalalayn: the Qur'an, enveloped by the truth, with veracity in what it announces. The verse's frame: the truth of the descent: the Book is not the report of a poet or the invention of a sage: the truth is the Book's badge.

"confirming what came before it" — the verse's confirmation: the musaddiqan lima bayna yadayhi — confirming what came before it. Ibn Kathir: from the previous divinely revealed Books, sent to the servants and Prophets of Allah — these Books testify to the truth of the Qur'an, and the Qur'an also testifies to the truth these Books contained — including the news and glad tidings of Muhammad's prophethood and the revelation of the Glorious Qur'an. The verse's frame: the two-way confirmation: the previous Books announced him; the Qur'an confirms their true content: the message is one — the confirmers and the confirmed meet in the single truth.

"as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel" — the verse's earlier revelations: the Tawrah — the Torah — to Musa — the Injil — the Gospel — to 'Isa. Ibn Kathir: He revealed the Torah and the Gospel — the previous scriptures of the People of the Book. The verse's frame: the three revelations: the Torah, the Gospel, the Qur'an — the chain: each confirming and confirmed: the People of the Book's own scriptures are the witnesses of this Book.

The verse's lesson: the Book in truth, confirming the Books before — the Qur'an is the confirmation's crown. And the reader's part: the revelation is one line from the Torah to the Gospel to the Qur'an — the truth that confirms is the truth that is confirmed.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 4

Verse 4 says: *previously, as a guide for people, and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺. Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations will suffer a severe torment. For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment.*

In simple terms, the verse continues: the Torah and the Gospel came previously as guidance for people; and He revealed the Standard (al-Furqan); and those who reject the revelations will suffer a severe torment — Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment.

"previously, as a guide for people" — the verse's guidance: the min qablu — previously — the hudan li al-nas — a guidance for people. Ibn Kathir: the Torah and the Gospel revealed before — as a guidance to mankind. The verse's frame: the scriptures' purpose: the guidance of the people — the Torah for the Children of Israel, the Gospel for its followers: the guidance's continuity.

"and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺" — the verse's Furqan: the al-Furqan — the Criterion, the Standard — the separator between the truth and the falsehood. Ibn Kathir (and the classical notes): the Furqan — the Qur'an, the decisive authority — the distinguisher between the right and the wrong, the guidance and the misguidance (the tafsir notes: "as a guidance to people, and sent down the Criterion" — the decisive authority; some: the miracles of the Prophet). The verse's frame: the revealed Standard: the judgment's scale: in the Qur'an the truth is separated from the falsehood — the decisive book for the disputes.

"Surely those who reject Allah's revelations will suffer a severe torment" — the verse's warning: the kafaru bi ayat Allah — those who reject the revelations — the 'adhab shadid — the severe torment. Ibn Kathir: the warning against those who reject His ayat and defy His honorable Messengers and great Prophets — the severe punishment. The verse's frame: the rejection's wage: the signs are clear — the denial after the clarity is the severe torment's cause.

"For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment" — the verse's close: the 'Aziz — the Almighty — the dhi intiqam — the capable of punishment, the possessor of retribution. The verse's frame: the Almighty's power and the punishment's capability: the One who revealed the Standard is the One who enforces it.

The verse's lesson: the revelation's history is the guidance's chain — the standard is the distinctor, and the rejection of the clear signs is the severe torment. And the reader's part: take the Standard — the truth and the falsehood are separated in it — and fear the Almighty's capability.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 5

Verse 5 says: *Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah.*

In simple terms, the verse declares: nothing on the earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah — His knowledge encompasses all.

"Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah" — the verse's encompassing: the la yakhfa — nothing is hidden — the 'ala Allah — from Allah — the fi al-ard — on the earth — the wa la fi al-sama' — nor in the heavens. Ibn Kathir: the knowledge of Allah encompasses all that is in the heavens and the earth — nothing is hidden from Him — the smallest and the greatest. The verse's frame: the divine knowledge: the hidden's domain — the secret of the wombs, the paths of the night, the intentions of the hearts: the not-hidden from the Knower.

The verse's place: between the revelation (3:1–4) and the womb's shaping (3:6) — the knowledge's frame: the One who revealed the Standard knows the hidden — the revelation's truth is the Knower's speech. The verse's frame: the connection: nothing is hidden from the One whose Book is in truth.

The verse's lesson: the hidden has no hiding — the earth's depths and the heavens' heights are the opened to His knowledge. And the reader's part: live in the open of His knowledge — the secret is His — and the revealed Standard is His speech.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 6

Verse 6 says: *He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise.*

In simple terms, the verse speaks of the womb's shaping: Allah forms each of you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills; and there is no god except Him — the Almighty, the All-Wise.

"He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills" — the verse's shaping: the yusawwirukum — He shapes you — the fi al-arham — in the wombs — the kayfa yasha' — as He wills. Ibn Kathir (and the classical notes): Allah forms the creation in the wombs of the mothers as He wills — the male and the female, the beautiful and the otherwise, the long and the short — the shaping's variety is the Will's work — as in the hadith of the creation's stages (the nutfah, the 'alaqah, the mudghah; the angel records the provision, the deeds, the term, and the misery or the happiness; the soul is breathed). The verse's frame: the womb's art: the shaping is the divine form: the mother's womb is the workshop of the Will — the unseen knowledge of the Not-Hidden (3:5) becomes the visible form.

"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise" — the verse's close: the la ilaha illa Huwa — the 'Aziz — the Almighty — al-Hakim — the All-Wise. The verse's frame: the Shaper is the Only — the Almighty's power in the womb's formation, the All-Wise's purpose in every shape: the formed and the forming.

The verse's lesson: every shape in every womb is the Will's signature — the One who forms is the Only — the Almighty, the All-Wise. And the reader's part: the shaping of your own form is the proof of the Shaper: the Only One, the Almighty, the All-Wise.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 7

Verse 7 says: *He is the One Who has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book, of which some verses are precise—they are the foundation of the Book—while others are elusive. Those with deviant hearts follow the elusive verses seeking ˹to spread˺ doubt through their ˹false˺ interpretations—but none grasps their ˹full˺ meaning except Allah. As for those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, “We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord.” But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason.*

In simple terms, the verse divides the Book: some verses are precise — the foundation; others are elusive; the deviant-hearted follow the elusive seeking doubt, but none knows their full meaning except Allah; the well-grounded in knowledge say: we believe — it is all from our Lord.

"of which some verses are precise—they are the foundation of the Book" — the verse's muhkamat: the ayat muhkamat — the precise, the entirely clear — the umm al-Kitab — the mother of the Book, the foundation. Ibn Kathir: the muhkamat — entirely clear and plain — the foundations of the Book, plain for everyone; the ayat that explain the abrogating rulings, the allowed, the prohibited, the laws, the limits, the obligations and the rulings that should be believed in and implemented. The verse's frame: the foundation verses: the clear commandments, the decisive rulings: the mother of the Book — the reference point of the understanding.

"while others are elusive" — the verse's mutashabihat: the ukhari mutashabihat — the others, the elusive, the ambiguous. Ibn Kathir: the mutashabihat — not entirely clear for many people — they include the abrogated ayat, the parables, the oaths, and what should be believed but not implemented — the wording encompasses a wide area of meanings. The verse's frame: the elusive: the verses of the multiple senses — the ones whose apparent meaning can be turned by the interpreters: the test of the faith.

"Those with deviant hearts follow the elusive verses seeking ˹to spread˺ doubt through their ˹false˺ interpretations" — the verse's deviants: the alladhina fi qulubihim zaygh — those in whose hearts there is deviation — the yattabi'una ma tashabaha — they follow the elusive — the ibtigha' al-fitnah — seeking the doubt — the ibtigha' ta'wilih — seeking its interpretation. Ibn Kathir: those who are misguided and deviate from truth to falsehood — they follow the mutashabih because they are able to alter its meanings to conform with their false interpretation — as for the muhkam they cannot be altered because they are clear. The hadith (al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, from 'A'ishah): the Prophet recited this ayah and said — "When you see those who seek the ambiguous verses in it, they are those whom Allah has named. Beware of them!" The verse's frame: the deviance's method: the elusive is the instrument of the fitnah — the false interpretation rides the multiple meanings: the deviants cite the ambiguous to fight the clear.

"but none grasps their ˹full˺ meaning except Allah" — the verse's knowledge: the wa ma ya'lamu ta'wilahu illa Allah — none knows its interpretation except Allah. Ibn Kathir: only Allah knows the true ta'wil of the mutashabihat — the tafsir's four types (Ibn 'Abbas): the tafsir the Arabs know in their language; the tafsir no one is excused of being ignorant of; the tafsir the scholars know; and the tafsir that only Allah knows. The verse's frame: the full meaning's reserve: the realities of the unseen (the Hour, the worlds' realities) are reserved: the pause at the divine Name is the school of Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn Kathir — the interpretation of the unseeable is Allah's alone.

"As for those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, "We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord."" — the verse's firmly rooted: the al-rasikhuna fi al-'ilm — those well-grounded in knowledge — they believe in the whole — the muhkam and the mutashabih — the kullun min 'indi Rabbina — it is all from our Lord. Ibn Kathir: "all of it is from our Lord" — both the muhkam and the mutashabih are true and authentic, and each one testifies to the truth of the other — because they both are from Allah, and nothing that comes from Allah is ever met by contradiction or discrepancy. The verse's frame: the rooted believers' stance: the whole is the Lord's: the clear is obeyed, the elusive is believed — the muhkam is the key of the mutashabih.

"But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason" — the verse's close: the ma yadhdhakkaru illa ulu al-albab — none will be mindful except the people of reason. Ibn Kathir: those who have good minds and sound comprehension — understand, contemplate and comprehend the meaning in the correct manner. The verse's frame: the reason's receivers: the mindful are the ones who refer the unclear to the clear and believe the whole.

The verse's lesson: the Book is the clear foundation and the elusive test; the deviants ride the elusive; the rooted believe the whole; and the mindful are the receivers. And the reader's part: take the muhkam as the key; believe the mutashabih; beware of the arguers of the ambiguous — and say with the rooted: we believe, it is all from our Lord.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 8

Verse 8 says: *˹They say,˺ “Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us. Grant us Your mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺.*

In simple terms, the verse is the prayer of the well-grounded: Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us; grant us Your mercy — You are the Giver of all bounties.

"Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us" — the verse's plea: the la tuzigh qulubana — do not make our hearts deviate — the ba'da idh hadaytana — after You have guided us. Ibn Kathir: "Do not deviate our hearts from the guidance after You allowed them to acquire it. Do not make us like those who have wickedness in their hearts, those who follow the mutashabih in the Qur'an." The verse's frame: the rooted ones' fear: the guidance's stability is the request: the deviance after the guidance is the ruin — the well-grounded do not boast of the knowledge; they pray for the heart's steadiness.

"Grant us Your mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺" — the verse's mercy: the hab lana min ladunka rahmah — grant us mercy from You — the innaka anta al-Wahhab — You are the Bestower, the Giver. Ibn Kathir: "and grant us mercy from You" — the mercy of the guidance's keeping — the Wahhab: the Giver without measure. The verse's frame: the mercy before the gathering (3:9): the steadfastness is the mercy's grant.

The verse's lesson: the guidance's keeper is the prayer: after the guidance, the request of the steadfastness and the mercy. And the reader's part: pray it with the rooted — do not let the hearts deviate after the guidance, and grant the mercy, O Giver of all bounties.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 9

Verse 9 says: *Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day—about which there is no doubt. Surely Allah does not break His promise.”*

In simple terms, the verse closes the prayer: You will gather all humanity for the Day about which there is no doubt — Allah does not break His promise.

"Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day" — the verse's gathering: the innaka jami' al-nas — You will gather the people — the li-yawmin la rayba fihi — for a Day about which there is no doubt. Ibn Kathir: "Our Lord! Verily, it is You Who will gather mankind together on the Day about which there is no doubt" — the rooted believers' confession of the resurrection: the same gathering of the first and the last: the Day of the standing before the Lord. The verse's frame: the prayer's horizon: after the guidance's plea (3:8), the Day's confession: the gathering is the Lord's promise.

"—about which there is no doubt. Surely Allah does not break His promise" — the verse's certainty: the la rayba fihi — no doubt in it — the la yukhlif Allah al-mi'ad — Allah does not break His promise. Ibn Kathir: the promise of the gathering — the Word Who does not fail: the believers' certainty is the promise's stability. The verse's frame: the promise's keeper: the Day is sure because the Promiser does not fail: the gathered after the guided.

The verse's lesson: the gathering is certain; the promise is unbreakable — the well-grounded believe the Day as they believe the Book. And the reader's part: stand in the Day's certainty — the One who guided promises the gathering, and the promise is the unbroken.

## Verse 10

Verse 10 says: *Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah—and they will be the fuel for the Fire.*

In simple terms, the verse warns: the wealth and children of the disbelievers will not benefit them against Allah — they will be the firewood of the Fire.

"Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah" — the verse's uselessness: the lan tughniya 'anhum amwaluhum — their wealth will not avail — the wa la awladuhum — nor their children — the min Allah — against Allah. Ibn Kathir (with 3:116 and the classical notes): the wealth and the children — the measures of this world's pride — do not avail against Allah: the Day's currency is the deed, not the counted. The verse's frame: the world's powerlessness: the treasures and the offspring are the world's strengths — and they are the strengths that fail at the meeting with Allah.

"—and they will be the fuel for the Fire" — the verse's fuel: the wa hum waqud al-nar — they are the fuel of the Fire. Ibn Kathir (cf. 2:24): the disbelievers are the firewood of the Fire — as the fire is fed by the wood, the Fire is fed by the ones who made the wealth and the children their refuge: the fuel of the place whose food is the denial. The verse's frame: the reversal: the pride's measure becomes the Fire's food.

The verse's lesson: the wealth and the children are the trusts, not the shields — the Day's benefit is the deed of the One who owns them. And the reader's part: do not lean on the counted — the wealth and the children avail nothing against Allah — and the fuel of the Fire is the denial.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 11

Verse 11 says: *Their fate will be like that of the people of Pharaoh and those before them —they all rejected Our signs, so Allah seized them for their sins. And Allah is severe in punishment.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the precedent: their fate is like the people of Pharaoh and those before them — they rejected the signs, and Allah seized them for their sins — and Allah is severe in punishment.

"Their fate will be like that of the people of Pharaoh and those before them" — the verse's precedent: the sunnat al-qawm — the way of the people of Fir'awn — the wa alladhina min qablihim — and those before them. Ibn Kathir: the people of Pharaoh and those before them — the rejectors of the past — their pattern: the denial, the warning, the seizure. The verse's frame: the history's law: the earlier nations' fate is the mirror of the current denial: the Qur'an gathers the precedents.

"they all rejected Our signs, so Allah seized them for their sins" — the verse's seizure: the kadhdhabu bi ayatina — they rejected Our signs — the fa-akhadhahum Allah bi dhunubihim — so Allah seized them for their sins. Ibn Kathir: the rejection of the signs, then the seizure — the punishment for the sins: the warning before and the punishment after. The verse's frame: the rejection-wage: the signs were clear, the denial was chosen, the seizure was the sin's harvesting.

"And Allah is severe in punishment" — the verse's close: the Allah shadid al-'iqab — severe in punishment. The verse's frame: the severity of the punishment for the persistent denial — the same attribute of the openings (cf. 2:196, 3:4).

The verse's lesson: the signs' rejection is the seizure's cause; the history's pattern is the warning's mirror. And the reader's part: read the people of Pharaoh in the warned: the signs come, and the seizure follows the denial.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 12

Verse 12 says: *˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the disbelievers, “Soon you will be overpowered and driven to Hell—what an evil place to rest!”*

In simple terms, the verse commands the announcement to the disbelievers: soon you will be defeated and gathered to Hell — what an evil resting place!

"˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the disbelievers" — the verse's address: the qul li alladhina kafaru — say to those who disbelieve. Ibn Kathir: "Say to those who disbelieve: 'You will be defeated and gathered together to Hell'" — the address to the disbelievers: the announcement to the opponents of the Qur'an and the Messenger. The verse's frame: the prophecy of the defeat: the word to the deniers: the coming of the victory and the gathering.

"Soon you will be overpowered and driven to Hell" — the verse's defeat: the satughlabun — you will be overcome — the wa tuhsharuna ila jahannam — and gathered to Hell. Ibn Kathir: the prediction of the defeat of the disbelievers at the hands of the believers (as in Badr) — the gathering to Hell at the end. The verse's frame: the two stages: the world's defeat and the Hereafter's gathering: the conquered in the war and the gathered in the Fire.

"—what an evil place to rest!" — the verse's close: the bi'sa al-mihad — what an evil resting place, the bed's evil. The verse's frame: the place's description: the Fire as the resting place of the ones who rejected the Place of the rest.

The verse's lesson: the denial's end is the overpower and the gathering — the place of the rest is the evil. And the reader's part: take the word — the disbelievers will be overpowered and gathered — and the evil place is the resting place of the refusal.

## Verse 13

Verse 13 says: *Indeed, there was a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle— one fighting for the cause of Allah and the other in denial. The believers saw their enemy twice their number. But Allah supports with His victory whoever He wills. Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight.*

In simple terms, the verse points to the sign of the two armies at Badr: one fighting for Allah's cause, the other in denial; the believers saw the enemy twice their number; but Allah supports whom He wills — a lesson for people of insight.

"Indeed, there was a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle— one fighting for the cause of Allah and the other in denial" — the verse's armies: the fi'atani — the two groups — the iltaqata — met — the tuqatilu fi sabil Allah — fighting in the cause of Allah — the ukhra kafirah — the other in denial. Ibn Kathir: the two camps that met in combat at Badr — one fighting in the cause of Allah — the Muslims — and as for the other, in disbelief — the idolaters of Quraysh at Badr. The verse's frame: the two sides: the cause and the denial — the battle of the truth and the falsehood: the meeting of the two armies is the meeting of the two creeds.

"The believers saw their enemy twice their number" — the verse's sight: the yarawnahum mithlayhim — they saw them twice their number — the ra'y al-'ayn — with the sight of the eye. Ibn Kathir: the idolators thought the Muslims were twice as many as they were — for Allah made this illusion a factor in the victory that Islam had over them. Ibn 'Abbas (Tanwir): the believers were 313 men; Abu Sufyan's army was 950 — and they (the disbelievers) saw themselves as twice the number of the believers. Al-Jalalayn: the disbelievers numbered almost a thousand and saw the Muslims as twice their number, more numerous than themselves as the eye sees. The verse's frame: the eyes' illusion: the enemy saw the twice in the believers — the divine defense: the numbers were made a vision: the small force appeared the bigger — the victory was the Will's work, not the count's.

"But Allah supports with His victory whoever He wills" — the verse's support: the yu'ayyid bi nasrihi man yasha' — He supports with His victory whom He wills. Ibn Kathir: Allah supports with His aid whom He wills. The verse's frame: the victory's giver: the aid is the Will's grant — the armies' sight and the victory's grant are both His.

"Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight" — the verse's close: the la 'ibrah li uli al-absar — a lesson for the people of insight, the possessors of the sight. The verse's frame: the lesson of the insight: the sight of the eyes saw the twice; the insight sees the support: the victory is the cause's proof.

The verse's lesson: the small and the great are the eyes' measures; the support and the victory are the Lord's. And the reader's part: see the insight in the armies — the cause was aided though the number was small — and the lesson is for the people of insight.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 14

Verse 14 says: *The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children, treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land—has been made appealing to people. These are the pleasures of this worldly life, but with Allah is the finest destination.*

In simple terms, the verse describes the world's appeal: desires — women, children, hoards of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land — are made attractive to people; these are the pleasures of the worldly life, but with Allah is the finest destination.

"The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children, treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land—has been made appealing to people" — the verse's list: the zuyyina li al-nas — made attractive to people — the hubb al-shahawat — the love of the desires — the nisa' — the women — the banin — the children — the qanatir al-muqantarah — the heaped treasures — the min al-dhahab wa al-fiddah — of gold and silver — the al-khayl al-musawwamah — the fine horses, the branded, the trained — the al-an'am — the cattle — the al-harth — the fertile land, the tilled. Ibn Kathir (with the classical notes): the enjoyment of the world's desires — the things made appealing: the women, the children, the hoards, the horses, the cattle, the lands — the world's ornaments and its lures. The verse's frame: the world's catalogue: the appealed: the six items that the world is made of — the desires' sum: the attractions of the human eye.

"These are the pleasures of this worldly life" — the verse's worth: the mata' al-hayat al-dunya — the enjoyment of the worldly life. Ibn Kathir: the worldly pleasures are brief — the mettle of the world: the passing enjoyment: the reached, the spent, the gone.

"but with Allah is the finest destination" — the verse's close: the wa Allah 'indahu husn al-ma'ab — and with Allah is the beautiful return, the finest destination. Ibn Kathir: the finest destination is with Allah — the return that is better than the world's countable. The verse's frame: the comparison: the world's pleasures against the Lord's destination: the appeal of the seen against the unseen's best.

The verse's lesson: the world's pleasures are the appeal and the passing — with Allah is the finest return. And the reader's part: enjoy the appeals within the measure, but do not make them the destination — the finest return is with Allah.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 15

Verse 15 says: *Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this? Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have Gardens with their Lord under which rivers flow, to stay there forever, and pure spouses, along with Allah’s pleasure.” And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants,*

In simple terms, the verse asks: shall I tell you of what is better than all this? For the mindful: Gardens with their Lord, rivers flowing beneath, forever, pure spouses, and Allah's pleasure — and Allah sees His servants.

"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ "Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this?"" — the verse's better: the qul a-unabbi'ukum bi khayrin min dhalikum — shall I inform you of what is better than that? Ibn Kathir: the answer to the world's appeal — the better than the women, the children, the hoards, the horses, the cattle, the lands: the appointed for the mindful. The verse's frame: the announcement of the better: the world's appeal is the lower; the informed better is the higher: the Prophet tells the comparison.

"Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have Gardens with their Lord under which rivers flow, to stay there forever, and pure spouses" — the verse's Gardens: the li alladhina ittaqaw jannat — for those who are mindful: Gardens — the tajri min tahtiha al-anhar — under which rivers flow — the khalidin fiha — staying forever — the wa azwaj mutahharah — pure spouses, purified. Ibn Kathir: the Gardens under the rivers, the eternity, and the pure spouses — the reward of the mindful against the world's appeal: the eternal rivers and the purified spouses for the ones who guarded. The verse's frame: the mindful's reward: the Garden below the rivers, the staying forever, the pure spouses — the better of the seen is answered by the unseen's complete.

"along with Allah's pleasure" — the verse's pleasure: the wa ridwan min Allah — the pleasure of Allah. Ibn Kathir: the greatest of the rewards — the pleasure of Allah — beyond the gardens and the spouses — the highest of the best: the pleasure of the Lord above the fruits of the Garden. The verse's frame: the apex: the Garden's rivers and the purified spouses, then the Lord's pleasure: the greatest of the great.

"And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants" — the verse's close: the wa Allah basir bi al-'ibad — Allah is All-Seeing of the servants. The verse's frame: the seeing of the mindful's deeds: the One who informs of the reward is the One who sees the deserving.

The verse's lesson: the better than the world's appeal is the mindful's Garden and the Lord's pleasure. And the reader's part: measure the world against the informed better — the rivers, the purification, the pleasure — the All-Seeing sees the ones who choose the better.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 16

Verse 16 says: *who pray, “Our Lord! We have believed, so forgive our sins and protect us from the torment of the Fire.”*

In simple terms, the verse describes the mindful: they pray — Our Lord, we have believed, so forgive our sins and protect us from the Fire's torment.

"who pray, "Our Lord! We have believed"" — the verse's prayer: the alladhina yaquluna — who say — the rabbana innana amanna — Our Lord, we have believed. Ibn Kathir: the description of the mindful — the ones who say: we have believed — the belief's confession as the prayer's opening. The verse's frame: the believers' address: the faith's declaration before the requests: the believing is the prayer's ground.

"so forgive our sins" — the verse's forgiveness: the fa-ighfir lana dhunubana — forgive us our sins. Ibn Kathir: the plea of the forgiveness — the believers' request: the sins' pardon after the confession: the faith's first request. The verse's frame: the mercy's request: the believed — then the forgiveness: the sins of the believers are the prayer's subject.

"and protect us from the torment of the Fire" — the verse's protection: the wa qina 'adhab al-nar — and protect us from the punishment of the Fire. Ibn Kathir: the protection from the Fire's torment — the second request: the saved from the Fire: the believers' prayer of the two: the forgiveness and the protection. The verse's frame: the believers' two asks: the past's pardon and the future's protection — the Fire's distance.

The verse's lesson: the mindful pray the two: forgive and protect — the faith's confession is the prayer's opening. And the reader's part: pray with them — we have believed, forgive, and protect from the Fire.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 17

Verse 17 says: *˹It is they˺ who are patient, sincere, obedient, and charitable, and who pray for forgiveness before dawn.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the mindful's five marks: the patient, the truthful, the obedient, the charitable, and the ones seeking forgiveness at the dawn's approach.

"˹It is they˺ who are patient" — the verse's first mark: the al-sabirin — the patient, the steadfast. Ibn Kathir (with the classical notes of 3:15–17): the description of the mindful: the patient — the ones who endure the obedience's burdens and the trials' weights. The verse's frame: the patience: the first of the character's list: the steadiness under the command and the trial.

"sincere" — the verse's second: the al-sadiqin — the truthful, the sincere. Ibn Kathir: the truthful — the words and the hearts' truth: the ones whose speech is the heart's report. The verse's frame: the sincerity: the second mark: the true in the claim of the belief: the believe as the truth is the say.

"obedient" — the verse's third: the al-qanitin — the devoutly obedient, the devoted. Ibn Kathir: the qanit — the obedient, the devoted to the obedience. The verse's frame: the submission: the third mark: the devoted standing: the obedience of the one who surrendered.

"and charitable" — the verse's fourth: the al-munfiqin — those who spend. Ibn Kathir: the spenders — the ones who give of the wealth in the cause and the good. The verse's frame: the giving: the fourth mark: the wealth's service: the spenders of the provision.

"and who pray for forgiveness before dawn" — the verse's fifth: the wa al-mustaghfirin bi al-as-har — the ones seeking forgiveness at the dawns. Ibn Kathir: the seekers of forgiveness before the dawn — the late-night prayer's people — the istighfar of the last part of the night: the ones who wake for the forgiveness before the light. The verse's frame: the dawn's mercy: the fifth mark: the forgiveness-seeking of the night's end: the hour of the descent's mercy.

The verse's lesson: the mindful are the five-marked: the patient, the truthful, the obedient, the spenders, the seekers of the dawn's forgiveness. And the reader's part: clothe yourself in the marks — the patience, the truth, the obedience, the giving, and the night's forgiveness.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 18

Verse 18 says: *Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—and so are the angels and people of knowledge. He is the Maintainer of justice. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise.*

In simple terms, the verse declares the threefold witness: Allah Himself, the angels, and the people of knowledge witness that there is no god except Him — the Maintainer of justice — the Almighty, the All-Wise.

"Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him" — the verse's first witness: the shahida Allah — Allah bears witness — the annahu la ilaha illa Huwa. Ibn Kathir: Allah bears witness that none has the right to be worshipped but He — His testimony: the proof and the signs: the declaration of the Oneness by the One. Al-Jalalayn: Allah has made it clear to His creation through proofs and signs that there is no god except Him. The verse's frame: the divine testimony: the highest witness: the One Himself declares the Oneness: the proofs of the creation are His witness.

"—and so are the angels and people of knowledge" — the verse's second and third: the wa al-mala'ikat — and the angels — the wa ulu al-'ilm — and those having knowledge. Ibn Kathir: Allah then mentioned the testimony of His angels and those who have knowledge after He mentioned His own testimony — the angels affirm it — and those having knowledge (the prophets and the believers) through their conviction and words — this ayah emphasizes the great virtue of those who have knowledge. The verse's frame: the threefold: the Lord's testimony, the angels' testimony, the learned's testimony: the witnesses of the Oneness: the highest, the unseen's hosts, the knowledge's holders.

"He is the Maintainer of justice" — the verse's justice: the qa'iman bi al-qist — standing, maintaining the justice. Ibn Kathir: (He always) maintains His creation in justice — in all that He does — the upright with equity: the justice of the law and the judgment. The verse's frame: the just Maintainer: the witness's content: the One who is witnessed is the One who maintains the justice.

"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise" — the verse's close: the la ilaha illa Huwa — the 'Aziz — the Almighty — al-Hakim — the All-Wise. The verse's frame: the repeated declaration: the Oneness with the power and the wisdom: the witness's seal.

The verse's lesson: the Oneness is the witnessed of the Lord, the angels, and the learned — the justice's Maintainer is the Almighty, the All-Wise. And the reader's part: bear the witness with them — the Lord, the angels, and the people of knowledge testify — and be among the knowledge's holders of the testimony.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 19

Verse 19 says: *Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam. Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them. Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning.*

In simple terms, the verse declares: the religion with Allah is Islam; the People of the Scripture did not dispute out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them; and whoever denies the signs — Allah is swift in reckoning.

"Certainly, Allah's only Way is Islam" — the verse's religion: the inna al-din 'inda Allah al-Islam — the religion with Allah is Islam. Ibn Kathir: Allah said, asserting that the only religion accepted with Him is Islam — (and the cross-reference 3:85: whoever seeks a religion other than Islam, it will never be accepted of him). Ma'arif (and Tanwir): the religion that is accepted with Allah is the surrender — the Islam — the submission to His will and guidance. The verse's frame: the accepted religion: the Islam — the submission: the way of the prophets before: the surrender to the One: the religion of the truth.

"Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them" — the verse's dispute: the ma ikhtalafa alladhina utu al-Kitab — those given the Scripture did not differ — the illa min ba'di ma ja'ahum al-'ilm — except after knowledge came to them — the baghyan baynahum — out of mutual envy, the transgression among them. Ibn Kathir: some of them wronged others — the differences came after the knowledge: the envy's dispute: the division of the People of the Book was not the ignorance's — it was the knowledge's and the envy's. The verse's frame: the division's cause: knowledge came and the envy followed: the dispute of the informed against the truth: the enmity made the sects.

"Whoever denies Allah's signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning" — the verse's close: the man yakfur bi ayat Allah — whoever denies the signs of Allah — the fa inna Allah sari' al-hisab — Allah is swift in reckoning. Ibn Kathir: the denial of the signs — the reckoning's swiftness: the One who gave the knowledge accounts the denial: the swift is the immediate's judgment. The verse's frame: the denier's account: the signs' denial is the swift reckoning's subject.

The verse's lesson: the religion with Allah is the surrender — the disputes after the knowledge are the envy's — and the denial is the swift reckoning's. And the reader's part: the submission is the way; the dispute is the envy's; and the signs' denial is the swift account.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 20

Verse 20 says: *So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.” And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?” If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the answer to the arguers: I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers; ask the People of the Scripture and the illiterate — have you submitted? If they submit, they are guided; if they turn away, your duty is only the delivery — and Allah sees the servants.

"So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, "I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers."" — the verse's answer: the fa in hajjuka — so if they dispute with you — the qul aslamtu wajhi li Allah — say: I have submitted my face to Allah — the wa mani ittaba'ani — and those who follow me. Ibn Kathir: "So if they dispute with you say: 'I have submitted myself to Allah, and (so have) those who follow me'" — the answer to the disputers: the submission's declaration: the face surrendered to the Lord: the Prophet's answer is the surrender, not the debate's prolongation. The verse's frame: the submission's proof: the argument is answered by the deed: I and my followers have submitted: the answer is the life, not the rhetoric.

"And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, "Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?"" — the verse's question: the wa qul li alladhina utu al-Kitab — say to those given the Scripture — the wa al-ummiyyin — and the illiterate, the unlettered (the Arab pagans) — the a-aslamtum — have you submitted? Ibn Kathir: say to those given the Scripture (Jews and Christians) and to those who are illiterates (Arab pagans): "Do you (also) submit yourselves?" — the question to both: the People of the Book and the pagans: the one call: the submission.

"If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided" — the verse's guidance: the fa in aslamu fa qadi ihtadaw — if they submit, they have been guided. Ibn Kathir: if they do, they are rightly guided. The verse's frame: the submission's result: the surrender is the guidance's attainment: the submitter is the guided.

"But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺" — the verse's delivery: the fa in tawallaw fa innama 'alayka al-balagh — but if they turn away, your duty is only the conveying. Ibn Kathir: their reckoning is with Allah and their return and final destination is to Him — the messenger's duty is the message; the reckoning is the Lord's. The verse's frame: the duty's limit: the delivery, not the compulsion: the message's bearer is not the hearts' turner.

"And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants" — the verse's close: the wa Allah basir bi al-'ibad — Allah is All-Seeing of the servants. The verse's frame: the seeing of the submitters and the turners: the One who sees the servants' responses.

The verse's lesson: the answer to the arguers is the submission; the call to the People of the Book and the pagans is the same; the submitter is guided, the turner is the message's limit. And the reader's part: answer with the submission — have you submitted? — and deliver and leave the reckoning to the All-Seeing.

===VERSE-END===
