## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:1

> Alif-Lãm-Mĩm.

**Commentary:**

### The Opening Letters

*Alif-Lām-Mīm* is one of the disconnected or opening letters (*al-ḥurūf al-muqaṭṭaʿāt*) that occur at the beginning of several Qur’anic sūrahs. Their complete intended meaning is known to Allah. The Prophet ﷺ recited them as revelation, but he did not provide an authentic, definitive explanation that assigns a particular meaning to each individual letter. The responsible position is therefore to believe in them, recite them, and avoid claiming certainty where Allah has not granted it.

These letters are not meaningless sounds. They are part of the revealed Qur’an, and their recitation is worship. At the same time, their hidden significance teaches the limits of human knowledge. The Qur’an gives people clear guidance, commands, narratives, and arguments, while also reminding them that revelation is not exhausted by what the human mind can immediately analyse.

### A Challenge in Familiar Letters

The letters are especially striking because the Qur’an is composed of the same Arabic letters known to its first hearers. The challenge is not that the material of the language is inaccessible; rather, it is that human beings cannot produce revelation of this order from those ordinary materials. The following verses immediately speak of the Book, its truth, its confirmation of earlier revelation, and its criterion between right and wrong. The opening therefore prepares the reader to approach the Qur’an as divine speech, not as an ordinary human composition.

The Qur’an itself challenges humanity to produce a sūrah like it (2:23; 10:38), and declares that if humanity and jinn combined, they could not produce its like (17:88). *Alif-Lām-Mīm* is not by itself a complete challenge, but its placement at the threshold of a sūrah about revelation and truth invites reflection on the miraculous character of the Qur’an.

### Humility before Revelation

The opening letters establish a method for reading the Qur’an. The believer uses reason, language, context, and sound interpretation, but does not make the human intellect the judge over revelation. There is a difference between asking sincere questions and imposing a conclusion upon a text before listening to it. The next passage will praise those grounded in knowledge who say, “We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord” (3:7).

This humility is not hostility to knowledge. Islam encourages learning and reflection, but genuine knowledge includes awareness of its boundaries. The angels said, “We have no knowledge except what You have taught us” (2:32). The opening letters place the reader in the same posture: attentive, respectful, and ready to learn.

### The Reward of Reciting the Qur’an

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites a letter from the Book of Allah receives a multiplied reward, and he explained that *Alif-Lām-Mīm* is not one letter but “Alif is a letter, Lām is a letter, and Mīm is a letter.” This report is recorded in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī. The hadith does not disclose the complete interpretation of the letters; it teaches the virtue of reciting them and the value of every part of revelation.

The letters are therefore not to be skipped because their full meaning is unknown. A servant may worship Allah through reciting what he understands and what he does not yet understand, while seeking beneficial explanation and guarding against invented certainty.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:1, 10:1, 29:1–2, and 31:1 on the occurrence of the disconnected letters.
- Qur’an 2:23, 17:88, and 52:33–34 on the Qur’an’s challenge and miraculous nature.
- Qur’an 3:7 and 2:32 on the humility of those grounded in knowledge.
- Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2910, the hadith explaining the multiplied reward for reciting each letter.
- For the opening letters, their recitation, and the limits of definitive interpretation, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:1.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:2

> Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him —the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining.

**Commentary:**

### The Foundation of Faith

This verse begins with the name *Allāh* and immediately declares the central truth of revelation: there is no deity worthy of worship except Him. This is *tawḥīd*, the oneness of Allah in His lordship, names and attributes, and right to worship. The statement rejects every rival, whether an idol, human being, angel, ideology, desire, or created power.

The Qur’an frequently joins the testimony of divine oneness to the truth of revelation. The Book that will be discussed in the following verses does not come from a committee of competing gods or from an impersonal force. It comes from the One who alone creates, knows, commands, and sustains.

### Al-Ḥayy: The Ever-Living

*Al-Ḥayy* means the Ever-Living. Allah’s life is perfect, beginningless, everlasting, and free from weakness, sleep, death, or dependence. Created beings live by a life given to them, pass through change, become weak, and eventually die. Allah’s life is not received from another and is never interrupted.

This name gives the believer certainty when every created support is unstable. Families change, governments fall, bodies age, and possessions disappear, but the Ever-Living remains. The servant’s relationship with Allah is therefore not attached to a temporary worldly circumstance. Allah is worthy of worship in prosperity and distress because His perfection does not change.

### Al-Qayyūm: The All-Sustaining

*Al-Qayyūm* means the One who stands by Himself and sustains everything else. Allah needs no support, while every creature needs Him at every moment. The heavens, earth, bodies, souls, causes, and outcomes continue only through His sustaining command.

The name also means that Allah manages and maintains creation. He knows its needs, sets its measures, and directs its affairs with wisdom. A believer may use food, medicine, work, family, and lawful institutions as means, but he knows that no means operates independently of the All-Sustaining.

### Worship and Reliance

The verse combines *tawḥīd* with dependence. If Allah alone is Ever-Living and All-Sustaining, then ultimate fear, hope, love, supplication, and reliance belong to Him alone. This does not require abandoning practical means. It means that the heart does not treat means as gods. A doctor may treat, but Allah grants healing; a teacher may explain, but Allah opens understanding; a farmer may plant, but Allah brings life from the soil.

The names are especially powerful in supplication. The Prophet ﷺ taught that the greatest name of Allah is found in supplications that call upon Him as the One God and as the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining. The servant asks from the One whose power and mercy never become exhausted.

### The Connection with Āyat al-Kursī

The same two names appear in Āyat al-Kursī (2:255), where they introduce a description of Allah’s knowledge, authority, and protection. There, the Ever-Living never slumbers, and the All-Sustaining owns whatever is in the heavens and earth. The repetition shows that these names are keys to understanding divine sovereignty: Allah does not merely begin creation and leave it unattended; He sustains it continuously.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:255 on *al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm*, Allah’s knowledge, authority, and sustaining power.
- Qur’an 20:111 and 25:58 on trusting the Ever-Living who does not die.
- Qur’an 16:20–21 and 35:15 on the dependence of created beings and Allah’s independence.
- Qur’an 112:1–4 on Allah’s unique and absolute oneness.
- Sunan Abī Dāwūd and Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, supplications invoking Allah as *al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm*.
- For *tawḥīd*, *al-Ḥayy*, *al-Qayyūm*, and the relationship between divine independence and creaturely dependence, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:2.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:3

> He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth, confirming what came before it, as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel

**Commentary:**

### Revelation Comes from One Lord

After declaring Allah’s oneness, the verse identifies the source of revelation: He revealed the Book to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The Qur’an is not presented as a human spiritual reflection or a product of the Prophet’s personal imagination. It is a revelation from Allah, delivered for guidance, correction, worship, and judgement.

The phrase “in truth” indicates both the truth of its source and the truthfulness of its content. The Qur’an speaks truthfully about Allah, creation, previous revelation, human responsibility, history, and the Hereafter. Its commands are just, its narratives are purposeful, and its promises and warnings are certain.

### Confirming Earlier Revelation

The Qur’an is *muṣaddiq*, confirming what came before it. This means that it affirms the original revelation given by Allah to His prophets, including the Torah given to Mūsā and the Gospel given to ʿĪsā. All true prophets brought the same foundation: worship Allah alone, obey His guidance, and prepare for the final return.

Confirmation does not mean that every wording found in later religious manuscripts is automatically identical to the original revelation. The Qur’an confirms the divine origin and surviving truth of previous scriptures while also functioning as a guardian and criterion over them (5:48). It corrects false beliefs, exposes concealment, and restores the universal prophetic message.

### The Torah and the Gospel

The *Tawrāh* and *Injīl* named here are revealed books. The Torah was given to Moses, and the Gospel was given to Jesus. The Qur’an honours both prophets and rejects the idea that faith in Muhammad ﷺ requires contempt for them. A Muslim believes in all Allah’s messengers and all the revelations Allah truly sent, without elevating one prophet into divinity or rejecting another.

The Qur’an’s account of Jesus is therefore neither a denial of his miraculous birth and mission nor an acceptance of claims that compromise divine oneness. It presents him as the Messiah, a word from Allah, a messenger to the Children of Israel, and a servant who called people to worship Allah alone.

### Continuity and Completion

The Qur’an’s relationship to earlier revelation is one of continuity and completion. It confirms the shared roots of the prophetic message, preserves the essential truths, and provides the final universal guidance. The earlier revelations were sent to particular communities and historical circumstances, while the Qur’an addresses humanity and remains protected as the final revelation.

This continuity also gives the People of the Book a basis for recognizing the Qur’an. A new revelation from the same Lord should not be judged by tribal loyalty or envy. It should be examined in light of the truth already known: the unity of God, the reality of revelation, the moral law, and the coming Day of Judgement.

### The Qur’an as a Trust

Receiving revelation is a mercy and a responsibility. The believer must recite the Qur’an, understand it, obey it, and convey it accurately. It is not enough to claim respect for a Book while ignoring its call to justice, worship, mercy, honesty, and repentance.

The verse also protects Muslims from religious chauvinism. They should not speak of the Torah and Gospel as though Allah never revealed them. Nor should they treat the Qur’an as merely one competing cultural document. The proper attitude is to honour the original revelations, believe in their prophets, and follow the final revelation sent for the present human community.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:136, 3:84, and 42:13 on believing in all prophets and revealed scriptures.
- Qur’an 5:44–48 on the Torah, Gospel, and the Qur’an as a guardian and criterion.
- Qur’an 6:91 and 46:12 on the revelation given to Moses and the confirmation of earlier scripture.
- Qur’an 19:30–36 on Jesus as a servant and prophet of Allah.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith that the prophets are brothers with one father and different mothers, signifying the unity of their essential message.
- For *muṣaddiq*, the Torah, the Gospel, continuity of revelation, and the Qur’an’s role as guardian, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:3.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:4

> previously, as a guide for people, and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺.[120] Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations will suffer a severe torment. For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment.

**Commentary:**

### Guidance for Humanity

The Torah, the Gospel, and the Qur’an were sent as guidance, not as ornaments for libraries or badges of identity. Revelation teaches people how to recognize their Creator, order their worship, distinguish justice from oppression, and prepare for the Hereafter. A scripture is fulfilled when its truth reaches the mind, humility reaches the heart, and obedience reaches conduct.

The phrase “for people” emphasizes the human need for revelation. Human beings possess reason and experience, but these alone cannot disclose everything about Allah, the unseen, worship, or the final consequences of moral choices. Revelation does not cancel reason; it gives reason reliable premises and a sound direction.

### Al-Furqān: The Standard

The “Standard” is *al-Furqān*, from the root *f-r-q*, meaning to separate or distinguish. It is the criterion that separates truth from falsehood, guidance from error, justice from injustice, and lawful obedience from rebellion. Allah gives revelation so that people do not treat every inherited custom, powerful opinion, or popular desire as equally true.

The Qur’an itself is called *al-Furqān* (25:1), and the day of Badr is called the Day of the Criterion (8:41) because Allah made the difference between truth and falsehood visible in history. The criterion must operate in personal life as well. A believer asks whether an attractive opportunity is lawful, whether a popular claim is true, and whether an apparent success is pleasing to Allah.

### Rejection after Clarification

The verse then warns those who reject Allah’s revelations. Rejection is not the same as honestly seeking clarification or struggling with a difficult question. The warning concerns a settled refusal to accept divine truth, especially when pride, envy, or worldly interest causes a person to turn away. Allah knows what each person understood, what reached him, and what he intended.

The severity of the warning reflects the importance of revelation. To reject Allah’s signs is to reject the guidance by which human beings were meant to live. It is also to expose oneself to a punishment no worldly resource can prevent. Wealth, ancestry, intellectual reputation, and political power do not protect a person from Allah’s judgement.

### Allah’s Might and Punishment

Allah is *ʿAzīz*, the Almighty whose power cannot be overcome. He is also capable of punishment, so the warning is not an empty threat. Yet the verse should be read together with the many Qur’anic verses describing Allah’s mercy, forgiveness, and acceptance of repentance. Fear is meant to restrain arrogance and prompt return, not to make sincere repentance seem impossible.

The believer therefore takes the warning seriously while never despairing. As long as life remains, the door of repentance remains open. But postponement is dangerous. A person does not know when death will end his opportunity to respond to revelation.

### A Universal Criterion, Not a Tool of Arrogance

Possessing a criterion does not give Muslims permission to become unjust toward people. The Qur’an commands justice even toward those one dislikes (5:8), and it forbids compulsion in religion (2:256). The Furqān exposes oppression wherever it occurs, including within the Muslim community. Anyone who uses revelation to excuse arrogance, racism, fraud, or cruelty has failed to apply the criterion to himself.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:185, 25:1, and 8:41 on the Qur’an and the Day of Badr as *al-Furqān*.
- Qur’an 5:44–48 on previous scripture and the Qur’an as a guardian and criterion.
- Qur’an 8:29 on taqwā granting a criterion for discernment.
- Qur’an 2:256 and 5:8 on no compulsion and justice even toward those one dislikes.
- Qur’an 39:53 and 4:17–18 on repentance, mercy, and the danger of delaying return.
- For *Furqān*, guidance, rejection of revelation, and Allah’s names *al-ʿAzīz* and *al-Muntaqim*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:4.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:5

> Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah.

**Commentary:**

### Complete Divine Knowledge

This verse establishes a foundational attribute of Allah: nothing in the heavens or on earth is hidden from Him. His knowledge encompasses the visible and invisible, the public and private, the past and future, the smallest movement and the largest event. Nothing is lost in the vastness of creation and nothing is obscured by darkness, distance, or secrecy.

The verse follows the discussion of revelation and rejection. People may conceal their motives, distort scripture, or deny signs, but their outward claims do not conceal the truth from Allah. He knows what a person says, what he means, what he understands, and what he deliberately refuses to acknowledge.

### Knowledge without Dependence

Human knowledge depends on observation, memory, teachers, instruments, and time. Allah’s knowledge depends on nothing. He knew creation before bringing it into existence, knows it while it exists, and knows its final outcome. His knowledge does not increase because He learns something new, nor does it diminish through forgetfulness.

This is part of the perfection of *tawḥīd*. Allah is not a limited observer within the universe. He is the Creator and Lord of the universe, and created reality cannot conceal itself from its Creator. The Qur’an therefore repeatedly joins knowledge to authority and judgement: the One who knows every deed is the One before whom every deed will be accounted for.

### The Inner Life Matters

People are often able to manage appearances. A person may speak religiously while seeking status, appear generous while desiring praise, or condemn injustice while benefiting from it privately. Allah knows the hidden motive. The Prophet ﷺ said that actions are judged by intentions, and this hadith is recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

This does not mean that people should claim to know the unseen contents of another person’s heart. Human beings judge what is apparent and leave ultimate judgement to Allah. It does mean that each servant must examine his own heart and remember that Allah is not deceived by performance.

### Comfort for the Oppressed

The verse is also a source of comfort. An oppressed person whose pain is ignored by institutions, or whose testimony is hidden by powerful people, is not unseen by Allah. Every injury, lost opportunity, and silent act of endurance is known. The absence of immediate justice does not mean the absence of divine knowledge.

Likewise, the quiet good deed that no one praises is preserved with Allah. A private prayer, an unnoticed act of charity, or restraint from a sin may be more valuable than public achievement. The believer works for Allah’s sight rather than the unstable approval of people.

### Knowledge and Responsibility

Because Allah knows everything, no one can excuse deliberate wrongdoing by saying, “No one saw.” The Qur’an’s moral law reaches private conduct as well as public life. A trustworthy person is trustworthy when unobserved; a truthful person remains truthful when lying would be profitable.

Awareness of divine knowledge should produce *murāqabah*, the vigilant awareness that Allah observes the servant. This awareness is not paranoia. It is a steady spiritual presence that encourages sincerity and protects from hidden corruption.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 6:59 on Allah’s knowledge of every leaf that falls and everything hidden and apparent.
- Qur’an 20:7, 50:16, and 67:13–14 on Allah’s knowledge of spoken words, concealed thoughts, and inner secrets.
- Qur’an 57:4 and 58:7 on Allah’s presence and knowledge of private gatherings.
- Qur’an 99:7–8 on every deed, however small, being brought forth.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, “Actions are only by intentions.”
- For divine knowledge, *ghayb*, *murāqabah*, and the connection between knowledge and accountability, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:5.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:6

> He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### Creation in the Womb

The verse directs attention to one of the most hidden stages of human existence: formation in the womb. Before a person has a name, social position, wealth, or public reputation, Allah knows and shapes him. The body’s development, the distinguishing features of each individual, and the emergence of a living human being all occur by Allah’s creative will.

The Qur’an elsewhere describes the stages of human creation in the womb (23:12–14; 22:5). These passages are not invitations to replace careful medical study with speculation. Rather, they direct observation toward gratitude and humility: scientific description can examine processes, while revelation identifies the Creator, purpose, and moral responsibility of the life being formed.

### Al-Muṣawwir: The Shaper

Allah’s name *al-Muṣawwir* means the One who gives form. Human beings share a common origin and basic bodily structure, yet every person has a distinct face, voice, fingerprint, temperament, and life story. This diversity is not evidence of divine disorder. It is a sign of creative wisdom and a foundation for recognizing one another (49:13).

The verse also corrects the illusion that human worth is produced by appearance. Since Allah shapes every person, beauty, disability, sex, ethnicity, and physical difference cannot be grounds for arrogance or contempt. Honour before Allah is tied to faith and righteousness, not to features over which a person had no choice.

### Allah’s Will and Human Means

Allah’s willing and shaping do not deny the ordinary means through which life develops. Parents, genetics, nutrition, medical care, and the environment are real created means. They do not operate independently of Allah, but studying them is not a denial of the Creator. The verse calls the believer to see both levels: the created process and the divine Lord who gives that process existence and order.

This understanding also guards against fatalism. A person may not control every aspect of his origin or body, but he remains responsible for his choices. Allah judges people according to what they were given and what they were capable of doing; no one is blamed for a feature or condition outside his control.

### Al-ʿAzīz and Al-Ḥakīm

The verse closes with two names. *Al-ʿAzīz* is the Almighty, whose power cannot be defeated. *Al-Ḥakīm* is the All-Wise, whose acts and decrees contain wisdom, even when the servant does not immediately perceive it. The One who shapes human beings in hidden stages is both powerful and wise; creation is neither accidental nor abandoned.

These names also answer the objections that will appear later in the sūrah concerning Jesus. Allah creates by His command and is not limited by ordinary human expectations. At the same time, every extraordinary act occurs with wisdom and does not make the created being divine.

### Gratitude for Life

Remembering one’s formation in the womb produces humility toward Allah and compassion toward people. It should lead parents to protect children, societies to care for the vulnerable, and individuals to treat their bodies as trusts. Life is not self-created property. It is a gift placed under moral responsibility.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 23:12–14, 22:5, and 75:37–39 on human creation and development.
- Qur’an 49:13 on human diversity and honour through taqwā.
- Qur’an 40:64 and 59:24 on Allah as Creator and Shaper.
- Qur’an 16:78 on human dependence and the gifts of hearing, sight, and understanding.
- For *al-Muṣawwir*, divine will, wisdom in creation, and the relation between created means and Allah’s power, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:6.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:7

> He is the One Who has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book, of which some verses are precise—they are the foundation of the Book—while others are elusive.[121] Those with deviant hearts follow the elusive verses seeking ˹to spread˺ doubt through their ˹false˺ interpretations—but none grasps their ˹full˺ meaning except Allah. As for those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, “We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord.”

> But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason.

**Commentary:**

### Clear and Less Explicit Verses

This verse gives one of the Qur’an’s most important principles of interpretation. The Book contains *muḥkamāt*, verses that are precise, clear, and decisive, and *mutashābihāt*, verses whose wording or subject matter requires greater care because its full reality, exact application, or intended interpretation is not immediately apparent.

The *muḥkamāt* are “the foundation of the Book.” They establish tawḥīd, worship, moral commands, lawful and unlawful conduct, accountability, and the essential truths of faith. Less explicit passages must be understood in harmony with these foundations, not used to overturn them. A sound interpretation lets the clear verses explain and control the less clear ones.

### What “Mutashābih” Means

The word *mutashābih* can refer to something resembling another thing or containing multiple possible readings. It may include descriptions of the unseen, matters whose full reality belongs to Allah, concise expressions requiring contextual explanation, or passages whose meaning becomes clearer when read with the rest of revelation. Not every *mutashābih* verse is meaningless, and not every difficult verse is inaccessible. The warning concerns the attitude with which a person approaches it.

The Qur’an itself explains that Allah has sent down “the best statement, a consistent Book” (39:23). Apparent difficulty is therefore not a contradiction in revelation. It may be an invitation to learn, a test of humility, or a reminder that the unseen exceeds human experience.

### The Disease of Deviant Hearts

The problem described is not merely intellectual weakness. Those with a deviation in their hearts deliberately pursue the elusive verses to create doubt and discord. The Arabic expression points to a heart that has already tilted from truth. Such a person does not approach a difficult passage in order to submit it to careful explanation; he selects it as a weapon against the clear body of revelation.

This warning applies to anyone, including Muslims. A person can misuse verses about freedom to deny responsibility, verses about mercy to deny justice, or verses about divine decree to deny human responsibility. The cure is not to ignore the difficult passage but to return it to the Qur’an’s decisive principles, authentic Sunnah, Arabic context, and responsible scholarship.

### The Meaning of Taʾwīl

The word *taʾwīl* can mean interpretation or the final reality and outcome of a matter. The verse is commonly recited with a pause after “none knows its full meaning except Allah,” followed by “those firmly grounded in knowledge say, ‘We believe in it.’” This reading emphasizes that the complete reality of certain matters belongs to Allah. Another grammatical reading connects those firmly grounded in knowledge with knowledge of interpretation, while still requiring humility and consistency with revelation.

Both readings agree on the central lesson: no interpreter may claim mastery over the unseen or invent meanings that contradict the clear teachings of the Qur’an. Scholars may explain what can be explained, while saying “Allah knows best” concerning what remains beyond them.

### The Attitude of Those Grounded in Knowledge

The *rāsikhūn fī al-ʿilm* do not allow a difficult passage to make them reject the Book. They say, “We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord.” Their knowledge produces faith, not vanity. They distinguish between what is clear and what requires restraint, between a legitimate interpretation and speculation, and between asking a question and spreading doubt.

This is not blind faith. The grounded use evidence, language, reason, and scholarly inheritance. Their humility is intellectual honesty: they do not claim to know more than the text and the evidence allow.

### The Prophetic Warning

The Prophet ﷺ recited this verse and warned that when people follow the ambiguous portions seeking discord, they should be avoided. This report is recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The warning concerns a recognizable method: isolating a disputed phrase, ignoring the clear verses, and using the result to confuse people or justify a prior agenda.

### A Method for the Reader

A believer should begin with clear verses, read passages in context, gather all relevant Qur’anic evidence, consult authentic hadith, respect Arabic usage, and refer to qualified scholars. He should not build a creed or legal ruling on a solitary speculative reading. Nor should he turn every difference among recognized scholars into a crisis of faith.

The verse ends by saying that only people of reason will take heed. Here, reason is not the rival of revelation. It is the faculty that recognizes revelation’s authority, separates evidence from desire, and accepts that wisdom includes knowing when to stop.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:82 and 39:23 on reflecting upon the Qur’an’s coherence and consistency.
- Qur’an 2:269 and 17:36 on wisdom, knowledge, and avoiding claims without evidence.
- Qur’an 6:114 and 16:43 on Allah’s revelation and asking people of knowledge.
- Qur’an 7:53 and 17:85 on the limits of human knowledge concerning the unseen.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith warning against those who follow the ambiguous verses to create discord.
- For *muḥkam*, *mutashābih*, *taʾwīl*, the two grammatical readings, and the method of the *rāsikhūn fī al-ʿilm*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:7.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:8

> ˹They say,˺ “Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us. Grant us Your mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺.

**Commentary:**

### A Prayer for a Steadfast Heart

After describing those grounded in knowledge, the Qur’an records their prayer: “Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us.” Knowledge has not made them self-confident or careless. It has made them more aware of the danger of deviation and more dependent upon Allah’s protection.

The heart, *qalb*, is called a heart because it changes and turns. A person may know the truth today and become distracted tomorrow; a community may possess revelation and later distort it. Guidance is therefore not a certificate of permanent safety. It is a gift that must be protected through worship, humility, sound knowledge, and continual supplication.

### Deviation after Guidance

The prayer seeks protection from *zaygh*, a deviation or crooked inclination. This may begin subtly: preferring desire to evidence, treating revelation as a tool for personal status, excusing a private sin, or allowing resentment to shape interpretation. A heart rarely abandons guidance in one dramatic movement; it often turns through repeated small compromises.

The request does not imply that Allah wrongs those who deviate. The Qur’an says that when people deliberately turn away, Allah allows their hearts to turn as a consequence of their choice (61:5). The believer asks for protection while taking responsibility for the choices that nourish or damage his heart.

### Mercy after Guidance

They ask Allah to grant mercy after asking for steadfastness. Guidance itself is an expression of mercy, but the servant needs continuing mercy to understand, practise, repent, and remain firm. The word *raḥmah* includes forgiveness, protection from error, kindness in hardship, and a good end.

The order is meaningful. A person may possess information but need mercy to live by it. He may perform deeds but need mercy to have them accepted. He may face a trial that exposes his weakness and need mercy to emerge with faith intact.

### Al-Wahhāb: The Bestower

The closing name, *al-Wahhāb*, means the One who gives freely and abundantly. Allah gives guidance, faith, knowledge, forgiveness, provision, and opportunities for service without being compelled by any creature. The servant does not earn the initial gift of guidance through a prior achievement; he receives it as a divine bestowal and then responds with gratitude and effort.

Calling upon Allah as *al-Wahhāb* teaches generous hope. No person should think that his past makes guidance impossible or that the community cannot be renewed. Allah can bestow understanding upon the confused, repentance upon the sinner, and steadfastness upon the fearful.

### A Prayer for Communities

The supplication is plural in meaning and can be prayed for the whole community. Scholars, teachers, parents, and leaders especially need to ask that knowledge not become a cause of pride. Communities need protection from factionalism, political manipulation of scripture, and disputes in which victory matters more than truth.

The prayer also teaches how to respond to doctrinal disagreement. Instead of assuming that one’s own heart is safe and everyone else is deviant, the believer asks Allah to protect him. This humility makes sincere correction possible.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:9

> Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day—about which there is no doubt. Surely Allah does not break His promise.”

**Commentary:**

### The Certain Gathering

The prayer of the grounded believers moves from the heart’s present condition to the future resurrection. They affirm that Allah will gather all humanity for a Day about which there is no doubt. The world contains scattered peoples, hidden deeds, unequal outcomes, and unresolved injustices. The gathering brings every person before the One who knows the entire record.

This is *Yawm al-Qiyāmah*, the Day of Resurrection, and also the Day of Judgement. “Gathering” indicates that death is not disappearance. Every generation, nation, ruler, victim, believer, and rejecter will be brought together. No person will be lost in the passage of time, and no action will be omitted because it was private or forgotten by people.

### No Doubt about the Appointment

The statement “about which there is no doubt” is both a creed and a moral warning. The Qur’an uses certainty about the Hereafter to correct the illusion that worldly life is the final reality (45:24). If there were no resurrection, injustice could remain permanently unanswered and righteousness could be irrational whenever it requires sacrifice. The promise of the Day gives moral meaning to patience, repentance, charity, and restraint.

The believer does not know the time of the Day, but he knows that it will come. The unknown date is not a reason to dismiss the appointment; it is a reason to prepare without delay.

### Allah Does Not Break His Promise

Allah’s promise is true because He is perfect in knowledge, power, and truthfulness. Human beings promise and fail through forgetfulness, weakness, death, or changing circumstances. Allah is not subject to any of these limitations. What He promises, whether reward, warning, resurrection, or victory for truth, is certain.

The believer’s confidence is therefore not optimism based on worldly appearances. It is trust in the promise of Allah. A righteous person may die without seeing justice, while a tyrant may die apparently successful. The final judgement has not been cancelled; it has been postponed to the appointed Day.

### The Link with the Previous Prayer

The previous verse asked Allah not to let hearts deviate after guidance. This verse asks the heart to remain oriented toward the final meeting. Remembering the resurrection strengthens sincerity: the servant no longer lives only for immediate praise, gain, or revenge. He knows that the true audience is Allah and the true completion of every matter is before Him.

The prayer also joins personal steadfastness to universal accountability. It is not enough to ask for one’s own guidance while forgetting that all humanity will be judged. The believer asks for a good end and hopes that others too will be guided before the gathering.

### Justice and Hope

The Day is a source of fear for the wrongdoer and hope for the oppressed. Allah’s justice does not mean that every person receives identical circumstances; it means that every person is judged with perfect knowledge of circumstances, capacity, intention, and action. No good deed is lost and no injustice is overlooked.

At the same time, the believer does not depend only on his deeds. He seeks Allah’s mercy, as the surrounding verses show. The certainty of the promise includes both the warning to the rebellious and the hope offered to those who repent and do good.

- Qur’an 16:38 and 30:6 on Allah’s promise and His truthfulness.
- Qur’an 99:6–8 and 18:49 on every person meeting the record of his deeds.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports concerning resurrection, gathering, judgement, and the bridge over Hell.
- For *Yawm al-Qiyāmah*, the certainty of the gathering, Allah’s promise, and the connection between resurrection and moral accountability, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:9.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:10

> Indeed, neither the wealth nor children[122] of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah—and they will be the fuel for the Fire.

**Commentary:**

### Worldly Security Cannot Defeat Allah

The verse addresses the illusion that wealth and children can protect a person from divine judgement. Wealth can purchase comfort, influence, legal representation, and worldly security, while children can provide affection, social standing, and descendants who carry a family name. None of these can rescue a person from Allah if he meets Him in rejection and unrepentant wrongdoing.

The Qur’an is not condemning wealth or family as such. Both can be blessings when used in obedience. The warning concerns reliance upon them as substitutes for faith and righteousness. A person may possess much and still be poor before Allah if he has no submission, gratitude, or repentance.

### The Greatest Test of Possessions

Wealth and children are repeatedly described as tests. Wealth tests whether a person earns lawfully, fulfils rights, gives charity, and remains humble. Children test whether parents raise them with faith, justice, and mercy rather than treating them as instruments of status. A blessing becomes destructive when it is loved above Allah or used in disobedience.

The verse’s footnote points to children as a form of worldly support that cannot ultimately avail the disbeliever. Family loyalty is valuable in this life, but it does not transfer moral responsibility. The Qur’an teaches that no bearer of burdens bears the burden of another (6:164), and that each soul will meet what it earned.

### Fuel for the Fire

The expression “fuel for the Fire” is severe. It describes the end of a life in which gifts were used against the Giver. Wealth that was hoarded, children who were used to reinforce arrogance, and worldly power that supported falsehood become evidence against the person. The objects themselves are not independent punishers; the metaphor shows that the disbeliever’s resources cannot save him and that his own choices have contributed to his ruin.

Elsewhere the Qur’an says that wealth and children are adornment for worldly life, while enduring righteous deeds are better with Allah (18:46). The believer therefore benefits from worldly gifts without allowing them to become his final identity.

### A Warning to the Powerful

The immediate context includes people who rejected revelation despite access to knowledge, influence, and communal status. The verse warns religious leaders, political authorities, wealthy families, and ordinary people alike. No lineage guarantees salvation, and no institution can make falsehood true.

This is also a warning to Muslims. A person should not read the verse only as a description of others while trusting his own wealth, family, or public religious image. The Qur’an’s warnings are mirrors. The question is whether one’s possessions assist obedience or distract from it.

### Real Benefit

The only benefit that survives the meeting with Allah is faith accompanied by righteous action and accepted mercy. Wealth can be converted into benefit through charity, fair employment, support for relatives, and service to the vulnerable. Children can become a continuing source of reward through their righteousness and prayers. The object is not to abandon blessings but to place them under Allah’s ownership.

- Qur’an 6:164 and 53:38–39 on individual responsibility.
- Qur’an 26:88–89 on the only wealth that benefits on the Day of Resurrection: a sound heart.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith describing the deceased as followed by family, wealth, and deeds, with only deeds remaining.
- For the metaphor of fuel, the limits of wealth and lineage, and the responsible use of blessings, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:10.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:11

> Their fate will be like that of the people of Pharaoh and those before them —they all rejected Our signs, so Allah seized them for their sins. And Allah is severe in punishment.

**Commentary:**

### History as a Warning

The verse connects the arrogant disbelievers of the Prophet’s time to Pharaoh’s people and earlier nations. They are not unique in their rejection. Human beings repeat patterns: signs are shown, worldly power produces confidence, warnings are dismissed, and punishment arrives after persistent wrongdoing.

Pharaoh’s people are a particularly clear example because they possessed political power, wealth, institutions, and an ideology of domination. Their power did not protect them from Allah. The Qur’an repeatedly presents Pharaoh as a warning to every ruler who treats people as property and every society that normalizes oppression.

### Rejecting the Signs

Allah’s signs (*āyāt*) include revealed verses, the miracles given to prophets, the order of creation, and historical events through which truth becomes clear. Rejection is not simply failure to understand. It is the deliberate refusal to submit after the evidence and warning have been made clear.

The people of Pharaoh witnessed signs through Mūsā, yet responded with denial and oppression. The Qur’an says that they rejected them inwardly even while their souls were convinced, because of injustice and arrogance (27:14). This is the disease described in the earlier verses of this sūrah: truth may be recognized and still refused when it threatens status or desire.

### Seized for Their Sins

Allah “seized them for their sins.” The wording links punishment to moral responsibility. Allah does not punish a people without justice, and He does not treat disbelief, oppression, murder, and corruption as harmless private preferences. The drowning of Pharaoh’s people was both a consequence of their rebellion and a sign for later generations.

A Muslim must be careful not to turn this principle into a license for declaring that every disaster is a specific punishment on its victims. Allah alone knows the hidden wisdom and individual accountability behind events. The verse teaches certainty about divine justice, while human beings remain cautious about applying final judgements to particular people.

### The Delay before Punishment

The Qur’an shows that Allah often gives wrongdoers time. Delay is not approval. It may be an opportunity for repentance, a test for the oppressed, or a means by which the wrongdoer’s case becomes fully established. When punishment comes, it can appear sudden to people, but it is not sudden to Allah.

This should motivate repentance and prevent the powerful from mistaking temporary success for permanent security. Pharaoh ruled for a time, but his rule ended in the sea; the truth of Mūsā outlived the regime that tried to extinguish it.

### Severe in Punishment, Open to Mercy

The closing description, “Allah is severe in punishment,” balances the many verses about mercy. Mercy does not mean that the deliberate destruction of truth and justice has no consequence. Yet the same Lord who is severe in punishment is the One who sends warnings, accepts repentance, and does not wrong anyone.

The believer responds by combining fear and hope. He fears becoming like the arrogant nations and hopes in Allah’s forgiveness when he returns sincerely.

- Qur’an 10:90–92 on Pharaoh’s drowning as a sign for later people.
- Qur’an 14:42–44 on Allah’s delay of the wrongdoers and the coming Day.
- Qur’an 39:53 and 4:17–18 on mercy, repentance, and the danger of postponement.
- For the historical pattern, *āyāt*, divine justice, and the punishment of persistent rejection, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:11.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:12

> ˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the disbelievers, “Soon you will be overpowered and driven to Hell—what an evil place to rest!”

**Commentary:**

### A Prophetic Warning

Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to warn those who reject the truth that worldly power will not protect them. The warning was addressed in the immediate context to opponents who relied upon numbers, wealth, alliances, and military strength. They would be overpowered in this life and then driven toward the greater consequence in the Hereafter.

The verse is not a command for ordinary believers to boast about power. It is a divine declaration that arrogance and persecution have an appointed limit. The Prophet ﷺ conveyed it as a warning, not as a personal threat motivated by anger or revenge.

### “You Will Be Overpowered”

The Arabic verb indicates defeat and being gathered or driven toward a final destination. In the historical context of the early Muslim community, it pointed to the defeat of the hostile forces at Badr and the collapse of the confidence of those who had treated the Muslims as insignificant. It also carries a wider lesson: no earthly power can guarantee victory to falsehood forever.

This does not mean that believers will never suffer temporary defeat. The Qur’an itself discusses the believers’ setback at Uḥud and teaches them how to learn from it (3:152–165). Victory and defeat alternate as tests. The ultimate promise is that falsehood will not escape Allah’s judgement, not that every believer will win every worldly contest.

### Hell as an Evil Resting Place

The phrase “what an evil place to rest” is a Qur’anic expression of irony. People may seek comfort, safety, and a permanent home through wealth and domination, yet Hell is the final resting place of those who reject Allah and persist in injustice. The temporary shelters and palaces of this world cannot change that destination.

The Qur’an contrasts Hell with Paradise, whose welcome is peace and whose dwelling is lasting. A wise person compares destinations before becoming intoxicated by the road that leads to them.

### Warning before Judgement

Divine warning is itself a mercy. Allah does not punish without sending guidance, clarification, and opportunity. The Prophet ﷺ warned his people openly, and the Qur’an preserved the warning so that later generations could examine themselves. The purpose is not to encourage hatred of individuals but to call people away from disbelief and oppression before the final account.

A Muslim who repeats this verse should ask whether he is merely condemning others or also warning himself against the roots of rejection: pride, injustice, denial, and attachment to power.

### Power and Moral Responsibility

The verse also speaks to rulers and movements. Power is temporary and accountable. An army, government, corporation, or wealthy family may appear invincible, but it remains under Allah’s authority. The proper response to power is justice, restraint, and gratitude, not the persecution of the weak.

The believers are not permitted to imitate the arrogance they oppose. They must uphold justice even with an enemy and keep the distinction between resisting oppression and hating every human being associated with an opposing group.

- Qur’an 3:12–13 on the warning and the sign in the two armies that met at Badr.
- Qur’an 8:36–37 and 54:45–48 on the defeat of denial and the Hereafter.
- Qur’an 3:139–165 on temporary setbacks and the tests of victory and defeat.
- Qur’an 14:42–44 on the delay of wrongdoers and their final return.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports concerning Badr and the Prophet’s warning to the leaders of Quraysh.
- For the historical context, the limits of worldly victory, and “the evil resting place,” see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:12.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:13

> Indeed, there was a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle— one fighting for the cause of Allah and the other in denial. The believers saw their enemy twice their number.[124] But Allah supports with His victory whoever He wills. Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight.

**Commentary:**

### The Sign at Badr

This verse points to the Battle of Badr, the first major armed confrontation between the early Muslim community and the Quraysh of Makkah. The Muslims were fewer and less equipped, while the opposing force had greater numbers and resources. Yet Allah granted the believers victory. The event became a sign because it displayed that numbers and material strength do not operate independently of Allah’s will.

The Qur’an does not present Badr as a celebration of violence. The Muslims were a persecuted community that had been expelled, deprived of property, and threatened by an armed force. “Fighting for the cause of Allah” means fighting under obedience to Allah, for a just purpose and within revealed limits—not fighting to gain wealth, dominate civilians, or satisfy tribal anger.

### Two Armies and Two Orientations

The verse distinguishes the armies by their purpose: one was fighting in Allah’s cause and the other was in denial. This is a moral distinction, not simply a tribal one. The believers were not righteous because they were Arabs or because they belonged to one political faction. They were required to fight sincerely and justly. Likewise, the opposing side’s condemnation was connected to rejection, persecution, and aggression, not to ethnicity alone.

The Qur’an’s language prevents nationalism from replacing faith. A Muslim army can lose its moral status if it becomes oppressive, and a person outside the Muslim community may still be treated with justice and dignity. Divine support is not a permanent guarantee for a group that abandons obedience.

### Perception and Divine Decree

The translation states that the believers saw their enemy as twice their number. The Qur’an elsewhere mentions that Allah made each side perceive the other in a way that served the battle’s unfolding (8:44). Classical exegetes discuss the precise stages and perceptions of the two armies. The purpose of the verse is clear even where historical details are discussed differently: Allah controlled the circumstances and gave the believers courage without making them forget their weakness.

The apparent odds were part of the lesson. If victory had been achieved through overwhelming material superiority, people could attribute it entirely to preparation. At Badr, preparation mattered, but the result made clear that victory comes from Allah.

### Allah’s Victory Does Not Cancel Effort

The Prophet ﷺ prepared, consulted his Companions, selected a strategic position, organized the ranks, and prayed intensely. The companions endured thirst, fear, and exhaustion. Divine aid did not make human effort unnecessary. It taught them to work with discipline while knowing that the outcome belongs to Allah.

This is the balance of *tawakkul*: take lawful means, then entrust the result to Allah. Claiming divine support without obedience or preparation is presumption, not reliance.

### A Lesson for People of Insight

The verse calls people of insight—*ulī al-abṣār*—to learn from Badr. Insight is more than seeing the battlefield. It recognizes the unseen moral and spiritual meaning within visible events. The person of insight does not conclude that the strongest party is always right. He asks about purpose, justice, obedience, patience, and the consequences of arrogance.

Badr also teaches believers not to become demoralized by small numbers. A minority can be morally strong, but minority status alone does not prove truth. The real question is whether the community obeys Allah, fulfils trusts, and protects justice.

- Qur’an 22:39–40 and 2:190–193 on permission to fight, limits, and the defence of the oppressed.
- Qur’an 3:152 and 8:46 on obedience, discipline, and avoiding dispute during struggle.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports concerning the Prophet’s supplication and the events of Badr.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:14

> The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children,[125] treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land—has been made appealing to people. These are the pleasures of this worldly life, but with Allah is the finest destination.

**Commentary:**

### The Attraction of Worldly Life

This verse acknowledges a reality of human nature: worldly desires have been made attractive. People are drawn to companionship, family, wealth, status, transport, animals, property, and productive land. The Qur’an does not pretend that these attractions do not exist, nor does it condemn every form of enjoyment. It teaches the believer to understand their place.

The Arabic word *zuyyina* means adorned or made appealing. Attraction itself is not necessarily sinful. The moral question is how a person responds to it: whether he seeks what is lawful, fulfils the rights attached to it, remains grateful, and refuses to let temporary pleasure replace Allah.

### Relationships and Family

The mention of women and children concerns powerful human desires for companionship, marriage, affection, and family continuity. Islam does not treat marriage or parenthood as obstacles to spirituality. The Prophet ﷺ married, loved his family, played with children, and instructed believers to fulfil the rights of spouses and relatives.

The danger comes when people are reduced to objects of possession or status. A spouse is a trust and partner, not a commodity. Children are a responsibility, not proof of superiority. Desire must be governed by chastity, consent, justice, and lawful commitment.

### Wealth and Property

Gold and silver represent accumulated wealth and the desire for financial security. Wealth can provide housing, education, health care, family support, and charitable service. It can also produce greed, hoarding, bribery, exploitation, and contempt for the poor. The Qur’an therefore does not simply ask whether a person owns wealth; it asks how he acquired it and what he did with it.

Livestock and fertile land represent productive assets and agricultural prosperity. In contemporary life, the same principle applies to businesses, technology, investments, vehicles, and professional influence. All are temporary trusts. Allah may bless them when they serve justice and worship, and they may become a trial when they command the heart.

### The Good and the Better

The verse calls these things the enjoyment of worldly life, then directs attention to what is better with Allah. It does not say that every worldly blessing is evil. It distinguishes between a lawful provision and the finest destination. The problem is not enjoying a gift; it is treating the gift as the final home.

Sūrah al-Kahf describes wealth and children as adornment, while enduring righteous deeds are better with Allah (18:46). Sūrah al-Ḥadīd compares worldly life to vegetation that grows, impresses, dries, and becomes debris (57:20). The comparison teaches proportion: use the world, but do not build your identity upon what must pass.

### The Best Destination

The “finest destination” is with Allah: His pleasure, forgiveness, and Paradise. The destination is better because it is lasting, secure, and free from the fear of loss that accompanies worldly possessions. A person who sacrifices unlawful gain for Allah has not lost; he has exchanged a temporary benefit for an enduring reward.

The Prophet ﷺ said that the world is a prison for the believer and a paradise for the disbeliever, meaning that the believer’s lawful life remains limited compared with the freedom and delight of the Hereafter, while the worldly life of the disbeliever is all the comfort he expects. The hadith, recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, does not prohibit lawful enjoyment; it places it beside the incomparable Hereafter.

### A Balanced Spirituality

Islamic spirituality is not contempt for creation. The Prophet ﷺ taught that the body, eyes, spouse, and guest have rights. The believer eats, works, marries, earns, and builds, but keeps Allah above every attachment. If wealth comes, he gives thanks; if it leaves, he remains patient. If family brings joy, he gives praise; if family brings difficulty, he fulfils responsibility without worshipping approval.

- Qur’an 9:24 on the danger of loving family and wealth more than Allah and His Messenger.
- Qur’an 24:32–33 and 30:21 on lawful marriage, chastity, and affection between spouses.
- Qur’an 64:15 and 8:28 on children and wealth as tests.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2956, the hadith comparing worldly life for the believer and disbeliever; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, reports on the rights of the body and family.
- For *zuyyina*, *matāʿ*, worldly adornment, and the balance between lawful enjoyment and the Hereafter, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:14.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:15

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this? Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have Gardens with their Lord under which rivers flow, to stay there forever, and pure spouses,[126] along with Allah’s pleasure.” And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants,

**Commentary:**

### What Is Better

After listing the attractions of worldly life, Allah directs the Prophet ﷺ to announce something better. The contrast is not between pleasure and no pleasure. It is between temporary, mixed, and fragile pleasures and the lasting gifts prepared for those mindful of Allah. The believer is not asked to pretend that the world has no beauty; he is shown a superior destination so that he can order his desires correctly.

### Paradise as a Garden

The Gardens of Paradise are described as places beneath which rivers flow. This image conveys life, freshness, abundance, shade, and security. In the Arabian environment of the first hearers, flowing water and cultivated gardens represented extraordinary comfort. Yet the description is not intended to reduce Paradise to physical scenery. The Qur’an says that no soul knows what joy has been hidden for it (32:17).

The gardens are “with their Lord,” a phrase that expresses nearness, honour, care, and acceptance. The greatest gift is not the created beauty by itself but the pleasure of Allah that accompanies it.

### Permanence

The word “forever” answers the anxiety built into worldly enjoyment. Every worldly possession can be lost through death, illness, theft, economic change, or separation. Paradise is not a temporary vacation from anxiety; it is an everlasting home in which fear and grief are removed.

This permanence changes the believer’s calculations. Giving charity may reduce immediate wealth, forgiving may appear to surrender advantage, and resisting temptation may require sacrifice. But what is given for Allah is not lost. The believer exchanges what passes for what remains.

### Pure Spouses and Pure Relationships

The phrase “pure spouses” indicates relationships free from betrayal, resentment, humiliation, illness, and every impurity that can afflict worldly life. It does not reduce Paradise to male pleasure or treat women as rewards for men. Allah promises complete joy and honour to every believing servant, male and female (4:124; 16:97).

The purity of Paradise also shows the moral goal of relationships in this life: affection, fidelity, dignity, and mutual care. Worldly marriage is a trust with duties; Paradise is its perfected reward for those who live in faith and righteousness.

### The Pleasure of Allah

The verse culminates in *riḍwān Allāh*, the pleasure and acceptance of Allah. This is greater than all physical gifts. A servant may receive comfort but still feel rejected, insecure, or purposeless. Allah’s pleasure gives the people of Paradise complete security and honour. The Qur’an says that Allah’s pleasure is greater than the gardens and their delights (9:72).

Seeking Allah’s pleasure purifies intention. A person does not worship merely to obtain a reward or escape punishment, although both are legitimate motives. He worships because Allah is worthy, and he hopes for the supreme reward of being accepted by Him.

### Allah Sees His Servants

The verse closes by reminding people that Allah sees His servants. He sees the struggle against desire, the private act of charity, the restraint from anger, and the repentance after failure. He also sees the hidden motive behind public worship. No effort for the Hereafter is overlooked, and no false claim of righteousness deceives Him.

- Qur’an 4:124 and 16:97 on equal reward for believing men and women.
- Qur’an 76:8–22 on the qualities of the righteous and their reward.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:16

> who pray, “Our Lord! We have believed, so forgive our sins and protect us from the torment of the Fire.”

**Commentary:**

### Faith Joined to Repentance

The people promised Paradise are described through their prayer. They say, “Our Lord, we have believed, so forgive our sins.” Their faith does not produce self-admiration. It produces humility, awareness of shortcomings, and hope in forgiveness. They do not claim that their deeds make them independent of Allah’s mercy.

The expression “we have believed” refers to *īmān*, a conviction that should be reflected in the heart, speech, and conduct. It is not a mere label. Yet even sincere believers commit errors. Their response is not denial or despair, but immediate return through repentance and supplication.

### Forgiveness of Sins

The word *dhunūb* includes sins and faults. The prayer recognizes that human beings need Allah to cover, pardon, and remove the consequences of wrongdoing. Forgiveness does not make injustice irrelevant. Where a sin involves another person’s rights, repentance requires returning the right, seeking pardon when appropriate, and repairing harm.

Allah’s forgiveness is not limited by the size of a past sin when repentance is sincere. The Qur’an calls people not to despair of Allah’s mercy (39:53). At the same time, the believer does not use hope as an excuse to persist knowingly. Hope pulls him toward obedience; it does not authorize carelessness.

### Protection from the Fire

They ask not only for forgiveness but also for protection from the Fire. This combines removal of past guilt with protection from future consequence. The Fire is real, and belief in it guards the heart against reducing morality to worldly outcomes. A deed may be profitable in this world and destructive in the Hereafter.

The request also reflects a healthy fear. Fear of Hell is not the whole of worship, but it is a mercy that restrains the servant when desire is strong. It encourages him to avoid injustice, unlawful wealth, broken promises, and hidden sins.

### Supplication in the Plural

The prayer is expressed as “we have believed” and “our sins.” The believers do not approach Allah as isolated individuals. They ask for themselves and their community. This teaches concern for the guidance and safety of others, while not transferring responsibility for one’s own actions.

A community shaped by this prayer will not celebrate sin or shame repentant people. It will command what is right, discourage what is wrong, and keep the door of return open.

### Faith, Hope, and Fear

The previous verse mentioned Paradise and Allah’s pleasure; this verse mentions forgiveness and protection from the Fire. Together they establish the balanced path of *rajāʾ* and *khawf*, hope and fear. Hope without fear can become false security, while fear without hope can become despair. The Qur’an cultivates both so that the servant moves toward Allah with love, humility, and seriousness.

- Qur’an 4:110 and 25:70 on forgiveness for the one who repents and reforms.
- Qur’an 66:8 on sincere repentance and the prayer for completion of light.
- Qur’an 23:60 on righteous people acting while their hearts remain fearful.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith qudsī in which Allah says that sins are forgiven for the one who asks Him and does not associate partners with Him; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, reports on repentance.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:17

> ˹It is they˺ who are patient, sincere, obedient, and charitable, and who pray for forgiveness before dawn.[127]

**Commentary:**

### A Portrait of the Righteous

This verse describes the people who ask for Paradise and protection from the Fire. Their righteousness is not a vague feeling. It appears in patience, truthfulness, obedience, generosity, and private repentance. The order moves from endurance to honesty, from submission to service, and finally to intimate worship before dawn.

These qualities complete one another. Patience without truth may become stubbornness; truthfulness without obedience may remain speech; obedience without charity may become ritualism; charity without repentance may become self-admiration. The righteous cultivate the whole person.

### Patience in Every Direction

*Al-ṣābirūn* are those who practise *ṣabr*. They remain firm in obedience, restrain themselves from sin, and endure trials without abandoning faith. Patience does not mean passively accepting oppression or refusing to seek lawful relief. It means responding to difficulty without rebellion against Allah and without violating the rights of others.

The Qur’an promises that Allah is with the patient (2:153) and that their reward is beyond ordinary measure (39:10). The quality is especially needed when the right path is slow, unpopular, or costly.

### Truthfulness

*Al-ṣādiqūn* are the truthful. Truthfulness includes honest speech, sincere intention, accurate testimony, faithful promises, and alignment between the inner and outer self. A person can avoid an obvious lie while still deceiving through concealment, manipulation, or exaggerated claims.

Truthfulness is essential to faith because revelation itself is truth. The believer asks whether his worship is sincere, whether his repentance is genuine, and whether his public advocacy matches his private conduct. The Prophet ﷺ said that truthfulness leads to righteousness and righteousness leads to Paradise, in the hadith recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

### Obedience and Devotion

*Al-qānitūn* are devoted and obedient. Their obedience is not the obedience of a slave to a human tyrant; it is willing submission to Allah, expressed through obligatory worship and lawful conduct. They maintain prayer, fulfil trusts, keep family and social rights, and return to Allah when they fall short.

Obedience also includes listening before speaking about revelation. A believer may seek understanding and ask questions, but he does not make personal desire the final authority over a clear command.

### Charity and Circulation of Blessings

*Al-munfiqūn* spend in Allah’s cause. Their generosity includes obligatory zakāh, voluntary charity, family support, hospitality, public benefit, and giving time and skill. Spending in prosperity is a sign of gratitude; spending in hardship is a sign of trust. The Qur’an praises those who spend secretly and openly and assures them that their reward is with Allah (2:274).

Charity must be lawful and dignified. It should not be accompanied by humiliation, reminders of debt, or harm. The best spending recognizes that the giver is only a trustee of what Allah provided.

### Before Dawn

Those who seek forgiveness before dawn practise *al-istighfār bi-al-asḥār*. The pre-dawn hours are quiet, and waking then often requires sincerity because there is little public recognition. The Qur’an praises people who sleep little at night and seek forgiveness before dawn (51:17–18).

This does not mean that a person who cannot wake before dawn is excluded from mercy. The verse describes an honoured practice, while obligatory prayers and ordinary repentance remain open to everyone. A short, sincere *astaghfirullāh* may be more valuable than a long act performed for display.

### The Last Third of the Night

The Prophet ﷺ taught that Allah’s mercy and invitation to supplication are especially emphasized in the last third of the night, in an authentic hadith recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The believer uses this time to pray, ask forgiveness, and speak to Allah privately.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:153, 39:10, and 76:24 on patience and its reward.
- Qur’an 9:119 and 33:35 on truthfulness and the truthful men and women.
- Qur’an 2:274, 3:92, and 64:16 on spending in prosperity and hardship.
- Qur’an 51:17–18 and 73:20 on seeking forgiveness at night and before dawn.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith on truthfulness and the hadith concerning supplication in the last third of the night.
- For *ṣabr*, *ṣidq*, *qunūt*, *infāq*, and *istighfār bi-al-asḥār*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:17.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:18

> Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—and so are the angels and people of knowledge. He is the Maintainer of justice. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### The Greatest Testimony

This verse presents the testimony of divine oneness as the greatest truth in existence. Allah Himself bears witness to it, and the angels and people of knowledge bear witness as well. The testimony is not merely a slogan. It means that no one deserves worship except Allah and that all created authority remains dependent upon Him.

Allah’s own testimony is established through His revelation, His signs in creation, and the perfection of His names and acts. He does not need witnesses to validate His existence. The mention of angels and scholars honours them because they recognize and convey what Allah has made clear.

### The Witness of the Angels

The angels bear witness through their constant obedience and glorification. They do not share Allah’s divinity, nor do they receive worship. Their testimony is expressed through knowledge of revelation and their submission to Allah’s commands. This corrects communities that respected angels yet turned them into intermediaries or objects of devotion.

Their witness also shows that true knowledge should lead to worship. An angel’s knowledge does not make him independent; it deepens obedience. Human learning should have the same effect.

### The People of Knowledge

The phrase “people of knowledge” (*ulū al-ʿilm*) refers especially to those who know Allah’s revelation and the evidence of His oneness. Their honour is not based merely on collecting information or winning arguments. They testify through understanding, sincerity, teaching, and conduct.

Knowledge carries responsibility. A scholar must distinguish evidence from speculation, explain truth faithfully, and remain humble before Allah. A person who uses knowledge for status, manipulation, or concealment has betrayed the purpose of knowledge, even if his vocabulary is impressive.

The verse also establishes a high station for learning. It places people of knowledge alongside the angels in bearing witness to tawḥīd, though never equating created beings with Allah. The honour is an honour of responsibility and proximity to truth.

### Allah Maintains Justice

Allah is *qāʾim bi-al-qisṭ*, standing by and maintaining justice. His creation is not governed by arbitrary injustice. He gives each thing its due, commands human beings to uphold justice, and will judge every person with perfect knowledge. His oneness is joined to justice because a true understanding of Allah cannot be separated from moral order.

This also means that worship is not a private escape from ethical responsibility. Anyone who claims devotion while oppressing people, cheating in trade, withholding rights, or spreading false testimony has contradicted the testimony of the verse.

### Repetition of Tawḥīd

The verse repeats “There is no god worthy of worship except Him,” then closes with *al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*, the Almighty and All-Wise. The repetition reinforces certainty, while the two names show that Allah’s power is governed by wisdom. He is capable of everything and never acts without perfect wisdom.

The believer repeats the testimony to renew knowledge, worship, and allegiance. It is a statement of liberation from fear of created beings and from obedience to any command that contradicts Allah’s guidance.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:255, 16:36, and 47:19 on tawḥīd and the testimony that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah.
- Qur’an 4:135 and 5:8 on maintaining justice and bearing witness fairly.
- Qur’an 35:28 on those who truly fear Allah through knowledge.
- Qur’an 30:30 and 51:56 on the natural orientation toward worship and the purpose of creation.
- Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī and Sunan Abī Dāwūd, reports on the virtue and responsibility of scholars as inheritors of prophetic knowledge.
- For *shahādah*, *ulū al-ʿilm*, *qisṭ*, *al-ʿAzīz*, and *al-Ḥakīm*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:18.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:19

> Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam.[128] Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them.[129] Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning.

**Commentary:**

### Islam as Submission to Allah

The word *Islām* comes from the root associated with submission, peace, and safety. In this verse it means submission to the one true God through sincere worship and obedience. The essential religion of all prophets was this submission: Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā, and Muhammad ﷺ all called people to worship Allah alone.

For the final community, Islam includes believing in the Qur’an and following Muhammad ﷺ as the final messenger. This does not erase the earlier prophets; it fulfils the continuous message and provides the final form of revealed guidance. The Qur’an therefore commands Muslims to believe in all messengers without rejecting some and accepting others (2:136; 3:84).

### One Truth and Many Communities

The verse does not deny that people have different cultures, languages, laws, and histories. It identifies the single spiritual foundation accepted by Allah: sincere submission and tawḥīd. Earlier communities received different legal details suited to their circumstances (5:48), but the foundation of worshipping Allah alone remained one.

The claim that Islam is the only accepted way is not permission to force conversion. The Qur’an says, “There is no compulsion in religion” (2:256), and commands believers to invite with wisdom and debate in the best manner (16:125). Truth is to be proclaimed clearly; faith cannot be manufactured through coercion.

### Dispute after Knowledge

The People of the Scripture were given knowledge of revelation and the signs of truth. Their disputes arose not simply from lack of information but from envy, rivalry, and unwillingness to submit. Knowledge can guide a person, but it can also become a tool of competition when the heart seeks status rather than truth.

The warning is not an accusation against every Jew or Christian. The Qur’an explicitly says that the People of the Book are not all alike and praises those who believe, worship, and do good (3:113–115). The verse criticizes those who receive knowledge and then dispute out of envy or reject what it requires. The same warning applies to Muslims who turn religious knowledge into factional pride.

### The Danger of Religious Envy

Envy, *ḥasad*, is wishing that another person lose a blessing or refusing to accept good because it came through someone outside one’s group. Religious envy can lead people to reject a prophet not because his message lacks evidence but because he does not belong to their lineage or institution. This is a serious spiritual disease because it places group identity above Allah’s command.

The cure is submission. A sincere seeker asks, “What is true?” rather than “What preserves my status?” He accepts truth even when it requires correction, apology, or change.

### Swift Reckoning

Allah is swift in reckoning because He has complete knowledge and needs no investigation or delay. The warning encourages people not to postpone obedience. Human courts may take years and may be deceived; Allah’s judgement is immediate in knowledge and perfect in justice, even if its visible manifestation is delayed until the Hereafter.

The believer therefore distinguishes between patience with people and delay in his own repentance. He may give others time to learn, but he should not use the existence of time as an excuse to resist clear guidance.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:130–133, 3:67, and 42:13 on the submission of the prophets to Allah.
- Qur’an 2:136 and 3:84 on believing in all messengers and revelations.
- Qur’an 5:48 on different revealed laws and the Qur’an as a guardian over previous scripture.
- Qur’an 2:256 and 16:125 on no compulsion and inviting with wisdom.
- Qur’an 3:113–115 on righteous people among the People of the Book.
- For *Islām*, *dīn*, envy after knowledge, and the universality of tawḥīd, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:19.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:20

> So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.” And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺,[130] “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?” If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants.

**Commentary:**

### The Prophetic Response to Argument

When people argue about the truth of revelation, the Prophet ﷺ is instructed to state his own position plainly: he has submitted himself to Allah, and so have his followers. This is not an evasive answer. It identifies the foundation of his mission: worship Allah alone, accept His revelation, and obey Him rather than surrendering to tribal loyalty, public opinion, or personal desire.

The phrase “I have submitted” is a declaration of humility. The Prophet ﷺ does not present himself as an independent source of authority. He is a servant and messenger who receives guidance and conveys it. His followers share the same submission, so their faith is not devotion to the personality of Muhammad but obedience to the Lord who sent him.

### A Question that Invites Reflection

The Prophet is told to ask both the People of the Scripture and the *ummiyyūn* whether they have submitted. The question invites an honest response rather than forcing one. If they submit to Allah, they are guided; if they turn away, the Prophet’s responsibility is to deliver the message clearly.

The word *ummiyyūn* is often translated as “illiterate people” or people without a previously revealed scripture. In this context it refers to those outside the communities who had received the earlier scriptures, especially the Arabs addressed by the Prophet. It should not be used as an insult. Lack of literacy or access to a previous scripture does not make a person less human or less capable of truth.

### Guidance Is the Result of Submission

The verse links guidance to submission. Guidance is not simply possession of information. A person may recognize an argument and still refuse what it requires. True guidance means accepting Allah’s authority and allowing revelation to shape worship, ethics, and choices.

This submission is not humiliation before human beings. It liberates the servant from created masters. The believer may respect parents, teachers, rulers, and institutions, but ultimate obedience belongs to Allah. If a created command conflicts with Allah’s command, it cannot be obeyed in that matter.

### The Duty to Convey

“If they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver the message.” This establishes the boundary of prophetic responsibility. The messenger must explain, warn, teach, and embody the truth, but he cannot force faith into a heart. The Prophet ﷺ grieved over rejection, yet Allah reminded him that he does not control guidance (28:56).

The same principle applies to Muslim callers and teachers. They must speak with knowledge, wisdom, good character, and patience. They are not responsible for controlling another person’s final choice. Coercion, manipulation, and abuse contradict the verse’s distinction between conveying and guiding.

### Debate with Good Conduct

The verse begins with the possibility of argument but directs the Prophet toward clarity rather than aggression. Other Qur’anic verses command believers to debate the People of the Scripture in the best manner, except those who act unjustly (29:46). A Muslim should represent Islam accurately, listen carefully, answer evidence with evidence, and avoid mockery and false accusations.

Submission does not require abandoning reason. It requires allowing reason to follow truth rather than using argument as a shield for pride.

### Allah Sees His Servants

The closing statement, “Allah is All-Seeing of His servants,” reminds both sides that Allah knows the entire exchange. He sees the sincerity of the seeker, the pride of the rejecter, the integrity of the caller, and the harm caused by an abusive disputant. A person may impress an audience while concealing his motives, but Allah sees the heart and the consequences.

This ending also gives comfort to the Prophet ﷺ and his followers. Their duty is faithful delivery; the final judgement belongs to Allah. They should not despair when people turn away, nor become arrogant when others accept.
