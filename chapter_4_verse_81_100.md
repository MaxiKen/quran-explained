## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:81

> And they say, “We obey,” but when they leave you, a group of them would spend the night contradicting what they said. Allah records all their schemes. So turn away from them, and put your trust in Allah. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

**Commentary:**

### Words and Conduct

The hypocrites say, “We obey,” in the presence of the Prophet ﷺ, but a group of them spend the night planning against what they had just professed. Their private contradiction exposes the gap between speech and obedience. The issue is not a person who asks for clarification or struggles to understand; it is deliberate deception.

### Secret Planning

The night setting suggests concealed meetings and schemes. Private discussion is not automatically sinful, but secret counsel intended to overturn justice, spread fear, or betray a community is morally serious. Allah records not only the public statement but also the hidden plan.

### The Prophet’s Response

The Prophet ﷺ is told to turn away from them and trust Allah. This does not mean ignoring danger or abandoning accountability. It means he should not allow their duplicity to control his heart or distract him from the mission. A leader may establish safeguards and consequences while refusing to be consumed by suspicion.

### Tawakkul and Practical Wisdom

Putting one’s trust in Allah, *tawakkul*, is not passivity. The Prophet ﷺ took precautions, consulted, verified information, and protected the community. Trust means that the heart relies on Allah after lawful effort, rather than relying on the success of a plan or the sincerity of people who may betray it.

### Allah as Trustee

Allah is sufficient as *wakīl*, the Trustee of Affairs. He knows which plans will succeed, exposes deception at the proper time, and protects the truth even when people attempt to undermine it. The believer can act responsibly without believing that every outcome depends on human control.

### A Warning against Double Speech

The verse calls Muslims to consistency. Agreement in public followed by sabotage in private is a form of betrayal in families, workplaces, organisations, and governments. Sincere disagreement should be expressed honestly and respectfully rather than hidden behind false obedience.

- Qur’an 8:27 and 23:8 on fulfilling trusts and covenants.
- Qur’an 3:159–160 and 65:3 on consultation, practical effort, and reliance upon Allah.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports before acting.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:82

> Do they not then reflect on the Quran? Had it been from anyone other than Allah, they would have certainly found in it many inconsistencies.

**Commentary:**

### Reflection, Not Mere Recitation

Allah asks people to reflect on the Qur’an, *tadabbur*. Recitation is a great act of worship, but the verse also calls readers to consider meaning, context, coherence, guidance, and consequences. A believer approaches the Qur’an with an open heart, sound reasoning, and willingness to be corrected.

### Coherence as a Sign

The Qur’an was revealed over many years amid changing circumstances, questions, conflict, law, history, and spiritual instruction. Yet its central message remains coherent: worship Allah alone, follow revelation, uphold justice, repent, and prepare for the Hereafter. The absence of genuine contradiction is presented as evidence of its divine source.

### Difficult Passages Require Learning

The verse does not claim that every reader will immediately understand every passage. Apparent difficulty can arise from translation, context, Arabic usage, legal progression, metaphor, or limited knowledge. The response is to study the verses together and return unclear matters to those grounded in knowledge, not to declare contradiction hastily.

### Reflection and Obedience

True reflection should affect conduct. If a person admires Qur’anic eloquence but continues to cheat, oppress, or betray trusts, he has not fully engaged with its guidance. The Qur’an is a book to be believed, understood, recited, and lived.

### The Qur’an and Hypocrisy

The surrounding passage discusses people who publicly say they obey and privately contradict their words. Reflecting on the Qur’an exposes this inner inconsistency. Revelation calls the reader to align speech, belief, and action.

### A Method of Reading

A responsible reader considers the whole Qur’an, the authentic Sunnah, Arabic language, historical context, and principles of interpretation. He does not isolate a phrase to create confusion or use one difficult question as an excuse to reject the entire revelation.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:81–82 on hypocrisy, private contradiction, and reflection on the Qur’an.
- Qur’an 3:7 on clear and less immediately clear verses and returning to sound knowledge.
- Qur’an 39:23 on the consistency and beauty of the Qur’an.
- Qur’an 17:9 and 38:29 on the Qur’an as guidance and a book to be contemplated.
- Qur’an 2:23 and 17:88 on the Qur’an’s challenge and divine origin.
- Qur’an 47:24 on reflecting deeply and avoiding sealed hearts.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on reciting, understanding, acting upon, and living by the Qur’an.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:83

> And when they hear news of security or fear,[211] they publicize it. Had they referred it to the Messenger or their authorities, those with sound judgment among them would have validated it. Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, you would have followed Satan—except for a few.

**Commentary:**

### News in a Crisis

The verse addresses reports of security or fear—matters that can affect public safety, military decisions, and community morale. Some people immediately publicized whatever they heard without verification. In a crisis, careless circulation can create panic, expose people to danger, assist an enemy, or cause an innocent person to be blamed.

### Refer Information Responsibly

Believers are instructed to refer such information to the Messenger ﷺ or to responsible authorities. Today this includes competent officials, trusted scholars, security professionals, journalists who verify claims, and subject-matter experts. The principle is not blind trust in every authority; it is responsible referral to people able to investigate and assess consequences.

### Sound Judgement and Verification

Those with sound judgement, *ulū al-amr* and people able to derive conclusions, would investigate and validate the report. They distinguish fact from rumour, significance from exaggeration, and urgent warning from manipulation. Expertise and consultation are therefore part of Islamic public ethics.

### Public Interest and Whistleblowing

The verse does not require hiding genuine wrongdoing or silencing victims. Information that must be reported for protection and justice should be taken to a competent and trustworthy channel. The prohibition concerns irresponsible publicity and unverified circulation, not truthful testimony made to prevent harm.

### Satanic Amplification

Satan benefits when fear moves faster than truth. A rumour can divide families, damage reputations, and make people act unjustly before evidence is known. Allah’s grace and mercy protect the community when believers pause, verify, consult, and speak with responsibility.

### Digital Application

Forwarding a message, reposting an alarming video, or publishing an accusation can reproduce the very behaviour condemned here. Muslims should check the source, date, context, and evidence before sharing. Silence can be wise when public repetition would cause harm.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying the report of a wrongdoer before harming people.
- Qur’an 24:15–19 on spreading unverified speech and indecency.
- Qur’an 33:60–61 on rumours and public harm.
- Qur’an 17:36 on not following what one does not know.
- Qur’an 5:2 and 16:90 on cooperation in righteousness and avoiding harm.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports warning against transmitting everything one hears and teaching verification, truthful speech, and responsible testimony.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:84

> So fight in the cause of Allah ˹O Prophet˺. You are accountable for none but yourself. And motivate the believers ˹to fight˺, so perhaps Allah will curb the disbelievers’ might. And Allah is far superior in might and in punishment.

**Commentary:**

### The Messenger’s Responsibility

The Prophet ﷺ is told to act in Allah’s cause and is reminded that he is accountable for his own duty. He cannot force every person to believe or guarantee that every companion will obey. His responsibility is to convey, lead, motivate, and act faithfully.

This also teaches individuals not to make their salvation dependent on the failures of others.

### Motivating Believers

The Prophet is instructed to motivate the believers. Leadership includes courage, encouragement, clear information, and protection of morale. Motivation must not be manipulation, false promises, or the concealment of danger. People should understand the cause, the risks, the limits, and the responsibilities involved.

### Curbing Aggression

The aim is to curb the might of hostile disbelievers who threaten and oppress the community. The verse belongs to the context of armed conflict and cannot be separated from Qur’anic prohibitions against transgression, betrayal, and harm to noncombatants.

The purpose of lawful defence is to stop aggression and protect people, not to dominate or force belief.

### Allah’s Greater Might

Allah is far superior in might and punishment. Human power is temporary, while Allah’s power is complete. This gives believers courage without turning them arrogant. They act responsibly but do not imagine that victory depends solely on their numbers or weapons.

### No Individual Vigilantism

The verse addresses the Prophet ﷺ as the legitimate leader of the community. It does not authorize individuals or private groups to declare wars, attack civilians, or impose their own interpretation of “Allah’s cause.” Lawful authority, just cause, proportionality, and protection of life remain essential.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:85

> Whoever intercedes for a good cause will have a share in the reward, and whoever intercedes for an evil cause will have a share in the burden. And Allah is Watchful over all things.

**Commentary:**

### Influence Carries Responsibility

Intercession, *shafāʿah*, includes advocating for, recommending, mediating, or helping to bring about an outcome. Whoever supports a good cause receives a share in its reward; whoever supports an evil cause carries a share of its burden.

A person is accountable not only for what he does directly, but also for the harm or benefit he knowingly helps to produce.

### Good Intercession

Good advocacy includes reconciling disputing people, recommending a qualified candidate, helping a victim access justice, securing medical care, defending an innocent person, supporting a charitable project, or encouraging someone toward repentance.

The advocate should remain truthful. A good outcome cannot be pursued through lies, bribery, or unfairly denying another person’s rights.

### Evil Intercession

Evil advocacy includes false testimony, lobbying for corruption, protecting an abuser, helping someone evade justice, spreading a rumour, arranging unlawful financial gain, or pressuring officials to favour an undeserving person. Even when the advocate does not perform the final act, participation in the chain of harm carries responsibility.

### Mediation and Fairness

A mediator should not confuse reconciliation with suppressing truth. He must protect the weak, hear both sides, disclose conflicts of interest, and refuse to make peace by rewarding oppression. A recommendation is good when it directs opportunity to someone qualified, not when it bypasses justice for a friend.

### Influence in Speech and Media

The verse applies to public speech, social media, fundraising, teaching, and leadership. Sharing a message can encourage charity or spread hatred. Endorsing a person can open a door to benefit or empower abuse. Before recommending, the believer should verify facts and consider consequences.

### Allah Is Watchful

Allah is Watchful, *raqīb*, over every influence and connection. He knows the intention of the advocate, the information available, and the benefit or harm that follows. Human beings may forget who helped an action happen, but Allah does not.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:86

> And when you are greeted, respond with a better greeting or at least similarly. Surely Allah is a ˹vigilant˺ Reckoner of all things.

**Commentary:**

### Returning the Greeting

Allah commands believers to return a greeting with one better than it or at least equivalent. The Islamic greeting, *al-salāmu ʿalaykum*, is a prayer of peace and safety. Returning it strengthens trust and turns an ordinary encounter into an act of worship.

A better response includes more complete words, warmth, and sincere goodwill, while a similar response fulfils the minimum duty.

### A Culture of Peace

Greetings are small but powerful acts of social recognition. They reduce suspicion, soften distance between strangers, and make people feel safe. The command applies at home, in public, at work, online, and across differences of class and ethnicity.

A person should not reserve courtesy only for those who can benefit him.

### Respectful Boundaries

Responding to a greeting does not require accepting abuse, manipulation, or an unsafe relationship. A person may reply briefly, set a boundary, or seek protection while remaining free from arrogance and needless hostility. Justice and safety remain part of Islamic manners.

### Beyond Spoken Words

The principle extends to invitations, condolences, congratulations, messages, and acts of courtesy. A believer should answer people respectfully and not use silence as a tool of humiliation. If he cannot respond immediately, he should do so when reasonably able.

### Allah Reckons Every Interaction

Allah is a vigilant Reckoner, *ḥasīb*. He records the greeting ignored out of pride, the kind reply that comforted someone, and the hostile response that caused injury. Manners are not insignificant before Him.

### Greeting and Brotherhood

The Prophet ﷺ encouraged spreading *salām* among believers as a means of love. Greeting people is therefore part of building the social trust needed for justice, consultation, and mutual support.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:87

> Allah, there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. He will certainly gather ˹all of˺ you together on the Day of Judgment—about which there is no doubt. And whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?

**Commentary:**

### Tawḥīd and Resurrection

The verse joins the testimony that Allah alone is worthy of worship with the certainty of resurrection. The One who created and sustains humanity is able to gather every person for judgement. Tawḥīd therefore includes belief in Allah’s exclusive worship, His perfect power, and His promise of the Last Day.

### Every Person Will Be Gathered

Allah will gather all people together. No one disappears into history, and no deed is lost because a court, government, or family failed to record it. The oppressed and the powerful will stand before the same Lord, with every claim examined.

### No Doubt about the Day

The Day of Judgment is described as certain, not a possibility. This certainty gives moral weight to everyday actions. A person may hide financial corruption, false testimony, or private betrayal from people, but he cannot escape the gathering before Allah.

### Allah’s Word Is Truth

“Whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?” is a rhetorical affirmation of divine truthfulness. Human promises can be broken by weakness, ignorance, or changing circumstances. Allah’s promise is based on perfect knowledge and power and cannot fail.

### A Foundation for Justice

The certainty of judgement protects the believer from despair when injustice appears to triumph. It also prevents him from becoming unjust when he has power. Every greeting, judgement, recommendation, and hidden intention will be brought before Allah.

### Living in Readiness

Belief in the Gathering is meant to produce repentance, honest dealing, prayer, and compassion. The believer prepares not through fear alone, but through faith, repair of wrongs, and hope in Allah’s mercy.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:88

> Why are you ˹believers˺ divided into two groups regarding the hypocrites while Allah allowed them to regress ˹to disbelief˺ because of their misdeeds? Do you wish to guide those left by Allah to stray? And whoever Allah leaves to stray, you will never find for them a way.

**Commentary:**

### Division over Hypocrites

The believers were divided regarding how to deal with people who claimed Islam while supporting hostile forces or retreating from the community. Some may have wished to excuse them, while others recognized the danger of their conduct. Allah calls the community to clarity rather than allowing uncertainty to destroy unity.

### Returning to Disbelief

The phrase “Allah allowed them to regress” describes a consequence of persistent misdeeds. Allah’s leaving a person to stray is not arbitrary; it follows a chosen pattern of rejection, deception, and refusal to repent. The person’s choices and Allah’s just decree are both real.

### Guidance Is from Allah

The believers cannot guide someone whom Allah leaves to stray. This does not excuse them from conveying truth, advising wisely, protecting the community, or keeping the door of repentance open. It reminds them that hearts are ultimately in Allah’s control.

### Do Not Rush to Label People

The verse concerns a known group whose conduct and allegiance had been exposed in the historical context. It is not permission to declare every Muslim who sins, doubts, or makes a mistake a hypocrite. Specific judgements require evidence, and Allah alone knows the final condition of hearts.

### Unity with Discernment

Unity does not mean ignoring betrayal or allowing dangerous manipulation. It means addressing disagreement through evidence, justice, consultation, and lawful authority rather than through rumours and factional hatred.

### Hope for Repentance

Other verses in this passage make clear that those who repent, mend their ways, hold fast to Allah, and become sincere can return to the believers. The warning is directed at persistence, not at a repentant person seeking reform.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:89

> They wish you would disbelieve as they have disbelieved, so you may all be alike. So do not take them as allies unless they emigrate in the cause of Allah. But if they turn away, then seize them and kill them wherever you find them, and do not take any of them as allies or helpers,[212]

**Commentary:**

### Hostile Allegiance in Context

This verse addresses a specific group of hypocrites and hostile opponents who sought to turn the believers back from Islam and were connected to an active conflict. The command concerns military allegiance and treason in a wartime setting, not ordinary relations with every person who does not share Islam.

The word *awliyāʾ* here refers to protective political or military alliance with those actively seeking the community’s destruction. It does not prohibit kindness, trade, neighbourly relations, or cooperation in good with peaceful non-Muslims.

### Emigration as a Sign of Allegiance

Emigration, *hijrah*, in the historical context meant leaving the hostile camp and joining the Muslim community in loyalty to Allah and His Messenger. It was evidence that a person had abandoned the effort to undermine the believers. Allegiance was demonstrated through conduct, not merely through a public claim.

### The Wartime Ruling

The order to seize and kill belongs to the legal circumstances of armed conflict, where a hostile group actively supported aggression and refused the terms that would end its threat. It is not a licence for individuals to attack civilians, punish apostasy privately, or kill people merely because they are non-Muslim.

The following verses immediately create exceptions for treaty allies and those who refrain from fighting, showing that the ruling is not indiscriminate.

### No Forced Conversion

The verse does not command killing people to make them believe. The surrounding concern is hostile action, betrayal, and a military threat. The Qur’an states that there is no compulsion in religion and commands peace when the enemy inclines toward peace.

### Authority and Due Process

Even in a historical armed conflict, seizure and punishment belong to legitimate authority and lawful procedure. Modern Muslims cannot revive wartime rulings through private vigilantism or apply them outside the conditions of a recognized conflict. Any use of the verse to target peaceful people is a betrayal of its context and the Qur’an’s justice.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:90

> except those who are allies of a people you are bound with in a treaty or those wholeheartedly opposed to fighting either you or their own people. If Allah had willed, He would have empowered them to fight you. So if they refrain from fighting you and offer you peace, then Allah does not permit you to harm them.

**Commentary:**

### Treaty Protection

The verse immediately limits the preceding wartime command. People allied with a community bound to the Muslims by treaty are protected from attack. A covenant is not a disposable political convenience; it creates obligations even when a military opportunity appears.

Breaking a treaty without lawful cause is betrayal and can produce wider injustice.

### Those Who Refuse to Fight

The verse also protects people who are wholeheartedly opposed to fighting the Muslims or their own people. They may have the ability to join one side, but they choose neutrality and peace. Their refusal to fight is a reason to leave them unharmed, not an excuse to pressure them into combat.

### Peace Ends Hostility

If they refrain from fighting and offer peace, Allah does not permit the believers to harm them. This is a clear limit on retaliation. The goal of a legitimate conflict is to stop aggression and protect people, not to punish everyone associated with an opposing population.

### Power and Restraint

Allah could have empowered these people to fight, but He did not. The statement reminds believers that power and opportunity are under Allah’s control. When an opponent chooses restraint, believers must respond with restraint rather than interpreting peace as weakness to exploit.

### No Harm to Peaceful People

The verse supports a broad principle: peaceful conduct protects a person from military harm. Non-Muslims who live peacefully, fulfil agreements, and do not participate in aggression are not included in a command directed at hostile forces.

### Modern Application

Treaties, refugee protections, ceasefires, diplomatic agreements, and civilian status must be honoured. Individuals cannot decide that a neighbour, migrant, or political opponent is outside protection and then use scripture to harm them. Lawful authority and due process are essential.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:91

> You will find others who wish to be safe from you and their own people. Yet they cannot resist the temptation ˹of disbelief or hostility˺.

> If they do not keep away, offer you peace, or refrain from attacking you, then seize them and kill them wherever you find them. We have given you full permission over such people.

**Commentary:**

### Double Allegiance and Hostility

The verse describes people who want security from the Muslims and from their own people, yet repeatedly return to hostility. Their conduct is not genuine neutrality; they attempt to use peace with one side as protection while joining aggression against the other. The issue is active betrayal and danger, not a person’s private belief.

### Cumulative Conditions

The permission to seize and fight is connected to the failure of all protective conditions: they do not withdraw from hostility, do not offer peace, and do not refrain from attacking. The verse must be read with the preceding protection for treaty allies and peaceful people.

It is not a command to kill everyone who has a different religion or political view.

### Peaceful Alternatives Remain Open

The wording shows that hostility can end the danger. If the group withdraws, offers peace, or stops attacking, the believers cannot harm them on the basis of past suspicion alone. The aim is to remove an active threat, not to pursue endless revenge.

### Lawful Authority

“We have given you full permission” addresses the Muslim community under legitimate prophetic leadership in a historical conflict. It does not authorise individuals, mobs, or modern factions to decide that someone is hostile and punish them. Evidence, command, due process, and protection of civilians remain essential.

### Distinguishing Belief from Combat

The verse mentions disbelief alongside hostility because the group’s refusal was connected to political betrayal and aggression. The Qur’an elsewhere protects non-Muslims who are peaceful. Belief is not itself a battlefield crime, and no one may be attacked merely for holding a different faith.

### The Ethics of Ending Conflict

Even when fighting is permitted, Muslims must avoid mutilation, torture, treachery, unnecessary destruction, and harm to noncombatants. The Prophet ﷺ commanded justice and prohibited killing women and children. A strict reading of the whole Qur’an prevents this passage from being turned into a general licence for violence.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:89–91 on hostile groups, peace, treaties, and the limits of military permission.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:92

> It is not lawful for a believer to kill another except by mistake. And whoever kills a believer unintentionally must free a believing slave and pay blood-money to the victim’s family—unless they waive it charitably. But if the victim is a believer from a hostile people, then a believing slave must be freed. And if the victim is from a people bound with you in a treaty, then blood-money must be paid to the family along with freeing a believing slave. Those who are unable, let them fast two consecutive months—as a means of repentance to Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### The Sanctity of Believing Life

The verse states that a believer may not kill another believer except by mistake. Human life is sacred, and conflict, anger, suspicion, or political disagreement cannot make intentional killing lawful. The exception of mistake does not make accidental death insignificant; it establishes duties of expiation, financial liability, and repair.

### Expiation and Compensation

Unintentional killing requires *kaffārah*, an act of expiation: historically, freeing a believing slave. It also requires *diyah*, blood-money paid to the victim’s family, unless they waive it charitably. The two duties address different dimensions—repentance before Allah and material support for those who lost a family member.

### Different Community Relationships

The verse distinguishes a believer killed within the Muslim community, a believer connected to a hostile people, and a person from a community bound by treaty. These rules recognize that family rights, political agreements, and community relations all matter. A treaty creates obligations even during conflict, and its violation cannot be excused by emotion.

The classifications require reliable evidence and lawful authority; individuals cannot invent them to avoid responsibility.

### Fasting when Emancipation Is Impossible

Those unable to free a believing slave must fast two consecutive months as repentance. The historical reference to slavery belongs to the legal society of revelation; modern kidnapping, trafficking, and ownership of persons are unlawful. Where emancipation is not possible, the Qur’anic expiation directs the responsible person toward sustained fasting and repentance.

### Accidents and Negligence

“By mistake” includes an unintended death, but people must still examine whether negligence contributed. Reckless driving, unsafe workplaces, careless weapons, and ignoring known hazards may create legal and moral liability even when death was not intended. Accident does not mean that every precaution was taken.

### Allah Knows the Full Circumstance

Allah is All-Knowing and All-Wise. He knows intention, carelessness, coercion, the relationship between communities, the family’s loss, and the sincerity of repentance. Human courts must investigate fairly, while Allah’s final judgement includes what no witness could see.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:93

> And whoever kills a believer intentionally, their reward will be Hell— where they will stay indefinitely.[213] Allah will be displeased with them, condemn them, and will prepare for them a tremendous punishment.

**Commentary:**

### The Gravity of Intentional Murder

After explaining the duties arising from accidental killing, the verse gives an exceptionally severe warning for intentional murder. Deliberately taking the life of a believer violates the sanctity Allah established and destroys a family, a community, and a trust that cannot be restored in this world.

No anger, ideology, wealth, honour claim, or political goal makes murder righteous.

### Four Expressions of Punishment

The verse mentions Hell, Allah’s displeasure, His condemnation, and a tremendous punishment. The accumulation of warnings shows that intentional murder is not a minor legal violation. It is a crime against the victim, the victim’s family, and Allah’s command.

### Repentance and Justice

The severe warning must not be used to close the door of repentance that Allah opens elsewhere. A murderer who sincerely repents must stop the wrongdoing, submit to lawful judgement, seek forgiveness, and repair the rights of the victim’s family as far as possible. Repentance does not permit a person to evade worldly justice or erase the family’s right to pursue lawful remedies.

Scholars discuss the relationship between this warning, repentance, retribution, pardon, and Allah’s final judgement. The safe attitude is to fear the threat and never rely upon a claim of easy forgiveness.

### No Vigilantism

Only legitimate authority may investigate, judge, and punish a killing. Families and individuals may not carry out revenge, target relatives of the accused, or use the verse to justify extrajudicial violence. Justice requires evidence, due process, and protection from false accusation.

### Human Life and Public Safety

The principle applies to murder in homes, streets, prisons, war zones, political conflicts, and communal violence. It also warns leaders whose policies knowingly expose civilians to lethal harm. The greater a person’s authority, the greater his accountability for lives placed under his care.

### Hope for Victims

The verse assures families of victims that Allah does not overlook their loss. Even when earthly justice is delayed or corrupted, the murderer cannot escape Allah’s knowledge. This hope should support lawful pursuit of justice rather than uncontrolled vengeance.

- Qur’an 39:53–54 on the breadth of Allah’s mercy and returning before death.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:94

> O believers! When you struggle in the cause of Allah, be sure of who you fight. And do not say to those who offer you ˹greetings of˺ peace, “You are no believer!”—seeking a fleeting worldly gain.[214] Instead, Allah has infinite bounties ˹in store˺. You were initially like them then Allah blessed you ˹with Islam˺. So be sure! Indeed, Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

**Commentary:**

### Verify before Acting

Allah commands believers to be sure of whom they fight. In a conflict, identity, intention, and conduct must not be guessed from appearance, rumour, language, or group association. A rushed decision can turn an innocent person into a victim and transform a claim of defence into injustice.

### Do Not Reject a Peaceful Greeting

The verse forbids declaring someone “not a believer” when that person offers a greeting of peace in order to obtain a fleeting worldly gain. The immediate context concerns a person who may have been protecting himself while a military patrol sought spoils, but the principle is broad: apparent peacefulness must not be dismissed simply because suspicion is profitable.

### The Prophet’s Warning

A famous report describes Usāmah ibn Zayd killing a man after he uttered the testimony of faith, fearing that the man had said it only to escape. The Prophet ﷺ strongly rebuked him and asked whether he had opened the man’s heart. This demonstrates the seriousness of judging hidden faith and the need to act upon outwardly offered safety.

### Muslims Were Once Like Them

The believers are reminded that they were once like those they now encounter before Allah blessed them with Islam. This memory should produce humility and hope, not contempt. Guidance is a gift, and the person who seems far from faith today may be guided tomorrow.

### Rejecting Greed in Conflict

Seeking a fleeting gain can mean taking property, spoils, status, revenge, or a reputation for victory. The promise of Allah’s abundant bounty is better than unlawful profit. A commander must not create religious excuses to enrich soldiers or satisfy political ambition.

### Evidence and Due Process

The command to be sure requires investigation, distinction between combatants and civilians, and lawful authority. Individuals cannot use a religious label to bypass evidence. Peaceful greetings, treaties, and offers of safety must be respected.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:95

> Those who stay at home—except those with valid excuses[215]—are not equal to those who strive in the cause of Allah with their wealth and their lives. Allah has elevated in rank those who strive with their wealth and their lives above those who stay behind ˹with valid excuses˺. Allah has promised each a fine reward, but those who strive will receive a far better reward than others—

**Commentary:**

### Different Degrees of Sacrifice

The verse distinguishes those who strive with wealth and life in Allah’s cause from those who remain at home with valid excuses. The distinction concerns the additional sacrifice and responsibility undertaken, not the inherent worth of a person. Allah promises a fine reward to both groups.

### Valid Excuses Are Honourable

The exception protects people who cannot participate because of illness, disability, age, poverty, family responsibilities, or other legitimate limitations. Islam does not shame the genuinely unable or treat noncombatants as spiritually worthless. A person may serve through prayer, care, finance, medical work, logistics, teaching, or other forms of support.

### Striving with Wealth and Life

Striving, *jihād*, includes spending resources and exerting oneself for a lawful cause. In the immediate context it concerns organized defence, but the broader concept includes resisting injustice, supporting the vulnerable, seeking knowledge, serving society, and struggling against sin. The cause and means must be righteous.

### Not a Licence for Aggression

The verse cannot be separated from Qur’anic limits on fighting. It does not authorize terrorism, private warfare, forced conversion, or harm to civilians. Sacrifice is praiseworthy only when directed toward Allah, grounded in justice, and carried out through lawful means.

### Comparing Effort without Contempt

Allah may elevate those who make greater sacrifices while still promising every sincere believer a fine reward. The person who participates must not despise those who are unable, and the person who remains behind must not mock those who serve. Different responsibilities can coexist within one faithful community.

### Intention and Opportunity

Allah knows what each person could do, what obstacles he faced, and whether he sincerely offered his available means. A small act by someone with little ability may be greater before Allah than a large act performed for reputation.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:96

> far superior ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Him. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### The Higher Reward

This verse completes the promise of the preceding verse. Those who strive sincerely receive superior ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Allah. Their rank is elevated because their sacrifice was greater in the circumstances Allah gave them, not because they are permitted to despise those with different responsibilities.

### Forgiveness with Sacrifice

Even people who strive greatly ask Allah’s forgiveness. Service does not make a person independent of mercy; it increases humility. A believer may perform a courageous deed while still needing forgiveness for shortcomings, intention, or other sins.

### Mercy beyond Measure

Allah’s mercy is the source of the reward. The servant offers effort, wealth, or life, and Allah grants far more than the deed itself could deserve. This protects the believer from pride and gives hope when the sacrifice produces no visible worldly result.

### Rank Is Connected to Circumstance

Allah knows the opportunity, ability, danger, and intention behind each deed. A person who serves while carrying heavy family responsibilities may receive a reward that people cannot compare from the outside. Human beings should not make quick rankings without knowing the full circumstances.

### The Two Names at the End

Allah is *Ghafūr*, abundantly forgiving, and *Raḥīm*, continually merciful. The names place the reward within a relationship of grace. The goal of striving is not fame or violence; it is Allah’s forgiveness, mercy, and pleasure.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:97

> When the angels seize the souls of those who have wronged themselves[216]—scolding them, “What do you think you were doing?” they will reply, “We were oppressed in the land.” The angels will respond, “Was Allah’s earth not spacious enough for you to emigrate?” It is they who will have Hell as their home—what an evil destination!

**Commentary:**

### Remaining under Oppression

The verse describes people who wronged themselves by remaining among hostile forces even though they could have left and joined a safer believing community. They allowed fear, wealth, property, or social attachment to prevent them from protecting their faith and escaping oppression.

The judgement concerns those who had a real ability to leave, not people who were genuinely trapped.

### The Angels’ Question

The angels ask what the people were doing, exposing the weakness of their excuse. “We were oppressed” is not always sufficient when a person possessed a safe way to leave. Allah’s earth is spacious, and migration can be an obligation when remaining in a place makes faith or life impossible to protect.

### Migration Is Not Easy

The verse does not romanticize migration. Leaving one’s home can involve poverty, uncertainty, family separation, and danger. The next verses explicitly exempt helpless men, women, and children who cannot find a way out. The obligation is tied to ability and genuine necessity.

### Wronging Oneself

A person wrongs himself when he knowingly chooses a situation that destroys faith, assists oppression, or makes obedience impossible despite having a viable alternative. He may not blame the environment forever while refusing every responsible means of change.

### Contemporary Applications

The principle may apply to escaping persecution, trafficking, coercive control, violent communities, or an environment that makes basic religious practice impossible. Migration may involve seeking asylum, relocating safely, or using lawful means to protect dependants. It does not justify abandoning contractual duties, entering danger recklessly, or condemning people who cannot leave.

### The Role of Community

A community that calls people to migrate must also offer safety, housing, work, education, and protection. It is unjust to demand that vulnerable people leave while refusing to support them. The command includes responsibility for those who receive migrants.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:98

> Except helpless men, women, and children who cannot afford a way out—

**Commentary:**

### The Exception for the Helpless

This verse qualifies the warning of the previous verse. Helpless men, women, and children who cannot find a way out are not blamed for remaining under oppression. Allah distinguishes inability from deliberate neglect and does not burden people for what they genuinely cannot do.

### Vulnerability Has Many Forms

Inability may involve lack of money, physical disability, illness, imprisonment, blocked borders, threats from abusers, absence of transport, dependent children, or the danger of being caught. A person may desire freedom and still lack a safe route to it.

Observers must not judge someone’s situation from a distance.

### The Duty of Those with Means

The exception is also a call to the strong. Those with resources should create routes of safety for vulnerable people: provide transport, food, shelter, legal assistance, medical care, documentation, and protection. It is not enough to tell an oppressed person to escape while refusing to help make escape possible.

### Children and Dependants

Children cannot usually make independent decisions about migration. Their guardians and communities carry responsibility for their safety, education, and faith. The verse places helpless people within Allah’s mercy rather than treating them as failures.

### Hope without Blame

The verse gives hope to people trapped in war, persecution, poverty, trafficking, or domestic abuse. Allah knows the difference between a person who refuses every safe option and a person for whom no safe option exists.

### Practical Responsibility

People facing danger should seek trusted help, lawful protection, and safe relocation where possible. Communities should support refugees and displaced people rather than using the preceding warning to shame them. Justice requires assessing actual capacity, not imposing an imagined capacity.

### Hope for Those without a Way

Those genuinely unable to escape oppression may hope for Allah’s pardon. Their inability is known to Allah, who judges people according to real capacity rather than impossible demands. ## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:100

> Whoever emigrates in the cause of Allah will find many safe havens and bountiful resources throughout the earth. Those who leave their homes and die while emigrating to Allah and His Messenger—their reward has already been secured with Allah. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### Leaving for Allah

Whoever emigrates for Allah’s sake will find spaciousness, safe havens, and resources in the earth. *Hijrah* in this context means leaving a place where faith or life is endangered for a place where a person can worship and live safely. It requires sincere intention, lawful planning, and trust in Allah.

The promise is not a guarantee of immediate comfort. It assures the migrant that Allah can open paths where the person sees only loss.

### Safety and Provision

The promise of *murāgham*—a place of escape, movement, or refuge—and abundant provision encourages people not to treat a hostile environment as their only possible future. The earth is wide, and Allah can provide through new work, new companions, and unexpected routes of safety.

Communities that receive migrants must help turn this promise into practical reality through housing, employment, protection, and belonging.

### The Reward of Intention

Some migrants leave home but die before reaching their destination. Allah secures their reward because He knows their intention and effort. This principle comforts people whose plans are interrupted by illness, danger, or death.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that actions are judged by intentions. A sincere effort is not erased merely because circumstances prevent completion.

### Migration and Responsibility

Migration should be undertaken with wisdom. A person should protect dependants, fulfil lawful obligations, secure documentation, and avoid exposing others to unnecessary danger. Trust in Allah does not cancel planning; it gives courage to plan without worshipping the plan.

### Forgiveness and Mercy

Allah closes with forgiveness and mercy. Migrants may leave with past mistakes, fear, and uncertainty, but sincere movement toward obedience is met by Allah’s mercy. The new beginning should include repentance, lawful conduct, and service to others.

### A Wider Spiritual Meaning

The verse also points to leaving sin, corrupt environments, and destructive habits for Allah’s sake. Physical relocation is not always possible or required, but every believer can migrate from disobedience toward obedience. The destination is Allah’s pleasure.
